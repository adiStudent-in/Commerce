Session 1

Features and Conditions for Credit Sales

1. What is a Credit Sale?

Credit sale — a sale in which credit is extended to the customer. The customer takes the product now and agrees to pay later. It is a type of trade credit and creates receivables (money owed to the company by customers).

  • Payment is often due within one month of the invoice date, but may be for longer.
  • The amount may be collected as a lump sum, under Hire Purchase, or under the Installment Purchase System.

Why retailers sell on credit:

Benefit Meaning
Meet the competition If competitors offer credit, a business must do the same to stay competitive
Increase in sales If competitors do not offer credit, offering it wins their customers
Better customer loyalty Credit shows trust and respect; customers reward this by continuing to buy

2. Characteristics of Credit Sales

  • The transferor normally deals in goods and services.
  • Title of the goods lies with the seller before they are sold on credit.
  • Fewer formalities, especially in an open account.
  • It is usually extended for three months.
  • It depends on the terms imposed by the seller.
  • No security is required.
  • It can be facilitated with different financial institutions on easy terms and at a continuous rate.
  • Almost half of the short financial requirement of retail is met by this mutual trust and good relation.

3. Credit Sale Agreement and Retail Credit Facility

Credit sale agreement — an agreement for the sale of goods under which the purchase price, or part of it, is payable in installments.

Retail credit facility — a financing method that provides a loan facility to retail consumers for purchasing goods and services. It lends funds to customers who want high-valued items but are short on capital. This gives more consumers access to the retailer's goods. The risk of default is the main reason for the high rate of interest charged.

Structure of a credit agreement — it details the borrower's responsibilities:

Included in the agreement Details
Loan warranties Guarantees connected with the loan
Lending amount How much is being lent
Interest rate Cost of the credit
Loan duration Period of the loan
Default penalties Charge for failure to pay
Repayment terms and conditions How and when to repay
Basic information and purpose Customer details and why the loan is taken

Repayment terms:

Loan type Repayment terms
Term loan Interest rate and date for repayment
Revolving loan Minimum payment amount and recurring payment dates

The agreement also states whether the interest rate may change and the date on which the loan matures, if applicable.

4. Contract of Sale

Contract of sale — a legal contract for the exchange of goods, services or property from seller to buyer for an agreed value in money paid, or the promise to pay it.

  • It is made when there is both an offer and an agreement to buy or sell goods for a price.
  • It can be made in writing or by word of mouth.
  • It is a generic term that includes (a) sale and (b) agreement to sell.

Agreement to sell — sets out the terms and conditions of sale by the seller to the buyer, including the amount at which the goods are to be sold and the future date of full payment.

Essential Elements of a Contract of Sale

Element Meaning
Essential elements of a contract All essentials of a valid contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 must be present: competent parties, free consent, lawful object
Bilateral contract At least two distinct parties — a seller and a buyer
Transfer of property The objective is to transfer general property in the goods from seller to buyer
Goods The subject matter must be goods; the purpose is to transfer property in them
Price as consideration Consideration is price, which may be partly money and partly goods

5. Condition vs Warranty

A condition is the fundamental stipulation of the contract. A warranty is an additional (subsidiary) stipulation.

Basis Condition Warranty
Meaning A requirement or event that must be performed before the completion of another action An assurance by the seller about the state of the product — that the stated facts are genuine
Defined in Section 12(2) of the Indian Sale of Goods Act, 1930 Section 12(3) of the Indian Sale of Goods Act, 1930
Nature Directly associated with the objective of the contract Subsidiary provision related to the object of the contract
Result of breach Termination of the contract Claim damages for the breach
Violation Violation of a condition can be treated as a violation of warranty Violation of a warranty does not affect the condition

Session 2

Credit Checks and Getting Authorisation

1. What is a Credit Check?

Credit check — a search performed by the retailer to evaluate a customer's creditworthiness. After the check, the retailer can assess whether the customer can handle money matters and fulfil the requirements for credit.

The purpose is to reduce the risk of bad debts and identify potential customers for credit sales.

2. Need for Credit Checking

  • It protects the interests of both parties and confirms each has the capacity to enter the transaction.
  • It should be run whenever a customer applies for a loan, hire purchase, credit card, store card or line of credit.
  • It gives information about the customer's mortgage, credit cards, overdrafts, personal loans, car finance, hire purchases and the repayment history of phone accounts.
  • A credit record is an account of any type of credit given to the customer over the last six years. It shows how much credit the customer is using and whether obligations were missed.
  • When applying for credit, the customer gives consent to check the credit file. This reveals the address, current commitments and reliability of the customer.

The criteria vary from customer to customer, based on financial profile and credit history.

3. Positive Credit Reporting

In the past, credit checks recorded only negative behaviour — when customers failed to meet financial obligations. Now the credit score is calculated from that information together with other credit activity, such as previous enquiries from credit providers. This gives a clear picture of the customer's finances and shows whether the customer has recovered from past credit difficulties.

4. Procedure for a Credit Check

Before offering credit, the retailer gets a credit application form filled and signed by the customer. The information collected is:

Information Why it is needed
Release of information The retailer needs the customer's permission for a complete credit check
Signature Shows the customer has read, understood and agrees to the terms and conditions
Address Ensures the credit check pulls up the right information
Employment Shows the employment history and how long the customer stayed at each job

5. Legal and Company Procedures for Authorisation

A person's credit history is private, so a retail firm must seek the customer's permission before accessing any information legally. The customer provides the necessary information for credit purchase; the retailer accesses it and makes a report. The retailer can also ask the customer directly about creditworthiness and record it in the credit report.

6. Steps Before Granting Credit

Step What to do
1. Create a credit policy Every store needs a credit policy with payment policies and expectations
2. Complete the credit application The application should give key information about the customer
3. Check references Ask the customer to list references
4. Run a credit check Reveals any outstanding payments against the customer
5. Request a personal guarantee Not always necessary for a retail store, but it is a personal guarantee from the customer
6. Take security interest in products Because customers can refuse to pay, the retailer should ideally charge security interests
7. Set credit limits and payment terms Set limits for creditworthy customers and decide how many days after delivery full payment is due

Session 3

Processing Credit Requisitions

1. Requisition and Credit Requisition

Requisition — a formal request by a buyer to the seller to sell the desired goods on conditions agreed upon. It generally includes the brand and model name, quantity and required delivery date.

Credit requisition — a request for credit. A valid credit requisition includes the amount and type of credit requested, the applicant's credit score and report, and the means of security for the loan.

Credit limit — the maximum amount of money extended through a line of credit, and the maximum amount of credit allowed to a customer to purchase in a retail store. Credit limits are normally prescribed by the vendor.

2. Information Required in a Credit Requisition

Information What it covers
Desired items or services Details of what the customer wants to buy on credit
Possible vendors Vendors who can supply the required goods
Budget quotations or proposals Vendor's name and details of quotations or proposals received
Delivery instructions How and where the goods are to be delivered
Initial capital details Detailed information about the initial capital
Contact information Details for reaching the buyer
Related accounting details Information connected with accounts

3. Process of Credit Applications

The performance criteria for processing applications from retail customers for credit facilities are:

Step Action
1 Identify the customer's needs and provide credit facilities
2 Clearly explain the features and conditions of the credit facility to the customer
3 The customer submits the application; the requisition must be approved by the retailer
4 Promptly refer to difficulties in processing applications
5 Once approved, the requisition goes to the Sales Manager for authorisation

Session 4

Techniques for Determining Credit Worthiness

1. What is Credit Worthiness?

Credit worthiness — a valuation performed by the retailer that determines the possibility of a customer defaulting, based on earlier debt obligations. It considers factors such as repayment history and credit score. Credit reporting agencies measure the creditworthiness of customers.

2. How a Customer Can Improve Their Credit Score

  • pay on time
  • pay more than the minimum monthly payment
  • pay off debt faster
  • reduce the assessment of late fees

3. How to Check a Customer's Credit Worthiness

Method What it means
Require a credit application Every customer should fill the credit application
Check publicly available information The retailer must check the customer's information before issuing credit
Use credit evaluation tools Tools calculate the customer's creditworthiness

4. The Five Cs of Credit

When retailers extend credit, they are effectively lending customers an amount equal to their purchases. The guidelines followed are the Five Cs of Credit:

C Meaning
Character The customer's integrity and willingness to repay the financial obligation
Capacity The customer's cash inflow and ability to repay the debt
Capital The customer's financial net worth
Collateral The security against the credit
Conditions The economic, family and personal conditions of the customer

Memory hook — Five Cs: Character (will they pay?) · Capacity (can they pay?) · Capital (what are they worth?) · Collateral (what secures it?) · Conditions (what is happening around them?).

  11. Define contract of sale. Is writing compulsory?

    Show answer

    A legal contract exchanging goods, services or property for an agreed price; it may be in writing or by word of mouth.

  12. Sale vs agreement to sell.

    Show answer

    Contract of sale is the generic term; agreement to sell fixes the price and a future date of full payment.

  13. Five essential elements of a contract of sale.

    Show answer

    Essentials of a valid contract, bilateral parties, transfer of property, goods, price as consideration.

  14. Condition vs warranty — meaning, section, breach result.

    Show answer

    Condition = fundamental term, Sec 12(2), breach allows termination. Warranty = subsidiary term, Sec 12(3), breach allows damages.

  15. Does breach of warranty end the contract?

    Show answer

    No. The buyer can only claim damages.

Exam lines (memorise):

  • "A credit sale creates receivables, since the customer takes the goods now and pays later."
  • "A breach of condition allows termination; a breach of warranty allows a claim for damages."
Session 2

Credit Checks and Getting Authorisation

The chapter in 10 lines

  • Credit check — a search by the retailer to evaluate a customer's creditworthiness.
  • Purpose: reduce bad debts and identify potential credit customers.
  • It protects both parties and confirms capacity to enter the transaction.
  • Used for loans, hire purchase, credit cards, store cards, lines of credit.
  • A credit record covers credit given over the last six years — how much is used and whether obligations were missed.
  • The customer gives consent; the file shows address, current commitments, reliability.
  • Positive credit reporting — the score now uses negative history plus other credit activity, such as previous enquiries, and shows recovery from past difficulties.
  • Application form (4 details): release of information · signature · address · employment.
  • Credit history is private — permission is needed before access; the retailer may also ask the customer directly.
  • 7 steps before granting credit.

Session 3

Processing Credit Requisitions

The chapter in 8 lines

  • Requisition — a formal request by a buyer to the seller to sell desired goods on agreed conditions; includes brand and model, quantity, delivery date.
  • Credit requisition — a request for credit; includes the amount and type of credit, the applicant's credit score and report, and the means of security.
  • Credit limit — the maximum amount extended through a line of credit; normally prescribed by the vendor.
  • Requisition details: items, vendors, quotations, delivery, capital, contact, accounts.
  • Process: identify needs → explain features → submit and approve → refer difficulties promptly → Sales Manager authorises.
  • The retailer approves the requisition; the Sales Manager authorises it.

Session 4

Techniques for Determining Credit Worthiness

The chapter in 8 lines

  • Credit worthiness — a valuation by the retailer of the possibility of default, based on earlier debt obligations, repayment history and credit score.
  • Credit reporting agencies measure it.
  • Improve a score: pay on time · pay more than the minimum · pay debt faster · reduce late-fee assessment.
  • 3 ways to check: credit application · publicly available information · credit evaluation tools.
  • Five Cs: Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions.
  • Memory questions: Will they pay? Can they pay? What are they worth? What secures it? What is around them?

The Five Cs

C Meaning Question
Character Integrity and willingness to repay Will they pay?
Capacity Cash inflow and ability to repay Can they pay?
Capital Financial net worth What are they worth?
Collateral Security against the credit What secures it?
Conditions Economic, family and personal conditions What is happening around them?

Recall drill — Session 4

  1. Define credit worthiness.

    Show answer

    A valuation by the retailer of the possibility of a customer defaulting, based on earlier debt obligations, repayment history and credit score.

  2. Who measures creditworthiness?

    Show answer

    Credit reporting agencies.

  3. Four ways a customer can improve their credit score.

    Show answer

    Pay on time, pay more than the minimum, pay off debt faster, reduce late-fee assessment.

  4. Three ways to check creditworthiness.

    Show answer

    Require a credit application, check publicly available information, use credit evaluation tools.

  5. The Five Cs.

    Show answer

    Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions.

  6. Which C is willingness, and which is ability?

    Show answer

    Character = willingness; Capacity = ability (cash inflow).

  7. Which C covers security, and which covers surroundings?

    Show answer

    Collateral = security; Conditions = economic, family and personal circumstances.
