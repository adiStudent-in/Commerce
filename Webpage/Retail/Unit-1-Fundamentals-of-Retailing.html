Session 1

Basics of Retailing

1. What is retailing?

Retailer — a merchant (or agent/business) whose main business is selling directly to ultimate consumers.

Retailing — the last link connecting the individual consumer with the manufacturing and distribution chain. It adds value by breaking bulk and offering variety.

Key points:

  • A retailer is an intermediary in the marketing channel — both a marketer and a customer.
  • Producers can sell directly, but it is inconvenient, expensive and time-consuming. So they depend on retailers.
  • Retail industry in India: 5th largest industry and 2nd largest employer after agriculture.

Distribution channel:

Manufacturer→Wholesaler→Retailer→Consumer
Party Sells to Purpose
Wholesaler Retailer / business Re-sale, buys in bulk, sells in small quantities
Retailer Final consumer Personal use

2. Organised vs Unorganised Retailing

Unorganised retailing — traditional, low-cost retailing. Local kirana shops, owner-manned general stores, paan shops, convenience stores, hand-cart and pavement vendors, mandis, haats, melas. Features: poor/old infrastructure, insufficient funds, no technology, no skilled manpower, not registered, no regular accounts, small and scattered.

Organised retailing — trading by licensed retailers (registered for sales tax, income tax). Corporate-backed hypermarkets, retail chains, large private retail businesses. Features: more convenience, choice and control; comfort and speed.

Base Organised Unorganised
Concept Modern; many items under one roof; systematic and scientific Family-run traditional; poor infrastructure, low funds, no skilled staff
Registration Compulsory by law Not compulsory
Items Large number, many varieties Few items, limited variety
Size and layout Large, attractive layout Small, improper layout
Employees Many, specialised roles Few, one person does many jobs
Capital High; ownership may not enjoy perpetual succession Low; owned by successors
Job security Regular terms, security exists No job security; outside government control
Style Corporate style Sole trading / partnership
Network Many branches at different places Confined to one locality, single unit
Examples Supermarkets, departmental stores, hypermarkets, malls, multilevel marketing, teleshopping Cobbler, vegetable/fruit vendor, kirana, Baniya, paanwala, mandis, haats, melas

4. Functions of a Retailer

  1. Breaking bulk — opens large packages and sells smaller quantities as per need.
  2. Providing product information — shows how the product meets the customer's need; product knowledge means more sales.
  3. Providing customer services — home delivery, gift wrapping, credit facility, after-sales service.
  4. Creating a convenient, comfortable and pleasant shopping experience — right place, right time, friendly environment, soft music, proper lighting, space to move, courteous staff.
  5. Providing feedback to producers — retailers meet customers first-hand, so they tell producers what customers want.

The book says "four main functions" but lists five. Learn all five.

Essential requirements of a retailer — the retailer should:

  • set up the shop where customers are attracted
  • stock goods customers need
  • sell quality goods at competitive prices
  • stay updated on market trends
  • ensure window and counter display
  • always be accessible to customers

Retailer's services to customers:

  • sell in small quantities at reasonable prices
  • meet demand and keep required stock
  • give information needed for buying
  • guide on replacement for damages
  • display and demonstrate goods
  • offer credit to regular, reliable customers

1. Customer vs Consumer

Customer Consumer
Buys the product Uses the product
May buy for re-sale (entrepreneur) or for end use Actual end-user

You can be both in one transaction.

Two broad segments of customers:

  2. End-user — does not sell, is the actual consumer.

Type Behaviour How to handle

Discount Visit often but buy only when discounts are offered or only low-cost products Use offers carefully

3. Customer Service

Customer service — identifying and fulfilling a customer's needs in a friendly and helpful manner. It includes assistance before, during and after sales.

The 4Ps of Customer Service

P Meaning
Promptness Deliver on time; no delay, including in cancellations
Politeness Put customer's needs first; welcome them, use their name, be respectful, listen, watch your language
Professionalism Show competence, knowledge and expertise; avoid anxiety, frustration, fear or weakness
Personalisation Remember name, preferences and unique needs; makes customers feel valued and increases loyalty

Customer Service Functions (6)

Function What it means
Customer support Answer questions about products/services; suggest other products
Pre-sales Give necessary product information before purchase
Marketing Reply to mails, telemarketing, manage campaigns, conduct surveys and polls
Sales Physical selling and e-selling
Technical support Data verification, address updates, application support, problem resolution
Social media customer service Serve customers through Facebook, Twitter, etc.; high engagement if handled well

4. Customer Satisfaction

Definition: a measure of how products and services supplied by a company meet or surpass customer expectation.

Why it matters:

  • shows repurchase intention and loyalty
  • acts as a point of discrimination (differentiator)
  • reduces customer retrenchment (loss of customers)
  • increases lifetime value of the customer
  • reduces negative word of mouth
  • helps retain customers

Customer retention strategies:

  • write blogs that educate customers
  • send emails for special promotions
  • conduct satisfaction surveys
  • offer personalised experience
  • set customer expectations early
  • use social media to build an engaged community
  • learn from the best
  • develop questionnaires and run surveys

Skills for Handling Retail Business

1. What is a Skill?

Skill — the ability to do a task with pre-determined results within a given period of time, energy, or both. It is the ability to work with expertise.

General skills Specific skills
Time management, teamwork, leadership, self-motivation Skills used in a particular job (e.g. carpentry for a carpenter)

2. Essentials of Skill Development

  1. Taking stock — assess where you stand; take realistic inventory of personal and professional assets.
  2. Creating a plan — a development plan that helps you reach goals.
  3. Creating an environment for development — practice consistent self-directed learning, note problems/mistakes, interact with peers professionally, pay attention to health.

3. The 9 Skills of a Sales Associate

Skill What it looks like on the floor
Patience Staying calm with difficult or angry customers; defusing tough situations
Attentiveness Accuracy at the POS and in arranging merchandise; attention to detail
Communication Listening effectively, explaining product benefits and transaction steps, pleasant demeanour
Product knowledge Reading up on products so questions are answered accurately
Positive language Language is part of persuasion; small changes create happy customers
Acting skills Handling boring, angry, complaining customers you cannot fully satisfy
Time management Solving customer problems in limited time, leaving time for improvement
Goal oriented Focusing on tasks and their end results; setting goals and using strategies
Resilience Thinking clearly, deciding quickly, not taking things personally on tough days

Q01 What is retailing? Who are the intermediaries in the distribution channel? ›

Retailing is the sale of goods and services to the ultimate consumer. A retailer is a merchant, or occasionally an agent or business enterprise, whose main business is selling directly to ultimate consumers. Retailing is the last link connecting the individual consumer with the manufacturing and distribution chain. It adds value by breaking bulk and providing a wide variety of goods and services as per customer needs.

The distribution channel has two intermediaries:

  1. Wholesaler — buys large quantities from manufacturers and sells smaller quantities to retailers for business or re-sale. It operates between the manufacturer and the retailer.
  2. Retailer — sells to the ultimate consumer. It operates between the wholesaler and the consumer.
Manufacturer→Wholesaler→Retailer→Consumer

Producers can sell directly, but that route is inconvenient, expensive and time-consuming, so they depend on retailers.

Q02 Differentiate between organised and unorganised retailing with examples. ›

Organised retailing is trading undertaken by licensed retailers, that is, those registered for sales tax, income tax, etc. It includes corporate-backed hypermarkets and retail chains and large privately owned retail businesses.

Unorganised retailing refers to traditional formats of low-cost retailing such as local kirana shops, owner-manned general stores, paan shops, convenience stores, hand-cart and pavement vendors, mandis, haats and melas. These units are not registered under any legal provision and do not maintain regular accounts.

Size and layout Large outlet with attractive layout and design Small outlet with improper layout

Q04 Explain the types of retail business on the basis of ownership. ›

1. Independent retailer. A small business owned and operated by an individual rather than a corporation. The retailer has direct contact with customers and decides the retail strategy based on store location and product mix. Examples: local Baniya, kirana store, paanwala.

2. Corporate retail chain. A group of stores engaged in the same kind of business in different locations, under the same ownership and management. Chain stores work to strengthen their brand identity in the market. Examples: Reliance, Bata, Arrow, Louis Philippe, Food World.

3. Franchising. An arrangement where the franchiser grants the franchisee the right to use its trademark or trade name and certain business systems and processes to produce and market a good or service according to given specifications. Examples: McDonald's, Pizza Hut, Van Heusen.

4. Consumer cooperative. An enterprise owned and managed by consumers to fulfil the needs and aspirations of its members. It usually takes the form of retail outlets owned and operated by consumers, who also provide the capital to launch or purchase the enterprise. Example: Apna Bazaar, Mumbai.

Q05 Narrate the functions of a retailer. ›

A retailer buys varieties of goods from manufacturers or wholesalers after estimating customer demand, and so provides a wide range of choice. Its functions are:

  1. Breaking bulk into small quantities. Producers ship goods in large quantities to reduce transport cost. Retailers open these large packages and make the product available in smaller quantities as per consumer needs.
  2. Providing product information to customers. The retailer shows how a product will address the customer's needs. Product knowledge means more sales.
  3. Providing customer services. Helping customers efficiently and in a friendly manner, including free home delivery, gift wrapping, credit facility and after-sales service.
  4. Creating a convenient, comfortable and pleasant shopping experience. Convenience means speed, being in the right place at the right time and delivering what the customer needs there and then. A warm welcome, right products, soft music, proper lighting, space for movement and courteous, well-dressed employees increase sales.
  5. Providing feedback to producers about customer needs. Retailers interact with customers first-hand, so they understand customer needs and pass this information to producers, who can then improve the product and keep it in demand at a competitive price.
Q06 Differentiate between a customer and a consumer. Explain the types of customers. ›

A customer buys products from a business. A consumer uses the business products. One person can be both a customer and a consumer in the same transaction.

Customer Consumer
Buys the goods or services Consumes or uses them
May buy for re-sale (entrepreneur) or for end use Is the actual end-user
Pays the price Judges the quality through use

Customers may belong to two broad segments: an entrepreneur, who purchases goods for re-sale, and an end-user, who does not sell but is the actual consumer.

Types of customers:

Type Behaviour
Loyal customers Few in number but bring more sales and profit; revisit the organisation over time, so they need regular contact, time and effort
Discount customers Frequent visitors, but buy only when discounts are offered on regular products and brands, or buy only low-cost products
Impulsive customers Have no specific item in mind; buy what they find good at that moment. They want the supplier to display all useful products
Need-based customers Product-specific; buy only habitual items or items they specifically need. Frequent visitors who rarely buy, so they are difficult to satisfy

Q07 Explain the 4Ps of customer service and the customer service functions of a sales associate. ›

Customer service is the task of identifying and fulfilling a customer's needs in a friendly and helpful manner. It includes assistance before, during and after sales. Its four features, the 4Ps, are:

P Meaning
Promptness Fulfil promises and deliver products on time, with no delay in delivery or in the case of cancellation
Politeness Put the customer's needs first; be welcoming, use the customer's name, be respectful and interested, listen and watch your language
Professionalism Every interaction should be professional; show competence, knowledge and expertise, and avoid anxiety, frustration, fear or weakness
Personalisation Remember the customer's name, preferences and unique needs; this makes customers feel valued and increases loyalty and engagement

Customer service functions of a sales associate:

  1. Customer support — interacting with potential customers, answering questions about products and services, and suggesting other products.
  2. Pre-sales — providing necessary information about products before purchase.
  3. Marketing — responding to customers, replying to mails, telemarketing, managing campaigns, conducting surveys and polling.
  4. Sales — physical selling as well as e-selling.
  5. Technical support — data verification, address updates, application support and problem resolution.
  6. Social media customer service — providing service through channels such as Facebook and Twitter.
Q08 What is customer satisfaction? Explain its importance and any four customer retention strategies. ›

Customer satisfaction is a measure of how products and services supplied by a company meet or surpass customer expectations. It occurs when the value and service provided through the retailing experience meet or exceed consumer expectations.

Importance:

  1. It indicates consumer repurchase intentions and loyalty.
  2. It shows a point of discrimination, setting the retailer apart from competitors.
  3. It reduces customer retrenchment.
  4. It increases the lifetime value of the customer.
  5. It reduces negative word of mouth.
  6. It helps in retaining customers.

Customer retention strategies (any four):

  1. Writing blogs to educate customers.
  2. Sending emails about special promotions.
  3. Conducting customer satisfaction surveys.
  4. Offering a personalised experience.
  5. Setting customer expectations early.
  6. Using social media to build a group of engaged customers.
  7. Learning from the best.
  8. Developing questionnaires and conducting surveys.
Q09 Explain the skills required by a sales associate. ›

A skill is the ability to do a task with pre-determined results within a given period of time, energy, or both. It is the ability to work with expertise. The skills required by a sales associate are:

  1. Patience — staying calm and diffusing tough situations with difficult customers.
  2. Attentiveness — focusing on detail so that POS transactions are accurate and the sales floor looks its best.
  3. Communication skills — listening effectively, explaining product benefits and transaction processes clearly, and keeping a pleasant, approachable manner.
  4. Product knowledge — reading about the product so that customer questions are answered accurately.
  5. Positive language — using language as a tool of persuasion, since customers form perceptions from the retailer's words.
  6. Acting skills — handling angry, boring or complaining customers whom one cannot fully satisfy.
  7. Time management skills — solving customer problems in limited time and still finding time to improve the business.
  8. Goal orientation — focusing on tasks and their end results, setting goals and using strategies to attain them.
  9. Resilience — thinking clearly, deciding quickly and not taking things personally on difficult days.
Q10 Explain the duties and responsibilities of a sales associate. ›

A sales associate, also called a customer services associate, is responsible for all sales activities — greeting customers, answering questions, offering assistance, suggesting items, lending opinions and giving product information.

Duties:

  1. Handling problems — handling customer complaints, checking that they are valid and solving them within the limits of authority.
  2. Assisting sales — helping customers identify and purchase products; selling, restocking and merchandising to maximise sales and revenue.
  3. Clerical tasks — receiving merchandise, totalling bills, accepting payments and taking orders.
  4. Job-specific responsibilities — greeting customers, responding to questions, improving engagement with merchandise, operating cash registers, managing financial transactions and balancing drawers.

Responsibilities:

  1. Interact with customers — face-to-face, over the phone, by email or through chat programs.
  2. Work directly with customers in the retail store.
  3. Resolve customer issues — incorrect price listed for a product, returns and similar problems.
  4. Listen to customers — effective listening solves problems easily and quickly.
  5. Remain friendly and polite at all times.

Special activities: prepare for customer inquiries, respond to inquiries, document inquiries, improve quality of service, identify new products, update job knowledge and participate in educational opportunities.

Also examinable (not part of the ten): e-retailers sell directly to consumers over the Internet (Amazon, Flipkart) and carry the risk of defective products and unsafe card transactions; vending machines dispense a product after money is inserted and a selection is made; automated retail stores are self-service standalone kiosks in high-traffic locations, open 24×7 and accepting credit cards (Zoom shops, Red-box).

Type In one line

4 types by ownership

Mnemonic: "I Can Franchise Cooperatives."

Type Meaning Example
Independent retailer Small business owned and run by an individual; direct customer contact; strategy by location and product mix Kirana, Baniya, paanwala
Corporate retail chain Same business, same ownership and management, different locations Reliance, Bata, Arrow, Food World

3 modern retailers — EVA

  • E-retailer — sells over the Internet (Amazon, Flipkart); convenient, but risk of defective products and unsafe card transactions.
  • Vending machine — insert money, select, product is dispensed.
  • Automated retail store — self-service standalone kiosks in high-traffic places, open 24×7, accept cards (Zoom shops, Red-box).

5 functions of a retailer — "Big Products Create Easy Feedback"

  1. Breaking bulk into small quantities.
  2. Providing product information.
  3. Customer services — home delivery, gift wrap, credit, after-sales.
  4. Experience — right place, right time, lighting, music, space, courteous staff.
  5. Feedback to producers about customer needs.

Essential requirements (6): shop where customers are attracted · stock what they need · quality at competitive price · stay updated on trends · window and counter display · always accessible.

Services to customers (6): small quantities at fair prices · maintain stock · give buying information · guide on replacements · display and demonstrate · credit to regular, reliable customers.

One person can be both in the same transaction. Two segments: entrepreneur (buys for re-sale) and end-user (actual consumer).

Type Behaviour Handle by

Wandering New; checking needs and prominent products; least interest in buying Information, no pressure

Customer service and the 4Ps

Customer service = identifying and fulfilling a customer's needs in a friendly and helpful manner, before, during and after sales.

P Meaning
Promptness On time, no delay, including cancellations
Politeness Customer first, welcome, use their name, listen, watch language
Professionalism Competence, knowledge, expertise; no anxiety/frustration/fear
Personalisation Remember name, preferences, needs; builds loyalty

6 customer service functions — "Customers Prefer More Sales, Tech & Social."

  1. Customer support — answer questions, suggest products.
  2. Pre-sales — information before purchase.
  3. Marketing — mails, telemarketing, campaigns, surveys, polls.
  4. Sales — physical selling + e-selling.
  5. Technical support — data verification, address updates, application support, problem resolution.
  6. Social media service — Facebook, Twitter, etc.

Customer satisfaction

Definition: how well products and services meet or surpass customer expectation.

Why it matters (6): repurchase intention and loyalty · point of discrimination · less customer retrenchment · higher lifetime value · less negative word of mouth · customer retention.

Retention strategies (any four): blogs that educate · promotion emails · satisfaction surveys · personalised experience · set expectations early · social media community · learn from the best · questionnaires and surveys.

Session 3

Skills for Handling Retail Business

The basics

  • Skill = ability to do a task with pre-determined results in a given time/energy; working with expertise.
  • General skills — time management, teamwork, leadership, self-motivation.
  • Specific skills — used in one job (carpentry for a carpenter).
  • Skill development (3): taking stock → creating a plan → creating an environment for development (self-directed practice, note mistakes, interact with peers, watch health).

9 skills of a sales associate — P-A-C-P-P-A-T-G-R

Patient Attentive Communicators Provide Product Answers, Track Goals, Recover.

Session 4

Duties and Responsibilities of a Sales Associate

Sales associate (also customer services associate) — responsible for all sales activities: greeting, answering questions, assisting, suggesting items, lending opinions, giving product information.

4 duties — "Handle All Customer Jobs."

  1. Handling problems — complaints, verify validity, solve within authority.
  2. Assisting sales — help customers identify and buy; selling, restocking, merchandising; maximise sales and revenue.
  3. Clerical tasks — receiving merchandise, totalling bills, accepting payments, taking orders.
  4. Job-specific responsibilities — greeting, answering, engaging customers with merchandise, operating cash registers, managing transactions, balancing drawers.

5 responsibilities — "I Will Really Listen First."

  1. Interact with customers — face-to-face, phone, email, chat.
  2. Work directly with customers in the store.
  3. Resolve customer issues — wrong price, returns.
  4. Listen to customers — effective listening solves problems fast.
  5. Friendly and polite at all times.

Special activities (7)

Prepare for inquiries · respond to inquiries · document inquiries · improve service quality · identify new products · update job knowledge · attend educational opportunities.

On-the-job skills (9)

Product knowledge · informing customers · data entry · analysing information · verbal communication · reporting · managing processes · general consulting · multitasking.

  1. Who is a sales associate?

    Show answer

    The person responsible for all sales activities — greeting, answering, assisting, suggesting, giving product information; also called customer services associate.

  2. Four duties.

    Show answer

    Handling problems, assisting sales, clerical tasks, job-specific responsibilities.

  3. Three clerical tasks.

    Show answer

    Receiving merchandise, totalling bills, accepting payments, taking orders.

  4. Five responsibilities.

    Show answer

    Interact, work directly with customers, resolve issues, listen, stay friendly and polite.

  5. Any four special activities.

    Show answer

    Prepare, respond and document inquiries; improve service quality; identify new products; update job knowledge; attend training.

  6. Any four on-the-job skills.

    Show answer

    Product knowledge, informing customers, data entry, analysing information, verbal communication, reporting, managing processes, consulting, multitasking.

  7. Duty vs responsibility.

    Show answer

    A duty is a task you perform (billing, restocking); a responsibility is an outcome or behaviour you own (resolving issues, listening, being polite).

Exam line: "A sales associate handles complaints within the limits of their authority and resolves issues such as wrong pricing and returns."
