Specialist Support to Customers

How a sales associate gives product information, motivates customers to buy, personalises service, and supports the customer after the sale.

Who gives this support? Customer support specialists are the direct link between a company and its existing and potential customers. They answer phones, give troubleshooting information, report and analyse customer needs, issue billing details, and open and close customer accounts.

Session 1

Providing Product Information

1. What is a product?

  • In marketing — anything offered to a market that might satisfy a want or need.
  • In retailing — products are called merchandise.
  • In manufacturing — bought as raw materials and sold as finished goods.
  • Every product is made at a cost and sold at a price. The price depends on the market, quality, marketing and the segment targeted.
Type Meaning Example
Tangible product Physical object perceived by touch Building, vehicle, gadget, clothing
Intangible product Perceived only indirectly Insurance policy, services

Services fall under intangible products, and can be durable or non-durable.

2. Features of a product

A product feature is a distinguishing characteristic of a product or service that boosts its appeal to potential buyers, and can be used to build a marketing strategy around its usefulness to the target consumer.

  • Features describe colour, branding, packing, labelling, varieties and capabilities.
  • The book also lists size, shape, weight, price, brand name, reliability and durability as product features.
  • Fig. 4.1 places six features around the product: item number, designation, size, colour, weight and price.
  • Each feature carries a matching benefit for the end user.

3. Why customers need product information (8 points)

Product information can persuade a consumer to buy. The product must fulfil a need or solve a problem — for example, a customer buying a camera for a night event needs a camera that works at night. Information helps the customer to:

  1. identify the kind of product they are going to buy
  2. check whether it meets their needs and interest
  3. know its size, shape, colour, price, style, brand and availability
  4. learn how to use the product properly
  5. compare products on quality, price, quantity, availability and substitutes
  6. understand packaging, modes of payment, modes of delivery and other specifications
  7. know the terms and conditions for return, insurance and damage
  8. understand any other information related to the product

4. Ways of providing product information (8)

Method What it involves
One-to-one Customer and sales associate meet face-to-face and information is given directly
Product videos Show how a product works, its features and its appearance
Product webpage Highlights product offerings with a brief description
Audio-tapes, CDs and podcasts Product information given through recorded audio
Newsletters Product information carried as advertisements in newsletters
Email Information distributed electronically in bulk through a mailer
Banners and posters Spread product information visually
Pamphlets, leaflets, brochures Carry name, price, brand, specifications, terms and conditions

Rich vs lean media (Fig. 4.2): effectiveness of communication falls as the medium becomes leaner.

  1. Face-to-face — most effective, richest
  2. Video conferencing
  3. Telephone
  4. 2-way radio
  5. Written, addressed documents (letters, email)
  6. Unaddressed documents (bulk mail, posters) — least effective, leanest

Benefits of good product information: it speeds up the time to bring a product to market, cuts the time to place new products in catalogues and websites, and increases revenue.

Session 2

Techniques to Encourage Customers to Buy Products

1. Customer motivation

Consumer motivation — an internal state that drives people to identify and buy products or services that fulfil conscious and unconscious needs or desires.

  • It is driven by the customer's thoughts, feelings and beliefs.
  • If a product fulfils customer needs, it motivates repeat purchase.
  • Motivation levels vary from low to high depending on how important the purchase is. Influences: familiarity with the purchase, status factors, overall expense and value.
  • Where rewards are low — as with groceries — motivation is low and involves little decision-making.

2. Six ways to motivate customers to buy

Technique What it means
Promote positivity Positivity persuades, inspires and motivates. An engaging experience with strong service creates positive emotions and acts as a hook for loyalty
Inform the customer Focus on the benefits of the product and what it does for the customer. Consumers look for trustworthy, knowledgeable people; trust leads to long-term relationships and repeat sales
Offer testers Let uncertain customers try the product before committing, giving a chance to build a relationship and convert them
Encourage brand loyalty Reward customers with stronger offers or incentives to motivate repeat custom
Communicate a consistent message One clear, compelling voice across all touch points; a clear offer is easier to sell
Improve the brand image Customer decisions are influenced by awareness and relationship with the brand; the image must reflect company values and engage the target audience

3. Customer service policy

A customer service policy is a written document that employees follow when dealing with customers who are not satisfied with the company's products or services. It also lists how employees should behave to improve the overall customer experience.

Why it matters:

  • every person understands the importance of serving customers consistently with the same high quality, whatever their department or rank
  • employees know they must put customer needs before their own
  • employees know that empathetic, caring and responsive service is everyone's responsibility

4. Policies for giving information to customers (7)

While giving information, a sales associate must:

  • be polite and friendly with the customer
  • respect customer differences such as values, cultures and beliefs
  • respect the dignity of all customers
  • listen carefully and respond attentively to inquiries
  • protect confidentiality of information
  • acknowledge customers by name
  • introduce himself or herself by name and role

5. Policies for customer complaints — 6 steps

A complaint handling policy lays down the clear process for resolving and managing customer complaints about products and services.

Step Action
1 Take the complaint seriously and check that the details given by the customer are correct
2 Take the matter to the store manager; document the complaint in all aspects
3 Take sufficient action according to the type of complaint — by the sales associate or store manager
4 If it is not solved at store level, inform the retailer's head office and take orders from them
5 If the same complaint recurs regularly, prepare an action plan for it
6 Do an evaluation on a pre-determined date to confirm improvement has occurred

6. Techniques to encourage customer loyalty (4)

Technique What to do
Keep customers informed Tell them about the business and how products and services help them. Send a newsletter or greeting card series by mail or email regularly; keep the website full of customer care information
Send handwritten notes The cheapest and most effective loyalty-building and retention tool; leaves a positive impact
Talk to the customers Call to inform them about offers, chat briefly, and ask if there is anything you can do for them
Remember important occasions Wish customers on birthdays, anniversaries and other important days; send personalised cards or letters

Session 3

Personalised Customer Service

1. What is personalisation?

Personalisation means meeting the customer's needs more effectively and efficiently, making interactions faster and easier, and so increasing customer satisfaction and the likelihood of repeat visits.

  • Consumers have a lot of choices, so personalised service sets the business apart from competitors.
  • For many consumers, a superior level of customer service is enough to decide which store to shop from.

2. Customer service standards

Customer service standards are a company's rules or guidelines that inform and shape the customer's relationship with the business at every step of the customer experience.

  • Companies adopt them to empower employees to resolve complaints, problems and questions as quickly and satisfactorily as possible, for both the customer and the company.
  • Strong standards help retain more loyal customers and increase profits significantly.
  • They cover all points of contact the business has with the customer.

The 7 standards (Fig. 4.3), in order

# Standard In simple words
1 Listen Hear the customer out
2 Understand Grasp the actual issue
3 Mitigate Reduce the harm or impact
4 Communicate Keep the customer informed
5 Act Take action
6 Remedy Fix the problem
7 Prevent Stop it from recurring

3. Best practices for personalised service (6)

  • Greet the customers
  • Listen effectively to the customers' requests
  • Promptly take necessary action
  • Inform the customer of unexpected delays
  • Touch base with customers to update them
  • Respond immediately

4. Customised sales support — 8 areas where the customer needs the associate

Area What to do
Respond as quickly as possible Speed is one of the biggest factors in good service, especially for time-sensitive requests
Know your customers Know their wants and needs, remember their names and previous conversations, and note what was discussed last time
Create and maintain rapport Trust builds rapport; keep commitments and show interest in the customer's well-being beyond profit. Finding information or connecting them with other suppliers makes a big difference
Identify needs, preferences and priorities Research the industry and ask lots of specific questions. Listen and ask; always check for additional or related needs; then present the right products, services and solutions
Ask how much the customer wants to spend Use their disposable income; as products become commodities, price matters even more
Provide information on additional or related products Tell the customer about other products in the store, whether or not they need them
Encourage the customer on purchase decisions A purchase decision is a process of thinking: identify the need, generate choices, choose a definite product
Maintain good relations Listen and support; give honest answers and no fake promises. Building relationships is a customer retention strategy

5. Steps in personalised sales support (7)

Step What it means
Track consumer actions Behavioural tracking and price targeting identify the items customers have enquired about; offers and prices are tailored to buyer behaviour
Analyse customers by context Customer analysis uses behavioural data for market segmentation and predictive analytics; used for direct marketing, site selection and CRM
Use individual targeting rules Information on an individual's behaviour — products of interest, searches made — decides which products to display to that customer
Create rules-based cross-selling recommendations Cross-selling = selling an additional product or service to an existing customer. Profitable for the seller; convenient for the customer, who buys supplementary products in one place and saves time
Mobile commerce personalisation Shoppers now complete the whole journey on the smart device. A personalised mobile site delivers tailored content based on browsing behaviour and context — location, weather, time of day
Generate loyalty through personalised services Rewards for new customers, discounts for store members, special and seasonal offerings, inviting customers to keep in touch
Generate repeat sales Tell customers about promotions, sell gift cards, send periodical updates and latest offers, offer product demonstrations, draw attention with additional benefits

Session 4

Post-Sales Service Support

1. What is post-sales service support?

Post-sales service support, also called after-sales service, is any service provided after a customer has purchased a product.

  • It may be provided by the retailer, the manufacturer, or a third-party customer service or training provider.
  • It includes warranty service, training, repair or upgrade, and other services.
  • It is part of the company's overall marketing strategy; some customers choose a company because of its after-sales service.
  • Without good after-sales support, a product can be difficult to sell.

2. Types of post-sales service support (3)

Type What it covers
Online support / technical support / help desk Assistance with technology merchandise — PCs, software products, mobile phones, televisions and most electrical or mechanical products
Onsite support / customer support Services that help customers with products in the store or mall at the time of sale; helps them make cost-effective use of products
Customer training Gives customers the knowledge, skills and competencies needed to use the product, so they know its actual utility

3. Customer data

Customer data is information held on file about customers by a store or other business, usually including names, contact details and buying habits. It also means the firsthand responses obtained from customers through investigation or by asking direct questions.

Why collect it: to tailor customer service programmes and grow the business. Find out what customers buy, why they buy and how often they buy. Potential customers who have made enquiries can also be included.

4. Ways to collect customer information (7)

Method How it works
Order forms Let customers order a product the business cannot supply immediately. For products with release dates, use pre-order forms; the customer commits to buy and often pre-pays
Enquiries Record details of every enquiry so you can follow up; also a chance to mention the website, mailing list or social media pages
Recording complaints Record the complaint, who made it, why, which staff member heard it and what was done to resolve it
Warranty cards Used where products or services come with a warranty
Customer satisfaction surveys Survey cards where customers rate aspects of service out of 5; the back of the card asks for personal details
Feedback Feedback cards on specific aspects of the business, or open-ended like a suggestion box
Company website A 'contact us' form for enquiries, or a mailing list sign-up for news and updates

5. Storing, maintaining and using customer information

Stage Key point
Storing Information is confidential and must be stored securely. Simple way: an electronic spreadsheet. Detailed information: a CRM (customer relationship manager) database, which helps analyse purchasing trends and identify the best customers
Maintaining Information is only useful if it is up-to-date; check accuracy regularly and update it
Using Ask customers whether they want information or updates, and give them the option to opt out. Spamming without consent gets a negative reaction

6. Benefits of maintaining records of customer information (7)

  • helps to work more efficiently
  • protects the interests of government and of the agency
  • protects employee and citizen rights
  • displays cost and impact on the business
  • enables re-examination of processes and decisions
  • helps in research and development activities
  • enables steadiness and stability in the business

7. Customer data protection

Data protection must rank with product quality, safety, environmental protection and anti-corruption. Customer information must be protected for the sake of the brand, and breaches can result in heavy fines.

How data gets compromised: sensitive data exposed by employees, virus and malware attacks, data lost or stolen, data lost in transit.

5 steps to keep data safe:

  1. Keep all IT systems' anti-virus and firewall protection up-to-date.
  2. Educate staff on a good data security policy — for example, locking computers when not in use.
  3. Prevent removal of sensitive customer data on removable media such as USB drives.
  4. Make mobile devices use secure connections such as a VPN when accessing company servers.
  5. Take regular backups, using an off-site backup service for redundancy.

8. Company laws and policies on data protection

Customer data identifies a person — name, address, contact number. A business must have legitimate reasons for collecting and using personal data, must not use it in ways with unjustified adverse effects, must be open and honest about how it will use the data, and must do nothing unlawful with it.

Collected data should be Collected data should not be
Accurate and up-to-date Communicated informally to customers
Gathered for lawful purposes only Stored for more than a specified amount of time
Processed within legal and moral boundaries Transferred to other retailers that lack adequate data protection policies
— Distributed to parties other than those agreed by the data's owner

Additional policies:

  • let customers know what data is collected
  • update them on how the company will process it
  • update them on who else will access it
  • have terms for lost or corrupted data
  • allow customers to modify, erase or correct their data

9. Promises to customers

Keeping promises is a business necessity for customer loyalty. Customers have many choices and are unlikely to stay with a company that does not inspire confidence and trust. Set clear expectations at the start, keep customers informed of inadvertent delays, and promise only as much as you can deliver.

Type What is promised
1. Offer-related Trade discount, cash discount, free shipping, free gift, weekly discount, discounts on special goods
2. Quality-related Usability, appearance, reliability, operability, stability, durability
3. Easy shopping Flexibility, availability, transportation, durability, delivery, efficiency, reputability
4. Competent service After-sale service — delivery of products, support by phone calls, follow-up with the customer, technical support

Q01 Define product and explain its features. (Short answer, 3–4 marks) ›

A product is anything that can be offered to a market that might satisfy a want or need. In retailing, products are called merchandise. In manufacturing, products are bought as raw materials and sold as finished goods. A product may be tangible — a physical object perceived by touch, such as a building, vehicle, gadget or clothing — or intangible, perceived only indirectly, such as an insurance policy. Services are broadly intangible products and may be durable or non-durable.

Every product is made at a cost and sold at a price; the price depends on the market, quality, marketing and the segment targeted.

A product feature is a distinguishing characteristic of a product or service that boosts its appeal to potential buyers. Features describe colour, branding, packing, labelling, varieties and capabilities, and also include size, shape, weight, price, brand name, reliability and durability. Each feature has a matching benefit for the end user, and features can be used to build a marketing strategy.

Customers need to know exactly what they are getting, and whether the product is better than the competition, faster, more efficient or more user-friendly.

Product information helps customers to:

  1. identify the kind of product they are going to buy
  2. check whether it meets their needs and interest
  3. understand its size, shape, colour, price, style, brand and availability
  4. learn how to use it properly
  5. compare products on quality, price, quantity, availability and substitutes
  6. understand packaging, modes of payment, modes of delivery and other specifications
  7. know the terms and conditions for return, insurance and damage

Q03 Explain the ways of providing product information. (Long answer, 5 marks) ›

A sales associate provides product information so the customer can make the right buying decision. The methods are:

  1. One-to-one — the customer and sales associate meet face-to-face and information is given directly.
  2. Product videos — a great way to show how a product works, its features and appearance.
  3. Product webpage — highlights product offerings with a brief description.
  4. Audio-tapes, CDs and podcasts — information given through recorded audio.
  5. Newsletters — product information carried as advertisements.
  6. Email — distributed electronically in bulk through a mailer.
  7. Banners and posters — spread information visually.
  8. Pamphlets, leaflets, brochures — carry the name, price, brand, specifications, terms and conditions.

Effectiveness of the medium (Fig. 4.2): face-to-face is the richest, followed by video conferencing, telephone and 2-way radio. Written, addressed documents such as letters and email come next, and unaddressed documents such as bulk mail and posters are the leanest. Good product information speeds up the time to bring a product to market, places new products faster in catalogues and websites, and increases revenue.

Q06 Explain the steps of the complaint handling process. (Long answer, 5 marks) ›

A complaint handling policy lays down the clear process and measures for resolving and managing customer complaints about products and services.

  1. Take it seriously — the sales associate must take the matter seriously and ensure the details given by the customer are correct.
  2. Escalate and document — take the matter to the store manager and document the complaint in all aspects.
  3. Act — take sufficient action according to the type of complaint, by the sales associate or the store manager.
  4. Head office — if the complaint is not solved at store level, inform the retailer's head office and take orders from them to solve the problem.
  5. Action plan — if the same type of complaint occurs regularly, prepare an action plan for it.
  6. Evaluate — undertake an evaluation on a pre-determined date to ensure improvement has occurred.
Q07 Explain the techniques to encourage customer loyalty. (Short answer, 3–4 marks) ›
  1. Keep customers informed — tell them about the business and how products and services help them; send a newsletter or greeting card series by mail or email regularly and keep the website full of customer care information.
  2. Send handwritten notes — the cheapest and most effective loyalty-building and customer retention tool; a personal note leaves a positive impact.
  3. Talk to the customers — call to inform them about offers, chat briefly and ask if there is anything you can do for them.
  4. Remember important occasions — wish customers on birthdays, anniversaries and other important days, and send personalised cards or letters.

The seven standards (Fig. 4.3) are:

  1. Listen — hear the customer out.
  2. Understand — grasp the actual issue.
  3. Mitigate — reduce the harm or impact.
  4. Communicate — keep the customer informed.
  5. Act — take action.
  6. Remedy — fix the problem.
  7. Prevent — stop it from recurring.

Personalised service itself means meeting needs more effectively and efficiently, making interactions faster and easier, and increasing satisfaction and repeat visits. Best practices include greeting customers, listening effectively, acting promptly, informing them of unexpected delays, touching base to update them, and responding immediately.

Q09 What is post-sales service support? Explain its types. (Long answer, 5 marks) ›

Post-sales service support, also called after-sales service, is any service provided after a customer has purchased a product. It may be provided by the retailer, the manufacturer, or a third-party customer service or training provider, and may include warranty service, training, repair or upgrade. It is part of the company's overall marketing strategy, and some customers choose a company because of its after-sales service. Without good after-sales support, a product can be difficult to sell.

Types:

  1. Online support / technical support / help desk — assistance with technology merchandise such as PCs, software products, mobile phones, televisions and most electrical or mechanical products.
  2. Onsite support / customer support — services that help customers with products in the store or mall at the time of sale, helping them make cost-effective use of the products.
  3. Customer training — providing the knowledge, skills and competencies required to use the product, so customers know its actual utility.
Q10 What is customer data? How is it collected, stored and protected? (Long answer, 5 marks) ›

Customer data is information held on file about customers by a store or other business, usually including names, contact details and buying habits. It also refers to the firsthand responses obtained from customers through investigation or by asking direct questions. Retailers collect it to tailor customer service programmes and grow the business — finding out what customers buy, why, and how often.

Ways of collecting it: order forms and pre-order forms · enquiries · recording complaints (who made it, why, which staff member heard it, what was done) · warranty cards · customer satisfaction surveys · feedback cards or a suggestion box · the company website's 'contact us' form and mailing list.

Storing and maintaining: information is confidential and must be stored securely — an electronic spreadsheet for simple data, a CRM database for detailed data, which helps analyse purchasing trends and identify the best customers. Records must be checked and updated regularly, and customers must be asked for consent with an option to opt out.

Protection: keep anti-virus and firewalls up to date; educate staff on data security and lock computers; prevent removal of data on USB drives; use secure connections such as a VPN on mobile devices; take regular back-ups, preferably off-site. Data must be accurate and up-to-date, gathered for lawful purposes only, and processed within legal and moral boundaries. It must not be communicated informally, stored longer than a specified time, transferred to retailers without adequate data protection policies, or distributed to parties other than those agreed by the data's owner. Customers should be told what is collected, how it is processed and who will access it, and should be allowed to modify, erase or correct their data.

Also examinable (not part of the ten): the four types of promises to customers — offer-related (trade discount, cash discount, free shipping, free gift), quality-related (usability, appearance, reliability, durability), easy shopping (flexibility, availability, transportation, delivery, efficiency) and competent service (delivery, phone support, follow-up, technical support).

Customer data protection

Data protection must rank with product quality, safety, environmental protection and anti-corruption. Customer information must be protected for the sake of the brand, and breaches can result in heavy fines.

How data gets compromised: sensitive data exposed by employees · virus and malware attacks · data lost or stolen · data lost in transit.

5 steps to keep data safe ("Always Educate People, Verify Backups."):

Letter Step
Always Keep all IT systems' anti-virus and firewall protection up to date
Educate Educate staff on a good data security policy — for example, locking computers when not in use
People Prevent removal of sensitive customer data on removable media such as USB drives
Verify Make mobile devices use secure connections such as a VPN when accessing company servers

Laws and policies — data should / should not

A business must have legitimate reasons for collecting and using personal data, must not use it in ways with unjustified adverse effects, must be open and honest, and must do nothing unlawful with it.
