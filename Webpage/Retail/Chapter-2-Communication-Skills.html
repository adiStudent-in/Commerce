Communication Skills

Class 11 Retail — Read, speak, write & listen well. Quick Revision Notes.

📌 Big Picture

Communication = the sharing of information between two or more people to reach a common understanding.
Communication skills = the ability to speak and write properly and listen and read well in any situation.

1. 💬 Introduction to Communication

Why Communication Matters

Communication skills are needed to:

  • Inform – give facts or information (e.g., telling a friend the exam timetable).
  • Influence – change or convince someone (e.g., negotiating a lower price with a shopkeeper).
  • Express feelings – share emotions (e.g., your happiness about scoring well).

Elements of Communication (Two-way exchange)

  • Giving information: Speaking, Writing
  • Receiving information: Listening, Reading

Perspectives in Communication

Perspectives = our ideas, views, or fixed ways of thinking that affect communication (e.g., thinking your father is strict even when he is polite).

8 Factors that act as communication barriers

FactorHow it blocks communication
LanguageWrong words, unfamiliar language, lack of detail (a Hindi speaker vs a Mandarin speaker)
Visual PerceptionBrain making sense of what we see (reading a half-drawn picture wrongly)
Past ExperiencePast hurt stops clarity ("This shopkeeper cheated me before")
PrejudiceFixed ideas ("No one in my class likes me")
FeelingsEmotions like distrust, lack of interest ("I'm not feeling well")
EnvironmentNoise or disturbance (loud music at a function)
Personal factorsFear, low confidence, habits
CultureSame sign may mean different things (a thumbs-up = 'good job' for some, insult for others)

The 7 Cs of Effective Communication

Clear · Concise · Concrete · Correct · Coherent · Complete · Courteous

2. 🎤 Verbal Communication

Verbal communication = sharing information using words.
TypeWhat it isExamples
Oral / SpokenTalking, where you can see/listenFace-to-face talk, phone calls, classroom teaching, public speeches
WrittenWritten or typed wordsLetters, notes, emails, SMS, books, newspapers

Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Advantages: Easy and quick; get a quick response; easier to exchange ideas.
  • Disadvantages: Cultural differences (languages, accents, colloquial phrases); meaning can become confusing if the right words are not used.

Public Speaking — The 3 Ps

  1. Prepare – Think about your topic, your listeners' needs, and the best way to explain; write what you plan to say.
  2. Practice – Practice alone, in front of a mirror, then with family/friends; speak clearly, loudly, at the right speed.
  3. Perform – Take deep breaths if nervous; think of your prep and speak confidently.

3. 👤 Non-Verbal Communication

Non-verbal communication = the message we send without using words — through expressions, gestures, and body posture.

Why it matters: Most of our day-to-day communication is non-verbal (body movements and voice control). The right gestures make your message stronger and help you understand your audience's reaction.

Types of Non-Verbal Communication

TypeWhat it meansHow to use it well
Facial ExpressionShows feelings (smile = happy, gloomy = sad)Keep face relaxed; match expression to your words; nod when agreeing
PosturePosition of the body; shows confidenceSit/stand straight, shoulders relaxed, hands by your sides
Gestures / Body LanguageMovements of hands/head (waving, pointing)Avoid pointing fingers; keep hands out of pockets; bend head slightly to show attention
TouchShake hands, pat on the backFirm handshake shows confidence; avoid hair-stroking, nose-scratching in formal talk
SpaceDistance between two peopleMaintain the right space based on the closeness of the relationship
Eye ContactLooking at the speaker shows interestLook at the speaker, pleasant face, break the look every few seconds
ParalanguageTone, speed, volume of voiceProper tone & volume; moderate speed (too fast = nervous, too slow = serious)

Visual Communication

Sending/understanding messages only through images or pictures — no language needed, simple and same everywhere.

💡 Examples: Traffic lights (red=stop, yellow=wait, green=go), no-horn sign, toilet signs, railway-crossing sign, flammable & slippery-surface signs, pause icon, no-smoking sign.

4. ✏️ Writing Skills: Parts of Speech

  • Sentence = a group of words that communicates a complete thought ("Raju goes to school"). Starts with a capital letter; ends with ?, ., or !.
  • Phrase = a group of words that does not make complete sense ("Raju goes").

5. 📝 Writing Skills: Sentences

Parts of a Sentence

  • Subject – the person/thing that does the action.
  • Verb – the action.
  • Object – the person/thing that receives the action. ("Divya reads a book" → Subject: Divya, Verb: reads, Object: a book)

Types of Objects

  • Direct object – directly acted on by the verb (answers "what?"). "Abdul gave a gift to his mother." → gift = direct
  • Indirect object – receives the direct object (answers "to whom?"). "…to his mother" → mother = indirect

Active vs Passive Voice

VoiceCharactersExample
Active voiceSubject does the action"Sanjay broke the glass."
Passive voiceSubject receives the action"The glass was broken by Sanjay."

4 Types of Sentences (by purpose)

TypePurposeEnding
Statement / DeclarativeStates a fact; gives information.
Question / InterrogativeAsks a question?
Exclamation / ExclamatoryStrong emotion (joy, sadness, fear)!
Order / ImperativeGives an order, command, request, advice. or !

Paragraphs

A group of sentences sharing a common idea = a paragraph. Start a new paragraph when the idea changes.

6. 🎤 Pronunciation Basics

Pronunciation = the way you say a word (spelling is how you write it).
Phonetics = the study of the sounds we make when we speak.

English has 26 letters (A–Z) but more than 26 sounds — so spelling does not always match pronunciation (e.g., "a" differs in *cat, car, call*).

Sound TypeWhat it isExamples
Vowels5 letters (a,e,i,o,u) → 12 vowel sounds; made with an open mouthbit, sleep, book
DiphthongsCombination of two vowel sounds, one into anotherhouse ('ou'), boat ('oa'), cow
ConsonantsAny sound that is neither a vowel nor a diphthongp, t, k, l sounds (pen, book)

7. 👋 Greetings and Introductions

Greetings = words we use before we start talking to someone. Introduction = telling others (or ourselves) about ourselves to people who don't know us.

GreetingUsed forExamples
FormalPeople we don't know, seniors, elders, teachers, customers"Good morning Sir/Ma'am!", "Hello Sir. How are you?"
InformalFriends, family, known people"Hey!", "Hi!", "Hey, how are you doing?"

Greetings by time of day

TimeGreeting
Early morning – 11.59 amGood morning
12 pm – 5 pmGood afternoon
5 pm – midnightGood evening (we say "Good night" at the end of a conversation)
  • Introducing yourself: tell your name + general info (hometown, job/subjects, interests). Look at the person and smile.
  • Introducing others: tell their name, what they do, how you know them, or something interesting about them.

8. 📜 Talking About Self

Use nouns (naming words — Ali, Delhi, football) and verbs (doing words — play, like, eat). Verbs ending in -ing can act as nouns (swimming, cycling).

A self-introduction covers: Name → What you do → Age → Hometown → Likes → Dislikes → Strengths → Weaknesses.

💡 Example: "I am Harish Sethi. I am 15 years old and I live in Agra. I like playing chess and I dislike summertime. I can write stories very well."

Filling a Form

A form = a printed/typed page with blank spaces for information. It usually has: Name, Date of Birth, Address, PIN code, Signature.

Example: Date of Birth: 05-12-1999 · Address: Sukh Nivas, No. 13, Raja Nagar, Agra · Pin: 282001

9. ❓ Asking Questions I (5W + 1H)

A question = a request for information. Asking questions helps us gain knowledge, check correctness, avoid confusion, and start conversations.

Get complete information with the 5W + 1H method

Question wordUsed to ask aboutExample
WhoPeopleWho is the new student?
WhereA placeWhere does she live?
WhenTimeWhen did she join school?
WhatA thing, idea, actionWhat is her favourite subject?
WhyReasonWhy is she not talking to anyone?
HowMethod / wayHow can I help her?

10. 💭 Asking Questions II — Open & Closed Questions

TypeAnswer styleHow to form itExample
Close-endedYes / NoAdd a helping verb (Be, Do, Have) before the subject, or swap subject & verb"Do I like it?" / "Is he there?"
Open-endedDetailed answerUse question words (What, Why, Who, How, When, Where)"What did you do?"
💡 Note: When converting a sentence to a close-ended question, the helping verb changes with the subject, but the main verb stays unchanged ("I cook" → "Do I cook?", "She can cook" → "Can she cook?").
