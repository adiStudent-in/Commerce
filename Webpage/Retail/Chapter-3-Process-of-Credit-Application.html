Class 11 Retail (Sales Associate) — Unit 2 · Quick Revision Notes

📌 Big Picture

Credit sale = goods supplied now, payment deferred for a period after supply.
Offering credit increases the risk of being paid late (or not at all) → for new customers, always consider upfront or on-delivery payment.

Steps the sales associate follows while granting customer credit:

  1. Create credit policy
  2. Obtain credit application
  3. Check customer references
  4. Get a personal guarantee
  5. Run a credit check
  6. Set limits of credit and payment terms

1. 💳 Features and Conditions for Credit Sales

1.1 Meaning & Benefits of Credit Sales

Credit sales refer to sales that involve extending credit to the customer — the customer takes the product now and agrees to pay later.

  • Credit sales are a type of trade credit; they create receivables (money owed to the company from customers).
  • Terms often require payment within one month of the invoice date; longer periods are also possible.
  • The due amount may be collected in different forms: lump-sum payment, Hire Purchase system, Installment Purchase System.

Why retail firms sell goods on credit — benefits

BenefitExplanation
Meet the competitionWhen competitors sell on credit, a business must do the same just to stay competitive.
Increase in salesIf competitors do NOT offer credit terms, you gain sales by offering them — customers buy from you instead of paying cash to competitors.
Better customer loyaltyOffering credit shows you respect and trust customers to pay before due dates; customers reward this confidence by continuing to buy.

Characteristics of credit sales

  • The transferor normally deals in goods and services.
  • The title of the goods lies with the seller before it is sold on credit.
  • There are fewer formalities, especially in case of an open account.
  • It is usually extended for three months.
  • It depends on terms imposed by the seller.
  • No security is required.
  • It can be facilitated with different financial institutions with easy terms and at a continuous rate.
  • Almost half of the short financial requirement of retail is met by this type of mutual trust and good relation.

1.2 Credit Sale Agreement & Retail Credit Facility

A credit sale agreement is an agreement for the sale of goods under which the purchase price (or part of it) is payable by installments.

Retail credit facility = a financing method that provides a loan facility to retail consumers for purchasing goods and services — it lends funds to customers who want high-valued items but are short on capital.

⚠️ Risk of default is the main factor behind the high rate of interest charged by retail credit facilities.

Structure of a credit agreement

The credit agreement details the borrower's responsibilities and includes:

  • Loan warranties · lending amounts · interest rates · loan duration
  • Default penalties · repayment terms and conditions
  • Basic information of the customer, and the purpose of the loan

Repayment terms of credit facility

  • Term loan → interest rate and date for repayment.
  • Revolving loan → minimum payment amount and recurring payment dates.
  • The agreement details whether interest rates may change, and specifies the date on which the loan matures (if applicable).

1.3 Contract of Sale

A contract of sale = a legal contract for the exchange of goods, services or property from seller to buyer for an agreed value in money paid or the promise to pay the same.

  • It is made when there is both an offer as well as an agreement to buy or sell goods for a price.
  • It can be made in writing or by word of mouth.
  • It is a generic term which includes: (a) Sale, and (b) Agreement to sell.

Agreement to sell constitutes the terms and conditions of sale — the amount at which it is to be sold and the future date of full payment.

Essential elements of a contract of sale

ElementExplanation
Essential elements of a contractAll essentials of a valid contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 — competent parties, free consent, lawful object, etc.
Bilateral contractAt least two distinct parties — a seller and a buyer.
Transfer of propertyObjective is to transfer the general property (ownership) of the goods from seller to buyer.
GoodsThe subject-matter must be some goods; the purpose is to transfer property in these goods.
Price — the considerationThe consideration is price, which may be partly in money and partly in goods.

1.4 Difference Between Condition and Warranty

Condition = the fundamental stipulation of the contract of sale. Warranty = an additional stipulation — a written guarantee issued to the buyer by the manufacturer or seller to repair or replace the product within a specified time if required.

BasisConditionWarranty
MeaningA requirement or event that should be performed before the completion of another action.An assurance given by the seller to the buyer about the state of the product — that the prescribed facts are genuine.
Defined inSection 12 (2) of Indian Sale of Goods Act, 1930Section 12 (3) of Indian Sale of Goods Act, 1930
What is it?Directly associated with the objective of the contract.A subsidiary provision related to the object of the contract.
Result of breachTermination of contractClaim damages for the breach
ViolationViolation of a condition can be regarded as a violation of the warranty.Violation of a warranty does not affect the condition.

2. 🔎 Credit Checks and Getting Authorisation

A retail store's primary objective is to enhance profits. Credit checks identify potential customers with repaying capacity → this reduces the risk of bad debts.

2.1 Meaning & Need for Credit Checking

A credit check = a sort of search performed by the retailer to evaluate a customer's creditworthiness — after the check, the retailer can assess whether the customer can handle money matters and fulfill the requirements for credit.

Need for credit checking

  • Protects the interests of parties — ensures each party has the capacity to enter into a transaction.
  • A credit check should be run whenever customers apply for a loan, hire purchase, credit card, store card or line of credit.
  • It provides information about the customer's mortgage, credit cards, arranged overdrafts, personal loans, car finance, hire purchases, and the repayment history of phone accounts, etc.
  • A credit record = an account of any type of credit given for the last six years — reveals how much money the customer accessed and whether the customer failed to make any obligations.
  • When applying for credit, the customer gives consent to check his/her credit file — this shows the customer's address, current commitments and reliability.
  • Criteria vary from customer to customer — based on financial profile and credit history.

Positive credit reporting

  • In the past, credit checks included only negative behaviour (when customers failed to meet obligations in financial dealings).
  • Now the credit score is calculated on this information together with other credit activity in the file — e.g., previous enquiries from credit providers.
  • This gives a clear picture of the customer's finances and shows if the customer has recovered from any credit difficulties in the past.

2.2 Procedure for Credit Check & Legal Authorisation

Before extending credit, retailers get a credit application form filled and signed by the customer. Information collected from the customer for the credit check:

Info requiredWhy it matters
Release of informationThe retailer needs the customer's permission to do a complete credit check.
SignatureMeans the customer has read, understood and agrees with all terms and conditions listed.
AddressA correct address ensures the credit check pulls up the right information.
EmploymentAccurate employment info lets the retailer look into the customer's employment history (how long at each job).

Legal and company procedures for getting authorisation

A person's credit history is private — a retail firm must seek permission from the customer before accessing any information legally.

How to get a credit report

  • Customers provide the necessary information for the credit purchase; the retailer accesses the information and makes a report.
  • The retailer can directly ask customers for their creditworthiness and write it in their credit report.

2.3 Steps to Follow Before Granting a Credit

  1. Create credit policy — every retail store must create its credit policy; it includes payment policies and expectations.
  2. Customers must complete the credit application — the application should provide key information about the customers.
  3. Check the customer's references — asking customers to list references helps.
  4. Run credit check — reveals any outstanding payments against the customer.
  5. Request personal guarantee from customer — not necessary in the case of a retail store, but it is a personal guarantee from the customer.
  6. Take security interest in products — since customers can refuse to pay as agreed, a retailer should ideally charge security interests.
  7. Set credit limits and payment terms — set limits for customers who seem creditworthy; decide how many days after delivery the full payment is made.

3. 📄 Processing Credit Requisitions

3.1 Requisition, Credit Requisition & Credit Limit

Requisition = a formal request by a buyer to the seller to sell the desired goods on conditions agreed upon — it generally includes the brand and model name, quantity and the required delivery date, etc.

Credit requisition = a request for credit — made when a buyer asks the seller to provide a credit facility for the purchase of goods. A valid credit requisition includes:

  • The amount and type of credit requested
  • The applicant's credit score and report
  • Means of security for the loan

The rules for availing credit facilities are normally provided by the organisation; normally, credit limits are prescribed by the vendor.

💡 Credit limit = the maximum amount of money extended through a line of credit, and the maximum amount of credit allowed to a customer to purchase in a retail store.

3.2 Information Required & Process of Applications

Required infoWhat it means
Desired items or servicesCustomers seeking credit must mention the details of the items or services to be purchased on credit.

Performance criteria for processing applications

  1. Identify the customer's needs and provide credit facilities.
  2. Clearly explain the features and conditions of credit facilities to the customer.
  3. The customer should submit the application, and the requisition must be approved by the retailer.
  4. Promptly refer to difficulties in processing applications.
  5. Once approved, the requisitions go to the Sales Manager for authorisation procedures.

4. 💯 Techniques for Determining Credit Worthiness

4.1 Meaning & Improving Credit Score

Creditworthiness = a valuation performed by the retailer that determines the possibility of a customer to default based on his/her earlier debt obligations. It considers factors like repayment history and credit score.

  • Credit reporting agencies measure the creditworthiness of customers.
  • Creditworthiness can also be determined by studying and analysing the income statement and balance sheet of the business.

How customers can improve their credit score

  • Pay on time
  • Pay more than the minimum monthly payment
  • Pay their debt faster
  • Reduce the assessment of late fees

How to check a customer's creditworthiness

  1. Require a credit application — every customer should fill the credit application.
  2. Check publicly available information — every retailer must check the customer's information before issuing credit.
  3. Use credit evaluation tools — to calculate the customer's creditworthiness.

4.2 The Five Cs of Credit

When retailers expand credit to customers, they are essentially providing credit equal to the amount of their purchases. The guidelines to be followed are inclusive of 'The Five Cs of Credit':

CWhat it refers to
CharacterThe customer's integrity and willingness to repay the financial obligation.
CapacityThe customer's cash inflow and ability to repay the debt.
CapitalThe customer's financial net worth.
CollateralThe security against credit.
ConditionsThe economic, family and personal conditions of customers.
