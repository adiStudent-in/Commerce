Mechanism for Customers to Choose Right Products

Class 11 Retail (Sales Associate) — Unit 3 · Quick Revision Notes

📌 Big Picture

Products are put on display only after analysing customer demand. The more customers know about a product, the better they can make a purchase decision. The product with maximum demand must be available instantly.

A smart sales associate performs demonstrative procedures in a logical manner — giving customers maximum value (“maximum through minimum investments”) while getting the maximum sale turnover for the business.

💡 Structure — 4 sessions: 1. Methods of Selling · 2. Sales Promotional Activities · 3. Responding to Questions and Comments · 4. Techniques of Closing a Sale

1. 🛒 Methods of Selling

Sale = the act of selling a product in return for money — the beginning of a relation between customer and vendor (or an extension of that relationship).

1.1 Retail Selling Methods

MethodMeaning
Direct salesDirect personal demonstration and sale to the consumer. Two business models: single-level marketing and multilevel marketing.
Proforma sales“Proforma” = a document done as a courtesy or formality; the proforma sale is a sales quote prepared in the form of a proforma invoice.
Agency-basedA contractual arrangement in which the agent has the right to negotiate on the sale of the principal's goods/services, and gets a commission or fee in exchange.
Travelling salesmanA representative of a firm who visits shops and businesses to show samples and gain orders — also called a commercial traveller, door-to-door salesman or hawker.
Auction saleA public sale — goods are sold to the highest bidder (one who quoted the highest price).
B2B saleBusiness-to-business — sales made to another businessman rather than an individual customer (industrial/professional sale).
Electronic-based salesA web-based sale — includes both B2B and B2C sales; uses EDI (Electronic Data Interchange).
Indirect salesSale of a good or service by a third party (partner or affiliate) rather than the company's own personnel.

Types of agents in agency-based sales: Sales agents · Sales outsourcing through direct branded representation · Transaction sales · Consultative sales · Consignment sales · Telemarketing or telesales · Retail sales.

1.2 Arranging Products in the Store

Products from different manufacturers are arranged systematically in shelves based on the space provided. Display is prioritised on the basis of the manufacturer's competitiveness — some manufacturers buy the display window section and floor space to promote their product.

Need: products divided section-wise give a clear view of all products, make it easy for customers to pick products, and improve the profit of the store.

Why rearrangement is done

ReasonExplanation
Repeat customersGives the store a fresh look — displays more products, so customers visit again and sales volume increases.
Sales promotionsRearranging stock for sales promotion increases customers.
Brand imageRoutine (monthly) rearrangement and changing colour schemes maintain a new, fresh style.
SeasonalProducts are rearranged to display seasonal products.

Procedure for arranging products — floor plans

Floor planFeatures
Straight floor planProducts arranged in a straight line; most commonly used in Indian retail; economical.
Diagonal floor planMore visibility for staff and customers; requires a small space.
Angular floor planGives a spacious look; used by high-value/high-end retailers; reduces display area and focuses on a few popular products.
Geometric floor planUses racks and other fixtures; used for trendy products like cosmetics and watches.

1.3 Identifying the Products Responsible for Sale (CSA Steps)

  1. The CSA must have full information about the retail store and the products being sold.
  2. Must follow the instructions of the store manager/supervisor regarding stock received from different manufacturers.
  3. The manager allocates product sections to the CSA (vegetable, housekeeping material, stationery, clothes sections, etc.).
  4. The CSA is responsible for the allotted section and must hold a full record of stock from receipt till the product is sold.
  5. The CSA must have complete knowledge of the product and the skills to sell it.

2. 🏁 Sales Promotional Activities

2.1 Meaning, Types & Objectives

Sales promotion = the fastest growing area of marketing activities — all activities which supplement, coordinate and make an effective effort of personal selling and advertising.
  • It is a major component of promotion — communicating with an audience through a variety of non-personal and non-media channels.
  • The firm provides special offers (discounts, etc.); offers are a direct proposition that forms part of the deal.
  • It increases sales and stimulates consumers to buy more.

Types of sales promotion activities

TypeTarget
Consumer promotionFinal buyers
Business promotionBusiness customers
Trade promotionWholesalers and retailers
Sales force promotionSales force members

Objectives of sales promotion (a short-term tactic to boost sales)

  1. Stimulate inquiries from consumers asking for detailed information about a product.
  2. Increase product trials — expecting the trial leads to customer satisfaction.
  3. Encourage repurchase of goods and develop loyalty in the customer's mind.
  4. Initiate inventory building by retailers to give consumers a ready supply.
  5. Get dealers' promotional assistance by coordinating with personal selling.

2.2 Sales Promotion Techniques

(a) Vendor or producer originated

TechniqueMeaning
In-store activitiesTargeted to give cash or kind advantage to customers — price-off pack, premiums, small gifts, cooperative promotion, sampling, coupons, buy-one-get-one-free, multipacks, etc.
Point-of-sale displayVendor materials placed near products customers often buy — special fixtures (paperback publishers' racks, dry battery stand, cold drink racks), display signs, leaflets, demonstrations.
Trade incentivesTradeoffs or cash incentives directly provided by vendors to retailers — cash discounts, special credit terms, goods in lieu of cash, staff incentives (benefit may not reach customers).
Direct promotion to customersVendor promotes at the consumer's doorsteps — distribution of coupons and free samples.
Pack designOne of the most important techniques — retailers support packs with a better package design, sometimes accepting the design over its contents.

(b) Retailer originated

TechniqueMeaning
Window displayDisplaying items in the shop window to attract customers to the store.
In-store displayMass display, multiple product promotion, demonstrations, store loyalty cards, free gifts, point-of-sale display, celebrity promotion, joint promotion with other retailers, incentives to staff, etc.

2.3 Influencing Customers & Product Knowledge

Ways to influence customer buying

  1. Identify the prospective customer and start a dialogue in a friendly and humorous manner.
  2. Collect as much relevant information as possible about the product's sale presentation.
  3. Enlighten the product's features and advantages in comparison with the competitor's product.
  4. Present or demonstrate the product with patience, in an interesting manner.
  5. Attend to objections immediately and clarify the issues.
  6. Enlighten on the product's warranty period and assure other sales services if needed.
  7. Maintain continuous relations with customers to get feedback on product satisfaction.
  8. Improve selling skills — patience, good communication, reliability, politeness, caring for customers.
  9. Do not lose temper when dealing with aggressive behaviour of a potential consumer.
  10. Use sales promotion techniques if required, to convince customers to buy.

Knowledge of the nature of product

The sales associate needs detailed knowledge of the product to attend to grievances, complaints or clarifications sought by the customer. Explicit characteristics = those over which there is a common agreement (existence and attributes); implicit characteristics = points of disagreement — every person sees the product differently.

Explicit characteristicMeaning
Physical configurationEvery product has shape, size, density, odour, taste, texture, colour, weight and other physical attributes.
Associated serviceBefore-sales service (product demonstration, informing about credit facilities) and after-sales service (delivery, installation, spare parts, repair service, warranties).
Package and brand nameHelps differentiate the product from competitors' brands and highlight product features.
Product sizeWidth = product lines; depth = number of items in every line; consistency = similarity among product lines.
Product life cycleStages: introduction → growth → maturity → decline; the SA must know which stage the product is in to formulate selling strategies.
Implicit characteristicMeaning
Product symbolismA product is a symbol by virtue of form, size, colour and function — study it as status symbol, economy, performance, etc., in relation to customers' needs.
Communication mediaThe consumer's personal interpretation of symbols, mediated by culture groups, group influence and personality — whether product information is given or hidden; what the product is used for, at what rate, made by whom, and where available.
Product perceptionPerception is a physio-psychological process; how consumers perceive products influences their marketability.
Product evaluationReveals consumer satisfaction — evaluation = comparing the efforts involved and rewards received by the consumer.

3. 💬 Responding to Questions and Comments

The sales associate is the only person directly approached by the customer — training is needed to handle tough questions and situations, and gives the flexibility to adapt and add to their own personality.

3.1 Handling Questions & Situations

When the sales associate is unable to answer a query

  • Do not panic — the responsibility is to show firmness to make things right.
  • Never say "I don't know" — instead say: "It is a valid question, let me find out the right thing for you."
  • Place the customer's need as important over the situation of not knowing the answer.
  • Take the question to the concerned staff or superior, find the answer, and respond.

When an item is not available

The main skill is the use of positive language — it avoids nasty or rash reactions:

  • With positive language: "The product will be available next month and I will place an order for you, if needed."
  • Without positive language: "The product required is unavailable."
💡 Tip: Positive language replaces negative phrases ("I can't…").

When transferring a call

Transfer the customer's call to the concerned department to help them — customers feel happy if their concerns are properly addressed.

Comments made on the product

  • Customers may have valuable comments and suggestions on usage and improvement, but holding the product's vision is the responsibility of the sales associate.
  • Do NOT respond "We will take a look" — it gives false hope to customers and may disappoint them later.

Favours that cannot be granted

  • Most requests are reasonable — make every effort to make the customer happy.
  • For difficult requests, respond using positive language and very politely.
  • Showing possible alternatives is one of the best ways to say 'No'.

3.2 Defective Products & Closing With a Customer

When a product is defective

Receiving a defective product is very discouraging to the customer — showing empathy is very important.

"I am so sorry to disappoint you. There was a mistake in the manufacturing process or shipping. Can I send a new one to you?"

Such a response completes two objectives: (a) understanding the customer's frustrating experience — explains what the problem is and how an immediate solution is given; (b) depending on the product and business, the SA can offer "Should I send you a full refund?"

Closing with a customer

A sales associate should always aim to 'close' a conversation — this does NOT mean closing the sale; it means the customer is happy about the product. Three important things:

  1. Care about getting what the customer requires.
  2. Provide the right information if they need any query answered.
  3. The customer determines what is needed and that the need is met in the right way.

End conversations with phrases like "I am happy to help you, Sir or Ma'am" or "We are happy to serve you" — they make a big impact.

3.3 Dealing with Angry Customers

StepWhat to do
Apologise sincerely"I am sorry" is a mandatory response — make a personal apology for the ugly experience.
SympathiseAngry customers need to be empathised with — "I understand how upsetting that must have been."
Accept responsibilityAccept responsibility for the customer's unhappiness — it does not make the SA 'at fault'.
Prepare to helpFix the problem — place a replacement order; even if handled perfectly, if a customer cannot calm down, still try to make the best effort.

4. ✅ Techniques of Closing a Sale

Closing a sale = completing a sales transaction — the final step while making a sales call. Effectively, it is the customer signing on the dotted line of the agreement. The sales process is adaptive — each situation may be different.

4.1 Factors Influencing Successful Closing of Sales

FactorMeaning
Salesman's personalityThe sum total of everything about the sales person that influences prospects favourably — physical (sound health, neat appearance, voice), mental (alertness, confidence, enthusiasm, observation), social (friendly appearance, courtesy, cooperation, well-mannered) and character.
Awareness of company policiesKnowledge of the company's history, origin and later changes, current affairs, reputation, philosophy and policies — helps clarify all queries and induce purchases.
Product informationEvery possible information about the product — models, size, weight, ingredients, packaging, quality, price, discount offered, benefits, replacement, after-sales service, etc.
Need of customerStudy the prospective customer's buying behaviour, likes, dislikes, nature and type to adopt suitable strategies.
Information on competing productsKnowledge of product availability and competitors helps highlight relative merits — closing becomes an easy task.

4.2 Techniques of Closing a Sale

TechniqueMeaning
Natural closeAlso called the 'ask for it' close — the customer should not sense a special formula; closing appears as a natural development from the sales conversation.
The order form closeQuestions asked are not in direct reference to the purchase; sound natural and avoid rejection — e.g., "Sir, shall I make the invoice or bill?"
The alternative closeAssumes the customer will definitely buy — only design, colour, style, shape are undecided; present alternative options to provoke a final choice; product presentation is very important.
The summary closeThe SA summarises the list of benefits — "We have reached the end of sale, so may I highlight the main points so that we could close a deal." One of the most natural methods — used for unpredictable customers.
The cautionary tale closeMotivates undecided customers to take a purchase decision by showing the implication of failing to decide at that very moment; the SA tries not to make up stories.
The final objection closeThe customer may have an objection right at the end of the sale process and feel hesitant to mention it; the SA must persuade the customer to communicate the objection point.
The conditional closeFor customers very slow in making decisions who find excuses to delay — the SA assures and develops confidence, offering help in a subtle way.
The lost sales approachDon't feel the sale is lost with hesitant or objecting customers — lost sales can be converted into actual sales with further conversation and additional selling efforts; maintain a sense of balance.
The assumption closeThe customer's manners, body language or comments may indicate a decision to buy — the SA makes an assumption close, giving the customer a chance to make a painless decision.
The balance sheet closeThe SA assists an indecisive prospect to list on paper the 'arguments for' and 'arguments against' a particular product choice.
