Mechanism for Customers to Choose Right Products

How a sales associate sells — the methods of selling, the promotions that pull customers in, the way to answer questions, and the techniques that close the sale.

4 sessions ≈ 25 min read 25 min quick recall 10 exam Q&A Built from both source notes

Unit at a glance

Why this unit exists: products are displayed after analysing demand, so the customer can find what they need. A sales associate identifies products, builds an effective product list, sorts and narrows choices by category, and educates the customer — the more the customer knows, the better the purchase decision. The product with maximum demand must be available instantly. A good display brings a wider range of customers. Customers always try to get the maximum out of minimum investment, and a smart associate delivers the best output through logical, demonstrative steps.

Session 1

Methods of Selling

1. What is a Sale?

Sale — the act of selling a product in return for money. It is the beginning of a relation between customer and vendor, or an extension of that relationship.

2. The 8 Methods of Selling

Method Meaning Key point
Direct sales Direct personal demonstration and sale of products and services to the consumer Two business models: single-level marketing and multilevel marketing. Makes money by selling directly to consumers
Proforma sales A sales quote prepared in the form of a proforma invoice "Proforma" = a document that serves as a courtesy or meets a minimum requirement, conforms to a norm, or is done as a formality
Agency-based Contractual arrangement where the agent negotiates the sale of the principal's goods and services Agent gets a commission or fee. Agent types: sales agents, sales outsourcing through direct branded representation, transaction sales, consultative sales, consignment sales, telemarketing/telesales, retail sales
Travelling salesman Representative of a firm who visits shops and businesses to show samples and gain orders Also called commercial traveller, door-to-door salesman or hawker
Auction sale A public sale where willing customers participate Goods go to the highest bidder
B2B sale Sales made to other businesses rather than an individual customer An industrial or professional sale: one business selling to another
Electronic-based sales A web-based sale; covers both B2B and B2C Uses EDI (Electronic Data Interchange)
Indirect sales Sale of a good or service by a third party, such as a partner or affiliate Not done by the company's own personnel

3. Arranging Products for Sale in the Store

Products of different manufacturers are arranged systematically on shelves, based on the space available. Display is prioritised by the manufacturer's competitiveness, because some manufacturers buy the display window section and floor space to promote their product.

Why arrange products at all?

Arrangement is a critical element for the success of a store. Products divided section-wise give a clear view of everything available, make it easy for the customer to pick products while moving through the store, and improve the store's profit.

Four reasons for rearranging products

Reason Why
Repeat customers Gives the store a fresh look and keeps current customers coming back; more products on display means customers visit again and sales volume rises
Sales promotions Retailers rearrange stock for promotion; rearrangement increases customers
Brand image Some retailers rearrange routinely to maintain brand image; monthly rearrangement and changing colour schemes build a new, fresh style
Seasonal Retailers display products in one area and rearrange to show seasonal products

Four floor plans

Floor plan What it looks like Where it is used
Straight Products arranged in a straight line Most commonly used in Indian retail; economical
Diagonal Angled arrangement More visibility for store staff and customers; needs small space
Angular Curves and angles, spacious look High-value and high-end retailers; reduces display area and focuses on a few popular products
Geometric Racks and other fixtures create a different style Trendy products such as cosmetics and watches

The choice depends on the size of the store, the type of product being sold, and the extent of customer focus required on a product.

5 steps for identifying the products responsible for sale

A Customer Service Associate (CSA) identifies the products to be sold:

  1. Have full information about the retail store and the products being sold.
  2. Follow the store manager's or supervisor's instructions about stock received from different manufacturers.
  3. Accept the product section allotted by the manager or supervisor — vegetable section, housekeeping material section, stationery section, clothes section, etc.
  4. Take responsibility for the allotted section and keep full records of stock received from manufacturers until the product is sold.
  5. Have complete knowledge of the allotted product and the skills to sell it.

Session 2

Sales Promotional Activities

1. What is Sales Promotion?

Sales promotion — the fastest growing area of marketing activity, and a major component of promotion. It helps the retailer improve sales and generate higher profits.

  • Promotion means communicating with an audience through a variety of non-personal and non-media channels.
  • The firm gives special offers such as discounts. These offers are a direct proposition forming part of the deal.
  • Sales promotion covers all activities that supplement, coordinate and make personal selling and advertising effective.
  • It increases sales and stimulates consumers to buy more.
  • It is designed as a short-term tactic to boost sales.

2. Five Objectives of Sales Promotion

  1. Stimulate inquiries from consumers asking for detailed information about a product.
  2. Increase product trials, expecting the trial to lead to customer satisfaction.
  3. Encourage repurchase and develop loyalty in the customer's mind.
  4. Initiate inventory building by retailers, so consumers have a ready supply.
  5. Get dealers' promotional assistance by coordinating with personal selling.

3. Sales Promotion Techniques

Two heads: vendor/producer originated and retailer originated.

The book's Fig. 3.1 also groups promotion by the audience it targets: consumer promotion (final buyers), sales force promotion (sales force members), trade promotion (wholesalers and retailers) and business promotion (business customers).

A. Vendor-originated techniques (5)

Technique What it is
In-store activities Targeted to give customers a cash or kind advantage: price-off pack, premiums, small gifts, cooperative promotion, sampling, coupons, buy one get one free, multipacks
Point-of-sale display material Material supplied by vendors and placed near products customers often buy. Special fixtures such as paperback publisher racks, dry battery stands, cold drink racks; also display signs, leaflets and demonstrations
Trade incentives Cash incentives or trade-offs given directly by the vendor to the retailer. The benefit may not reach the customer. Types: cash discounts, special credit terms, goods in lieu of cash, staff incentives
Direct promotion to customers Promotion at the customer's doorstep: distribution of coupons and free samples
Pack design One of the most important techniques for the retailer. A retailer may accept a package design over its contents, and normally supports packs with better design

The book prints the first in-store activity as "prose off pack"; it means a price-off pack.

B. Retailer-originated techniques (2)

Technique What it is
Window display Displaying items for sale, or a display designed to attract customers into the store
In-store display Mass display, multiple product promotion, demonstrations, store loyalty cards, free gifts, point-of-sale display, celebrity promotion, joint promotion with other retailers, incentives to staff

4. Ways to Influence Customer Buying

Consumer behaviour covers the decisions and actions that influence a consumer's purchasing behaviour. Most of the selection process is based on emotions and reasoning. A sales associate converts a prospective customer into a user by:

  1. Identifying the prospective customer and starting a dialogue in a friendly and humorous manner.
  2. Collecting as much relevant information as possible about the product's sale presentation.
  3. Explaining the product's features and advantages compared with the competitor's product.
  4. Presenting or demonstrating the product with patience and in an interesting manner.
  5. Attending to any objection immediately and clarifying the issue.
  6. Explaining the warranty period and assuring other sales services if needed.
  7. Maintaining continuous relations with customers for feedback on satisfaction.
  8. Improving selling skills: patience, good communication, reliability, politeness, caring for customers.
  9. Not losing temper when a potential customer behaves aggressively.
  10. Using sales promotion techniques if required to help the buying decision.

5. Knowledge of the Nature of the Product

Explicit characteristics — where everyone agrees

These are characteristics over which there is common agreement on both the existence and the attributes of the product. Five attributes:

Attribute What the associate must know
Physical configuration A product is a bundle of physical stuff: shape, size, density, odour, taste, texture, colour, weight and other physical attributes
Associated service Before-sales: product demonstration, information on credit facilities if available. After-sales: delivery, installation, spare parts, repair service, warranties
Package and brand name Packaging and brand name let the associate differentiate from a competitor's brand and highlight the product's features against others in the market
Product size Width = the product lines. Depth = number of items in every product line. Consistency = similarity among the product lines
Product life cycle Introduction, growth, maturity, decline. Knowing the stage lets the associate frame different selling strategies

Implicit characteristics — where people disagree

Every person sees a product differently, so perceptions do not match. These points of disagreement are the implicit characteristics:

Attribute What the associate must know
Product symbolism A product is a symbol through its form, size, colour and function. Significance varies with needs and social interaction, so study it as a status symbol, for economy, for performance — in relation to customer needs
Communication media The consumer's personal interpretation of symbols is mediated by culture groups, group influence and personality. Study whether product information is given or hidden, and communicate what the product is used for, at what rate, made by whom and where it is available
Product perception Perception is a physio-psychological process. How consumers perceive a product influences its marketability; gathering this information helps reformulate product policies
Product evaluation Comparing the efforts involved and rewards received by the consumer. It shows the retailer how satisfied the consumer is with the product used

Every product has both explicit and implicit characteristics. Knowing them helps the retailer frame successful product policies.

Session 3

Responding to Questions and Comments

1. Why Training Matters

The sales associate is the only person directly approached by the customer. Training to handle tough questions and situations gives the associate an idea of what to say and the flexibility to adapt and add to their own personality.

The associate must analyse the situation and answer accordingly. This satisfies the customer, who then holds a positive attitude towards the person and the service provider.

2. Situation-by-Situation Responses

Situation What to do What to avoid
You cannot answer a query Do not panic — your responsibility is to show firmness to make things right. Say: "It is a valid question, let me find out the right thing for you." Take the question to the concerned staff or superior and respond Never say "I don't know" — it does not help the customer
The item is not available Use positive language. Say the product will be available next month and offer to place an order: "I will place an order for you, if needed" Do not just say the product is unavailable. Positive language replaces negative phrases such as "I can't…"
A call must be transferred Transfer it to the concerned department. Customers feel happy when their concerns are properly addressed —
Customer comments on the product Customers often give valuable comments and suggestions on usage and improvement. Holding the product's vision is the associate's responsibility Do not say "We will take a look" — it gives false hope and disappoints the customer weeks later
A favour cannot be granted Most requests are reasonable, so make every effort to make the customer happy. Use positive language, very politely. Showing possible alternatives is one of the best ways to say no Do not refuse bluntly
The product is defective Show empathy. Say: "I am so sorry to disappoint you. There was a mistake in the manufacturing process or shipping. Can I send a new one to you?" Then, depending on the product and how the business runs, offer: "Should I send you a full refund?" Do not ignore the customer's frustration

The defective-product response achieves two objectives:

  1. Understanding the customer's frustrating experience — it explains what the problem is and gives a clear immediate solution.
  2. Offering a remedy — a replacement or a full refund, depending on the product and the business.

3. Closing a Conversation

A sales associate should always aim to "close" a conversation. This is not closing the sale — it means the customer is happy about the product and leaves perfectly happy.

Three things show this:

  1. The associate cares about getting what the customer requires.
  2. If the customer needs information or an answer, the associate provides the right information.
  3. The customer determines what is needed, and the need is met in the right way.

End with phrases such as "I am happy to help you, Sir or Ma'am" or "We are happy to serve you" — they make a big impact.

4. Dealing with Angry Customers

Be very polite; you are carrying the burden of an emotional customer.

Step What to do
Apologise sincerely "I am sorry" is a mandatory response. Make a personal apology to the customer who faced an ugly experience
Sympathise Angry customers often need empathy. "I understand how upsetting that must have been" shows the team wants to make things right
Accept responsibility Accept responsibility for the customer's unhappiness. This does not make the associate "at fault"
Prepare to help Fix the problem by setting things right — for example, place a replacement order. Some customers cannot calm down even when handled perfectly; make the best effort

Session 4

Techniques of Closing a Sale

1. What is Closing a Sale?

Closing a sale is an important part of the personal selling process. It means completing a sales transaction — the final step in a sales call, where the customer signs on the dotted line of the agreement.

The sales process is adaptive: each situation differs, so the associate must understand what matters to each customer and where that customer is in the buying process. To sell adaptively, the associate must thoroughly understand the steps of the selling process.

2. Five Factors for a Successful Close

Factor What it covers
Salesman's personality The sum total of everything about the sales person that influences the prospect favourably. Physical: sound health, neat appearance, voice. Mental: alertness, confidence, enthusiasm, observation. Social: friendly appearance, courtesy, cooperation, good manners. Character is the fourth part
Awareness of company policies Know the company's history, origin and later changes, current reputation, philosophy and policies. This lets the associate clarify queries and induce the customer to buy
Product information Models, size, weight, ingredients, packaging, quality, price, discount offered, benefits, replacement, after-sales service
Need of the customer Study buying behaviour, likes, dislikes, nature and type of customer, then adopt suitable strategies
Information on competing products Know what competitors offer so the relative merits of your product can be highlighted; closing then becomes easy

3. Ten Techniques of Closing a Sale

Technique What the associate does Best used when
Natural close Also called the "ask for it" close. The close must look like a natural development of the sales conversation, so the customer never senses a special formula or tactic The conversation has run its natural course
Order form close Asks questions not directly about the purchase: "Sir, shall I make the invoice or bill?" Sound natural to avoid rejection The associate wants to avoid rejection
Alternative close Assumes the customer will definitely buy; only design, colour, style or shape is undecided. Present an alternative that suits the need and provoke a final choice between the options shown Product presentation can carry the decision
Summary close Summarises the list of benefits the customer gets: "We have reached the end of the sale, so may I highlight the main points so that we could close a deal?" One of the most natural methods Customers who are unpredictable
Cautionary tale close Motivates an undecided customer by showing the implication of failing to decide at that very moment. The associate tries not to make up stories The customer keeps postponing
Final objection close The customer may hold an objection right at the end and hesitate to mention it. Persuade the customer to state the objection, since it can affect the buying decision An objection surfaces at the last moment
Conditional close For the customer who is very slow to decide and finds an excuse to delay. Assure them of confidence and service, and put the close forward in a subtle way The customer intends to buy but keeps delaying
Lost sales approach Do not feel the sale is lost. Continue the conversation, keep a sense of balance, and convert the lost sale into an actual sale with additional selling effort The customer leaves without buying
Assumption close Read the customer's manners, body language or comment as a sign of the decision, and proceed without asking for the purchase. Gives the customer a painless decision Signals of readiness are clear
Balance sheet close Help an indecisive prospect list on paper the "arguments for" and "arguments against" the product choice The prospect cannot weigh the choice

Q01 Explain the various methods of selling in retail. ›

A sale is the act of selling a product in return for money. It begins a relation between customer and vendor, or extends an existing one. The methods are:

  1. Direct sales — direct personal demonstration and sale of products and services to the consumer. It has two business models, single-level and multilevel marketing, and makes money by selling directly to consumers.
  2. Proforma sales — a sales quote prepared as a proforma invoice. "Proforma" describes a document that serves as a courtesy or meets a minimum requirement, conforms to a norm, or is done as a formality.
  3. Agency-based — a contractual arrangement in which the agent may negotiate the sale of the principal's goods and services and receives a commission or fee. Agents include sales agents, sales outsourcing through direct branded representation, transaction sales, consultative sales, consignment sales, telemarketing or telesales, and retail sales.
  4. Travelling salesman — a firm's representative who visits shops and businesses to show samples and gain orders; also called a commercial traveller, door-to-door salesman or hawker.
  5. Auction sale — a public sale in which willing customers participate and goods go to the highest bidder.
  6. B2B sale — sales to other businesses rather than individual customers; an industrial or professional sale from one business to another.
  7. Electronic-based sales — a web-based sale covering B2B and B2C, using EDI.
  8. Indirect sales — sale of a good or service by a third party such as a partner or affiliate, rather than the company's own personnel.
Q02 Discuss the need for arranging products in a retail store. ›

Products from different manufacturers are displayed in retail stores and arranged systematically on shelves according to the space available. Display is prioritised on the basis of the manufacturer's competitiveness, since some manufacturers buy the display window section and floor space to promote their product.

Arrangement is a critical element for the success of a store. Products are divided section-wise, which gives a clear view of all products available, makes it easy for customers to pick products as they move through the store, and improves the store's profit. A perfect display brings a wider range of customers who need specific products.

Products are rearranged for four reasons:

  1. Repeat customers — a fresh look keeps current customers coming back; more products on display bring another visit and raise sales volume.
  2. Sales promotions — retailers rearrange stock to promote sales, and rearrangement increases customers.
  3. Brand image — some retailers rearrange routinely; monthly rearrangement and colour changes build a new and fresh style.
  4. Seasonal — products are displayed in one area and rearranged to show seasonal products.

Floor plan Description

Diagonal Gives more visibility to store staff and customers; requires small space
Angular Gives a spacious look; used by high-value and high-end retailers; reduces display area and focuses on a few popular products
Geometric Uses racks and other fixtures to create a different style; used for trendy products such as cosmetics and watches
Q04 Elaborate the steps adopted in identifying the products responsible for sale. ›

Customer service associates (CSAs) identify the products to be sold:

  2. Follow the store manager's or supervisor's instructions about stock received from different manufacturers.
  3. Accept the product section allotted by the manager or supervisor — vegetable, housekeeping material, stationery, clothes, etc.
  4. Take responsibility for the allotted section and keep a full record of stock received from manufacturers until the product is sold.
  5. Have complete knowledge of the allotted product and the skill to sell it to customers.

The firm provides special offers such as discounts; these are direct propositions forming part of the deal. Objectives:

Q06 Explain vendor-originated and retailer-originated sales promotion techniques. ›

Vendor-originated techniques:

  1. In-store activities — give customers a cash or kind advantage: price-off pack, premiums, small gifts, cooperative promotion, sampling, coupons, buy one get one free, multipacks.
  2. Point-of-sale display material — vendor material placed near products customers often buy: special fixtures such as paperback publisher racks, dry battery stands and cold drink racks, plus display signs, leaflets and demonstrations.
  3. Trade incentives — cash incentives or trade-offs given directly by the vendor to the retailer, which may not reach the customer: cash discounts, special credit terms, goods in lieu of cash, staff incentives.
  4. Direct promotion to customers — promotion at the customer's doorstep: coupons and free samples.
  5. Pack design — a retailer may accept a package design over its contents and normally supports packs with better design.

Retailer-originated techniques:

  1. Window display — displaying items for sale, or a display designed to attract customers into the store.
  2. In-store display — mass display, multiple product promotion, demonstrations, store loyalty cards, free gifts, point-of-sale display, celebrity promotion, joint promotion with other retailers, staff incentives.
Q07 Differentiate between explicit and implicit characteristics of a product. ›

Explicit characteristics are those over which there is common agreement on both the existence and the attributes of the product. They are:

  1. Physical configuration — shape, size, density, odour, taste, texture, colour, weight and other physical attributes.
  2. Associated service — before-sales (demonstration, credit information) and after-sales (delivery, installation, spare parts, repair, warranties).
  3. Package and brand name — used to differentiate from competitors and highlight features.
  4. Product size — width (product lines), depth (items per line), consistency (similarity among lines).
  5. Product life cycle — introduction, growth, maturity, decline.

Implicit characteristics are the points of disagreement, because every person perceives a product differently. They are:

  1. Product symbolism — a product is a symbol through form, size, colour and function; significance varies with needs and social interaction (status symbol, economy, performance).
  2. Communication media — interpretation of symbols is mediated by culture groups, group influence and personality; study whether information is given or hidden, and communicate what the product is used for, at what rate, made by whom and where available.
  3. Product perception — a physio-psychological process; perceptions influence marketability and help reformulate product policies.
  4. Product evaluation — comparing efforts involved and rewards received, which shows consumer satisfaction.

Q08 How should a sales associate respond when unable to answer, when an item is unavailable, and to comments on the product? ›

When unable to answer: do not panic, because the responsibility is to show firmness in making things right. Never say "I don't know", as it does not help the customer. Say, "It is a valid question, let me find out the right thing for you." Place the customer's need above the fact that you do not know, take the question to the concerned staff or superior, and respond.

When an item is unavailable: the main skill is positive language, which avoids nasty or rash reactions. Say the product will be available next month and offer to place an order if needed. Do not simply state that the product is unavailable. Positive language replaces negative phrases such as "I can't…".

When a comment is made on the product: customers often give valuable comments and suggestions on usage and improvement, but holding the product's vision is the associate's responsibility. Do not reply "We will take a look", because it gives false hope and disappoints the customer who checks back weeks later.

When a favour cannot be granted: most requests are reasonable and every effort should be made to satisfy them. Refuse using positive language and very politely; showing possible alternatives is one of the best ways to say no.

Q09 How should a sales associate handle a defective product and a severely angry customer? ›

Defective product. Receiving a defective product is very discouraging for the customer, so empathy is essential. A suitable response is: "I am so sorry to disappoint you. There was a mistake in the manufacturing process or shipping. Can I send a new one to you?" This achieves two objectives: it understands the customer's frustrating experience by explaining the problem and giving a clear immediate solution, and it offers a remedy — depending on the product and the business, the associate can ask, "Should I send you a full refund?"

Angry customer. The associate must be very polite while carrying the burden of an emotional customer:

  1. Apologise sincerely — "I am sorry" is mandatory, given as a personal apology.
  2. Sympathise — "I understand how upsetting that must have been" shows the team wants to make things right.
  3. Accept responsibility for the customer's unhappiness, which does not make the associate "at fault".
  4. Prepare to help — set things right, for example by placing a replacement order. Some customers cannot calm down even when handled perfectly; the associate makes the best effort.
Q10 What is closing a sale? Explain the factors and techniques of closing a sale. ›

Closing a sale is an important part of the personal selling process. It means completing a sales transaction — the final step in a sales call, where the customer signs on the dotted line of the agreement. The sales process is adaptive, so the associate must understand what matters to each customer and where the customer is in the buying process.

Five factors influencing a successful close:

  1. Salesman's personality — the sum total of everything about the sales person that influences the prospect favourably: physical (sound health, neat appearance, voice), mental (alertness, confidence, enthusiasm, observation), social (friendly appearance, courtesy, cooperation, good manners) and character.
  2. Awareness of company policies — history, origin and later changes, current reputation, philosophy and policies, so queries can be clarified.
  3. Product information — models, size, weight, ingredients, packaging, quality, price, discount, benefits, replacement, after-sales service.
  4. Need of the customer — buying behaviour, likes, dislikes, nature and type, so suitable strategies can be adopted.
  5. Information on competing products — to highlight relative merits and make closing easy.

Ten techniques:

Technique Essence
Natural close The "ask for it" close; looks like a natural development of the conversation
Order form close Questions not directly about the purchase, e.g. "Shall I make the invoice or bill?"
Alternative close Assumes the sale; the customer chooses between options presented
Summary close Summarises the benefits; used for unpredictable customers
Cautionary tale close Shows the implication of not deciding at that moment; no made-up stories
Final objection close Draws out an objection raised at the very end
Conditional close For very slow deciders; subtle assurance of service
Lost sales approach Keeps the conversation alive and converts the lost sale with extra effort
Assumption close Reads body language or comments and proceeds, giving a painless decision
Balance sheet close Lists arguments for and against on paper for an indecisive prospect

Also examinable (not part of the ten): a CSA must hold full records of stock from receipt to sale; electronic-based sales use EDI; the angular floor plan reduces display area and focuses on a few popular products; customers always try to get the maximum from minimum investment, which is why a logical demonstration matters.

Session 1

Methods of Selling

The chapter in 12 lines

  • Sale — the act of selling a product in return for money; it begins or extends the customer–vendor relation.
  • 8 methods: direct, proforma, agency-based, travelling salesman, auction, B2B, electronic-based, indirect.
  • Direct sales — personal demonstration and sale to the consumer; models are single-level and multilevel marketing.
  • Proforma sale — a quote prepared as a proforma invoice (a courtesy document or formality).
  • Agency-based — the agent negotiates the principal's goods for a commission or fee.
  • Travelling salesman — visits shops and businesses with samples to gain orders; also commercial traveller, door-to-door salesman, hawker.
  • Auction — public sale to the highest bidder.
  • B2B — one business selling to another.
  • Electronic-based — web-based, covers B2B and B2C, uses EDI.
  • Indirect — sale by a third party such as a partner or affiliate.
  • Products are arranged section-wise on shelves; display priority goes to the manufacturer's competitiveness (some buy window and floor space).
  • Arrangement is critical to store success — clear view, easy pick-up, higher profit.

5 CSA steps for identifying products — "I Follow All Retail Knowledge."

  1. Information about the store and its products.
  2. Follow the manager's/supervisor's instructions on stock from manufacturers.
  3. Accept the allotted section (vegetable, housekeeping, stationery, clothes).
  4. Responsibility for the section and full records of stock until sale.
  5. Knowledge of the product and the skill to sell it.

Session 2

Sales Promotional Activities

The chapter in 10 lines

  • Sales promotion — the fastest growing area of marketing activity; a major part of promotion.
  • Promotion communicates through non-personal and non-media channels.
  • Offers such as discounts are a direct proposition forming part of the deal.
  • It supplements, coordinates and makes personal selling and advertising effective.
  • It is a short-term tactic to boost sales.
  • 5 objectives: inquiries · trials · repurchase and loyalty · inventory building · dealers' assistance.
  • Two origins: vendor/producer and retailer.
  • Audiences (Fig. 3.1): consumer, sales force, trade, business.
  • Explicit traits — everyone agrees; implicit traits — perceptions differ.
  • Every product has both explicit and implicit characteristics.

5 objectives — "Inquiries Trigger Repeat Inventory Deals."

  1. Inquiries — consumers ask for detailed product information.
  2. Trials — trial is expected to lead to satisfaction.
  3. Repurchase — build loyalty.
  4. Inventory building — retailers keep a ready supply.
  5. Dealers' assistance — coordinate with personal selling.

Vendor-originated techniques (5) — "In-Store Promotions Target Direct Purchase."

Technique What it is
In-store activities Cash or kind advantage to the customer: price-off pack, premiums, small gifts, cooperative promotion, sampling, coupons, buy one get one free, multipacks
Point-of-sale display material Vendor material near fast-moving products: paperback racks, battery stands, cold drink racks; signs, leaflets, demonstrations
Trade incentives Cash incentive or trade-off from vendor to retailer; may not reach the customer — cash discounts, special credit terms, goods in lieu of cash, staff incentives
Direct promotion to customers Doorstep distribution of coupons and free samples
Pack design A retailer may accept the package design over its contents; better design gets support

Retailer-originated techniques (2)

  • Window display — items for sale, or a display to attract customers inside.
  • In-store display — mass display, multiple product promotion, demonstrations, loyalty cards, free gifts, POS display, celebrity promotion, joint promotion with other retailers, staff incentives.

10 ways to influence buying

  1. Identify the prospect and start a friendly, humorous dialogue.
  2. Collect relevant information for the sale presentation.
  3. Explain features and advantages against competitors.
  4. Demonstrate patiently and interestingly.
  5. Attend to an objection immediately.
  6. Explain the warranty and other services.
  7. Keep continuous relations for feedback.
  8. Improve skills — patience, communication, reliability, politeness, caring.
  9. Do not lose temper with aggressive customers.
  10. Use sales promotion techniques when needed.

Explicit vs implicit characteristics

Explicit — common agreement on existence and attributes (5): "Physical Attributes Package Size Life."

Attribute Must know
Physical configuration Shape, size, density, odour, taste, texture, colour, weight
Associated service Before-sales (demonstration, credit info); after-sales (delivery, installation, spares, repair, warranty)
Package and brand name Differentiates from competitors; highlights features
Product size Width = product lines · Depth = items per line · Consistency = similarity among lines
Product life cycle Introduction, growth, maturity, decline; each stage needs a different strategy

Implicit — points of disagreement (4): "Symbols Communicate Perceptions Everywhere."

Attribute Must know
Product symbolism Form, size, colour, function carry meaning — status, economy, performance
Communication media Culture groups, group influence and personality shape interpretation; what it is used for, at what rate, by whom, where available
Product perception A physio-psychological process; perception affects marketability and product policy
Product evaluation Compares efforts involved and rewards received; shows satisfaction

Session 3

Responding to Questions and Comments

The chapter in 10 lines

  • The sales associate is the only person directly approached by the customer, so training gives flexibility to adapt.
  • Never say "I don't know" — say: "It is a valid question, let me find out the right thing for you."
  • If an item is unavailable, use positive language and offer to order.
  • Transfer a call to the concerned department.
  • For product comments, hold the product's vision; never say "We will take a look."
  • If a favour cannot be granted, use positive language, very politely, and show alternatives.
  • For a defective product, show empathy, apologise, explain, and offer replacement or full refund.
  • Closing a conversation = the customer is happy, not closing the sale.
  • Angry customer: apologise → sympathise → accept responsibility → prepare to help.
  • End with "I am happy to help you" or "We are happy to serve you."

Situation Say / do Avoid
Cannot answer "It is a valid question, let me find out the right thing for you." Take it to the concerned staff or superior "I don't know"
Item unavailable Say it will be available next month; "I will place an order for you, if needed" "I can't…", blunt refusal
Call transfer Transfer to the concerned department —
Product comment Note the valuable suggestion; hold the product's vision "We will take a look" — false hope
Favour refused Positive language, very politely; show possible alternatives Blunt refusal
Defective product "I am so sorry to disappoint you… Can I send a new one to you?" then "Should I send you a full refund?" Ignoring frustration

Defective response does 2 things: understands the frustrating experience and offers a remedy (replacement or refund).

Closing a conversation — 3 signs

  1. The associate cares about getting what the customer requires.
  2. The right information or answer is provided.
  3. The customer decides the need and it is met the right way.

Step Action

Sympathise "I understand how upsetting that must have been"

Techniques of Closing a Sale

The chapter in 10 lines

  • Closing a sale — completing a sales transaction; the final step where the customer signs the agreement.
  • The sales process is adaptive — understand what matters to each customer and where they are in the buying process.
  • 5 factors: personality · company policies · product information · customer need · competing products.
  • Personality has physical, mental, social parts, plus character.
  • 10 techniques, from natural close to balance sheet close.
  • Pick the technique from the customer's behaviour: ready, hesitating, delaying, or undecided.
  • A close must feel like a natural development of the conversation.
  • Never make up stories in a cautionary tale close.
  • Assumption close gives a painless decision.
  • Balance sheet close lists arguments for and against on paper.
