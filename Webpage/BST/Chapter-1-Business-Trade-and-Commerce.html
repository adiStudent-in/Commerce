Section 1

Business and the Economy

The idea of this section. Business is the engine of an economy. This part shows how wide the word "business" really is, how rich India's trade history is, the indigenous banking instrument Hundi, and how the economy moved from the British era to the 1991 reforms and today's India.

1.1 Why Does Business Matter to the Economy?

The big picture — the three layers of business:

Business = a wider term that includes Industry + Trade + Commerce Business starts with production and ends with consumption.

LayerWhat it does
IndustryEconomic activities connected with the conversion of resources into useful goods
TradeMakes goods available from producers to consumers
CommerceTrade plus auxiliaries to trade; its purpose is to remove hindrances in the exchange of goods and services

The chain in one line:

Production → (Industry + Trade + Commerce) → Consumption

  • Industry turns raw resources into useful goods.
  • Trade carries those goods from the producer to the consumer.
  • Commerce adds the services — transport, banking, insurance, warehousing, advertising — that make the exchange possible.
  • Commerce removes six hindrances: persons, place, time, risk, finance and information (full list in section 3.3).

Exam tip. Whenever a question says "explain the meaning of business in the widest sense", write the three layers and the line "business starts with production and ends with consumption".

Check yourself4 questions

  3. Q3. Which layer of business makes goods available from producers to consumers? (a) Industry (b) Trade (c) Commerce (d) Banking and Finance
  4. Q4. What is the stated purpose of commerce?
Show answers
  1. Industry + Trade + Commerce.
  2. Production; consumption.
  3. (b) Trade.
  4. To remove hindrances in the exchange of goods and services.

1.2 India's Ancient Trade and Trade Centres

The key facts of ancient trade

  • Trading via the Silk Route and maritime trade were the main routes.
  • India had the largest economy in the ancient/medieval world between 1st–7th centuries CE.
  • India controlled 1/3 to 1/4 of the world's wealth.
  • India was called 'Swaran Bhoomi and Swaran Deep' by travellers — Megasthenes, FaHien, Huen Tsang, Al Beruni, Ibn Batuta and Francois.
  • Karkhanas were family-based workshops for manufacturing in ancient India.

1.4 From the British Era to Economic Liberalisation

The British era impact

  • The East India Company used Indian revenues to buy Indian raw materials.
  • India shifted from exporter of processed goods → exporter of raw materials + buyer of manufactured goods.

After independence

  • Planned development with centralised economic planning.
  • Emphasis on public investment in basic industries.
  • Problems faced: lack of capital, rising population, weak financial system, high fiscal deficit.
  • 1991: Economic Liberalisation → a three-pronged reform: stabilisation, restructuring, globalisation.

Current India

  • A preferred FDI destination.
  • Initiatives in action: Make in India, Skill India, Digital India.

Memory hook. 1991 → S-R-G: Stabilisation, Restructuring, Globalisation.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. In which year did Economic Liberalisation take place in India? (a) 1952 (b) 1973 (c) 1991 (d) 1900
  2. Q2. Name the three-pronged reform measures of 1991.
  3. Q3. How did India's trade position change under the East India Company?
  4. Q4. Name the three national initiatives listed for current India.
Show answers
  1. (c) 1991.
  2. Stabilisation, restructuring and globalisation.
  3. India shifted from exporter of processed goods to exporter of raw materials and buyer of manufactured goods; the East India Company used Indian revenues to buy Indian raw materials.
  4. Make in India, Skill India and Digital India.
Section 2

Understanding Business

The idea of this section. This part defines business, separates economic work from non-economic work, lists the seven marks of a business activity, and sets business beside profession and employment.

2.1 What Is Business?

The definition — learn this line word for word:

Business = an economic activity involving production and sale of goods/services with a motive of earning profit by satisfying human needs.

Break the definition into parts — every keyword carries a mark:

Part of the definitionWhat it tells you
Economic activityThe work is done to earn a livelihood
Production and sale of goods/servicesGoods are produced or procured, and then sold
Motive of earning profitProfit is the reason the business is run
Satisfying human needsThe customer gains something useful

Where the word comes from. The term 'business' is derived from the word 'busy'.

Exam tip. For a 1-mark answer, write "an economic activity involving production and sale of goods/services with the motive of earning profit". For a 3-mark answer, add "by satisfying human needs" and give the origin of the word — 'busy'.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. The term 'business' is derived from which English word? (a) buy (b) busy (c) bust (d) buzz
  2. Q2. What are the two essential parts of any business activity?
  3. Q3. Write the full meaning of business in one sentence.
  4. Q4. Why does a business have to satisfy human needs?
Show answers
  1. (b) 'busy'.
  2. Production or procurement of goods/services, and their sale or exchange.
  3. Business is an economic activity involving production and sale of goods/services with a motive of earning profit, by satisfying human needs.
  4. Because the customer must gain something useful — satisfaction of human needs is the purpose the business serves.

2.2 Human Activities and Economic Activities

Two types of human activities

TypeMeaningExample
EconomicDone to earn livelihoodWorker in factory, doctor in clinic
Non-economicDone out of love, sympathy, emotionHousewife cooking for family, helping old man

Three types of economic activities

  1. Business — production/sale of goods/services for profit.
  2. Profession — personalised expert services (e.g., doctor, lawyer).
  3. Employment — work done as per service contract for salary.

Exam tip. A question on "types of economic activities" wants the three words — business, profession, employment — each with a one-line meaning. A question on "types of human activities" wants economic and non-economic.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. A housewife cooking for her family is an example of which activity? (a) Economic activity (b) Non-economic activity (c) Business (d) Employment
  2. Q2. Name the three types of economic activities.
  3. Q3. Give one example each of a profession and of employment.
  4. Q4. What is the difference in purpose between economic and non-economic activities?
Show answers
  1. (b) Non-economic activity — it is done out of love, sympathy or emotion.
  2. Business, profession and employment.
  3. Profession — a doctor or a lawyer giving personalised expert service. Employment — a bank job or a government job done as per a service contract for a salary.
  4. Economic activities are done to earn a livelihood; non-economic activities are done out of love, sympathy or emotion.

2.3 Characteristics of Business Activity

Seven characteristics, in order:

#CharacteristicExplanation
iEconomic activityDone for earning money/livelihood; the activity is economic
iiProduction or procurementGoods must be produced or procured before being sold
iiiSale or exchangeMust involve transfer of goods/services for value
ivRegular dealingsDealings must go on regularly for it to be business
vProfit earningMain purpose is to earn profit; no business survives without profit
viUncertainty of returnFuture profit/loss is never certain; capital is at risk
viiElement of riskRisk = uncertainty associated with exposure to loss; it can be minimised and always stays

Two everyday examples that settle the doubt

  • Cooking food at home serves the family and carries no sale and no profit motive; cooking and selling in a restaurant is business.
  • Selling an old radio set once is a one-time deal; selling radio sets regularly is business.

Exam tip. Characteristic iv and characteristic vii are asked most often. For iv, the key word is regular. For vii, the key word is risk = uncertainty linked to exposure to loss.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. Which characteristic requires dealings to go on regularly? (a) Sale or exchange (b) Regular dealings (c) Profit earning (d) Element of risk
  2. Q2. What must happen before goods can be sold?
  3. Q3. Why is capital said to be at risk in a business?
  4. Q4. Give the seven characteristics of business activity in order.
Show answers
  1. (b) Regular dealings.
  2. Goods must be produced or procured.
  3. Because the future profit or loss is never certain.
  4. Economic activity, production or procurement, sale or exchange, regular dealings, profit earning, uncertainty of return, element of risk.

Section 4

Objectives, Risks and Starting a Business

The idea of this section. A business runs to meet objectives, it carries risk by nature, and it starts with eight planned factors. This part covers all three.

4.1 Objectives of Business

Profit is the leading objective, and the other objectives of business stand alongside it.

Why profit is important

  • Source of income for business persons.
  • Finance for expansion.
  • Indicates efficient working.
  • Society's approval of business utility.

4.2 Business Risk: Meaning and Two Types

Business Risk = possibility of inadequate profits or losses due to uncertainties or unexpected events.

TypeMeaningExample
Speculative RiskPossibility of gain OR lossChanges in demand/supply, price fluctuations, fashion changes
Pure RiskPossibility of loss only (no gain if it does not occur)Fire, theft, strike

How the two differ:

  • A speculative risk can end in a gain or in a loss — the outcome goes both ways.
  • A pure risk carries only the possibility of a loss.

5.2 Case Study with Model Answer

Aarav has set up a business in Madurai (the ancient centre Madura), which is famous for its pearl fisheries in the Gulf of Mannar and where Roman traders came. He collects these pearls and takes them for sale to Kanchipuram (the ancient centre Kanchi), known for pearls, glass and rare stones, and which received gold and silk. To move money safely between Madurai and Kanchipuram, Aarav uses Hundi, the indigenous banking instrument that literally means 'to collect' and is written in the vernacular language; it allows the safe transfer of money between parties and avoids robbery during travel. Aarav now wants to understand how his business fits into the wider framework of business, trade and commerce, and the risks he must plan for.

Questions

  • (a) Define business activity and state the characteristics of a business activity that Aarav's enterprise shows.
  • (b) Name the hindrances that stand in the way of exchange, and state which trade or auxiliary removes each one.
  • (c) Distinguish between speculative risk and pure risk, with examples that apply to Aarav's business.
  • (d) Which factors should Aarav settle before launching his enterprise? Name any five of the eight factors.

Model answer

  • (a) Definition. Business is an economic activity involving production and sale of goods/services with a motive of earning profit by satisfying human needs. The term 'business' is derived from the word 'busy'.

Characteristics shown by Aarav's enterprise: (i) it is an economic activity, done to earn a livelihood; (ii) it involves production or procurement — he procures pearls; (iii) it involves sale or exchange — he sells pearls in Kanchipuram; (iv) the dealings are regular, as the collection and sale of pearls go on from season to season; (v) the motive is profit earning; (vi) the return is uncertain, as the future profit or loss is never certain and his capital is at risk; (vii) it carries an element of risk, that is, uncertainty associated with exposure to loss.

  • (b) Hindrances and their removal. Persons — removed by trade, which makes goods available from producers to consumers; Place — removed by transport, which moves goods from production to markets; Time — removed by storage/warehousing, which holds goods until required; Risk — removed by insurance, which protects against loss/damage; Finance — removed by banking, which provides capital for business; Information — removed by advertising, which informs consumers about goods and services. Aarav's enterprise uses trade and transport (Madura to Kanchipuram), banking and finance (through Hundi and other instruments), and insurance for the risk of pearls being lost.
  • (c) Speculative vs pure risk. Speculative risk carries the possibility of a gain or a loss — for Aarav, a change in the demand for pearls or a fluctuation in pearl prices can go either way. Pure risk carries the possibility of a loss only — for example, a fire or theft destroying his stock of pearls, or a storm at sea during the journey from Madurai. Business risk itself is the possibility of inadequate profits or losses due to uncertainties or unexpected events.
  • (d) Factors before launching. Any five of the eight: (i) selection of type of business — the nature and type of business to undertake, influenced by customer requirements and the technical knowledge and interest of the entrepreneur; (ii) size of business — whether to run an MSME or a large-scale unit, manufacturing or tertiary, depending on confidence in demand and the capital available; (iii) location of business — this affects the cost of production, and raw materials, labour, power supply, banking, transport, communication and warehousing must be considered; (iv) financing the proposition — capital for fixed assets (land, building, machinery) + current assets (raw materials, stock) + day-to-day expenses; (v) physical facilities — machines, equipment, building and supportive services; (vi) competent and committed workforce — identifying the requirement of skilled and unskilled workers and planning their training and motivation; (vii) tax planning — considering tax liability under various laws, which impacts all aspects of business functioning; (viii) launching the enterprise — mobilising resources, fulfilling legal formalities, starting production, initiating sales promotion, and deciding the capital requirement, the source of capital and the best use of capital.

    Show answer

    Tamralipti, today Tamluk, was the greatest port and linked sea and land with the West and Far East; Broach, today Bharuch, on the river Narmada, was the greatest seat of commerce in Western India; and Madura, today Madurai, was known for pearl fisheries in the Gulf of Mannar and for Roman traders.

  10. What is Hundi, and how do its seven types divide?

    Show answer

    Hundi is an ancient indigenous banking instrument that literally means 'to collect' and is written in the vernacular language, used for the safe transfer of money so that robbery during travel is avoided; its seven types divide into four Darshani and three Muddati.

  11. Name the four Darshani and three Muddati hundis, and say which one is drawn against goods.

    Show answer

    The Darshani types are Dhani-jog, Sah-jog, Firman-jog and Dekhan-har, the Muddati types are Dhani-jog, Firman-jog and Jokhmi, and Jokhmi Muddati is drawn against dispatched goods, with the drawer or holder bearing the cost if the goods are lost.

  12. How did the East India Company change India's trade, what problems followed independence, and what did 1991 and current India bring?

    Show answer

    The East India Company used Indian revenues to buy Indian raw materials and India shifted from exporter of processed goods to exporter of raw materials and buyer of manufactured goods; after independence came centralised planning and public investment in basic industries, facing a lack of capital, rising population, a weak financial system and a high fiscal deficit; 1991 brought stabilisation, restructuring and globalisation, and today India is a preferred FDI destination with Make in India, Skill India and Digital India.

Understanding Business

What is business?

Definition — learn this word for word: Business = an economic activity involving production and sale of goods/services with a motive of earning profit by satisfying human needs.

Part of the definitionWhat it tells you
Economic activityThe work is done to earn a livelihood
Production and sale of goods/servicesGoods are produced or procured, and then sold
Motive of earning profitProfit is the reason the business is run
Satisfying human needsThe customer gains something useful

Origin: the term 'business' is derived from 'busy'. For 1 mark write the definition up to "motive of earning profit"; for 3 marks add "by satisfying human needs" and the origin.

Human activities and economic activities

Type of human activityMeaningExample
EconomicDone to earn a livelihoodWorker in a factory, doctor in a clinic
Non-economicDone out of love, sympathy or emotionHousewife cooking for family, helping an old man

Three types of economic activities:

  1. Business — production or sale of goods/services for profit.
  2. Profession — personalised expert services, such as a doctor or lawyer.
  3. Employment — work done as per a service contract for a salary.

The 7 characteristics of business activity

#CharacteristicExplanation

iiProduction or procurementGoods must be produced or procured before being sold
iiiSale or exchangeMust involve transfer of goods/services for value
ivRegular dealingsDealings must go on regularly for it to be business
vProfit earningMain purpose is profit; no business survives without profit
viUncertainty of returnFuture profit or loss is never certain; capital is at risk
viiElement of riskRisk is uncertainty associated with exposure to loss; it can be minimised and always stays

Two examples that settle the doubt:

  • Cooking food at home serves the family with no sale and no profit motive; cooking and selling in a restaurant is business.
  • Selling an old radio set once is a one-time deal; selling radio sets regularly is business.

Exam tip. Characteristics iv and vii are asked most. For iv the key word is regular; for vii the key word is risk = uncertainty linked to exposure to loss.

Industry — meaning and three types

Industry = economic activities connected with the conversion of resources into useful goods.

IndustryTypeMeaningExamples

Objectives, Risks and Starting a Business

Objectives of business

Profit is the leading objective, and the other objectives of business stand alongside it.

Why profit is important (5): source of income for business persons · finance for expansion · indicates efficient working · society's approval of business utility · builds reputation.

The six objectives

#ObjectiveMeaning
iMarket StandingMaintaining goodwill and reputation; forming a distinct identity against competitors
iiInnovationIntroducing new ideas or methods; two types — innovation in product/services and innovation in skills/activities
iiiProductivityOutput value in relation to input value; a measure of efficiency and the best use of resources
ivPhysical and Financial ResourcesAcquiring plants, machines, offices and funds efficiently
vEarning ProfitsProfitability is profit in relation to capital investment; necessary for survival and growth
viSocial ResponsibilityContributing resources to solve social problems and working in a socially desirable manner

Memory hook: M-I-P-P-E-S — Market standing, Innovation, Productivity, Physical and financial resources, Earning profits, Social responsibility.

Exam tip. Innovation has two types — in product/services and in skills/activities. Missing the second costs a mark.

Business risk — meaning and two types

Business Risk = the possibility of inadequate profits or losses due to uncertainties or unexpected events.

TypeMeaningExamples
Speculative RiskPossibility of gain OR lossChanges in demand/supply, price fluctuations, fashion changes
Pure RiskPossibility of loss only — no gain if it does not occurFire, theft, strike

A speculative risk can end in a gain or in a loss, while a pure risk carries only the possibility of loss. Uncertainty of return and element of risk, the two characteristics of business activity, both point here: future profit or loss is never certain.

Nature of business risk — 4 points

#Nature
iRisk is an essential part of every business — it can be minimised and it always stays
iiRisks arise due to uncertainties — natural calamities, demand changes, government policy changes
iiiDegree of risk depends on nature and size of business — fashion goods carry high risk; large scale carries higher risk
ivProfit is the reward for risk taking — 'No risk, no gain'; greater risk means a higher chance of profit

Causes of business risk — 4 groups

CauseExamples
Natural causesFlood, earthquake, lightning, heavy rains, famine
Human causesDishonesty, negligence of employees, strikes, riots, power failure, management inefficiency
Economic causesDemand changes, competition, price changes, change in technology, rise in interest rate, higher taxes
Other causesPolitical disturbances, mechanical failures such as bursting of a boiler, exchange rate fluctuations

Memory hook: Natural → Human → Economic → Other — outside human control, from people, from the market, and from politics and machines.

Starting a business — the eight basic factors

Entrepreneurship = the process of setting up one's own business. Entrepreneur = the person who sets up the business. Business Enterprise = the output or business unit.

#FactorKey points
iSelection of type of businessNature and type to undertake; influenced by customer requirements plus the technical knowledge and interest of the entrepreneur
iiSize of businessMSME or large scale, manufacturing or tertiary; depends on confidence in demand and available capital
iiiLocation of businessAffects cost of production; consider raw materials, labour, power supply, banking, transport, communication and warehousing
ivFinancing the propositionCapital for fixed assets such as land, building and machinery, plus current assets such as raw materials and stock, plus day-to-day expenses
vPhysical facilitiesMachines, equipment, building and supportive services; depends on nature and size of business and the production process
viCompetent and committed workforceIdentify the requirement of skilled and unskilled workers; plan training and motivation
viiTax planningConsider tax liability under various laws; it impacts all aspects of business functioning
viiiLaunching the enterpriseMobilise resources, fulfil legal formalities, start production, initiate sales promotion; decide capital requirement, source of capital and best use of capital

The eight in one line: type → size → location → financing → physical facilities → workforce → tax planning → launching.

Exam tip. Launching carries three decisions — capital requirement, source of capital, best use of capital. Write all three for full marks.
