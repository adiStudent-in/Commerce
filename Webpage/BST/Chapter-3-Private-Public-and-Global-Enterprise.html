1. Introduction

After independence in 1947, India adopted a Socialistic Pattern of Economy. This led to the emergence of a Mixed Economy — where both Public Sector (government) and Private Sector (individuals) play their respective roles in economic development.

📗 Key Timeline:
1948 & 1956 → Public sector dominance. Industries reserved for government.
1991 → Industrial Policy Reforms → Disinvestment, enhanced private sector role, encouraged FDI.

3.1 Departmental Undertaking

Definition: The oldest and most traditional form of public enterprise. It is considered as one of the departments of the government — no separate existence apart from government.

Examples: Indian Railways, Defence, Post & Telegraph, Broadcasting (Doordarshan), All India Radio

Features:

FeatureExplanation
FinanceFunded through Annual Budget. Revenue goes to Government Treasury
AccountingSubject to same audit controls as other government activities
ManagementManaged by govt department under direct ministry control
EmployeesGovernment employees — terms set by government
AccountabilityAccountable to Minister → Parliament

Merits & Limitations

✅ Merits❌ Limitations
Complete government controlLack of flexibility
Answerable to ParliamentDelay in decision-making
Revenue goes to govt treasuryRed tapism & bureaucracy
Suitable for national securityPolitical interference
 Indifferent to consumer needs

Suitability:

  • Where utmost secrecy required (defence, atomic power)
  • Where absolute government control needed (broadcasting)
  • Where private sector cannot enter due to huge capital (iron & steel)
  • Where undertaking is a source of revenue (Indian Railways)

3.2 Statutory Corporation (Public Corporation)

Definition: A corporate body with a separate legal existence, set up under a Special Act of Parliament or State Legislature. The Act defines its powers, functions, rules, and regulations.

Examples: RBI, LIC, FCI, Air India, ICAI, ICSI

Features:

FeatureExplanation
FormationBy a Special Act of Parliament
OwnershipWholly owned by government
Legal StatusSeparate legal entity — can sue and be sued
FinanceFinancially independent — borrows from govt or from revenues
AccountingNot subject to govt budgetary rules
StaffNot govt employees — own recruitment rules

Merits & Limitations

✅ Merits❌ Limitations
Operational flexibilityTheoretical autonomy — freedom only on paper
Freedom from govt interferenceBureaucratic & political interference in reality
Autonomous set-upOfficials may misuse autonomy (corruption)
Facilitates economic growthRigid structure — changing Act is time-consuming

3.3 Government Company

Definition: Under Section 2(45) of Companies Act, 2013 — any company in which not less than 51% of paid-up capital is held by Central/State Government(s). Includes subsidiaries of govt companies.

Examples: SAIL, BHEL, BEML, BSNL, IOCL, HPCL, BEL, GAIL

💡 Key Point: Shares of government companies are purchased in the name of the President of India.

Features:

FeatureExplanation
IncorporationUnder Companies Act, 2013 — executive decision, not legislation
Legal EntitySeparate legal existence independent of government
ManagementBoard of Directors nominated by government
FinanceAt least 51% from govt; rest from capital market
AuditFree from govt budgetary controls; Annual Report to Parliament

Merits & Limitations

✅ Merits❌ Demerits
Easy formation — no separate Act neededFreedom only in name — govt controls everything
Operational autonomyLack of accountability
Separate legal entityDefeats purpose of registering under Companies Act
Good quality products at reasonable prices

4. Global Enterprises (Multinational Companies / MNCs)

Definition: A company whose business operations extend beyond the country in which it is incorporated. Also known as 'Supernatural Companies' or 'Transnational Companies'. 
            graph LR
              HC["Home Country
Head Office"] -->|Controls| HC1["Branch in Country A"]
              HC -->|Controls| HC2["Branch in Country B"]
              HC -->|Controls| HC3["Branch in Country C"]
              HC1 & HC2 & HC3 --> HO["Host Countries"]

Examples: Coca-Cola, McDonald's, Intel, Sony, Apple, Google, Samsung, Nestle

🍟 McDonald's Example: USA = Home Country. India, Pakistan, etc. = Host Countries. They make India-specific products like McAloo Tikki and McMaharaja Mac!

Features of MNCs:

FeatureExplanation
Huge Capital ResourcesMassive financial resources — can raise funds globally
Centralised ControlHead Office in home country controls all operations
Foreign CollaborationCollaborate with local companies for production & technology
Advanced TechnologyUse latest, capital-intensive technology & innovative techniques
Product InnovationContinuously engaged in R&D for new products
Aggressive MarketingUse aggressive strategies & up-to-date market info
Expanded MarketOperate globally through subsidiaries & affiliates

Benefits & Concerns:

✅ Benefits to Host Country❌ Concerns / Demerits
Employment opportunitiesDisregard of national priorities
Advanced technology transferCreation of monopoly & kill local businesses
Inflow of foreign capitalDepletion of natural resources
Improved standard of livingObsolete technology dumping
Growth of domestic firmsThreat to national sovereignty
Healthy competitionProfit repatriation

5. Joint Venture

Definition: When two or more firms join together to establish a new enterprise. They contribute capital and participate in management. In simple words: A partnership between two firms.

Examples:

  • Maruti Suzuki (India + Japan)
  • Hero Honda (India + Japan)
  • ICICI Prudential (India + US)
  • TATA Starbucks (India + US)

Features:

FeatureExplanation
Increased ResourcesBoth firms bring financial & human resources → faster growth
Access to New MarketsForeign companies get access to vast Indian market
Access to TechnologyAdvanced techniques → better quality, lower cost
InnovationsNew ideas & technology → innovative products
Low Cost of ProductionBenefit from India's low-cost raw material & labour
Established Brand NameOne partner benefits from other's goodwill & brand

6. Public Private Partnership (PPP)

Definition: A legally binding contract between the government (public) and a private business firm for the provision of public assets/services for public benefit. 
            graph LR
              GOV["Public Sector
Government"] -->|Provides:
Funds, Land,
Approvals, Permissions| PPP["PPP
Project"]
              PRI["Private Sector
Company"] -->|Provides:
Technology, Expertise,
Management| PPP
              PPP --> OUT["Result
Public Infrastructure
& Services
e.g., DMRC"]

Example: Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) — government provided land & approvals, private sector brought technology & management.

Features of PPP:

  • Public Partner: Government entities (ministries, municipalities, state-owned enterprises)
  • Private Partner: Local/foreign investors with technical or financial expertise
  • Role of Public: Ensure social obligations are fulfilled
  • Role of Private: Bring expertise in operations, management & innovation
  • Contract: Private party bears financial, technical & operational risk
  • Cost: Borne by users (not taxpayers) in some cases
  • Capital Subsidy: Government may give a one-time grant
  • Revenue Sharing: Revenue shared in agreed ratio

7. Practice Questions

Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. When utmost secrecy is required, which public sector enterprise is suitable?

  • (a) Public Corporation
  • (b) Government Company
  • (c) Departmental Undertaking ✔️
  • (d) Public Private Partnership

Q2. Which is the oldest form of organisation in the public sector?

  • (a) Public Sector Undertaking
  • (b) Multi-National Corporation
  • (c) Departmental Undertaking ✔️
  • (d) Statutory Corporation

Q3. Which statement is NOT true?

  • (a) Air India is a limited company but not owned by Government of India
  • (b) Reliance Industries is a public limited company owned by the government ✔️
  • (c) LIC is a government company
  • (d) India Post is a departmental undertaking

Q4. "In the Railway Budget, the minister provides for more trains for his home state." Which limitation does this reflect?

  • (a) Insensitive towards consumers
  • (b) Political Interference ✔️
  • (c) Lack of flexibility
  • (d) Red tapism

Q5. Centralised control in MNCs implies control exercised by:

  • (a) Branches
  • (b) Subsidiaries
  • (c) Headquarters ✔️
  • (d) Parliament

Assertion-Reason Questions

Q6. Assertion (A): Statutory Corporation gets funds from public through its issue of shares.
Reason (R): Statutory Corporation is established under a Special Act of Parliament.

  • (d) (A) is False but (R) is True ✔️

Q7. Assertion (A): Separate legislation of Parliament is needed for a Government Company.
Reason (R): Government Company can be established under the Companies Act.

  • (d) (A) is False but (R) is True ✔️

Case Study: Indian Railways

Indian Railway is part of the Railway Ministry. Finances come from govt treasury, revenue deposited there. Employees recruited like civil servants.

Questions:

  1. Type of enterprise? → Departmental Undertaking
  2. Status of employees? → Government employees
  3. How does it get finance? → Budget allocations from govt treasury
  4. What about revenue? → Deposited into govt treasury

Case Study: GAIL

Gas Authority of India Ltd. Majority shares held by GOI. Registered under Companies Act, 2013.

Questions:

  1. Type of enterprise? → Government Company
  2. Shares bought in whose name? → President of India
  3. Where are reports submitted? → Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha
