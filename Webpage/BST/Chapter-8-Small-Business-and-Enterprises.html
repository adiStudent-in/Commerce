1. What is Entrepreneurship

Definition: Entrepreneurship is systematic purposeful creative activity of identifying need and organising production with risk.

Points:

  1. It identifies a need of customers.
  2. It mobilises resources.
  3. It organises production.
  4. It delivers value to customers.
  5. It gives returns to investors.
  6. It earns profits for self.
  7. It bears risks and uncertainties.

CHECK: Say the definition of Entrepreneurship in one sentence.

2. Importance of Entrepreneurship

Definition: Importance means need of entrepreneurship for nation and society.

Points:

  1. Developing countries must stop outflow of national wealth.
  2. They must produce import substitute goods locally.
  3. They must produce more consumer goods and services locally.
  4. They must produce goods for export to earn foreign exchange.
  5. All three needs are met by more entrepreneurs.

2.1 Lifeline of Nation

  1. Entrepreneurship is yardstick of development of country.
  2. No country can grow without enterprises.
  3. US UK and other developed countries have well established enterprises.
  4. Every nation increases trade by having more enterprises.

2.2 Innovation

  1. Entrepreneur is innovator.
  2. Entrepreneur tries new methods of production.
  3. Entrepreneur tries new technology.
  4. Entrepreneur tries new ways of marketing.
  5. New ideas enhance economic development.

2.3 Growth of Economy

  1. Entrepreneurs adapt to changes in environment.
  2. Changes in enterprises bring changes in society.
  3. Latest technology brings growth in enterprises.
  4. It also brings growth of economy at large.

2.4 Employment Opportunities

  1. Entrepreneurship gives scope for self employment.
  2. It also gives employment to large number of people.
  3. Growth in entrepreneurial activities leads to more employment.

2.5 Social Benefits

  1. Entrepreneurs adopt latest technology.
  2. They raise standard of living.
  3. They give good quality products at lowest cost.
  4. They set enterprises in backward areas.
  5. They bring balanced regional growth.
  6. They save scarce resources by optimum utilisation.

Note: Increased profits by minimum cost is benefit to entrepreneur. It is not macro importance.

CHECK: Name three national needs met by entrepreneurs. Answer: Import substitutes. Local consumer goods. Exports.

3. Characteristics of Entrepreneurship

3.1 Systematic Activity

Definition: Entrepreneurship is systematic step by step purposeful activity.

  1. It needs temperamental skill and knowledge.
  2. Skills can be acquired by formal education.
  3. Skills can be acquired by vocational training.
  4. Skills can be acquired by observation and work experience.

3.2 Lawful and Purposeful Activity

  1. Entrepreneurship aims to conduct lawful business.
  2. Purpose is to create value lawfully.

3.3 Related to Innovation

  1. It involves continuous search for new ideas.
  2. It introduces new products.
  3. It finds new markets.
  4. It finds new sources of supply of inputs.
  5. It brings technological breakthroughs.
  6. It introduces newer organisation forms.
  7. Aim is to do things better cheaper faster.

3.4 Organisation of Production

  1. Production needs land labour capital technology.
  2. Entrepreneur mobilises these resources into firm.
  3. Entrepreneur may have only idea.
  4. Entrepreneur promotes idea among resource providers.

3.5 Risk-taking

  1. Entrepreneurship has bigger risk than employment.
  2. There is no assured payoff.
  3. Essence is willingness to assume risk.
  4. Risk arises from creation of new ideas.
  5. Without willingness to assume risk it never succeeds.

CHECK: New product reflects which characteristic? Answer: Innovation.

4. Process of Entrepreneurship

Definition: Process is sequence of steps from self-discovery to harvest.

  1. Self-discovery comes first.
  2. Identifying opportunities comes next.
  3. Generating and evaluating ideas comes next.
  4. Planning comes next.
  5. Raising capital and resourcing comes next.
  6. Start-up launch comes next.
  7. Growth comes next.
  8. Harvest comes last.

4.1 Self-discovery

  1. Entrepreneur assesses strengths and weaknesses.
  2. Entrepreneur checks what he enjoys doing.
  3. Entrepreneur checks work experience.

4.2 Identifying Opportunities

  1. Entrepreneur finds need want and problems.
  2. He finds opportunity to overcome problems.
  3. He finds opportunity to fulfil need.

4.3 Generating and Evaluating Ideas

  1. He uses discussion and brainstorming.
  2. He uses creativity and own experience.
  3. He develops ideas to meet expectations of customers.

4.4 Planning

  1. Entrepreneur conducts research.
  2. Entrepreneur makes business plan.
  3. Entrepreneur makes strategies.

4.5 Raising Capital and Resourcing

  1. Entrepreneur uses business plan to attract finance.
  2. Source is venture capitalist and partners.
  3. Resourcing also includes team technology suppliers networks.

4.6 Start-up

  1. Entrepreneur launches enterprise.
  2. He tries to develop more customers.
  3. He makes changes as per environment.

4.7 Growth

  1. Entrepreneur tries to expand enterprise.
  2. Entrepreneur tries to grow enterprise.

4.8 Harvest

  1. Harvest means selling business and harvesting reward.
  2. Many entrepreneurs sell existing business.
  3. They start a new venture.

CHECK: Name steps in order. Answer: Self-discovery. Opportunity. Idea. Planning. Capital. Start-up. Growth. Harvest.

5. Meaning of Small Business

Definition: Small Business is business with small investment turnover and employment.

Historical definition:

  1. Immediately after independence definition was given by Industries Development and Regulation Act 1951.
  2. Limit was not more than 50 persons when using power.
  3. Limit was 100 persons when not using power.
  4. Capital assets did not exceed 5 lakh.
  5. This is historical definition only. It is not current limit.

CHECK: What was 1951 capital limit? Answer: 5 lakh.

6. MSME Classification

Definition: MSME classification uses composite criteria of investment and turnover.

Rules:

  1. Same limits apply to manufacturing and services.
  2. Both investment and turnover conditions must be met.
  3. Registration is done on Udyam portal.

6.1 Lecture and NCERT Limits — 2020 Notification

Small EnterpriseMore than Rs 1 crore but does not exceed Rs 10 croreDoes not exceed Rs 50 crore

Read: Small means investment up to 10 crore and turnover up to 50 crore.

Note: Budget 2025-26 revised limits from 1 April 2025.

CHECK: Small turnover limit as per lecture table? Answer: 50 crore.

7. Role of Small Business in India

7.1 Output Generation

  1. Small industries contribute almost 40 percent of gross industrial value added.
  2. They contribute 45 percent of total exports from India.
  3. Figures are as per NCERT. They are required for exams.

7.2 Employment Generation

  1. After agriculture small industries are second largest employers.
  2. They use more labour intensive techniques.
  3. They use less capital intensive techniques.
  4. This is blessing for labour surplus country like India.

7.3 Balanced Regional Development

  1. Small industries use simple products.
  2. They use simple technologies.
  3. They depend on locally available resources.
  4. They can be set up anywhere.
  5. They can be widely spread.
  6. They contribute to balanced development.

7.4 Variety of Products

  1. They produce enormous variety of products.
  2. Examples are consumer goods and readymade garments.
  3. Examples are hosiery stationery soaps detergents utensils.
  4. Examples are rubber plastic leather wood steel furniture.
  5. Examples are television refrigerator electronics.

7.5 Opportunity for Entrepreneurship

  1. Small scale is good platform for skill and talent.
  2. It requires little capital.
  3. Lot of support is given by government.

7.6 Low Cost Production

  1. They use locally available resources.
  2. They have low overhead expenses.
  3. They have low running cost.
  4. This is their competitive strength.

CHECK: What percent of exports as per NCERT? Answer: 45 percent.

8. Startup India Scheme

Definition: Startup India is Government of India action plan to promote and nurture entrepreneurship.

  1. Campaign was announced by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 15 August 2015.
  2. Action Plan was formally launched on 16 January 2016.
  3. Aim is to develop ecosystem for entrepreneurship.
  4. Government offers bank financing schemes to promote startups.

CHECK: Announcement date? Answer: 15 August 2015.

9. Meaning of Startup

Definition as per 2016 lecture and NCERT: Startup is business enterprise in India started less than 5 years ago with annual turnover less than 25 crore.

Lecture conditions:

  1. Form is private limited company.
  2. Form can be partnership.
  3. Form can be limited liability partnership.
  4. Registered in India.
  5. Started less than 5 years ago.
  6. Annual turnover less than 25 crore.

Note — Current DPIIT update 2019: Age is up to 10 years from incorporation. Turnover is up to Rs 100 crore in any financial year. Entity is Private Ltd Company or registered Partnership Firm or LLP. Sole proprietor is not included in DPIIT list. Work is innovation improvement scalable model. Needs DPIIT recognition on portal.

Exam tip: For exams write 5 years and 25 crore. Add 10 years and 100 crore as current note.

CHECK: Lecture turnover limit? Answer: 25 crore.

10. Startup India Benefits

10.1 No Inspection

  1. No inspection for 3 years regarding labour laws.
  2. Startups need only self-certification.
  3. Inspection is only on complaint for 9 labour and 3 environment laws.

10.2 Registration

  1. Government launched mobile app and portal.
  2. Startup can get registered in one day.
  3. There is single window for clearance approvals registration.
  4. Portal became operational around April 2016.

10.3 Income Tax Exemption

  1. Income tax exemption is available for first 3 years.
  2. It is under Section 80-IAC.
  3. It is 100 percent deduction for 3 consecutive years out of first 10 years.
  4. It needs DPIIT recognition and Inter-Ministerial Board certificate.
  5. Company must be incorporated after 1 April 2016.

10.4 Capital Gains Tax Exemption

  1. Startup can claim capital gains exemption.
  2. It is under Section 54GB with conditions.
  3. It applies on investment of residential property gains in eligible startup equity.
  4. There is 5 year lock-in and other conditions.
  5. It is not blanket 3 year holiday.

10.5 Patent and IP Benefit

  1. Startup can claim 80 percent rebate in patent fees.
  2. Government gives 80 percent rebate in fees.
  3. Facility includes fast-track examination.
  4. Needs DPIIT recognition.

10.6 Funding Schemes

  1. Government announced Rs 10,000 crore Fund of Funds for Startups.
  2. Fund is routed via SIDBI to SEBI registered AIFs.
  3. It is not given directly to startups.
  4. Rs 500 crore was initial credit guarantee corpus in 2016 plan.
  5. Later Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups covers loans up to about Rs 10 crore.

CHECK: Patent fee rebate? Answer: 80 percent.

11. Funding Options for Startups

11.1 Self-funding

  1. Self-funding is first option to start business.
  2. Entrepreneur uses own money.
  3. At later stage investors may get attracted.

11.2 Crowdfunding

  1. Entrepreneur puts detailed description on crowdfunding platform.
  2. He mentions goals plans profit funding need reasons.
  3. Investors read and give money if they like idea.
  4. Contributors make online pledges.
  5. Promise is pre-buying product or donation.
  6. Anyone can contribute small money.

11.3 Angel Investment

  1. Angel investors are individuals with surplus cash.
  2. They have keen interest in upcoming startups.
  3. They helped Google Yahoo Alibaba.
  4. Investment is in early stages of growth.
  5. They take more risk for higher returns.
  6. They also give mentorship.

11.4 Venture Capital

  1. Venture capitals are professionally managed funds.
  2. They invest in companies with huge potential.
  3. Investment is scalable high growth firms.
  4. Funding is in stages with equity.

11.5 Bank Loans

  1. Banks are first place entrepreneurs go for funding.
  2. Examples are SBI HDFC ICICI Axis banks.
  3. Banks offer many options including collateral free loans.
  4. Collateral free loans are only via guarantee schemes.
  5. Examples of schemes are CGTMSE CGSS MUDRA.

11.6 NBFC Loans

  1. NBFC means Non Banking Financial Company.
  2. NBFCs serve those without access to conventional banking.
  3. It is popular for limited requirements.
  4. It works when credit rating is not favoured by bank.
  5. Benefits are flexible terms faster approval less paperwork.
  6. Rate of interest is generally higher than banks.

11.7 Government Programmes

  1. Government launched Rs 10,000 crore Startup Fund in Union Budget.
  2. SIDBI offers business loans to MSME sector.
  3. MUDRA extends benefits to micro small units. Full form is Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency.
  4. State example is Kerala Startup Mission in Kerala.
  5. Maharashtra Centre for Entrepreneurship Development in Maharashtra.
  6. iStart Rajasthan and Rajasthan Startup Fest in Rajasthan.

CHECK: Many persons contribute small money. What is it? Answer: Crowdfunding.

12. IPR — Meaning and Categories

Definition: IPR means property rights on creations of mind. It allows innovative entrepreneurs to protect inventions.

  1. IPR gives edge over competitor.
  2. IPR helps to become leader in field.

Categories:

CategoryIncludes
Industrial PropertyInventions patents trademarks industrial designs geographical indications
CopyrightLiterary and artistic works

CHECK: Name two IPR categories. Answer: Industrial property. Copyright.

13. Types of IPR

13.1 Copyright

  1. Protection gives exclusive right to copy work.
  2. Right includes modify work and create derivative works.
  3. Right includes distribute perform display publicly.
  4. Examples are films music songs books novels paintings drawings.
  5. In India term is life of author plus 60 years.

13.2 Patent

  1. Patents protect novel non-obvious useful processes methods inventions.
  2. If granted it gives right to sell use make import invention.
  3. Requirements for patent are complex.
  4. Examples are machines devices solutions.
  5. In India term is 20 years.

13.3 Trademark

  1. Trademark is like brand name.
  2. It is any word or symbol that represents product.
  3. It identifies and distinguishes product in marketplace.
  4. Example is Nike of Nike company.
  5. No one else can use this trademark.
  6. Examples are logo symbol brand name.
  7. In India term is 10 years and renewable.

13.4 Geographical Indication

  1. GI identifies agricultural natural manufactured products.
  2. Products include handicrafts industrial goods food stuffs.
  3. Products originate from definite territory.
  4. Quality reputation features are due to geographical origin.

13.5 Plant Variety

  1. Plant variety groups plants into categories.
  2. Grouping is based on botanical characteristics.

13.6 Design

  1. Design includes shape pattern arrangement of lines.
  2. It includes colour combination applied to article.

13.7 Semiconductor Layout Design

  1. It means layout of transistors and circuitry elements.
  2. It includes lead wires connecting elements.
  3. It is expressed in semiconductor integrated circuits.

CHECK: Nike is example of what? Answer: Trademark.

14. IPR as Asset for Entrepreneur

  1. IPR is very important for innovative entrepreneur.
  2. IPR includes copyrights patents trademarks.
  3. IPR gives edge over competitor.
  4. IPR helps to become leader in field.

CHECK: Name three assets under IPR. Answer: Copyrights. Patents. Trademarks.

16. Exam Questions With Answers

  1. Define Entrepreneurship.
    A: Entrepreneurship is systematic purposeful creative activity of identifying need and organising production with risk.
  2. Which is important role of entrepreneurship?
    A: Generating employment opportunities.
  3. Entrepreneurship contributes by what?
    A: Creating employment and promoting innovation.
  4. Which is characteristic of entrepreneurship?
    A: Risk-taking.
  5. New product reflects which characteristic?
    A: Innovation.
  6. State 1951 definition of small scale industry.
    A: Not more than 50 persons with power and 100 without power and capital assets not exceeding 5 lakh.
  7. Give MSME limits as per lecture table.
    A: Micro 1 cr 5 cr. Small 10 cr 50 cr. Medium 50 cr 250 cr.
  8. Small industries share in exports as per NCERT?
    A: 45 percent of total exports.
  9. Small industries share in industrial value added?
    A: Almost 40 percent of gross industrial value added.
  10. Contribution from more than one person is called what?
    A: Crowdfunding.
  11. No interference for first 3 years relates to which scheme?
    A: No inspection with self-certification.
  12. How much rebate in patent fees?
    A: 80 percent.
  13. How much fund announced for startups?
    A: Rs 10,000 crore Fund of Funds plus Rs 500 crore initial credit guarantee corpus.
  14. Who are angel investors?
    A: Individuals with surplus cash investing in early stage startups with high risk.
  15. What is venture capital?
    A: Professionally managed funds investing in high potential companies.
  16. What is NBFC?
    A: Non Banking Financial Company serving those without easy bank access.
  17. Expand SIDBI and MUDRA.
    A: SIDBI is Small Industries Development Bank of India. MUDRA is Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency.
  18. Riya started handmade unit and employed locals. Identify importance. How does it solve unemployment? One other importance?
    A: Employment generation. Entrepreneurs establish businesses and employ people. Other importance is innovation or economic development or balanced regional development.
  19. Define Patent with term.
    A: Right over novel non-obvious useful invention for 20 years.
  20. Define Copyright with term.
    A: Exclusive right to copy adapt distribute perform display work for life plus 60 years.
  21. Define Trademark with example.
    A: Word or symbol identifying product e.g. Nike. Term 10 years renewable.
  22. Define GI.
    A: Indication identifying products from definite territory where quality is due to origin.
