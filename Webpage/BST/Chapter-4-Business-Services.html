Section 1

The Service Sector: Meaning, Importance and Nature of Services

The idea of this section. Goods alone cannot run a business. This section explains what the service sector is, why every business depends on it, and the five features that make a service different from a product.

1.1 What Is the Service Sector?

The definition — learn this line:

The service sector includes all those economic activities which are required for uninterrupted production and exchange of goods. It includes specialised services required for removing various hindrances of trade.

Why the service sector matters:

PointWhat the lecture says
Production in timeIt is very important that goods are produced in time
Reach in timeIt is equally important that such goods reach the consumers at the right time
Twin goalsService sector helps to achieve these twin goals
Hindrances of tradeSpecialised services remove the various hindrances of trade
BackboneService sector is the backbone of every business and industry
  • Trade and industry is not possible without the various service sectors like banking, insurance, communication, transportation, warehousing, etc.
  • In short, service sector is the backbone of every business and industry.

Section 3

Banking Services

The idea of this section. Banking is the biggest service business in this chapter. This section covers what a bank is, the five kinds of accounts it offers, the services it renders, and the way it is going digital.

3.1 What Is a Bank?

The definition — learn this line:

A bank is an institution which attracts money on deposits for the purpose of being lent to industry or trade.

  • A bank lends what it borrows from others.
  • A bank is buyer and seller of money.
  • A bank earns a margin of profit by lending money which it borrows.

The legal definition — Indian Banking Regulation Act, 1949:

"Accepting deposits of money from the public for the purpose of lending or investments."

3.3 Services Provided by Banks

Demand Draft (Bank Draft)

  • A bank draft can be obtained from a bank after depositing the required amount in the bank.
  • It is a financial instrument through which money can be remitted.
  • The bank also charges some commission for issuing a bank draft.
  • A bank draft can never be dishonoured, as it is issued by the bank after receiving the payment.

Banker's Cheque (Pay Order)

  • This is a document which instructs a bank to pay a certain sum to a third party.
  • Such orders are normally acknowledged by the bank, which provides a guarantee that the payment will be made.
  • It is a kind of bank draft which is payable within the town, so it can be called a local bank draft.
  • The commission charged on pay orders is less than that on a bank draft.

Overdraft

  • Under this arrangement, a customer having a current account is allowed to withdraw more than the balance in his account.
  • He can overdraw up to a specific limit and for an agreed period.
  • Interest is charged on the amount virtually withdrawn.

Cash Credit

  • Under this arrangement, the borrower is allowed to withdraw up to a certain limit against security like shares, stocks, bonds, etc.
  • It is helpful for meeting day-to-day working capital needs.

Short / Medium / Long-term Loans

  • A loan is a lump sum advance, repayable on expiry of a specified period.
  • It may be secured or unsecured, and the borrower repays it in instalments.
Type of loanDuration

Exam tip. Overdraft vs Cash Credit — an overdraft is a current account facility with a limit and an agreed period, and interest is charged on the amount virtually withdrawn. A cash credit is allowed against security like shares, stocks and bonds, and it helps with day-to-day working capital needs.

Section 4

Insurance, Communication and Warehousing Services

The idea of this section. Insurance protects against risk, communication moves messages and warehousing holds goods. This section covers the three together and closes with the goods–services comparison.

4.1 What Is Insurance?

The definition — learn this line:

Insurance is a contract between the insurer and insured in which the insurer agrees to make good the loss of insured on happening of an event, in consideration of a regular payment called premium. This agreement or contract is in writing and is known as insurance policy.

The four important terms in an insurance contract:

TermMeaning
InsurerThe individual or firm, known as the insurance company, which agrees to compensate the loss of the insured
InsuredThe individual or firm who gets compensation of loss; the insured pays a regular amount of premium
Happening of the eventIt refers to the subject matter of policy or the kinds of losses covered under the policy
PremiumThe amount paid regularly by the insured — quarterly, half-yearly or annually — for the cover; in life insurance the premium can be paid monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually

Exam tip. Four terms, four marks: Insurer, Insured, Happening of the event, Premium. Add "insurance policy" for the written form and the definition earns full marks.

4.2 The Seven Principles of Insurance

1 · Principle of Utmost Good Faith

  • Insurance is a contract based on faith. The insured and insurer must disclose all the material facts to each other, and both parties should not hide any fact related to the insurance policy from each other.
  • If the insured hides any material fact and the insurer comes to know about it later, the insurer can refuse to pay compensation.
  • Example: a person takes a life insurance policy hiding the fact that he is a heart patient, and later dies of a heart attack. A factory owner hides that the electricity board has issued him a statutory warning letter to get his factory's wiring changed, and later the factory catches fire due to a short circuit of the wiring.

2 · Principle of Insurable Interest

  • The insured must have an insurable interest in the subject matter of the insurance policy. Without interest, taking an insurance policy is a gamble and fraudulent activity, and the law does not permit it.
  • A person can take a life insurance policy for his spouse or for his/her children; for a stranger he/she cannot.
  • In fire insurance the insured must suffer a financial loss if the subject matter is damaged, to prove the interest, or he must be the owner of the subject matter.
  • A lender who has taken a loan against the security of a factory premises can take a fire insurance policy of that factory without being the owner, because he has a financial interest in the factory premises.
Type of insuranceWhen must the insurable interest be present?
Life insuranceAt the time of taking the policy
Fire insuranceAt the time of taking the policy as well as at the time of loss of the subject matter
All other types, including marine insuranceAt the time of loss of the subject matter

3 · Principle of Indemnity

  • Insurance is not a contract of making profit. The purpose of insurance is to bring back the insured in the same financial position as he was before the loss.
  • It is not applicable on a life insurance policy, because one cannot estimate the loss due to the death of a person.
  • Example: a person insured his factory for 2 lakh against fire. Due to fire he suffered a loss of 1 lakh, so the insurance company will compensate him 1 lakh only and not the policy amount of 2 lakh.

4 · Principle of Contribution

  • If a person has taken more than one insurance policy for the same subject matter, all the insurers will contribute to the amount of loss and compensate him for the actual amount of loss. Separately, he cannot claim the total loss from each insurer.
  • The insurer contributes in proportion to the amount assured by each.

The formula:

Compensation from an insurer = (Sum assured with that insurer × Actual loss) ÷ Total sum insured

  • Example: a person gets his house insured against fire for 1 lakh with insurer A and for ₹50,000 with insurer B. A loss of ₹75,000 occurred. Then A is liable to pay ₹50,000 and B is liable to pay 25,000.

5 · Principle of Subrogation

  • After paying the compensation, the insurer steps into the shoes of the insured. When the insured is compensated for loss or damage to the property insured, the right of ownership of such property passes on to the insurer.
  • It is a corollary to the principle of indemnity, because once the compensation is given, if the insured later realises some money by the sale of the damaged property, that will bring profit for him, and insurance is not a contract of making profit.
  • Example: a fire insurance policy on a factory; loss of ₹1,00,000 and the same amount received as compensation. Later on, half burnt goods were sold for ₹10,000 — this ₹10,000 is kept by the insurance company and not by the insured.

6 · Principle of Causa Proxima

  • The cause or reason for the loss must be related to the subject matter of the insurance contract. If the loss is due to some other cause, the insurer can deny to pay the compensation.
  • Example: under a marine insurance policy for sending wheat bags, if a rat spoiled the wheat on the way, no compensation is given, because the cause of loss should be sea perils. If the rat makes a hole in the ship through which water enters and spoils the wheat, compensation is given, because the loss of wheat is due to sea water.

7 · Principle of Mitigation of Loss

  • The insured must take care of his property or subject matter of insurance in the same way as he would take care of it without taking the insurance policy. He should not become careless of his property after taking the policy.
  • It is the duty of the insured to make a reasonable effort and take all available precautions to save the insured property.
  • Example: if a person has taken a fire insurance policy for his house and fire breaks out, he should take all the measures to stop the fire and minimise the loss.

Memory hook (lecture order). G → I → I → C → S → C → M — Good Faith (Utmost) → Insurable Interest → Indemnity → Contribution → Subrogation → Causa Proxima → Mitigation of Loss.

Life Insurance

  • People take an insurance policy when they have the fear of dying too early; then, to provide financial support to their family, they take a life insurance policy.
  • When people think of living too late, they take an insurance policy to get financial support during old age.
  • The sum assured is paid at the death or on the maturity period, whichever comes earlier. If a person dies early, his family gets the compensation amount; if a person survives, he/she himself/herself can get the policy amount.
  • The insured pays a fixed amount of premium — monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually — and the insurer pays the policy amount at the death or on expiry of a specific time period, which is also called the maturity time of the policy.

Main elements of life insurance:

  • It is a contract of utmost good faith — the insured should be honest and must disclose all material facts about his health to the insurer.
  • The insurable interest must be present at the time of taking the policy; insurable interest at the time of maturity is not necessary.
  • It is not a contract of indemnity — the life of a human being cannot be compensated.
  • The principles of indemnity, contribution and subrogation are not applicable to a life insurance policy.

Fire Insurance

  • Fire insurance is a contract under which one party (the insurer), in return for a consideration (premium), agrees to indemnify the other party (the insured) for the financial loss which the latter may suffer due to damage to the property insured by fire during a specific period of time and up to a specific amount.
  • The contract containing the terms and conditions is known as the policy. Generally the fire policy contains the name of the parties, description of the insured property, the sum for which the property is insured, amount of premium payable and the period insured against.
  • Generally the period of insurance is one year.

Main elements of fire insurance:

  • The insurable interest should be present at the time of taking the policy as well as at the time of loss.
  • It is a contract of utmost good faith, so the insured must disclose all material facts related to the property for which insurance is taken.
  • The principle of indemnity is strictly applicable — the insured gets only the loss amount or the policy amount, whichever is less.
  • Compensation is paid only when the loss is due to fire, so the doctrine of causa proxima is also strictly followed.

Marine Insurance

  • A contract of marine insurance is an arrangement under which the insurer undertakes to indemnify the insured in the manner and to the extent thereby agreed against marine losses.
  • Marine risks are the perils of sea, e.g., storm, collision, capture, seizure, sinking of ship, etc.
  • It is a contract between the insured and the insurer. The insured may be a cargo owner or a ship owner or a freight receiver.

5.2 Case Study with Model Answer

Dhani Mani Bank is a type of private sector bank offering various services to its customers. It offers various types of bank account options to its customers. Rakesh being businessman has chosen the type of account in which the deposits are the most liquid and there are no limit to the number of transactions or the amount of transaction in a day. On the other hand, his mother has opened a type of account where she can conveniently deposit the money she saves. She has got cheque facility and lot of flexibility for deposits and withdrawal whereas Rakesh's sister Reema opened the account where she will deposit 2,000 per month for next two years.

Questions

  • (a) Identify the various types of bank accounts used by Rakesh and his family.
  • (b) Describe briefly two other types of accounts.

Model answer

(a) The accounts used

  • Rakesh's account — Current Account. He needs the most liquid account with no limit on the number of transactions or on the amount transacted in a day. A current account is opened by businessmen; deposits and withdrawals can be made whenever desired, withdrawals are made by cheque, overdraft and credit limit facility is available, and a statement of account is sent every week/fortnight/month. No interest is paid on it, and a service charge is charged by the bank.
  • Rakesh's mother's account — Savings Account. She can conveniently deposit the money she saves and enjoys cheque facility with flexibility for deposits and withdrawal. She is issued a cheque book, passbook and a debit card, and all amounts deposited and withdrawn are recorded in the passbook.
  • Rakesh's sister Reema's account — Recurring Deposit Account. She deposits ₹2,000 per month for the next two years. A fixed amount is deposited every month for a fixed period, and the amount together with interest is repaid on maturity. The rate of interest is higher than on a savings account.

(b) Two other types of accounts

  • (i) Fixed Deposit Account. It is repayable after the expiry of the specified period, which may vary from six months to five years. The longer the period of deposit, the higher the rate of interest. The bank issues a fixed deposit receipt, and the rate of interest in FD is the highest.
  • (ii) Multiple Option Deposit Account. It can be a combination with a savings A/c or a current A/c. The depositor enjoys the liquidity of savings and current A/c together with the rate of interest of a fixed deposit, and when the amount of deposit exceeds a particular limit, that amount is automatically transferred into fixed deposit.

6.3 Common Traps to Avoid

  1. Inventory — services need no stock. In the lecture MCQ "which of the following is not a characteristic of services", the printed key is (b) Inventory.
  2. RTGS vs NEFT — RTGS is real-time, gross settlement, controlled by the RBI, and carries a minimum of ₹2,00,000. NEFT works on net settlement and has no minimum value.
  3. Demand Draft vs Banker's Cheque — a bank draft can never be dishonoured; a banker's cheque (pay order) is payable within the town and carries a lower commission.
  4. Overdraft vs Cash Credit — an overdraft is a current account facility with a limit and an agreed period, and interest is charged on the amount virtually withdrawn. A cash credit is allowed against security like shares, stocks and bonds, for working capital needs.
  5. Interest order of accounts — the current account pays no interest; the fixed deposit pays the highest.
  6. Life insurance is not indemnity — the principles of indemnity, contribution and subrogation do not apply to a life insurance policy. Life insurance is a contract of assurance.
  7. Insurable interest timing — life: at the time of taking the policy; fire: at taking and at loss; marine: at the time of loss.
  8. Contribution vs Subrogation — contribution shares the loss between insurers in proportion to the sum assured; subrogation passes the ownership of the damaged property to the insurer after compensation.
  9. Causa proxima — a rat that spoils the wheat on a ship means no claim; a rat that makes a hole and lets sea water in means a claim.
  10. Postal services — the printed MCQ key for the drawback is (a) Slow in nature; courier services are the fast private alternative and give desk-to-desk service.

Banking Services

What is a bank?

A bank is an institution which attracts money on deposits for the purpose of being lent to industry or trade.

  • A bank lends what it borrows from others.
  • A bank is the buyer and seller of money.
  • A bank earns a margin of profit by lending money which it borrows.
  • It accepts deposits, pays interest, makes investments and lends to industry and trade.

Legal definition, Indian Banking Regulation Act, 1949: "Accepting deposits of money from the public for the purpose of lending or investments."

What is insurance?

Insurance is a contract between the insurer and the insured in which the insurer agrees to make good the loss of the insured on the happening of an event, in consideration of a regular payment called premium. The agreement is in writing and is known as the insurance policy.

TermMeaning
InsurerThe individual or firm, the insurance company, which agrees to compensate the loss of the insured
InsuredThe individual or firm who gets compensation of the loss and pays the regular premium
Happening of the eventThe subject matter of the policy or the kinds of losses covered under the policy
PremiumThe amount paid regularly by the insured — quarterly, half-yearly or annually; in life insurance it may be monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually

Exam tip: four terms, four marks — insurer, insured, happening of the event and premium; add "insurance policy" as the written form.

The four types of insurance — life, fire, marine, health

Life insurance is taken when people fear dying too early, to provide financial support to the family, or when they think of living too late, to get support in old age.

  • The sum assured is paid at death or on maturity, whichever comes earlier — the family gets it on early death, the person gets it on survival.
  • The insured pays a fixed premium monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually, and the insurer pays at death or on expiry of the maturity period.
  • It is a contract of utmost good faith — the insured must disclose all material facts about health.
  • Insurable interest must be present at the time of taking the policy; it is not necessary at maturity.
  • It is a contract of assurance, not a contract of indemnity, because the life of a human being cannot be compensated.
  • The principles of indemnity, contribution and subrogation do not apply to life insurance.

Fire insurance is a contract under which the insurer, in return for a premium, agrees to indemnify the insured for the financial loss caused by damage to the insured property by fire during a specific period and up to a specific amount.

  • The policy contains the names of the parties, description of the insured property, the sum insured, the premium payable and the period insured.
  • The period of insurance is generally one year.
  • Insurable interest is present at the time of taking the policy and at the time of loss.
  • It is a contract of utmost good faith, so all material facts about the property must be disclosed.
  • Indemnity applies strictly — the insured gets the loss amount or the policy amount, whichever is less.
  • Causa proxima applies strictly — compensation is paid only when the loss is due to fire.

Marine insurance is an arrangement under which the insurer undertakes to indemnify the insured, in the manner and to the extent agreed, against marine losses.

  • Marine risks are the perils of the sea — storm, collision, capture, seizure, sinking of the ship.
  • The insured may be a cargo owner, a ship owner or a freight receiver.

Recall drill — Session 4

  1. Define insurance, name the written form, and explain insurer, insured, happening of the event and premium.

    Show answer

    Insurance is a contract between the insurer and the insured in which the insurer agrees to make good the loss of the insured on the happening of an event, in consideration of a regular payment called premium, and the written agreement is known as the insurance policy; the insurer is the company which compensates the loss, the insured is the person or firm who gets compensation and pays the premium, the happening of the event is the subject matter of the policy or the kinds of losses covered, and the premium is the regular amount paid quarterly, half-yearly or annually, or monthly in life insurance.

  2. Name the seven principles of insurance in order.

    Show answer

    Utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, contribution, subrogation, causa proxima and mitigation of loss.

  3. Explain utmost good faith with the source examples and the Harish case.

    Show answer

    Both parties must disclose all material facts truthfully and hide nothing, and if the insured hides a material fact the insurer can refuse compensation; a heart patient who hides his condition and dies of a heart attack, or a factory owner who hides the electricity board's warning letter and suffers a wiring fire, illustrates this, and Harish's failure to disclose the highly explosive chemical in his factory is the same violation of utmost good faith.

  4. Explain insurable interest, state when it must exist, and apply the Priya case.

    Show answer

    The insured must have a financial interest in the subject matter, because without it the policy is a gamble and fraudulent; the interest must exist at the time of taking the policy in life insurance, at taking and at loss in fire insurance, and at the time of loss in marine and all other types, and Priya violated this principle because she had no financial interest in her friend's boutique, where the damage was ₹10 lakhs.

  5. Explain indemnity with the ₹2 lakh example and its exception.

    Show answer

    Insurance is not a contract of making profit, so it restores the insured to the same financial position as before the loss; a factory insured for 2 lakh that suffers a loss of 1 lakh gets only 1 lakh, not the policy amount, and the principle does not apply to life insurance because the loss caused by death cannot be estimated.

  6. Explain contribution with the formula and the two worked examples.

    Show answer

    When more than one policy covers the same subject matter, all insurers share the actual loss in proportion to the amount assured by each, using compensation from an insurer equals sum assured with that insurer times actual loss divided by total sum insured; with A insuring a house for 1 lakh and B for ₹50,000 against a loss of ₹75,000, A pays ₹50,000 and B pays ₹25,000, and in the Chetan case Easy General pays ₹1,50,000 while Risk Free pays ₹4,50,000 on the ₹6 lakh loss.

  7. Explain subrogation with the ₹1,00,000 example.

    Show answer

    After paying compensation the insurer steps into the shoes of the insured and the ownership of the damaged property passes to the insurer, which is a corollary to indemnity; when a fire loss of ₹1,00,000 is fully compensated and the half-burnt goods are later sold for ₹10,000, the insurer keeps the ₹10,000 and not the insured.

  8. Explain causa proxima and mitigation of loss with their examples.

    Show answer

    The cause of the loss must be the insured risk, otherwise the insurer can deny compensation, so under a marine policy for wheat a rat spoiling the wheat gives no compensation because the cause is not a sea peril, but if the rat makes a hole and sea water enters and spoils the wheat, compensation is given because the loss is then due to sea water; mitigation requires the insured to care for the property as if uninsured and to make a reasonable effort with all available precautions to save it, so when fire breaks out in an insured house the owner must take all measures to stop the fire and minimise the loss.

  9. Name the four types of insurance and the three marine policies, and give the main features of life insurance.

    Show answer

    The four types are life, fire, marine and health, and the marine policies are cargo, hull and freight; life insurance pays the sum assured at death or maturity, whichever is earlier, charges a fixed premium monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually, is a contract of utmost good faith, needs insurable interest at the time of taking the policy, is not a contract of indemnity, and the principles of indemnity, contribution and subrogation do not apply to it.

  10. Give the main features and elements of fire and marine insurance.

    Show answer

    Fire insurance indemnifies the financial loss from fire damage to the insured property for a specific period, generally one year, and marine insurance indemnifies marine losses caused by perils of the sea such as storm, collision, capture, seizure and sinking; both require utmost good faith, apply indemnity strictly so the insured gets the loss amount or policy amount, whichever is less, and follow causa proxima strictly, while fire needs insurable interest at taking and at loss and marine needs it at the time of loss.

  11. Compare life with general insurance, and life with fire and marine insurance.

    Show answer

    Life insurance covers a certain risk, is a contract of guarantee with protection and investment, needs insurable interest at taking, has no partial loss cover, needs a medical examination, fixes the premium permanently, allows surrender and runs 15-20 years, while general insurance covers an uncertain risk, is a contract of indemnity for fire and marine, gives protection only, may offer partial protection, needs no medical examination in fire and marine, may alter the premium at renewal, does not allow surrender and usually runs one year; against fire and marine, life covers human life, has surrender value and an unlimited policy amount, does not apply indemnity and has a certain claim, while fire covers property and marine covers ship, cargo or freight, both apply indemnity, have measurable loss, no surrender value and a policy amount limited to the value of the subject matter.

  12. Describe postal services, their drawbacks and their mail services.

    Show answer

    Postal services are controlled by the government, reach national and international levels, also offer financial services, started speed post to compete with couriers and are highly reliable, but they are slow in speed and bureaucratic in nature, so businessmen use private courier services that give desk-to-desk service; the mail services are UPC, registered post, parcel post, speed post and courier services.

  13. Name the six telecom services and the four types of warehouses.

    Show answer

    The six telecom services are cellular mobile, radio paging, fixed line, cable, VSAT and DTH, with Tata Sky as a DTH provider, and the four warehouses are private, public, bonded and government, where a bonded warehouse stores imported goods without customs duty until they are cleared.
