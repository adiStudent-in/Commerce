1. What is Internal Trade

Definition: Internal Trade is buying and selling within limits of a country.
  1. 1. Buying and selling takes place within a country.
  2. 2. Payments are made in home currency only.
  3. 3. Payments are received in home currency only.
  4. 4. No formalities are needed.
  5. 5. Or very few formalities are needed.
  6. 6. Local modes of transport are used.
CHECK: Say the definition of Internal Trade in one sentence.

1.1 Types of Internal Trade

  1. 1. Wholesale Trade sells in large quantities.
  2. 2. Retail Trade sells in small lots.
TypeSimple Meaning
Wholesale TradeSell in large quantities
Retail TradeSell in small lots to consumers
CHECK: Name two types of Internal Trade. Answer: Wholesale and Retail.

2. What is Wholesale Trade

Definition: Wholesale Trade is trade in large quantities.
  1. 1. Wholesaler buys directly from manufacturer.
  2. 2. Wholesaler buys in bulk.
  3. 3. Wholesaler sells in small lots to retailer.
  4. 4. Wholesaler is link between manufacturer and retailer.
  5. 5. Wholesaler is first link in chain.

2.1 Features of Wholesale Trade

  1. 1. Wholesaler is link between producer and retailer.
  2. 2. Wholesaler deals in large quantities.
  3. 3. Wholesaler deals in one line only.
  4. 4. Example is stationery and foodgrains.
  5. 5. Wholesaler invests large capital in stock.
  6. 6. Wholesaler sells in small lots to retailers.
  7. 7. Wholesaler generally does not sell to ultimate consumer.
  8. 8. Generally margin of profit is low.
CHECK: Wholesaler is link between whom? Answer: Manufacturer and Retailer.

3. Services of Wholesalers to Manufacturers

3.1 Facilitate Large Scale Production

  1. 1. Wholesaler buys in large quantity.
  2. 2. Maker gets benefit of large scale.
  3. 3. Large scale gives economies of scale.
CHECK: Large buying gives what? Answer: Economies of scale.

3.2 Help in Marketing Functions

  1. 1. Salespersons of wholesalers persuade retailers.
  2. 2. They ask retailers to display goods.
  3. 3. They ask retailers to sell goods.

  1. 1. Wholesaler gives information about customer preferences.
  2. 2. Wholesaler gives information about customer expectations.
  3. 3. Wholesaler gives information about competitors products.
  4. 4. Wholesaler gives information about price.
  5. 5. Wholesaler suggests types required by consumers.
  6. 6. Wholesaler suggests quality required by consumers.

3.4 Storage

  1. 1. Wholesaler buys goods in bulk.
  2. 2. Wholesaler takes full storage responsibility.
  3. 3. Wholesaler bears risk of theft.
  4. 4. Wholesaler bears risk of spoilage.
CHECK: Who bears storage risk? Answer: Wholesaler.

3.5 Financial Assistance

  1. 1. Wholesaler buys goods for cash.
  2. 2. Wholesaler sometimes pays in advance.
  3. 3. Advance is paid while placing order.
  4. 4. Maker needs minimum capital.
CHECK: Advance payment helps whom? Answer: Manufacturer.

3.6 Bearing Risks

  1. 1. Wholesaler deals in own name.
  2. 2. Wholesaler takes delivery of goods.
  3. 3. Wholesaler keeps goods in warehouses.
  4. 4. Wholesaler bears fall in prices.
  5. 5. Wholesaler bears theft and spoilage.
  6. 6. Wholesaler bears fire risk.
CHECK: Name one risk of wholesaler. Answer: Fall in prices.

3.7 Facilitate Production Continuity

  1. 1. Wholesaler buys goods when produced.
  2. 2. Wholesaler stores goods till demand comes.
  3. 3. Production continues throughout the year.
CHECK: Wholesaler buys goods when? Answer: When produced.

4. Services of Wholesalers to Retailers

4.1 Availability of Goods

  1. 1. Retailer needs variety of products.
  2. 2. Wholesaler gives products of many makers.
  3. 3. Retailer need not keep large stock.
CHECK: Who gives variety to retailer? Answer: Wholesaler.

4.2 Grant of Credit

  1. 1. Wholesaler sells goods on credit.
  2. 2. Retailer needs less working capital.
  3. 3. Retailer pays after getting payment from customers.
CHECK: Credit sale helps retailer how? Answer: Less working capital.

4.3 Risk Sharing

  1. 1. Wholesaler bears change in demand.
  2. 2. Wholesaler bears damage in transit.
  3. 3. Wholesaler bears theft in warehouse.
  4. 4. Wholesaler bears spoilage in warehouse.
  5. 5. Retailer is free from transport risk.
  6. 6. Retailer is free from storage risk.
CHECK: Who bears transit risk? Answer: Wholesaler.

4.4 Specialised Knowledge

  1. 1. Wholesaler has direct link with makers.
  2. 2. Wholesaler knows latest technology.
  3. 3. Wholesaler knows new products.
  4. 4. Wholesaler informs retailers about new techniques.
  5. 5. Wholesaler informs retailers about product use.
CHECK: Who updates retailer knowledge? Answer: Wholesaler.

4.5 Publicity

  1. 1. Wholesaler advertises brands kept with him.
  2. 2. Publicity is by pamphlets and banners.
  3. 3. Publicity is by painting transport vehicles.
  4. 4. Publicity helps increase sales of retailers.

4.6 Marketing Support

  1. 1. Wholesaler does advertising for retailers.
  2. 2. Wholesaler does sales promotion for retailers.
  3. 3. Promotion induces customers to buy goods.
  4. 4. Retailer gets more demand for new products.
CHECK: Marketing support increases what? Answer: Demand for new products.

4.7 Economies of Scale

  1. 1. Wholesaler buys in large quantity.
  2. 2. Wholesaler gets big discount from maker.
  3. 3. Wholesaler shares discount with retailers.
  4. 4. Wholesaler charges low profit margin.
  5. 5. Retailer buys in small lots.
  6. 6. Retailer cannot get direct discount.
CHECK: Why retailer gets low price? Answer: Shared discount.

  1. 1. Retailer buys goods from wholesaler.
  2. 2. Retailer sells goods to consumer.
  3. 3. Retailer is link between wholesaler and consumer.
  4. 4. Retailer is last link in chain.
  5. 5. Retailer sells directly to consumers.
  6. 6. Retailer deals with variety of products.
  7. 7. Retailer buys small quantity.
  8. 8. Retailer sells small quantity.
  9. 9. Retailer is generally located in residential areas.
  10. 10. Retailer keeps personal relations with customers.
  11. 11. Retailer may contact by telephone and Internet.
  12. 12. Retailer may contact by TV and showroom.
CHECK: Retailer is link between whom? Answer: Wholesaler and Consumer.

22. GST Introduction

Definition: GST is tax on supply of goods and services.
  1. 1. GST started in India from July 2017.
  2. 2. Earlier taxes were service tax and VAT.
  3. 3. Earlier tax was excise duty also.
  4. 4. GST merged these taxes into one.
  5. 5. GST is single tax at national level.
  6. 6. GST is levied from manufacture to consumption.
CHECK: GST started in which year? Answer: July 2017.

23. Key Features of GST

  1. 1. GST applies in whole country.
  2. 2. GST applies on supply of goods or services.
  3. 3. GST is destination based consumption tax.
  4. 4. Earlier tax was origin based tax.
  5. 5. Import is treated as interstate supply.
  6. 6. Import pays IGST plus customs duty.
  7. 7. CGST, SGST and IGST rates are fixed together.
  8. 8. Rates are fixed by centre and states.
  9. 9. Guidance comes from GST Council.
  10. 10. Four slabs are 5, 12, 18 and 28 per cent.
  11. 11. No tax on export and SEZ supplies.
CHECK: Name four GST slabs. Answer: 5, 12, 18, 28 per cent.

24. GST Council Constitution

  1. 1. Chairperson is Finance Minister.
  2. 2. Vice chairperson is chosen from state ministers.
  3. 3. Members are MOS Finance and state ministers.
  4. 4. Quorum is 50 per cent of members.
  5. 5. States have two third weightage.
  6. 6. Centre has one third weightage.
  7. 7. Decisions need 75 per cent majority.
  8. 8. Council advises on rules and rates.
  9. 9. Council covers all GST matters.
CHECK: Chairperson of GST Council is who? Answer: Finance Minister.

26. Solved Questions From Source

Q1. Wholesaler is a link between? A: Manufacturer and Retailer. Use (c) Manufacturer and Retailer.

Q2. What do you mean by Internal Trade? A: Buying and selling within limits of country is Internal Trade.

Q3. Large quantity buying is characteristic of which trade? A: Wholesale Trade.

Q4. Which is function of wholesaler? A: Purchasing goods from manufacturers in bulk. Use (c).

Q5. Niranjan buys in bulk from makers and supplies to shops with advance payment. Which business? Name two parties. A: Wholesale Trade. Parties are Manufacturer and Retailer.

Q6. Arihant sells plastic items in weekly market on fixed days with temporary structure. Which retailer and category? A: Itinerant retailer. Category is Periodic Market Trader.

Q7. Birju sells vegetables on hand cart moving place to place. Which retailer? A: Hawkers and Pedlars. Moves street to street and supplies at doorstep.

Q8. Aman sells low cost footwear on fixed days in markets. Classify? A: Itinerant retailer. Category is Periodic Market Trader.

Q9. Harmeet Singh has large outlet with textiles, footwear, cosmetics in divisions. Identify? A: Departmental Store. Many departments under one roof.

Q10. PQR Ltd sells organic products through identical outlets with uniform price and look. Identify? A: Chain Stores or Multiple Shops. Uniform price and uniform decoration.

27. Exam Questions With Answers

Q1. Define Internal Trade. A: Internal Trade is buying and selling within limits of a country.

Q2. Name features of Internal Trade. A: Within country, home currency, few formalities, local transport.

Q3. Define Wholesale Trade. A: Wholesale Trade is trade in large quantities.

Q4. Give features of Wholesale Trade. A: Link between maker and retailer, large quantity, one line, large capital, low profit.

Q5. How wholesaler helps maker in finance? A: Buys for cash and pays advance.

Q6. How wholesaler helps retailer in credit? A: Sells on credit. Retailer needs less capital.

Q7. Define Retail Trade. A: Retail Trade is sale in small lots to final consumers.

Q8. Give features of Retail Trade. A: Last link, direct sale, variety, small quantity, residential location.

Q9. Give services of retailers to consumers. A: Regular supply, wide selection, demo, home delivery, credit.

Q10. Give services of retailers to makers. A: Distribution help, personal selling, promotion, market information.

Q11. What are Itinerants? A: Itinerants are retailers with no fixed place of sale.

Q12. Who are Hawkers and Pedlars? A: Move street to street. Hawker uses carts. Pedlar carries on head.

Q13. Who are Periodic Market Traders? A: Sell on fixed days in weekly markets with temporary structures.

Q14. Who are Cheap Jacks? A: Display in hired shops or tents temporarily in colonies.

Q15. Who are Street Traders? A: Display on corners and pavements near public places.

Q16. What are General Stores? A: Small shops in residential areas for daily use items.

Q17. What are Single Line Stores? A: Shops dealing in one line with variety in that line.

Q18. What are Second-hand Goods Shops? A: Shops selling used goods at low price from auctions.

Q19. What are Seconds Shops? A: Shops selling new defective goods at heavy discount.

Q20. Define Departmental Store. A: Departmental Store has many departments under one roof.

Q21. Define Chain Store. A: Chain Store is identical shops in different parts of city.

Q22. Give difference in price between two large stores. A: Departmental prices not uniform. Chain prices fixed and uniform.

Q23. Define Mail Order Business. A: Mail Order Business is sale through post without direct contact.

Q24. Define GST. A: GST is tax on supply of goods and services.

Q25. Name GST slabs. A: 5, 12, 18 and 28 per cent.

Q26. Who heads GST Council? A: Finance Minister.
