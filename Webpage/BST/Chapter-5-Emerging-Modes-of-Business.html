Emerging Modes of Business

Class 11 Business Studies — Complete Revision Notes

1. Introduction

In the modern period, there has been a considerable change in the manner of conducting business. 'Emerging Modes of Business' refers to the recent trends and technologies shaping the way business is done today.

Why the shift? To meet ever-growing competition, maintain quality standards, offer lower prices, ensure faster order processing, and provide better customer care — businesses must take advantage of emerging technologies.

The strongest trend shaping modern business is e-Business (Electronic Business).

2. E-Business

What is e-Business?

Electronic Business, commonly known as e-Business, refers to conducting business activities over the internet or any other computer network.

In the words of Philip Kotler:
"Electronic business is the general term for buying and selling processes that are supported by electronic means."

A. B2B Commerce (Business to Business)

Transactions taking place between business firms are referred to as B2B transactions.

Example: Maruti Suzuki buys tyres, glass for windscreens, door handles, etc. from different business suppliers — all are B2B transactions.

Common B2B activities include: Inviting tenders, commercial negotiations, distribution of goods, creation of utilities, collaborations.

B. B2C Commerce (Business to Customer)

Transactions taking place between business and customers. It enables the business to remain in touch with its customers on a round-the-clock basis.

Example: Withdrawal of money from ATMs — previously tedious, now possible 24×7 from any ATM.

B2C activities include: Selling and distribution of goods, lodging of complaints, after-sale service, promotional activities.

Popular platforms: Amazon, Flipkart, Snapdeal, eBay

C. C2C Commerce (Customer to Customer)

Transactions taking place between two or more customers.

Example: OLX and Quikr — consumers sell their goods and services directly to other consumers.

C2C activities include: Selling used books/clothes, selling antique items, warning customers about fraudulent suppliers, sharing product quality information.

D. Intra-B Commerce (Within the Business)

e-Business is wider than e-commerce. It includes not only B2B, B2C, and C2C but also Intra-B Commerce — interaction and dealings among various departments and persons within the same firm.

This involves the use of Intranet to interact and coordinate between departments. The use of Intra-B Commerce has facilitated flexible manufacturing.

Example: The marketing department interacts regularly with the production department to get products made as per customer requirements.

🌐 VPN Technology: Companies use Virtual Private Networks (VPN) so employees can access the organisation's network and work from anywhere securely.

1. Ease of Formation & Lower Investment Very easy to start — no procedural requirements. Success depends on network (contacts), not on investment (net worth). Benefits accrue to big and small businesses alike.
2. Convenience Access anything anywhere, anytime (24×7). Customers can shop at their own convenience without time or location constraints.
3. Speed Very less time to complete the business cycle — entire process with a click of a mouse. Digital products (music, movies, e-books) delivered instantly. Money transferred instantly via electronic funds transfer.
4. Global Reach / Global Access Sellers can operate at national and global levels. Buyers and sellers from any part of the world can interact with each other.
5. Movement Towards a Paperless Society Reduced dependence on paperwork — most work done electronically. Even government departments use computers; e.g., filing income tax returns online.

3. Outsourcing

What is Outsourcing?

Outsourcing refers to contracting out some of its activities to a third party which were earlier performed by the organisation itself.

In simple words: Instead of doing everything yourself, hire another company to do certain tasks for you.

Need for Outsourcing

1. Focusing of Attention Allows the organisation to focus on its core business activities while non-core tasks are handled by specialists.
2. Quest for Excellence Third-party vendors are often specialists who deliver high-quality output — the organisation benefits from their expertise.
3. Cost Reduction Helps reduce operational costs — the third party may have economies of scale or lower labour costs (e.g., BPO in India).
4. Growth through Alliance Creates a partnership relationship — the organisation builds strategic alliances with vendors for mutual growth.
5. Boost to Economic Development Outsourcing creates employment and economic activity in the vendor's location — e.g., the BPO industry in India.
6. Risk Sharing By outsourcing, some of the business risk is transferred to the third-party vendor, reducing the burden on the organisation.

Case Studies

Case 1: OLX Sofa Sale

Mr. Rajiv wants to buy a new sofa set but doesn't have space. He plans to sell his old sofa through OLX. He gets a great response and chooses the buyer who gives him the best price.

(a) Which are the two parties involved?

(b) What branch of e-business is referred to?

Answers:

  • (a) Customer and Customer (Consumer-to-Consumer)
  • (b) C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer) Commerce

Case 2: General Technologies (Work from Home)

Software engineers at 'General Technologies' get freedom to work from home using company-provided laptops. They take daily tasks from the work manager and complete them at home.

(a) Identify the dimension of e-business.

(b) State any two other dimensions of e-business.

Answers:

  • (a) Intra-B Commerce (employees accessing company network remotely)
  • (b) Any two of: B2B, B2C, C2C

Case 3: TrendZilla Online Store

Ritika starts an online store named TrendZilla selling handmade jewellery via Instagram and a website. Customers order and pay online. She uses a courier partner for delivery.

(a) Which e-business model is Ritika using?

(b) State any two benefits of running a business online.

Answers:

  • (a) B2C (Business to Consumer)
  • (b) Convenience (24×7 shopping), Speed (quick ordering), Ease of Formation (low setup cost)

Case 4: B2B vs B2C

Company A supplies raw materials (steel, plastic) to manufacturers. Company B sells electronics (mobiles, headphones) directly to customers via website.

(a) Identify which is B2B and which is B2C.

(b) Give one example of each.

Answers:

  • (a) Company A = B2B | Company B = B2C
  • (b) B2B: Tyre manufacturer supplying to Maruti Suzuki | B2C: Amazon selling to individual customers
