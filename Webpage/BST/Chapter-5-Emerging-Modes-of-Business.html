Section 1

Emerging Modes of Business and E-Business

The idea of this section. The way business is done has changed completely. This part explains what 'emerging modes of business' means, why every firm now has to adopt them, and what e-business actually stands for.

1.1 What Are Emerging Modes of Business?

  • In the modern period there has been considerable change in the manner of conducting business.
  • 'Emerging modes of business' = the recent trends taking place in the way business is done.
  • These modes come from emerging technologies that a business now uses as a normal part of working.

The two modes studied in this chapter:

ModeWhat it means in one line
E-Business (Electronic Business)Running the business over the internet or any other computer network
OutsourcingContracting out activities to a third party which were earlier performed by the organisation

Exam tip. "Emerging modes of business" is a trend line. The question "what are emerging modes of business?" is answered with the exact phrase — recent trends taking place in the way business is done.

  4. Q4. Name the two modes of business studied in this chapter.
Show answers
  1. The recent trends taking place in the way business is done.
  2. The modern period.
  3. (b) E-business.

1.2 Why Businesses Need Emerging Modes

In order to meet the following, a business needs to take the benefit of emerging technologies:

NeedWhat the business gains
Ever growing competitionA way to stay ahead of rivals
Quality standardsProducts and services that match the standard buyers expect
Lower pricesCost saving that can be passed on to the customer
Faster order processingOrders move quickly from the click to the dispatch
Better customer careCloser, faster service to every customer
  • One of the strongest trends that is shaping business is e-business (Electronic Business).

Exam tip. These five needs are the "why" of the whole chapter. Write them as a single line of five items for a 1-mark question and as five short bullets for a 3-mark question.

1.3 E-Business: Meaning and the Kotler Definition

The definition — learn this line word for word:

Electronic Business, commonly known as 'eBusiness' or 'e-Business', refers to conducting business activities over internet or any other computer network.

In the words of Philip Kotler:

"Electronic business is the general term for buying and selling process that is supported by electronic means."

The two halves of e-business:

E-BUSINESS
     |
     +--> Industry  (Production)
     |
     +--> Commerce  (Trade and Distribution)
HalfWhat it covers
Industry (Production)Making the goods and services
Commerce (Trade and Distribution)Buying and selling those goods and services

Exam tip. Two names, two marks — Philip Kotler for the quote, and the phrase conducting business activities over internet or any other computer network for the definition.

1.4 E-Business vs E-Commerce

  • E-business is a wider term than e-commerce, in the same way as business is a broader term than commerce.
  • E-commerce deals with the distribution of goods and services.
  • E-business includes e-commerce, and it also includes other electronically conducted business functions.
BasisE-CommerceE-Business
ScopeCovers the distribution of goods and servicesWider — covers e-commerce plus every other business function
Functions coveredBuying and selling, distributionProduction, accounting, finance, personnel, administration, etc.
MeaningThe trade and distribution part of businessThe whole of business, run electronically

Exam tip. If the question says "How is e-business different from e-commerce?", the answer is: e-business is the wider term; e-commerce covers the distribution of goods and services while e-business also covers production, accounting, finance, personnel and administration.

Check yourself4 questions

  3. Q3. (MCQ) E-commerce deals with — (a) production, finance and personnel (b) distribution of goods and services (c) recruitment and selection (d) inventory management
  4. Q4. Why is e-business compared with business and e-commerce with commerce?
Show answers
  1. E-business is the wider term. It includes e-commerce together with other electronically conducted business functions.
  2. Production, accounting, finance, personnel and administration.
  3. (b) distribution of goods and services.
  4. Because business is a broader term than commerce. E-business carries all the functions of a business, while e-commerce carries the buying, selling and distribution part of it.
Section 2

Scope of E-Business: The Four Branches

The idea of this section. E-business runs in four directions. This part takes each one — B2B, B2C, C2C and Intra-B — with its meaning, its example and the transactions that sit under it.

2.1 B2B Commerce: Business to Business

  • B2B Commerce = the transactions taking place between business firms, referred to as business to business or B2B transactions.
  • The example from the lecture: an automobile manufacturer (like Maruti Udyog) makes several B2B transactions, such as buying tyres, glass for windscreens, door handles, etc. for its vehicles from different business units.

Some of the B2B transactions are:

#B2B transaction
1Inviting tenders
2Commercial negotiations
3Distribution of goods
4Creation of utilities
5Collaborations

Exam tip. The keyword in B2B is "between firms". Anything one business buys from another business is a B2B transaction.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. What is B2B commerce?
  2. Q2. Give the B2B example of an automobile manufacturer from the lecture.
  3. Q3. Name any three B2B transactions listed in the lecture.
  4. Q4. (MCQ) Sending quotations of supplying raw material by one businessman to another is called — (a) B2B (b) B2C (c) C2C (d) Intra B
Show answers
  1. The transactions taking place between business firms, called business to business or B2B transactions.
  2. An automobile manufacturer (like Maruti Udyog) buys tyres, glass for windscreens, door handles, etc. for its vehicles from different business units.
  3. Any three of — inviting tenders, commercial negotiations, distribution of goods, creation of utilities, collaborations.
  4. (a) B2B.

2.2 B2C Commerce: Business to Customer

  • B2C Commerce = the transactions taking place between business and customers, known as Business-to-customer or B2C transactions.
  • It enables the business to remain in touch with its customers on a round-the-clock basis.
  • The example from the lecture: withdrawal of one's own money from banks was a tedious process in the past. With the introduction of ATM, it is now possible to withdraw money round the clock in a convenient and fast manner.

B2C transactions may include:

#B2C transaction
1Selling and distribution of goods
2Lodging of complaints by customer
3After-sale service
4Promotional activities

Platforms named in the lecture: Amazon, eBay, Snapdeal.

Exam tip. The keyword in B2C is "round the clock" — that is the advantage the lecture attaches to it. Keep it in the answer.

  1. The transactions taking place between business and customers, known as Business-to-customer or B2C transactions.
  2. It enables the business to remain in touch with its customers on a round-the-clock basis.

  4. (b) B2C.

2.3 C2C Commerce: Customer to Customer

  • C2C Commerce = the transactions taking place between two or more customers.
  • The example from the lecture: Quikr and OLX, where consumers can sell their goods and services to other consumers.

C2C transactions involve:

#C2C transaction
1Selling used books, clothes, etc.
2Selling antique items
3Warning customers about fraudulent suppliers
4Information about the quality and durability of the product

Platforms named in the lecture: OLX, CarDekho.

Exam tip. In C2C, both sides of the transaction are customers. One customer is the seller and the other is the buyer.

2.4 Intra-B Commerce: Within the Firm

  • E-business is wider than e-commerce. E-business includes e-commerce (B2B, B2C and C2C) and also Intra-B Commerce.
  • Intra-B Commerce = the interaction and dealings among various departments and persons within the firm.
  • It includes the use of intranet to interact and deal between various departments and persons within a firm.
  • The use of intra-B commerce has facilitated flexible manufacturing.
  • The example from the lecture: the marketing department may interact regularly with the production department and get the products made as per the requirements of the customers.

Intra-B Commerce transactions may be conducted for:

#Intra-B purpose
1Inventory and cash management
2Reporting by employees to their seniors
3Human resource management
4Recruitment and selection
5Training, development and education

VPN technology. Companies are also resorting to Virtual Private Network (VPN) technology, through which employees can access to the organisation's network and can work from anywhere through computer network.

Section 3

E-Business vs Traditional Business

The idea of this section. A student often mixes up the two. This part puts them side by side on eleven bases, then lists what e-business gains and where it falls short.

3.1 Basis-by-Basis Comparison

BasisE-BusinessTraditional Business
Ease of formationIt is easy to form.It is difficult to form.
Physical presenceIt does not require physical presence.It requires physical presence.
Locational requirementsThere is no requirement for location.Location is preferred near raw materials and market.
Cost of setting upLow cost of setting due to no need for physical facilities.It involves high cost of setting.
Operating costThe operating cost is low.High operating cost due to investment in production, marketing, etc.
Direct/Indirect contact with suppliers and customersIt has direct contact.It has indirect contact through intermediaries.
Nature of internal communicationCommunication can flow in any direction.It involves hierarchical flow of communication.
Response time in meeting customer's requirementIt gives instant response.Response takes a lot of time.
Organisational structureHorizontal or flat structure due to direct command and communication.Vertical or tall structure due to chain of command.
Business processes and length of the cycleShort business process cycle.It is long due to number of sequential processes.
Opportunity for interpersonal touchLess opportunity for inter-personal touch.More opportunity for inter-personal touch.

Exam tip. There are 11 bases. In a 3-mark answer, any three rows are enough — but write the basis name on the left and the answer for both sides on the right.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. On which basis is e-business easier to form than traditional business?
  2. Q2. What difference does the lecture note in the nature of internal communication?
  3. Q3. Which business has the short business process cycle, and why?
  4. Q4. (MCQ) Which structure does e-business have? (a) Vertical or tall (b) Horizontal or flat (c) Hierarchical (d) Chain of command
Show answers
  1. Ease of formation. E-business is easy to form and does not require physical presence; traditional business is difficult to form and requires physical presence.
  2. In e-business, communication can flow in any direction. In traditional business, it involves hierarchical flow of communication.
  3. E-business, because the entire process is done with the click of a mouse. The traditional business process cycle is long due to number of sequential processes.
  4. (b) Horizontal or flat structure due to direct command and communication.

Section 4

Outsourcing

The idea of this section. Outsourcing is the second emerging mode. This part explains what outsourcing means and the six reasons that push a firm to hand work to a third party.

4.1 What Is Outsourcing?

Outsourcing refers to contracting out some of its activities to a third party which were earlier performed by the organisation.

  • The organisation identifies activities it used to perform itself.
  • It contracts them out to a third party, which now performs them.
  • The firm keeps its own core work and gains time, expertise and lower cost.

Exam tip. Two keywords carry the answer — contracting out and third party. Write both for the full mark.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. What is outsourcing?
  2. Q2. In outsourcing, who performs the activities that were earlier performed by the organisation?
  3. Q3. (MCQ) Outsourcing means — (a) the firm carries on every activity in-house (b) activities are contracted out to a third party (c) the firm closes down a branch (d) the firm hires more staff
Show answers
  1. Contracting out some activities to a third party which were earlier performed by the organisation.
  2. A third party — the outside vendor or specialist firm to which those activities are contracted out.
  3. (b) activities are contracted out to a third party.

4.2 Why Firms Outsource: Six Reasons

NEED FOR OUTSOURCING
        |
        +--> Focusing of Attention
        +--> Quest for Excellence
        +--> Cost Reduction
        +--> Growth through Alliance
        +--> Boost to Economic Development

5.2 Case Study with Model Answer

Mr. Rajiv wants to buy a new sofa set for his house, but he did not have space. He planned to sell the old sofa through OLX, so that he can get some surplus money and space for the new sofa set. He got very good response and out of many buyers, he chose the buyer who gave him the best price.

Questions

  • (a) Which are the two parties involved in the transaction mentioned in the above case?
  • (b) What branch of e-business is referred to in the above case?

Model answer

  • (a) Customer and Customer (Consumer-to-Consumer). In this transaction Mr. Rajiv is the seller of his old sofa and the buyer he selects is the second party. Both sides are individuals, so the two parties are customer and customer.
  • (b) C2C Commerce. The sale happens on a consumer-to-consumer platform (OLX), where consumers sell their goods and services to other consumers. The firm is not a party to the deal, which is what separates C2C from B2C and B2B.

Meaning — the exact line

In the modern period, the manner of conducting business has changed considerably. Emerging modes of business refers to the recent trends taking place in the way business is done. A business uses emerging technologies — the tools it uses as a normal part of working — so that it can meet competition, maintain quality standards, offer lower prices, process orders faster and give better customer care.

Why businesses need emerging modes — 5 needs

A firm takes the benefit of emerging technologies to meet these five needs.

NeedWhat the business gains
Ever growing competitionA way to stay ahead of rivals
Quality standardsProducts and services that match the standard buyers expect
Lower pricesCost saving that can be passed on to the customer
Faster order processingOrders move quickly from the click to the dispatch
Better customer careCloser, faster service to every customer

Memory hook: C-Q-P-F-C — Competition, Quality, Price, Processing, Care. Of these five, e-business is the strongest trend shaping business.

E-business — definition and the Kotler line

Electronic Business, commonly known as eBusiness or e-Business, refers to conducting business activities over internet or any other computer network.

Philip Kotler: "Electronic business is the general term for buying and selling process that is supported by electronic means."

The two halves of e-business:

HalfWhat it covers

Exam tip: two names carry two marks — Philip Kotler for the quote, and the phrase conducting business activities over internet or any other computer network for the definition.

E-business vs e-commerce — 3 bases

E-business is a wider term than e-commerce, in the same way as business is a broader term than commerce. E-commerce deals with the distribution of goods and services, while e-business includes e-commerce and also the other electronically conducted business functions.

BasisE-commerceE-business
ScopeCovers the distribution of goods and servicesWider — covers e-commerce plus every other business function
Functions coveredBuying and selling, distributionProduction, accounting, finance, personnel, administration, etc.
MeaningThe trade and distribution part of businessThe whole of business, run electronically

So e-commerce sits inside e-business. A question asking "how is e-business different from e-commerce?" is answered with the wider scope and the extra functions.

The Four Branches of E-Business

The four branches at a glance

E-business includes e-commerce (B2B, B2C and C2C) and also Intra-B commerce. The first three run between separate parties; the fourth runs inside one firm.

BranchBetween whomSource example
B2BTwo business firmsMaruti Udyog buying tyres, glass for windscreens and door handles
B2CA business and its customersWithdrawal of one's own money from an ATM, round the clock
C2CTwo or more customersQuikr and OLX, where consumers sell to other consumers
Intra-BDepartments and persons within one firmMarketing department interacting with the production department

B2B Commerce — business to business

B2B commerce means the transactions taking place between business firms, also called business to business or B2B transactions.

Source example: an automobile manufacturer such as Maruti Udyog makes several B2B transactions when it buys tyres, glass for windscreens, door handles and similar parts for its vehicles from different business units.

#B2B transaction
1Inviting tenders
2Commercial negotiations
3Distribution of goods
4Creation of utilities
5Collaborations

Exam line: the keyword is "between firms". Anything one business buys from another business is a B2B transaction. Sending quotations for supplying raw material from one businessman to another is therefore B2B.

B2C Commerce — business to customer

B2C commerce means the transactions taking place between business and customers, known as business-to-customer or B2C transactions. It enables the business to remain in touch with its customers on a round-the-clock basis.

Source example: withdrawal of one's own money from banks was a tedious process in the past; with the introduction of the ATM, it is now possible to withdraw money round the clock in a convenient and fast manner. This is the chapter's example of round-the-clock B2C service.

#B2C transaction
1Selling and distribution of goods
2Lodging of complaints by customer
3After-sale service
4Promotional activities

Platforms named in the source: Amazon, eBay, Snapdeal.

Exam line: the keyword in B2C is "round the clock", and the ATM example belongs to B2C, not to B2B or C2C.

C2C Commerce — customer to customer

C2C commerce means the transactions taking place between two or more customers.

Source example: Quikr and OLX, where consumers can sell their goods and services to other consumers. CarDekho is the other C2C platform named in the chapter.

#C2C transaction
1Selling used books, clothes, etc.
2Selling antique items
3Warning customers about fraudulent suppliers
4Information about the quality and durability of the product

Exam line: in C2C both sides are customers — one customer is the seller and the other is the buyer, and the platform firm is not a party to the deal.

Intra-B Commerce — within the firm

Intra-B commerce means the interaction and dealings among various departments and persons within the firm. It includes the use of the intranet to interact and deal between departments and persons inside a firm.

The use of Intra-B commerce has facilitated flexible manufacturing. Source example: the marketing department may interact regularly with the production department and get the products made as per the requirements of the customers.

#Intra-B purpose
1Inventory and cash management
2Reporting by employees to their seniors
3Human resource management
4Recruitment and selection
5Training, development and education

VPN technology: companies also use a Virtual Private Network (VPN), through which employees can access the organisation's network and work from anywhere through a computer network. The General Technologies work-from-home case is the source's illustration of Intra-B through VPN.

    Success depends on network (contacts) rather than on investment (net worth), and the benefits of internet technology accrue to big or small business alike, so size of investment is not the deciding factor.

Outsourcing

Outsourcing — the exact definition

Outsourcing refers to contracting out some of its activities to a third party which were earlier performed by the organisation.

  • The organisation identifies activities it used to perform itself.
  • It contracts them out to a third party, which now performs them.
  • The firm keeps its core work and gains time, expertise and lower cost.

Two keywords carry the full mark: contracting out and third party.

Six reasons for outsourcing

#ReasonWhat it means
1Focusing of attentionThe organisation focuses on its core business activities while specialists handle the non-core tasks
2Quest for excellenceThird-party vendors are specialists, so the organisation benefits from their expertise and higher quality output
3Cost reductionThe third party brings economies of scale and lower labour costs, so operational costs come down
4Growth through allianceOutsourcing builds a partnership — the organisation forms strategic alliances with its vendors
5Boost to economic developmentIt creates employment and economic activity in the vendor's location
6Risk sharingPart of the business risk is transferred to the third-party vendor

Memory hook: F-Q-C-G-E-R — Focus, Quest, Cost, Growth, Economic development, Risk.

BPO and KPO — the two labels

TermWhat it means
BPO (Business Process Outsourcing)Contracting out a standard business process to a third party; the BPO industry in India is the example used for the boost to economic development
KPO (Knowledge Process Outsourcing)Contracting out knowledge-intensive work that needs specialist expertise and judgement

MarksWhat to write

Recall drill — Session 4

  1. Define outsourcing.

    Show answer

    Outsourcing refers to contracting out some of its activities to a third party which were earlier performed by the organisation, and it is the second emerging mode studied in the chapter.

  2. Which two keywords must appear in the definition?

    Show answer

    The two keywords are contracting out and third party, and writing both is what earns the full mark in a definition question.

  3. In outsourcing, who performs the activity that the organisation used to perform?

    Show answer

    A third party performs it — the outside vendor or specialist firm to which the activity is contracted out after the organisation identifies work it used to do itself.

  4. What does the firm gain by outsourcing?

    Show answer

    The firm keeps its own core work and gains time, expertise and lower cost, because the third party now handles the activities that were earlier performed inside the organisation.

  5. Name the six reasons for outsourcing.

    Show answer

    The six are focusing of attention, quest for excellence, cost reduction, growth through alliance, boost to economic development and risk sharing, and each should be written as a heading with one line of meaning.

  6. Explain focusing of attention.

    Show answer

    Focusing of attention means the organisation concentrates on its core business activities while the specialists handle the non-core tasks, so management attention is not divided across everything.

  7. Explain quest for excellence.

    Show answer

    Quest for excellence means third-party vendors are specialists, so the organisation benefits from their expertise and higher quality output instead of building that capability in-house.

  8. Explain cost reduction.

    Show answer

    Cost reduction happens because the third party brings economies of scale and lower labour costs, so the operational costs of the organisation come down.

  9. Explain growth through alliance.

    Show answer

    Growth through alliance means outsourcing builds a partnership relationship, in which the organisation forms strategic alliances with its vendors rather than dealing with them at arm's length.

  10. Explain the boost to economic development with the source example.

    Show answer

    Outsourcing creates employment and economic activity in the vendor's location, and the source's example is the BPO industry in India, which illustrates employment and activity developing around the vendor.

  11. Explain risk sharing.

    Show answer

    Risk sharing means part of the business risk is transferred to the third-party vendor, so the organisation does not carry the entire risk of that activity alone.

  12. How should a 3-mark and a 6-mark outsourcing answer be built?

    Show answer

    For 3 marks, write the definition and any three reasons, each as a heading with one line of meaning; for 6 marks, write all six reasons in the same heading-plus-line form and add the BPO industry in India example under the boost to economic development.
