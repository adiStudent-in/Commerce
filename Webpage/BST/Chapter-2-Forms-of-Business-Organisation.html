Forms of Business Organisation

Class 11 Business Studies — Quick Revision Notes

📌 Overview of Forms

Business activities cannot be performed in isolation. Choosing the right form of organisation is one of the most important decisions while starting or expanding a business. The best form is chosen by weighing the merits and limitations of each type against one's own requirements (capital, risk, control, continuity, secrecy).

💡 Why each new form emerged: Sole proprietorship → too little capital & skills → HUF → limited resources & Karta's dominance → Partnership → limited resources & unlimited liability → Cooperative (service motive) & Company (large-scale needs).

1. 🧍 Sole Proprietorship

Definition: A form of business organisation which is owned, managed and controlled by an individual who is the recipient of all profits and bearer of all risks.
It is the oldest and simplest form of business organisation.

Features

FeatureDetail
Formation & ClosureVery easy to start; no legal formalities, no separate law governs it; can be dissolved at the will of the owner
LiabilityUnlimited – personal property may be sold to pay business debts if business assets are insufficient
Sole Risk Bearer & Profit RecipientOwns all business affairs; bears all risk alone; receives all profits as reward
ControlOne-man show; all decisions lie with the proprietor; no need to consult anyone
No Separate EntityNo legal distinction between firm and owner; owner is responsible for all business activities
Lack of Business ContinuityDeath, physical ailment or insolvency of owner directly affects business – may even close it

✅ Merits

  • Quick decision making – complete freedom, no consultation needed
  • Confidentiality of information – sole authority keeps business info secret
  • Direct incentive – keeps all profits; no sharing
  • Sense of accomplishment – personal satisfaction & confidence for self-employed
  • Ease of formation and closure – minimum legal formalities

❌ Limitations

  • Limited resources – only personal savings + borrowings; small size, low growth rate
  • Unlimited liability – creditors can recover dues from personal assets too
  • Limited life of business – illness, death, insolvency of owner affects the business
  • Limited managerial ability – one person cannot be expert in all areas
💡 Best suited for: small-scale businesses & personalised services (retail shops, beauty parlours, medical shops) where limited capital is needed and personal contact with customers matters.

2. 🏠 Joint Hindu Family (HUF) Business

Definition: A unique form of business organisation found only in India, owned and carried on by members of the Hindu Undivided Family.
Governed by the Hindu Law (Hindu Succession Act, 1956); one of the oldest forms in the country.

Key Members

  • Karta (Manager) – the eldest male member; manages & controls the business; his decisions are binding on all members
  • Co-parceners – all members having equal ownership right over ancestral property
  • Membership basis = birth in the family; up to 3 successive generations can be members

Two Systems of Inheritance (Hindu Law)

SystemWhere PrevailsWho Can Be Co-parcener
DayabhagaWest Bengal & Assam onlyBoth male and female members
MitaksharaAll over India except West Bengal & AssamOnly male members; right acquired by birth

Features

FeatureDetail
FormationAt least 2 members + ancestral property inherited; no agreement needed
LiabilityKarta = unlimited (self-acquired property can be attached) | Co-parceners = limited (to their share in coparcenary property)
ControlLies with Karta; decisions binding on all members
ContinuityNot affected by death of members; next eldest male becomes Karta
Minor MembersMinors can be members (inclusion by birth)

✅ Merits

  • Effective control – Karta's absolute decision-making avoids conflicts
  • Continued business existence – death of Karta doesn't stop business
  • Limited liability of members – risk of co-parceners is well-defined and precise
  • Increased loyalty & cooperation – family members work together

❌ Limitations

  • Limited resources – depends mainly on ancestral property; limited scope for expansion
  • Unlimited liability of Karta – burdened with decisions + full liability
  • Dominance of Karta – may cause conflict, even breakdown of the family unit
  • Limited managerial skills – Karta can't be expert in all areas; unwise decisions → losses
📉 On the decline in modern India due to shrinking joint-family system.

3. 🤝 Partnership

Definition (Indian Partnership Act, 1932):
"Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profit of the business carried on by all or any one of them acting for all."

Partnership emerged from the limitations of Sole Proprietorship and HUF – it answers the needs of greater capital, varied skills and sharing of risks.

Features

FeatureDetail
FormationGoverned by Indian Partnership Act, 1932; comes into existence through a legal agreement (terms, profit-sharing, manner of business)
LiabilityUnlimited – personal assets usable for debts; partners liable jointly AND individually (a paying partner can recover proportionate share from others)
Risk BearingRisks borne as a team; profits & losses shared in agreed ratio
Decision-Making & ControlShared among partners; decisions by mutual consent
MembershipMin: 2 (Sec 464, Companies Act 2013) | Max: 50 (Rule 10, Companies (Miscellaneous) Rules, 2014)
Mutual AgencyEvery partner acts as agent (binds others) and principal (bound by others' acts)

✅ Merits

  • Ease of formation & closure – agreement-based; can start even without registration
  • Balanced decision-making – varied knowledge, skills & experience; division of work
  • More funds – capital from many partners; larger resources than sole proprietorship
  • Sharing of risks – reduces anxiety and burden on individual partners
  • Secrecy – not required to publish accounts

❌ Limitations

  • Unlimited liability – joint & individual; a wealthy partner may have to repay entire debts if others fail
  • Limited resources – legal ceiling on partners restricts large-scale growth
  • Possibility of conflicts – differences of opinion; one wrong decision can ruin all
  • Lack of continuity – ends on death/insolvency/insanity of a partner (fresh agreement needed to continue)
  • Lack of public confidence – accounts not published; true financial status unknown

💡 Types of partners — quick MCQs: Lends name & goodwill → Nominal | Contributes capital, no day-to-day role → Sleeping | Capital + management, identity hidden → Secret

Minor as Partner

  • A minor cannot be a full partner (incompetent to contract), but can be admitted to benefits with consent of all partners
  • Liability limited to his capital contribution; cannot take part in management
  • On attaining majority → must decide within 6 months whether to remain a partner; if no notice given → becomes a full partner with unlimited liability

3.2 Types of Partnerships

On basis of Duration:

TypeMeaning
Partnership at WillLife depends on will of partners; can be dissolved by any partner on giving notice; not for a fixed period or specific venture
Particular PartnershipFormed for a particular project / specified period; dissolves automatically on completion/expiry

On basis of Liability:

TypeMeaning
General PartnershipLiability of every partner unlimited; all may take active part in management; acts of each bind the firm
Limited PartnershipAt least one partner unlimited; rest may be limited; limited partners cannot manage (their acts don't bind the firm); registration compulsory

3.3 Partnership Deed

Written agreement specifying terms & conditions of partnership, including:

  • Name of the firm
  • Nature & location of business
  • Duration of business
  • Investment by each partner
  • Distribution of profits & losses
  • Duties & obligations of partners
  • Salaries & withdrawals; interest on capital & drawings
  • Procedure for dissolution
  • Preparation & auditing of accounts
  • Method of solving disputes
  • Terms of admission, retirement & expulsion
💡 The agreement may be verbal or written — written is advisable as it is evidence of agreed conditions.

3.4 Registration of Partnership Firm

AspectDetail
NatureOptional but highly advisable; registration gives conclusive proof of existence
Procedure① Submit application to Registrar of Firms (name, location, other business places, date of joining, names & addresses of partners, duration) → ② deposit fees → ③ entry in register → ④ certificate of registration
Consequences of non-registrationPartner cannot file suit against firm/other partners; firm cannot file suit against third parties; firm cannot file case against partners. (Outsiders can sue the unregistered firm.)

4. 🤲 Cooperative Society

Definition: A voluntary association of persons who join together with the motive of welfare of the members – an association of persons, not of capital.
Registration under Cooperative Societies Act, 1912 is compulsory. Minimum 10 adult persons required.

Features

FeatureDetail
Voluntary MembershipOpen to anyone 18+ with common interest; no force, no bar on religion/caste/gender
Legal StatusSeparate legal entity after registration; can own property, make contracts, sue & be sued in own name; unaffected by entry/exit of members
Limited LiabilityLimited to capital contributed; max risk = subscribed share capital
ControlDemocratic pattern; members elect a managing committee; one-man-one-vote
Service MotiveFormed for service, not profit maximisation; surplus distributed as dividend

✅ Merits

  • Equality in voting status – one vote per member irrespective of capital
  • Limited liability – risk limited and known
  • Stable existence – corporate body; not affected by death/insolvency/insanity of members
  • Economy in operations – managed on honorary basis; eliminates middlemen
  • Support from government – low taxes, subsidies, low interest loans
  • Ease of formation – just 10 adult members; governed by Cooperative Societies Act, 1912

❌ Limitations

  • Limited resources – members have limited means; low dividend deters capital
  • Inefficiency in management – can't pay experts; committee not professionally equipped
  • Lack of secrecy – affairs discussed openly in meetings
  • Government control – bound by rules on auditing, submission of accounts, etc.
  • Differences of opinion – internal quarrels; difficulty in decision-making

4.1 Types of Cooperative Societies

TypePurpose
Consumers'Formed by consumers for good quality products at reasonable prices; buys in bulk from manufacturers/wholesalers to eliminate middlemen; surplus shared by capital contribution or purchases
Producers' (Industrial)Formed by small producers to procure inputs for production; fights big capitalists, enhances bargaining power; 2 types – (i) supplies raw materials/equipment, (ii) sells members' produced goods; profits by contribution to total pool
MarketingVoluntary association of small producers to sell output through one centralised agency; pools production, eliminates middlemen; performs transport, warehousing, packaging; profits by contribution to common pool
Farmers' (Agricultural)Farmers jointly take up farming for large-scale benefits; provides quality seeds, fertilisers, machinery; consolidates fragmented holdings into viable economic holdings
CreditShort-term loans to members at low interest; protects from exploitative lenders charging high rates; lends out of capital & deposits collected
HousingHelps people with limited income construct houses at reasonable cost; constructs flats or provides plots; payment in instalments

5. 🏢 Joint Stock Company

Definition: An association of persons formed for carrying out business activities, having a legal status independent of its members.
The most suitable form for large-scale business. Governed by Companies Act, 2013.

Features

FeatureDetail
Artificial PersonCreated by law – no body, soul, conscience; can't breathe/eat/run/talk, BUT can own property, borrow, contract, sue & be sued
Separate Legal EntityOn incorporation, assets & liabilities are separate from owners
FormationTime-consuming, expensive, complicated; several documents & legal requirements; registration compulsory
Perpetual SuccessionNot affected by death/insolvency/entry/exit of members; "Members may come, members may go, but the company remains forever"; ends only through legal winding up
ControlOwnership (shareholders) & management (Board of Directors) in different hands; directors are legal representatives accountable to shareholders; shareholders can't manage day-to-day
LiabilityLimited to amount unpaid on shares held. e.g. 500 shares of ₹10, ₹7 paid → liability only ₹3 × 500 = ₹1,500
Common SealCompany acts through directors using the common seal as its signature; if no seal, signing person must be authorised by board resolution
Risk BearingRisk spread over a large number of shareholders
Transfer of SharesShareholders can transfer shares in open market/stock exchange at prevailing price

✅ Merits

  • Limited liability – personal assets safe; debts settled only from company assets
  • Transfer of interest – free transfer of shares avoids blockage of investment
  • Perpetual existence – not affected by death/retirement/insolvency/insanity of members; liquidated only per Companies Act
  • Scope for expansion – large financial resources; capital from public + banks
  • Professional management – specialists head each department; balanced, rational decisions

❌ Limitations

  • Complexity in formation – time, effort, legal knowledge required
  • Lack of secrecy – information filed with Registrar available to public
  • Impersonal work environment – no direct link between effort and reward; no personal contact
  • Numerous regulations – audit, voting, filing of reports, certificates; time-consuming & expensive
  • Delay in decision-making – important decisions by Board/general meeting; opportunities lost
  • Oligarchic management – democracy in theory, rule by a few in practice; scattered shareholders take little interest; directors may act against their interests
  • Conflict of interests – employees want higher salaries, consumers want low prices, shareholders want high dividends

A. One Person Company (OPC) – Section 2(62)

  • Only one person as member; incorporated as a private company
  • Avails benefits of private company: separate legal entity, protection of personal assets, perpetual succession
  • Only a natural person, Indian citizen & resident (stay ≥ 182 days in preceding calendar year) can incorporate/be nominee
  • One person cannot have more than one OPC or be nominee in more than one
  • No minor can be member/nominee
  • Cannot be incorporated under Section 8; cannot do financial investment in other corporations
  • Cannot convert voluntarily into any other kind of company until 2 years from incorporation, unless paid-up capital crosses ₹50 lakh or average annual turnover exceeds ₹2 crore
  • Words "One Person Company" must appear in brackets below its name

B. Private Company – Section 2(68)

By its Articles of Association:

  • Restricts transfer of shares
  • Min 2, Max 200 members (excluding past/present employees who are members)
  • Prohibits invitation to public to subscribe for shares
  • Must use "Private Limited" in name
  • Contravening any provision → ceases to be private company, loses all privileges

C. Public Company – Section 2(71)

  • Any company which is not a private company
  • Min 7 members, no maximum limit
  • No restriction on transfer of shares
  • Can invite subscription from general public
  • A private company which is a subsidiary of a public company is also treated as a public company

5.2 Privileges of Private Company over Public Company

  • Can be started with just 2 members (public needs 7)
  • No prospectus needed
  • Can allot shares without minimum subscription
  • Can start work right after certificate of incorporation
  • Only 2 directors needed (public needs 3)
  • No need to keep index of members
  • Can lend money/give guarantee to directors without government approval

6. 📄 Formation of a Company (4 Stages)

Formation of a company is complex & lengthy – unlike sole proprietorship or partnership. The 4 stages: 
            graph LR
              S1["1. PROMOTION"] --> S2["2. INCORPORATION"]
              S2 --> S3["3. CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION
public company only"]
              S3 --> S4["4. COMMENCEMENT OF BUSINESS"]
⚠️ Stage order: Promotion → Incorporation → Capital Subscription → Commencement of Business. A private company can start work right after incorporation. Preliminary contracts are signed before incorporation.

Stage 1: Promotion

Identification of business opportunity, analysis of its prospects, steps to implement it.

  • The person performing these tasks = Promoter (individual, group or institution); conceives the idea, takes all initiatives

Functions of a Promoter (6 Steps):

  1. Identification of Business Opportunity – new business / expansion / merger; preliminary analysis of profitability, risk, resources
  2. Feasibility Studies (before investing money):
    • Technical – can it be implemented? (technology, raw materials availability)
    • Financial – can funds be arranged within available means?
    • Economic – will it be profitable?
  3. Name Approval – select name, get it approved from Registrar of Companies; must not be identical/too close to an existing name (3 names given in order of preference)
  4. Fixing up Signatories to Memorandum of Association – signatories usually become first directors; written consent to act as director & buy qualification shares; MOA signed by min 7 persons (public) / 2 persons (private)
  5. Appointment of Professionals – merchant bankers, auditors, etc. to prepare & submit documents
  6. Preparation of Necessary Documents – MOA, AOA, consent of directors, etc.

Stage 2: Incorporation (Important Documents)

Documents submitted to Registrar:

DocumentWhat It Is
Memorandum of Association (MOA)Principal document = "Charter of the Company"; contains powers & objectives; defines scope of operations; company works within limits laid down in it
Articles of Association (AOA)Rules & regulations for internal management; subsidiary to MOA; cannot include powers prohibited by MOA
Consent of Proposed DirectorsWritten consent to act as directors & buy qualification shares
AgreementIf company appoints Managing Director/Whole-time Director/Manager
Statutory DeclarationThat all legal requirements of incorporation are complied with; signed by Advocate (HC/SC), practising CA/CS, director named in articles, or manager/secretary
FeesFiling + registration fees (based on authorised share capital)

Forms of MOA (Schedule 1, Companies Act):

  • Table A – company limited by shares
  • Table B – limited by guarantee & not having share capital
  • Table C – limited by guarantee & having share capital
  • Table D – unlimited & not having share capital
  • Table E – unlimited & having share capital

Forms of AOA:

  • Table F – company limited by shares
  • Table G – limited by guarantee & having share capital
  • Table H – limited by guarantee & NOT having share capital
  • Table I – unlimited & having share capital
  • Table J – unlimited & NOT having share capital

Contents of MOA (6 Clauses):

  1. Name Clause – name of company
  2. Registered Office Clause – state where registered office is situated
  3. Objects Clause – purpose; company can't do business not authorised by it (divided into Main Objects and Other Objects)
  4. Liability Clause – liability limited to unpaid amount on shares
  5. Capital Clause – maximum (authorised) capital; e.g. ₹2 crore divided into 20 lakh shares of ₹10
  6. Association Clause – signatories state intention to be associated + consent to buy qualification shares; signed by min 7 (public) / 2 (private)

MOA vs AOA:

BasisMOAAOA
ObjectivesDefines objects for which company is formedRules of internal management; how objectives are achieved
PositionMain document; subordinate to Companies ActSubsidiary; subordinate to both MOA & Companies Act
NecessityFiling compulsoryNot compulsory for public company (may adopt Table F)
AlterationDifficult, lengthy; government approval neededEasy – by special resolution
ValidityActs beyond MOA invalid – can't be ratified even unanimouslyActs beyond AOA can be ratified by special resolution (if not violating MOA)
RelationshipCompany ↔ outsidersMembers ↔ management

Stage 3: Capital Subscription (only for public companies)

  • Invitation to public through Prospectus (document inviting general public to subscribe to shares)
  • Private companies & OPCs cannot issue prospectus

Stage 4: Commencement of Business

  • Company obtains Certificate of Commencement of Business and starts operations

9. 🎯 Factors Affecting the Choice of Form of Organisation

The best form is chosen by weighing the merits and limitations of each type against one's own requirements. Key factors:
FactorMost AdvantageousLeast Advantageous
Availability of CapitalCompanySole Proprietorship
Cost of FormationSole ProprietorshipCompany
Ease of FormationSole ProprietorshipCompany
Transfer of OwnershipCompany (except private)Partnership
Managerial SkillsCompanySole Proprietorship
RegulationsSole ProprietorshipCompany
FlexibilitySole ProprietorshipCompany
ContinuityCompanySole Proprietorship
LiabilityCompanySole Proprietorship

Key Factors to Consider:

  1. Cost & ease of setup – sole proprietorship cheapest; company most expensive
  2. Liability – company & cooperative = limited; sole proprietorship & partnership = unlimited
  3. Continuity – company & cooperative most stable; sole proprietorship most unstable
  4. Management ability – company (professionals) vs sole proprietorship (one person does all)
  5. Capital – company can raise the most; sole proprietorship the least
  6. Degree of control – want full control → sole proprietorship; okay sharing → partnership/company
  7. Nature of business – personal contact needed → sole proprietorship; large-scale manufacturing → company; professional services → partnership
