Section 1

Social Responsibility: Meaning and the Case for It

The idea of this section. Social responsibility is the duty a business owes to the society around it. This part gives the definition and the six reasons that make the duty good for business itself.

1.1 What Is Social Responsibility?

Economic objectives and social objectives

  • A business cannot survive for a long time by pursuing only economic objectives.
  • Along with economic objectives, it must have some social objectives.

The definition — learn this line word for word

Social responsibility relates to the voluntary efforts on the part of businessmen to contribute to the social well-being. Social responsibility is the obligation of businessmen towards the society.

Part of the definitionWhat it tells you
Voluntary effortsThe businessman chooses to do it
ContributeBusiness gives something back
Social well-beingThe good of people and the community
Obligation of businessmenIt is a duty, not a favour
Towards the societyThe society is the party that is owed

Why the obligation exists

  • The business cannot exist in isolation.
  • Businessmen make use of resources of society.
  • Businessmen earn from the members of society.
  • So they must do something for the society.

Exam tip. For a 1-mark answer write the second line of the definition. For a 3-mark answer write the full definition plus the four points under "Why the obligation exists".

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. Write the definition of social responsibility in one sentence.
  2. Q2. Why can a business not survive by pursuing only economic objectives?
  3. Q3. Give two reasons why business carries an obligation towards society.
  4. Q4. Business uses the resources of society and earns from its members. What must business do in return?
Show answers
  1. Social responsibility is the obligation of businessmen towards the society. It relates to the voluntary efforts on the part of businessmen to contribute to the social well-being.
  2. Because along with economic objectives, it must have some social objectives.
  3. Business cannot exist in isolation; it makes use of the resources of society.
  4. It must do something for the society.

1.2 The Case for Social Responsibility

Six reasons, in this order. Learn them as S-B-P-A-S-C.

1 · Self-interest

  • Business is the creation of society and makes use of resources of society.
  • If business fails to satisfy the valuable needs of society, society can revoke back its resources.
  • The way society gives business what it requires, in the same way business must give society what it wants.

2 · Better Environment for Business

  • Social responsibility improves quality of life, which results in raising the standard of living.
  • Business will get a better community to conduct business.
  • The labour will be of better quality and the customer will be of better quality.
  • So better opportunities for business will be available.

3 · Public Image

  • Satisfied employees, workers, customers and suppliers contribute in the success of business.
  • Consumers expect better quality products at reasonable prices.
  • Workers expect fair wages.
  • If business satisfies their expectations, the businessmen improve their image in the eyes of consumers, employees, etc.

4 · Avoidance of Government Interference

  • Government intervention is costly to businessmen.
  • It restricts their flexibility and freedom of making decisions.
  • The Government has passed the Consumer Protection Act to prevent adulteration, black marketing and other anti-social practices.
  • Social responsibilities are essential for avoiding governmental action against business.

5 · Social Power

  • A business firm has considerable social power and corresponding responsibility towards the society.
  • There must be a balance between the power and responsibility.
  • Any imbalance opens the door to irresponsible behaviour.
  • The power is given to meet the responsibility. If businessmen avoid it, some of its powers can be withdrawn by the government and other social groups.

6 · Contribution to Social Problems

  • Business has created some social problems such as pollution, creation of unsafe workplaces, discrimination.
  • Businessmen must take initiative to solve social problems.
  • The reason is that these are created as a result of business operations.
ReasonOne-line meaning
Self-interestHelp society now, gain the benefit in the long run
Better Environment for BusinessA good society gives better labour and better customers
Public ImageHappy people give the business a good name
Avoidance of Government InterferenceDo the duty now and avoid strict government action later
Social PowerMore power means more duty
Contribution to Social ProblemsYou created the pollution, you must solve it

Exam tip. "Self-interest" is the reason that carries the longest-run benefit, and it is the answer to the MCQ "a firm which fulfils all its social obligations gets benefitted in the long run".

  2. Q2. Which reason says that society can take back the resources it gave to business?

Section 2

Social Responsibilities Towards Different Interest Groups

The idea of this section. A business answers to many groups at the same time. This part lists what business owes to each of the six interest groups — consumers, employees, owners, government, community and suppliers.

2.1 Responsibilities Towards Consumers

Seven duties:

  1. Production of safe items by maintaining quality standards.
  2. To ensure regular supply of goods and services.
  3. To educate the customer regarding the use and contents of the product.
  4. Being truthful in advertising.
  5. To follow fair trade practices.
  6. To handle consumer complaints and grievances quickly.
  7. To design and develop the product keeping in mind the needs and requirements of the customers.

Exam tip. Truthful advertising and fair trade practices together make the consumers' list in the exam. The other five are easy one-mark points.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. How many duties does business owe to consumers, and name any three.
  2. Q2. Which duty covers "handle consumer complaints and grievances quickly"?
  3. Q3. A company advertises a product with exaggerated claims. Which consumer duty has it broken?
Show answers
  1. Seven duties. Any three of — safe items with quality standards; regular supply of goods and services; educating the customer; truthful advertising; fair trade practices; quick handling of complaints and grievances; designing products around customer needs.
  2. Handling consumer complaints and grievances quickly.
  3. Being truthful in advertising, along with following fair trade practices.

2.2 Responsibilities Towards Employees

Five duties:

  1. Providing fair compensation and benefits.
  2. Providing good and safe working conditions.
  3. Providing opportunities for personal growth and development.
  4. To develop a sense of belongingness.
  5. To provide incentives and services like housing, medical, insurance, retirement benefits, etc.
What employees expectThe duty that answers it
Fair payFair compensation and benefits
Safe workplaceGood and safe working conditions
A career of their ownOpportunities for personal growth and development
A place in the organisationA sense of belongingness
Security after and during workHousing, medical, insurance and retirement benefits
Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. List the five responsibilities of business towards employees.
  2. Q2. Which duty covers giving housing, medical, insurance and retirement benefits?
  3. Q3. Give one way business can develop a sense of belongingness among employees.
Show answers
  1. Fair compensation and benefits; good and safe working conditions; opportunities for personal growth and development; a sense of belongingness; incentives and services like housing, medical, insurance and retirement benefits.
  2. Providing incentives and services like housing, medical, insurance, retirement benefits, etc.
  3. Any one of — fair compensation and benefits, good and safe working conditions, opportunities for personal growth and development, or the use of incentives and services.

2.3 Responsibilities Towards Owners, Shareholders and Investors

Five duties:

  1. To ensure safety of investment.
  2. To ensure fair and regular return on investment.
  3. To give complete information regarding the financial position of the business.
  4. To ensure appreciation of investment by proper utilisation of resources.
  5. To give them opportunities to participate in decision-making.

Exam tip. "Fair and regular return on investment" is the most quoted line of this list. It belongs to shareholders in every MCQ on the subject.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. Which group is also called owners, shareholders and investors?
  2. Q2. What is the duty of giving complete information about the financial position of the business?
  3. Q3. "To ensure fair and regular return on investment" belongs to which group? (a) Consumers (b) Employees (c) Community (d) Shareholders
Show answers
  1. Owners / Shareholders / Investors.
  2. Giving complete information regarding the financial position of the business.
  3. (d) Shareholders.

2.4 Responsibilities Towards Government

Five duties:

  1. To abide by rules, regulations and laws.
  2. To pay taxes and duties on time.
  3. To help in solving social problems.
  4. To adopt fair dealing specially when involved in external trade, so that the country's reputation is maintained.
  5. To cooperate in planning, investigation and administrative activities of the Government.

Exam tip. "Pay taxes and duties on time" is the single line that is asked most often. Write it first in the answer.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. List the responsibilities of business towards the Government.
  2. Q2. Why does business need fair dealing in external trade?
  3. Q3. Which duty of business to the Government also appears in the case for social responsibility?
Show answers
  1. Abide by rules, regulations and laws; pay taxes and duties on time; help in solving social problems; adopt fair dealing in external trade; cooperate in planning, investigation and administrative activities of the Government.
  2. So that the country's reputation is maintained.
  3. Helping in solving social problems.

2.5 Responsibilities Towards Community

Seven duties:

  1. To protect the environment from all types of pollution.
  2. To provide more employment opportunities.
  3. To promote national integration.
  4. To bring improvement in the local area where the plant is located by providing amenities such as drinking water, electricity, dispensaries, etc.
  5. To help the weaker section of the society.
  6. Providing economic stability.
  7. To preserve social and cultural values.

Exam tip. "Eco-friendly method of production" is a duty to the community. This is the most asked MCQ of the chapter.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. Name the seven responsibilities of business towards the community.
  2. Q2. A firm uses an eco-friendly method of production. Towards which group is it performing social responsibility? (a) Customer (b) Employees (c) Community (d) Shareholders
  3. Q3. What amenities does business provide to improve the local area around its plant?
Show answers
  1. Protect the environment from all types of pollution; provide more employment opportunities; promote national integration; improve the local area around the plant; help the weaker section of the society; provide economic stability; preserve social and cultural values.
  2. (c) Community.
  3. Drinking water, electricity and dispensaries.

2.6 Responsibilities Towards Suppliers

Four duties:

  1. To ensure regular payment to the suppliers.
  2. To adopt fair dealings with the suppliers.
  3. To protect and assist small-scale suppliers by placing orders with them.
  4. To guide and assist the suppliers in improving quality of their products.

Exam tip. "Protecting small-scale suppliers" is the duty that connects this section with small business and MSME chapters.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. List the responsibilities of business towards suppliers.
  2. Q2. How does business protect and assist small-scale suppliers?
  3. Q3. "Ensure regular payment to the suppliers" is a duty to which group?
Show answers
  1. Ensure regular payment to the suppliers; adopt fair dealings with the suppliers; protect and assist small-scale suppliers by placing orders with them; guide and assist suppliers in improving the quality of their products.
  2. By placing orders with them.
  3. The suppliers.

2.7 Interest Groups and Their Conflicting Interests

  • The business interest groups (stakeholders) are: Consumer, Shareholders, Suppliers, Community, Government, Employees.
  • The interests of all the groups run into conflicts with each other.
  • Business persons have proved that they have the required skill to reconcile the conflicting interests of different groups.
  • They serve them to the best of their satisfaction.
Interest groupWhat it wants from the business
ConsumersSafe quality products, fair prices, truthful information
EmployeesFair wages, safe conditions, growth and benefits
ShareholdersSafety of investment, fair and regular return, full information
GovernmentLaws followed, taxes paid on time, cooperation
CommunityClean environment, jobs, local amenities, help for the weaker section
SuppliersRegular payment, fair dealing, help to improve quality

Exam tip. The six groups and the one-line duty to each group make a clean 6-mark table answer.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. Name the six business interest groups.
  2. Q2. Why are the interests of the groups not always in harmony?
  3. Q3. What must a business person do about the conflicting interests?
Show answers
  1. Consumer, Shareholders, Suppliers, Community, Government, Employees.
  2. Each group presses for its own interest, so the interests of the groups often pull in different directions.
  3. Business persons have the required skill and reconcile the conflicting interests of the different groups, serving them to the best of their satisfaction.
Section 3

Business and Environment Protection

The idea of this section. Pollution is the biggest problem created by industry. This part defines it, names its three causes, gives the five reasons for pollution control, and lists what business must do to protect the environment.

3.1 Business and the Environment: The Pollution Problem

  • The greatest problem that industries and businessmen are creating is that of pollution.
  • The absence of an effective legal control system has also encouraged many air- and water-polluting industries being set up in urban areas.
  • Their emissions are seriously affecting the health of workers and local residents.
  • This damage to the environment has contributed to increasing disease and disaster.

Exam tip. The chapter links pollution straight back to section 1.2 — business created these social problems through its own operations, so business must take the initiative to solve them.

Check yourself3 questions
  1. Q1. What is the greatest problem created by industries and businessmen?
  2. Q2. Why have many air- and water-polluting industries been set up in urban areas?
  3. Q3. Who is affected by the emissions of these industries?
Show answers
  1. Pollution.
  2. Because there is an absence of an effective legal control system.
  3. The health of workers and local residents.

3.2 Causes of Environmental Pollution

The definition — learn this line word for word:

Pollution is "the injection of harmful substances into the environment".

  • Since Independence, a large number of small- and medium-scale industries have been set up in cities and towns.
  • As a result, environmental pollution has become a major problem.

Three causes of pollution

TypeMeaningMain cause
Air PollutionInjection of harmful substances in the atmosphere, which lowers the quality of the air we breathe inSmoke and chemicals emitted by factories; smoke emitted by vehicles
Water PollutionDumping of chemical and other wastes in rivers, lakes, etc.Factories located near rivers and streams dump their waste into them
Land PollutionDumping of toxic waste on the land, which affects the fertility of land and makes it unfit for agricultureDumping of toxic waste on land

Extra points on air pollution.

  • It is a serious problem for the whole world.
  • It has created a hole in the ozone layer of the atmosphere.

Two aspects of land pollution.

  1. How to improve the quality of land which is already damaged.
  2. How to check further contamination of land.
Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. Define pollution.
  2. Q2. Name the three types of pollution and the main cause of each.
  3. Q3. What are the two aspects of land pollution?
  4. Q4. Which pollution has created a hole in the ozone layer?
Show answers
  1. Pollution is the injection of harmful substances into the environment.
  2. Air pollution — smoke and chemicals from factories and smoke from vehicles; Water pollution — waste dumped by factories located near rivers and streams; Land pollution — dumping of toxic waste on land.
  3. Improving the quality of land which is already damaged, and checking further contamination of land.
  4. Air pollution.

3.3 Need for Pollution Control

Five reasons.

1 · Ensure Safety

  • Due to environmental pollution and smoke, the visibility is reducing.
  • Poor visibility brings more chances of accidents of automobiles on highways.
  • Poor visibility is hazardous for the landing and taking off of aeroplanes.
  • There must be a check on pollution so that visibility gets improved.

2 · Economic Losses

  • Air pollution is blackening the buildings.
  • Water pollution is affecting the sea life.
  • Land pollution is affecting the fertility of land.
  • All of these are bringing heavy economic losses for the country.
  • Example: the Taj Mahal is losing its beauty due to pollution caused by industries around it. It is a great economic loss for our country.

3 · To Maintain the Natural Beauty

  • Pollution has a direct impact on the natural beauty.
  • Natural beauty consists of mountains, rivers, trees, land, etc.
  • Polluted rivers and streams are spreading foul odour instead of adding to beauty.
  • Beautiful buildings and monuments are getting blackened with pollution.

4 · To Ensure Healthy Life

  • Air pollution is the main cause of heart and lung diseases.
  • Water pollution is the cause of liver problems and problems of kidney, etc.
  • Pollution control is a must to keep a check on these diseases.

5 · To Lead a Comfortable Life

  • Pollution creates nuisance and inconvenience.
  • Polluted rivers become unfit for swimming or fishing.
  • Air pollution causes eye irritation or coughing and breathing problems.
  • Pollution control is required to reduce inconvenience so that people can lead a comfortable and healthy life.

Memory hook. Five reasons in order: Safety → Economic loss → Natural beauty → Healthy life → Comfortable life. Read it as "S-E-N-H-C".

Check yourself4 questions

  4. Q4. How does poor visibility affect road and air safety?
Show answers
  1. Ensure Safety; Economic Losses; Maintain the Natural Beauty; Ensure Healthy Life; Lead a Comfortable Life.
  2. Economic Losses. The Taj Mahal is losing its beauty due to pollution caused by industries around it, which is a great economic loss for the country.
  3. Air pollution causes heart and lung diseases. Water pollution causes liver problems and problems of the kidney.
  4. Poor visibility brings more chances of accidents of automobiles on highways, and it is hazardous for the landing and taking off of aeroplanes.

3.4 Role of Business in Environment Protection

Seven actions:

  1. Making use of eco-friendly techniques of production.
  2. Recycling industrial waste.
  3. Treating the waste through technologies before discharging them into water or dumping on the land.
  4. Participating in eco-friendly activities such as plantation of trees, cleaning rivers, ponds, etc.
  5. Making use of eco marks by producing eco-friendly products.
  6. Abiding by the bye-laws and policies framed by the Government.
  7. Spreading awareness among the general public regarding the importance of eco-friendly techniques and products.

Section 4

Business Ethics and Its Elements

The idea of this section. Business ethics is the moral standard of business life. This part explains how ethics stands above the law and gives the five elements that put ethics into practice.

4.1 What Is Business Ethics?

Ethics and law side by side

PointLawEthics
BasisReflects the ethical or moral values of societyStands on the moral values of society
What it decidesWhat conduct is permitted in society and what conduct is prohibitedWhat is right and what is wrong
If you break itYou are punished or penalisedYou lose the trust of society
LevelThe lowest level of conductAbove the behaviour required by law
  • Ethical behaviour is above the behaviour required by law.
  • Both law and ethics decide what is right and what is wrong.
  • Laws reflect the ethical or moral values of society — what conduct is permitted in the society and what conduct is not permitted.
  • If one is breaking the law, then one is punished or penalised.
  • Business ethics also decides what is the expected conduct of the businessman and what the society does not expect.
  • Social responsibility is broader than legal responsibility.

Exam tip. The line "ethical behaviour is above the behaviour required by law" carries the whole section. Write it as the first line of any business ethics answer.

Check yourself4 questions
  1. Q1. Where does ethical behaviour stand in relation to the law?
  2. Q2. What do laws reflect, and what happens when a person breaks the law?
  3. Q3. What does business ethics decide?
  4. Q4. Social Responsibility is — (a) same as legal responsibility (b) broader than legal responsibility (c) narrower than legal responsibility (d) none of them
Show answers
  1. Ethical behaviour is above the behaviour required by law.
  2. Laws reflect the ethical or moral values of society. A person who breaks the law is punished or penalised.
  3. It decides what is the expected conduct of the businessman and what the society does not expect.
  4. (b) broader than legal responsibility.

4.2 Elements of Business Ethics

These are the basic elements of business ethics while running a business enterprise.

1 · Top Management Commitment

  • Top management has a very important role to guide the entire organisation towards ethical behaviour.
  • The CEO and other top level managers must be openly and strongly committed towards ethical conduct.
  • They must guide people working at middle and low level to follow ethical behaviour.

2 · Publication of a "Code"

  • Ethical organisations define a code of conduct and a set of ethics for all the employees.
  • These principles are defined in a written document called code.
  • The code covers areas such as health and safety in the workplace and fair dealing in selling and marketing activities.

3 · Establishment of Compliance Mechanism

  • A suitable mechanism must be established to make sure that actual decisions match the firm's ethical standards.
  • Examples: corporate ethics in training, recruitment, selection, etc.
  • The employees of the organisation must be free to report about matters of unethical behaviour in the organisation.

4 · Involving Employees at All Levels

  • Employees must be involved while making ethical programmes and policies.
  • The reason is that these policies have to be practised by them.
  • By forming small groups of employees, the ethical policies can be discussed by them.
  • Their attitude towards these policies can be examined.

5 · Measuring Results

  • It is very difficult to measure the ethical results.
  • It must be verified and audited how far work is being carried on according to ethical standards.
  • From time to time the top level must meet the employees to set the future plan of action.
ElementWhat the business actually does
Top Management CommitmentThe CEO and top managers openly commit to ethical conduct and guide the middle and low level staff
Publication of a CodeA written code of conduct for all employees, covering health and safety at work and fair dealing in selling and marketing
Establishment of Compliance MechanismA system (ethics in training, recruitment, selection) plus free reporting of unethical behaviour
Involving Employees at All LevelsSmall groups of employees discuss ethical policies and their attitude to them is examined
Measuring ResultsAuditing how far work follows ethical standards, with the top level meeting employees to set the next plan of action

Memory hook. Five elements in order: Top commitment → Code → Compliance → Involve employees → Measure results. Read it as "T-C-C-I-M".

6.3 Common Traps to Avoid

  1. "Social responsibility" vs "legal responsibility" — social responsibility is broader than legal responsibility, and ethics stands above the law.

The chapter in 12 lines

  • Social responsibility = the obligation of businessmen towards the society; the voluntary efforts of businessmen to contribute to social well-being.
  • A business cannot survive long on economic objectives alone; it needs social objectives too.
  • Business cannot exist in isolation — it uses society's resources and earns from society's members.
  • Six reasons make the case: self-interest, better environment for business, public image, avoidance of government interference, social power, contribution to social problems.
  • Six interest groups claim duties: consumers (7), employees (5), owners/shareholders/investors (5), government (5), community (7), suppliers (4).
  • Their interests conflict; business persons reconcile them and serve all groups to their satisfaction.
  • Pollution = the injection of harmful substances into the environment; it is the greatest problem created by industry.

1.1 What Is Social Responsibility?

A business cannot survive for a long time by pursuing only economic objectives — profit goals alone. Along with economic objectives, it must have social objectives — goals set for the good of society.

The definition — word for word:

Social responsibility relates to the voluntary efforts on the part of businessmen to contribute to the social well-being. Social responsibility is the obligation of businessmen towards the society.

Part of the definitionWhat it tells you
Voluntary effortsThe businessman chooses to do it
ContributeBusiness gives something back
Social well-beingThe good of people and the community
Obligation of businessmenIt is a duty, not a favour
Towards the societySociety is the party that is owed

Why the obligation exists:

  • Business cannot exist in isolation.
  • Businessmen make use of resources of society.
  • Businessmen earn from the members of society.
  • So they must do something for the society.

Exam tip. For 1 mark, write "Social responsibility is the obligation of businessmen towards the society." For 3 marks, add the four reasons why the obligation exists.

1.2 The Case for Social Responsibility

Six reasons, in this order — S-B-P-A-S-C.

1 · Self-interest. Business is the creation of society and uses its resources. If it fails to satisfy the valuable needs of society, society can revoke back its resources. Society gives business what it requires; business must give society what it wants.

2 · Better Environment for Business. Social responsibility improves quality of life, raising the standard of living. Business gets a better community, better labour and better customers, and so better opportunities.

3 · Public Image. Satisfied employees, workers, customers and suppliers contribute to success. Consumers expect better quality at reasonable prices; workers expect fair wages. Satisfying them improves the businessman's image.

4 · Avoidance of Government Interference. Government intervention is costly and restricts flexibility and freedom of decisions. The Government passed the Consumer Protection Act to prevent adulteration, black marketing and other anti-social practices. Social responsibility avoids government action.

5 · Social Power. A firm has considerable social power and a corresponding responsibility. There must be a balance between power and responsibility; imbalance opens the door to irresponsible behaviour. Power is given to meet responsibility — if businessmen avoid it, some powers can be withdrawn by the government and social groups.

6 · Contribution to Social Problems. Business has created pollution, unsafe workplaces and discrimination. Businessmen must take the initiative to solve these problems because business operations created them.

ReasonOne-line meaning
Self-interestHelp society now, gain in the long run
Better Environment for BusinessA good society gives better labour and better customers
Public ImageHappy people give the business a good name
Avoidance of Government InterferenceDo the duty now, avoid strict government action later
Social PowerMore power means more duty
Contribution to Social ProblemsYou created the pollution, you must solve it

Exam tip. "A firm which fulfils all its social obligations gets benefitted in the long run" is Self-interest (option a).

Exam line to memorise: "Social responsibility is the obligation of businessmen towards the society — voluntary efforts to contribute to social well-being."

2.7 Interest Groups and Their Conflicting Interests

The business interest groups, also called stakeholders, are the consumer, shareholders, suppliers, community, government and employees. Their interests run into conflict with each other. Business and Environment Protection

3.1 Business and the Environment: The Pollution Problem

  • The greatest problem created by industries and businessmen is pollution.
  • The absence of an effective legal control system has encouraged many air- and water-polluting industries to be set up in urban areas.
  • Their emissions seriously affect the health of workers and local residents, contributing to increasing disease and disaster.
  • Link back to Session 1: business created these social problems through its own operations, so it must take the initiative to solve them.

3.2 Causes of Environmental Pollution

The definition — word for word:

Pollution is "the injection of harmful substances into the environment".

Since Independence, a large number of small- and medium-scale industries have been set up in cities and towns, and environmental pollution has become a major problem.

TypeMeaningMain cause
Air pollutionInjection of harmful substances into the atmosphere, which lowers the quality of the air we breatheSmoke and chemicals emitted by factories; smoke emitted by vehicles
Water pollutionDumping of chemical and other wastes in rivers, lakes and streamsFactories located near rivers and streams dump their waste into them
Land pollutionDumping of toxic waste on land, which affects fertility and makes land unfit for agricultureDumping of toxic waste on land

Extra points on air pollution: it is a serious problem for the whole world, and it has created a hole in the ozone layer of the atmosphere.

Two aspects of land pollution:

  1. How to improve the quality of land that is already damaged.
  2. How to check further contamination of land.

3.3 Need for Pollution Control

1 · Ensure Safety. Pollution and smoke reduce visibility. Poor visibility brings more chances of automobile accidents on highways and is hazardous for the landing and taking off of aeroplanes. Pollution must be checked so visibility improves.

2 · Economic Losses. Air pollution blackens buildings, water pollution affects sea life, and land pollution affects fertility. All of these bring heavy economic losses for the country. Example: the Taj Mahal is losing its beauty due to pollution caused by industries around it — a great economic loss for India.

3 · Maintain the Natural Beauty. Pollution directly affects natural beauty, which consists of mountains, rivers, trees, land, etc. Polluted rivers and streams spread foul odour, and beautiful buildings and monuments are blackened.

4 · Ensure Healthy Life. Air pollution is the main cause of heart and lung diseases. Water pollution causes liver problems and kidney problems. Pollution control keeps these diseases in check.

5 · Lead a Comfortable Life. Pollution creates nuisance and inconvenience. Polluted rivers become unfit for swimming or fishing; air pollution causes eye irritation, coughing and breathing problems. Control reduces inconvenience so people can live comfortably.

Memory hook: S-E-N-H-C — Safety, Economic loss, Natural beauty, Healthy life, Comfortable life.

3.4 Role of Business in Environment Protection

  1. Use eco-friendly techniques of production — methods that harm the environment less.
  2. Recycle industrial waste — reuse it instead of throwing it away.
  3. Treat waste through technologies before discharging it into water or dumping it on land.
  4. Participate in eco-friendly activities such as plantation of trees and cleaning rivers and ponds.
  5. Use eco marks by producing eco-friendly products.
  6. Abide by the bye-laws and policies framed by the Government — the rules and policies made for business to follow.
  7. Spread awareness among the general public about eco-friendly techniques and products.

Exam line to memorise: "Pollution is the injection of harmful substances into the environment."

Business Ethics and Its Elements

4.1 What Is Business Ethics?

Business ethics is the expected conduct of a businessman — it decides what is right and what is wrong.

  • Ethical behaviour is above the behaviour required by law.
  • Both law and ethics decide what is right and what is wrong.
  • Laws reflect the ethical or moral values of society — what conduct is permitted and what is not.
  • Breaking the law brings punishment or penalty.
  • Business ethics decides the expected conduct of the businessman and what society does not expect.
  • Social responsibility is broader than legal responsibility.

Exam tip. Start every business ethics answer with "Ethical behaviour is above the behaviour required by law."

4.2 Elements of Business Ethics

1 · Top Management Commitment. Top management has a very important role in guiding the whole organisation towards ethical behaviour. The CEO and top-level managers must be openly and strongly committed to ethical conduct, and must guide middle- and low-level people to follow it.

2 · Publication of a "Code". Ethical organisations define a code of conduct and a set of ethics for all employees. These principles are written in a written document called the code. It covers areas such as health and safety in the workplace and fair dealing in selling and marketing.

3 · Establishment of Compliance Mechanism. A suitable mechanism must ensure that actual decisions match the firm's ethical standards. Examples: corporate ethics in training, recruitment and selection. Employees must be free to report unethical behaviour in the organisation.

4 · Involving Employees at All Levels. Employees must be involved in making ethical programmes and policies, because those policies have to be practised by them. Small groups of employees discuss the policies, and their attitude towards them is examined.

5 · Measuring Results. It is very difficult to measure ethical results. Work must be verified and audited to see how far it follows ethical standards. From time to time the top level must meet employees to set the future plan of action.
