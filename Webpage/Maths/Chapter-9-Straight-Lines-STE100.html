Straight Lines

Class 11 Mathematics — Easy Revision Notes (STE100) · CBSE 2026-27

Big picture. A straight line is the simplest shape in coordinate geometry. In this chapter you learn one job: to write the equation of a line. You also learn to read a line back — its slope, its angle, and its distance from a point. Every question in the exam is one of these two jobs, or a mix of them.

3. 🧱 Recall from earlier classes — the four formulas

The syllabus says "brief recall of two-dimensional geometry from earlier classes". The book repeats four formulas from Class 10 and Class 11 (Sets and Functions). You must know all four, because later sections use them.

3.1 Distance formula

Definition. The distance between the points P(x1, y1) and Q(x2, y2) is
PQ = √((x2 − x1)2 + (y2 − y1)2)

Understanding.

  • It is the Pythagoras theorem on the coordinate plane.
  • The order of the two points does not matter. The distance formula squares the differences, so the sign disappears.
  • Distance is never negative.
  • The unit is the same as the unit of the axes.

Worked example 1. Find the distance between (6, −4) and (3, 0).

  • x2 − x1 = 3 − 6 = −3, so the square is 9.
  • y2 − y1 = 0 − (−4) = 4, so the square is 16.
  • PQ = √(9 + 16) = √(25) = 5 units.

✅ The distance is 5 units.

3.2 Section formula (internal division)

Definition. The coordinates of the point that divides the line segment joining P(x1, y1) and Q(x2, y2) internally in the ratio m : n are
((mx2 + nx1)/(m + n), (my2 + ny1)/(m + n))

Understanding.

  • The ratio m : n means "distance to P is m parts, distance to Q is n parts".
  • The point is closer to the end with the bigger number in the ratio.
  • "Internally" means the point lies between P and Q.
  • Memory trick: the x that is multiplied by m is x2, the far end. But the x that is divided by m+n sits with x1. Just write the formula once and check with numbers.

Worked example 2. Find the point that divides the segment joining A(1, −3) and B(−3, 9) internally in the ratio 1 : 3.

  • Here m = 1, n = 3.
  • x = (1 · (−3) + 3 · 1)/(1 + 3) = (−3 + 3)/(4) = 0.
  • y = (1 · 9 + 3 · (−3))/(1 + 3) = (9 − 9)/(4) = 0.

✅ The point is (0, 0) — the origin.

3.3 Mid-point formula

Definition. The mid-point of the segment joining P(x1, y1) and Q(x2, y2) is
((x1 + x2)/(2), (y1 + y2)/(2))

Understanding.

  • It is the section formula with m = n = 1 (the ratio 1 : 1).
  • It is only the average of the two x-values, and the average of the two y-values.
  • Use it for "centre", "half-way", "bisects", and "mid-point of a diagonal".

3.4 Area of a triangle

Definition. The area of the triangle with vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2) and (x3, y3) is
Area = (1)/(2)| x1(y2 − y3) + x2(y3 − y1) + x3(y1 − y2) |

Understanding.

  • The vertical bars mean absolute value. Area is never negative.
  • The pattern is a cycle: each x is multiplied by the difference of the two y-values that follow it.
  • If the answer is exactly zero, the three points lie on one line. They are collinear.
  • This gives you a second way to test collinearity. Section 7 gives the faster way, using slope.

Worked example 3. Find the area of the triangle with vertices (4, 4), (3, −2) and (−3, 16).

  • x1(y2 − y3) = 4((−2) − 16) = 4(−18) = −72
  • x2(y3 − y1) = 3(16 − 4) = 3(12) = 36
  • x3(y1 − y2) = −3(4 − (−2)) = −3(6) = −18
  • Sum = −72 + 36 − 18 = −54
  • Area = (1)/(2)|−54| = 27 square units.

✅ The area is 27 square units.

CHECK 1. Find the distance between (−2, 3) and (4, −5). Then find the mid-point of the segment joining them.

Show answer
  • Distance: √((4+2)2 + (−5−3)2) = √(36 + 64) = √(100) = 10 units.
  • Mid-point: ((−2 + 4)/(2), (3 + (−5))/(2)) = (1, −1).

4. 📈 Slope of a line

Slope is the most important idea in this chapter. Every form of the line equation in Section 8 uses the slope.

4.1 Inclination

Definition. The angle θ made by a line l with the positive direction of the x-axis, measured anticlockwise, is called the inclination of the line. Also,
0° ≤ θ ≤ 180°

Understanding.

  • Always measure from the positive x-axis.
  • Always turn anticlockwise.
  • A line that is parallel to the x-axis has θ = 0°.
  • A vertical line (parallel to the y-axis) has θ = 90°.
  • Every line in the plane forms two angles with the x-axis. They add up to 180°. We always take the angle between 0° and 180°.

4.2 Slope is the tangent of the inclination

Definition 1. If θ is the inclination of a line l, then tanθ is called the slope or gradient of the line l. The slope is written as m. Thus
m = tanθ, θ ≠ 90°

The slope of a line whose inclination is 90° is not defined.

Understanding.

  • The slope is a number. The inclination is an angle. Do not mix them up.
  • Slope 0 means the line is flat (horizontal).
  • Slope not defined means the line is vertical. Never write "slope = ∞" in an exam answer. Write "slope is not defined".
  • Positive slope: the line goes up as you move right (θ is acute).
  • Negative slope: the line goes down as you move right (θ is obtuse).
  • The slope of the x-axis is 0. The slope of the y-axis is not defined.

4.3 Slope from two points on the line

Definition. The slope m of a line through the points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is
m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1), x1 ≠ x2

Understanding.

  • Top: the change in y (rise). Bottom: the change in x (run).
  • Slope = (rise)/(run).
  • Keep the same order in the top and the bottom. If you start with point 2 on top, start with point 2 at the bottom too. Mixing the order flips the sign.
  • If x1 = x2, the denominator is 0. The line is vertical, and the slope is not defined.
  • If y1 = y2, the numerator is 0. The slope is 0, and the line is horizontal.

Why the formula works. Draw a right triangle under the line. The horizontal leg has length x2 − x1. The vertical leg has length y2 − y1. The angle at the left corner is the inclination θ. In that triangle, tanθ = (vertical leg)/(horizontal leg). So m = tanθ = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1).

Worked example 4. Find the slope of the line through each pair of points.

  • (a) (3, −2) and (−1, 4): m = (4 − (−2))/(−1 − 3) = (6)/(−4) = −(3)/(2).
  • (b) (3, −2) and (7, −2): m = (−2 − (−2))/(7 − 3) = (0)/(4) = 0. The line is horizontal.
  • (c) (3, −2) and (3, 4): m = (4 − (−2))/(3 − 3) = (6)/(0). Not defined. The line is vertical.
  • (d) Inclination 60°: m = tan 60° = √(3).

5. ✂️ Parallel and perpendicular lines

5.1 Parallel lines

Definition. Two non-vertical lines l1 and l2 are parallel if and only if their slopes are equal:
m1 = m2

Understanding.

  • Parallel lines have the same inclination, so tanθ is the same, so the slopes are the same.
  • The word "if and only if" means the statement works in both directions. Same slope → parallel. Parallel → same slope.
  • Two vertical lines are also parallel. Their slopes are both undefined. Use the picture, not the formula, for vertical lines.
  • Two different lines with the same slope never meet.

Why it works. Parallel lines make the same angle with the x-axis. So α = β. Then tanα = tanβ. Therefore m1 = m2.

5.2 Perpendicular lines

Definition. Two non-vertical lines l1 and l2 are perpendicular if and only if the product of their slopes is −1:
m1 m2 = −1 or m2 = −(1)/(m1)

Understanding.

  • The slopes are negative reciprocals of each other.
  • Flip the fraction and change the sign. If m1 = (2)/(3), then m2 = −(3)/(2).
  • A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular, and the rule does not apply (one slope is 0, the other is undefined). See this case in the picture.
  • If m1 = 0, then the partner must be vertical. There is no number m2 with 0 × m2 = −1.

Why it works. If the lines are perpendicular, then β = α + 90°. So

m2 = tanβ = tan(α + 90°) = −cotα = −(1)/(tanα) = −(1)/(m1).

Multiply both sides by m1 to get m1 m2 = −1.

Worked example 5. The line through (−2, 6) and (4, 8) is perpendicular to the line through (8, 12) and (x, 24). Find x.

  • Slope of the first line: m1 = (8 − 6)/(4 − (−2)) = (2)/(6) = (1)/(3).
  • Slope of the second line: m2 = (24 − 12)/(x − 8) = (12)/(x − 8).
  • The lines are perpendicular, so m1 m2 = −1:
(1)/(3) × (12)/(x − 8) = −1 → (4)/(x − 8) = −1 → x − 8 = −4 → x = 4.

✅ x = 4.

CHECK 3. The points (4, 4), (3, 5) and (−1, −1) form a triangle. Check whether the line through (4, 4) and (3, 5) is perpendicular to the line through (−1, −1) and (3, 5).

Show answer
  • Line 1 through (4, 4) and (3, 5): m1 = (5 − 4)/(3 − 4) = (1)/(−1) = −1.
  • Line 2 through (−1, −1) and (3, 5): m2 = (5 − (−1))/(3 − (−1)) = (6)/(4) = (3)/(2).
  • m1 m2 = −1 × (3)/(2) = −(3)/(2) ≠ −1.

So these two sides are not perpendicular to each other.

The lesson. A triangle has three sides and three possible pairs. Only one pair is perpendicular (in a right-angled triangle). Here the right angle is at A(4, 4), between AB and AC — not between AB and BC. Always check the pair the question names, and do not assume the first pair you try is the right one.

6. 📐 Angle between two lines

6.1 The formula

Definition. Let L1 and L2 be two non-vertical lines with slopes m1 and m2. The acute angle θ between them is given by
tanθ = | (m2 − m1)/(1 + m1 m2) |, 1 + m1 m2 ≠ 0

The obtuse angle φ is φ = 180° − θ.

Understanding.

  • The vertical bars give the acute angle. The answer is always between 0° and 90°.
  • The obtuse angle is just 180° minus the acute angle. You rarely need it.
  • The order of m1 and m2 does not matter, because of the absolute value.
  • If 1 + m1 m2 = 0, the denominator is 0 and the angle is 90°. That is exactly the perpendicular case of Section 5.2. The formula and the perpendicular rule agree.
  • If m1 = m2, the numerator is 0, so tanθ = 0 and θ = 0°. That is the parallel case.

Why the formula works. Let the inclinations be α1 and α2. The angle between the lines is θ = α2 − α1. Use the tangent subtraction rule:

tanθ = tan(α2 − α1) = (tanα2 − tanα1)/(1 + tanα1 tanα2) = (m2 − m1)/(1 + m1 m2).

Add the absolute value because a tangent can be negative, and we want the acute angle.

6.2 Two answers in one question

Worked example 6. The angle between two lines is (π)/(4). The slope of one line is (1)/(2). Find the slope of the other line.

  • Let m1 = (1)/(2), let m2 = m, and let θ = (π)/(4). So tanθ = 1.
  • Apply the formula:
| (m − (1)/(2))/(1 + (1)/(2)m) | = 1
  • Remove the absolute value. This gives two equations:
(m − (1)/(2))/(1 + (1)/(2)m) = 1 or (m − (1)/(2))/(1 + (1)/(2)m) = −1
  • First equation: m − (1)/(2) = 1 + (1)/(2)m, so (1)/(2)m = (3)/(2), giving m = 3.
  • Second equation: m − (1)/(2) = −1 − (1)/(2)m, so (3)/(2)m = −(1)/(2), giving m = −(1)/(3).

✅ The slope of the other line is 3 or −(1)/(3).

Teacher's note. Whenever a question gives an angle and asks for a slope, expect two answers. The absolute value opens two cases. The picture shows why: the second line can tilt up or tilt down.

CHECK 4. Find the acute angle between the lines y = 2x + 3 and y = −3x + 1.

Show answer
  • m1 = 2 and m2 = −3.
  • tanθ = | (−3 − 2)/(1 + (2)(−3)) | = | (−5)/(1 − 6) | = | (−5)/(−5) | = 1.
  • So θ = 45°.

7. 📏 Collinearity of three points

Definition. Three points A, B and C are collinear if and only if
slope of AB = slope of BC

Understanding.

  • "Collinear" means all three points lie on one straight line.
  • The test is a slope comparison. It is faster than the area formula.
  • You can also use the area formula from Section 3.4. Three collinear points give area 0.
  • Use whichever the question pushes you toward. "Show that the points are collinear" — slope is usually faster. "Show that the area is zero" — use the area formula.

Worked example 7. Show that (3, 0), (−2, −2) and (8, 2) are collinear.

  • Slope of AB for A(3, 0), B(−2, −2): (−2 − 0)/(−2 − 3) = (−2)/(−5) = (2)/(5).
  • Slope of BC for B(−2, −2), C(8, 2): (2 − (−2))/(8 − (−2)) = (4)/(10) = (2)/(5).
  • The slopes are equal.

✅ The three points lie on one line.

CHECK 5. Are the points (1, 1), (3, 3) and (5, 4) collinear?

Show answer
  • Slope of the first pair: (3 − 1)/(3 − 1) = 1.
  • Slope of the second pair: (4 − 3)/(5 − 3) = (1)/(2).
  • The slopes are not equal. So the points are not collinear.

8. 🧩 Forms of the equation of a line

The syllabus lists six forms. Learn what each form needs as input. Then a question becomes "which form fits the data I was given?"

8.1 Lines parallel to the axes

Definition. If a horizontal line L is at a distance a from the x-axis, its equation is either y = a or y = −a. If a vertical line is at a distance b from the y-axis, its equation is either x = b or x = −b. The sign depends on which side of the axis the line lies.

Understanding.

  • A horizontal line has one fixed y-value. Every point on it has that same y. So the equation is y = constant.
  • A vertical line has one fixed x-value. So the equation is x = constant.
  • The x-axis is y = 0. The y-axis is x = 0.
  • To write the line through (h, k) parallel to the x-axis: the answer is y = k.
  • To write the line through (h, k) parallel to the y-axis: the answer is x = h.

Worked example 8. Write the equations of the lines through (−2, 3) that are parallel to the axes.

  • Parallel to the x-axis: every point has y = 3. So the line is y = 3.
  • Parallel to the y-axis: every point has x = −2. So the line is x = −2.

8.2 Point-slope form

Definition. The point (x, y) lies on the line with slope m through the fixed point (x0, y0) if and only if
y − y0 = m(x − x0)

Understanding.

  • Use this form when the question gives one point and the slope.
  • It is only the slope formula in disguise. Start with m = (y − y0)/(x − x0) and cross-multiply.
  • Keep the brackets. Do not write y − y0 = mx − x0. The m multiplies the whole bracket.
  • This is the most used form in the chapter. When you are stuck, ask yourself: "Do I know a point and a slope?"

Worked example 9. Find the equation of the line through (−2, 3) with slope −4.

  • Here m = −4 and (x0, y0) = (−2, 3).
  • y − 3 = −4(x − (−2)) = −4(x + 2)
  • y − 3 = −4x − 8
  • 4x + y + 5 = 0

✅ The equation is 4x + y + 5 = 0.

8.3 Two-point form

Definition. The equation of the line through the points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is
y − y1 = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1)(x − x1)

Understanding.

  • Use this form when the question gives two points and no slope.
  • Read it as: "point-slope form, with the slope worked out from the two points".
  • You can put either point in the (x1, y1) slot. The final equation is the same.
  • The fraction (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1) is just the slope from Section 4.3.

Worked example 10. Write the equation of the line through (1, −1) and (3, 5).

  • Slope: m = (5 − (−1))/(3 − 1) = (6)/(2) = 3.
  • Point-slope with (1, −1): y − (−1) = 3(x − 1)
  • y + 1 = 3x − 3
  • −3x + y + 4 = 0, or 3x − y − 4 = 0.

✅ The equation is 3x − y − 4 = 0.

8.4 Slope-intercept form

Definition. The point (x, y) lies on the line with slope m and y-intercept c if and only if
y = mx + c

If the line with slope m makes x-intercept d, then its equation is
y = m(x − d)

Understanding.

  • The y-intercept c is the y-value where the line cuts the y-axis. The point is (0, c).
  • c is positive above the origin, and negative below it.
  • The x-intercept d is the x-value where the line cuts the x-axis. The point is (d, 0).
  • The second formula is only point-slope form with the fixed point (d, 0).
  • Use this form when the question gives the slope plus one intercept.

Worked example 11. The inclination of a line is θ with tanθ = (1)/(2).

  • (i) If the y-intercept is −(2)/(3): here m = (1)/(2) and c = −(2)/(3).
y = (1)/(2)x − (2)/(3) → 6y = 3x − 4 → 3x − 6y − 4 = 0
  • (ii) If the x-intercept is 4: here m = (1)/(2) and d = 4.
y = (1)/(2)(x − 4) → 2y = x − 4 → x − 2y − 4 = 0

✅ (i) 3x − 6y − 4 = 0; (ii) x − 2y − 4 = 0.

8.5 Intercept form

Definition. The equation of the line that makes intercepts a and b on the x-axis and the y-axis is
(x)/(a) + (y)/(b) = 1

Understanding.

  • The line meets the x-axis at (a, 0) and the y-axis at (0, b).
  • Use this form when the question gives both intercepts, or when it talks about "intercepts" and gives a relation between them.
  • To check your answer, put y = 0. You must get x = a. Put x = 0. You must get y = b.
  • An intercept may be negative. A negative a means the line cuts the x-axis on the left of the origin.

Why it works. Put the two points (a, 0) and (0, b) in the two-point form:

y − 0 = (b − 0)/(0 − a)(x − a) → −ay = b(x − a) → bx + ay = ab → (x)/(a) + (y)/(b) = 1.

Worked example 12. Find the equation of the line that makes intercepts −3 and 2 on the axes.

  • Here a = −3 and b = 2.
  • (x)/(−3) + (y)/(2) = 1
  • Multiply by 6: −2x + 3y = 6, or 2x − 3y + 6 = 0.

✅ The equation is 2x − 3y + 6 = 0.

8.7 Normal form 📝 (formative only)

Read this box for school tests, not for the board paper. CBSE 2026-27 marks Normal form as formative only. It carries no board weightage.
Definition. The equation of the line whose perpendicular distance from the origin is p, and whose normal makes an angle ω with the positive x-axis, is
xcosω + ysinω = p

Understanding.

  • The normal is the line from the origin that meets the given line at 90°.
  • p is the length of that normal. So p ≥ 0.
  • ω is the angle of the normal, not the angle of the line itself.
  • The line's own inclination is ω + 90° (or ω − 90°).
  • Special case: if ω = 0, the normal lies on the x-axis, and the line is x = p.

8.8 General equation of a line 📝 (formative only)

Read this box carefully. The syllabus marks "General equation of a line" as a formative topic. But the form itself Ax + By + C = 0 appears in the distance formula in Section 9. So you must be able to read a line in general form, even though the topic is not a board question.
Definition. Any equation of the form
Ax + By + C = 0

where A and B are not both zero, is called the general linear equation, or the general equation of a line.

Understanding.

  • Every line in the plane can be written in this form. The earlier forms are special cases.
  • A = 0 gives By + C = 0, a horizontal line.
  • B = 0 gives Ax + C = 0, a vertical line.
  • From Ax + By + C = 0: the slope is m = −(A)/(B), the x-intercept is −(C)/(A), and the y-intercept is −(C)/(B).
  • To find the slope of a line in general form, solve for y in terms of x. Then read the coefficient of x.

Worked example 13. Find the slope and the two intercepts of 6x + 3y − 5 = 0.

  • Solve for y: 3y = −6x + 5, so y = −2x + (5)/(3).
  • Slope m = −2. y-intercept = (5)/(3).
  • For the x-intercept, put y = 0: 6x = 5, so x = (5)/(6).

✅ Slope = −2; y-intercept = (5)/(3); x-intercept = (5)/(6).

9. 📢 Distance of a point from a line

Definition. The distance of a point from a line is the length of the perpendicular drawn from the point to the line. The perpendicular distance d of the line Ax + By + C = 0 from the point (x1, y1) is
d = (| Ax1 + By1 + C |)/(√(A2 + B2))

Understanding.

  • First make the equation look like Ax + By + C = 0. Move everything to one side.
  • Substitute the point into the left side. Take the absolute value.
  • Divide by √(A2 + B2) — the square root of the sum of the squares of the coefficients of x and y. The constant C is not used in the denominator.
  • The absolute value makes d positive. Distance is never negative.
  • If the point is on the line, the numerator is 0, so d = 0. This is a good check.
  • This is the most-asked question from this chapter. Learn the formula exactly.

Why the formula works (short version). Draw the line and the point P(x1, y1). Drop a perpendicular PM to the line. The line meets the axes at Q(−(C)/(A), 0) and R(0, −(C)/(B)). The area of triangle PQR computed in two ways gives

(1)/(2) × PM × QR = (1)/(2)|(Ax1 + By1 + C)/(AB)| · (a factor).

Solving for PM, and using QR = |C|√((1)/(A2) + (1)/(B2)), gives the formula above.

Worked example 14. Find the distance of the point (3, −5) from the line 3x − 4y − 26 = 0.

  • Compare with Ax + By + C = 0: A = 3, B = −4, C = −26.
  • Substitute (x1, y1) = (3, −5):
d = (| 3(3) − 4(−5) − 26 |)/(√(32 + (−4)2)) = (| 9 + 20 − 26 |)/(√(9 + 16)) = (|3|)/(5) = (3)/(5)

✅ The distance is (3)/(5) units.

Worked example 15. Find the distance of the point (−1, 1) from the line 12(x + 6) = 5(y − 2).

  • Put the line in general form:
12x + 72 = 5y − 10 → 12x − 5y + 82 = 0
  • So A = 12, B = −5, C = 82, and (x1, y1) = (−1, 1).
d = (| 12(−1) − 5(1) + 82 |)/(√(144 + 25)) = (| −12 − 5 + 82 |)/(13) = (65)/(13) = 5

✅ The distance is 5 units.

CHECK 7. Find the distance of the origin from the line 3x − 4y + 10 = 0.

Show answer
  • (x1, y1) = (0, 0), A = 3, B = −4, C = 10.
  • d = (|0 − 0 + 10|)/(√(9 + 16)) = (10)/(5) = 2.
  • Answer: 2 units.

10. ↔️ Distance between two parallel lines

Definition. The distance between the parallel lines
Ax + By + C1 = 0 and Ax + By + C2 = 0

is
d = (| C1 − C2 |)/(√(A2 + B2))

If the lines are written as y = mx + c1 and y = mx + c2, then
d = (| c1 − c2 |)/(√(1 + m2))

Understanding.

  • The two lines must have the same A and the same B. If they do not, make them the same first.
  • Example: 3x + 2y + 6 = 0 and 6x + 4y − 7 = 0 are parallel. Multiply the first by 2 to get 6x + 4y + 12 = 0. Now both have A = 6, B = 4.
  • Only the constant terms differ in the numerator.
  • The method: pick one point on the first line, then use the point-to-line formula. The shortcut formula gives the same answer.
  • For parallel lines, the answer is never negative. Use the absolute value.
  • If C1 = C2, the lines are the same line. Then d = 0. That is correct.

Worked example 16. Find the distance between the parallel lines 3x − 4y + 7 = 0 and 3x − 4y + 5 = 0.

  • Here A = 3, B = −4, C1 = 7, C2 = 5.
d = (|7 − 5|)/(√(32 + (−4)2)) = (2)/(5)

✅ The distance is (2)/(5) units.

Worked example 17 (the equalisation step). Find the distance between 9x + 6y − 7 = 0 and 3x + 2y + 6 = 0.

  • The coefficients are not the same. Multiply the second line by 3:
9x + 6y + 18 = 0
  • Now both lines have A = 9 and B = 6. Here C1 = −7 and C2 = 18.
d = (|−7 − 18|)/(√(81 + 36)) = (25)/(√(117)) = (25)/(3√(13)) = (25√(13))/(39)

✅ The distance is (25√(13))/(39) units.

CHECK 8. Find the distance between the parallel lines 15x + 8y − 34 = 0 and 15x + 8y + 31 = 0.

Show answer
  • A = 15, B = 8, C1 = −34, C2 = 31.
  • d = (|−34 − 31|)/(√(225 + 64)) = (65)/(√(289)) = (65)/(17).
  • Answer: (65)/(17) units.

12. 🪤 Common traps

  1. Slope not defined vs slope zero. A vertical line has a slope that is not defined. A horizontal line has slope exactly 0. These are different, and examiners test the difference. Never write "slope = ∞".
  2. Keep the order in the slope formula. (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1) and (y1 − y2)/(x1 − x2) give the same answer. But (y2 − y1)/(x1 − x2) flips the sign. Be consistent.
  3. m1 m2 = −1 is not m1 m2 = 1. Perpendicular is the minus one. A very common lost mark.
  4. Perpendicular is the negative reciprocal, not the negative. If m1 = (3)/(4), then m2 = −(4)/(3), not −(3)/(4).
  5. The m in y − y0 = m(x − x0) multiplies the whole bracket. Write y − 3 = −4(x + 2), never y − 3 = −4x + 2.
  6. In the distance formula, the denominator uses only A and B. Not C. The denominator is √(A2 + B2).
  7. Make the line equal to zero first. For "distance from 12(x+6) = 5(y−2)", first expand and rearrange to 12x − 5y + 82 = 0. Never read A, B, C straight from the bracket form.
  8. Parallel lines must have the same A and B before you subtract. For 9x + 6y − 7 = 0 and 3x + 2y + 6 = 0, multiply the second line by 3 first. Otherwise the formula gives a wrong answer.
  9. Absolute value everywhere in distance. Distance is never negative. If your answer is negative, you forgot the bars.
  10. An angle question usually has two answers. The absolute value in tanθ = |(m2 − m1)/(1 + m1 m2)| opens two cases. Do not stop at the first slope.
  11. "Equally inclined" means two equal angles, and you must handle both sign cases. Set the two tangent expressions equal and set one equal to the negative of the other.
  12. Intercepts can be negative. If the line cuts the x-axis to the left of the origin, then a < 0. Keep the sign when you substitute into (x)/(a) + (y)/(b) = 1.
  13. Do not confuse the intercept with the point. The x-intercept is the number a, not the point (a, 0). The point is (a, 0).
  14. Collinear means one line, concurrent means one point. Read the question word carefully.
  15. The normal form's angle is the angle of the NORMAL. In xcosω + ysinω = p, the line itself is at 90° to the direction ω. 📝 formative topic.
  16. Choose the right form before you start writing. Read the data the question gives, then use the decision table in Section 8.6. Starting with the wrong form wastes time and invites algebra errors.

Topic A — Slope and inclination

Q1. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the slope of the line passing through the points (3, −2) and (−1, 4).

Solution.

  • Step 1 — write the formula: m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1).
  • Step 2 — substitute: m = (4 − (−2))/(−1 − 3) = (6)/(−4).

✅ Answer: m = −(3)/(2).

Q2. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the slope of the line whose inclination is 120°.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope = tanθ.
  • Step 2 — tan 120° = tan(180° − 60°) = −tan 60° = −√(3).

✅ Answer: m = −√(3).

Q3. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the slope of the line which makes an angle of 30° with the positive direction of the y-axis, measured anticlockwise.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — the positive y-axis is at 90° from the positive x-axis.
  • Step 2 — turning anticlockwise by 30° brings the line to 90° + 30° = 120° from the positive x-axis. So the inclination is θ = 120°.
  • Step 3 — m = tan 120° = −√(3).

✅ Answer: m = −√(3).

Trap check. An angle "with the y-axis" is not the inclination. Convert it to an angle from the x-axis first.
Q4. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the slope of the line passing through the origin and the mid-point of the segment joining P(0, −4) and B(8, 0).

Solution.

  • Step 1 — mid-point: ((0 + 8)/(2), (−4 + 0)/(2)) = (4, −2).
  • Step 2 — slope through (0, 0) and (4, −2): m = (−2 − 0)/(4 − 0) = −(1)/(2).

✅ Answer: m = −(1)/(2).

Q5. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the slope of the line through (3, −2) and (7, −2). Find the slope of the line through (3, −2) and (3, 4). Explain both answers in one line each.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — first line: m = (−2 − (−2))/(7 − 3) = (0)/(4) = 0.

The y-values are equal, so the line is horizontal (parallel to the x-axis). Its inclination is 0°.

  • Step 2 — second line: m = (4 − (−2))/(3 − 3) = (6)/(0), which is not defined.

The x-values are equal, so the line is vertical (parallel to the y-axis). Its inclination is 90°.

✅ Answers: 0; not defined.

Q6. (3 marks · ★★★) The slope of a line is double the slope of another line. If the tangent of the angle between them is (1)/(3), find the slopes of the two lines.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — let the slopes be m1 = 2m and m2 = m.
  • Step 2 — apply the angle formula:
| (m1 − m2)/(1 + m1 m2) | = (1)/(3) → | (2m − m)/(1 + 2m2) | = (1)/(3) → | (m)/(1 + 2m2) | = (1)/(3)
  • Step 3 — remove the absolute value. Two cases.
    • Case +: (m)/(1 + 2m2) = (1)/(3) → 3m = 1 + 2m2 → 2m2 − 3m + 1 = 0 → (2m − 1)(m − 1) = 0. So m = (1)/(2) or m = 1.
    • Case −: (m)/(1 + 2m2) = −(1)/(3) → 2m2 + 3m + 1 = 0 → (2m + 1)(m + 1) = 0. So m = −(1)/(2) or m = −1.
  • Step 4 — read off the pairs (m1, m2) = (2m, m):
(1, (1)/(2)), (2, 1), (−1, −(1)/(2)), (−2, −1)

✅ Answer: the slopes are 1 and 2; or (1)/(2) and 1; or −1 and −2; or −(1)/(2) and −1.

Topic B — Parallel, perpendicular and collinear

Q7. (3 marks · ★★☆) The line through (−2, 6) and (4, 8) is perpendicular to the line through (8, 12) and (x, 24). Find x.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope of the first line: m1 = (8 − 6)/(4 − (−2)) = (2)/(6) = (1)/(3).
  • Step 2 — slope of the second line: m2 = (24 − 12)/(x − 8) = (12)/(x − 8).
  • Step 3 — perpendicular, so m1 m2 = −1:
(1)/(3) · (12)/(x − 8) = −1 → (4)/(x − 8) = −1 → x − 8 = −4 → x = 4

✅ Answer: x = 4.

Q8. (3 marks · ★★☆) Without using the Pythagoras theorem, show that the points (4, 4), (3, 5) and (−1, −1) are the vertices of a right-angled triangle.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — label the points A(4, 4), B(3, 5), C(−1, −1).
  • Step 2 — find the three slopes:
mAB = (5 − 4)/(3 − 4) = (1)/(−1) = −1, mBC = (−1 − 5)/(−1 − 3) = (−6)/(−4) = (3)/(2), mAC = (−1 − 4)/(−1 − 4) = (−5)/(−5) = 1
  • Step 3 — check each product:
mAB · mAC = (−1)(1) = −1
  • Step 4 — so AB ⊥ AC. The right angle is at A.

✅ Answer: mAB · mAC = −1, so the triangle is right-angled at A(4, 4). ∎

Q9. (3 marks · ★★☆) Without using the distance formula, show that (−2, −1), (4, 0), (3, 3) and (−3, 2) are the vertices of a parallelogram.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — label A(−2, −1), B(4, 0), C(3, 3), D(−3, 2).
  • Step 2 — the slopes of the opposite sides:
mAB = (0 − (−1))/(4 − (−2)) = (1)/(6), mDC = (3 − 2)/(3 − (−3)) = (1)/(6)
mBC = (3 − 0)/(3 − 4) = −3, mAD = (2 − (−1))/(−3 − (−2)) = (3)/(−1) = −3
  • Step 3 — mAB = mDC, so AB ∥ DC. Also mBC = mAD, so BC ∥ AD.
  • Step 4 — both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

✅ Answer: both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, so ABCD is a parallelogram. ∎

Q10. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Show that the points (3, 0), (−2, −2) and (8, 2) are collinear.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope for the first pair: (−2 − 0)/(−2 − 3) = (−2)/(−5) = (2)/(5).
  • Step 2 — slope for the second pair: (2 − (−2))/(8 − (−2)) = (4)/(10) = (2)/(5).
  • Step 3 — the two slopes are equal.

✅ Answer: the slopes are equal, so the three points are collinear. ∎

Q11. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the slope of a line parallel to 3x − 4y + 7 = 0, and the slope of a line perpendicular to it.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope of the given line: m = −(A)/(B) = −(3)/(−4) = (3)/(4).
  • Step 2 — parallel line: the same slope, (3)/(4).
  • Step 3 — perpendicular line: the negative reciprocal, −(4)/(3).

✅ Answer: parallel m = (3)/(4); perpendicular m = −(4)/(3).

Q12. (3 marks · ★★☆) The line through the points (h, 3) and (4, 1) intersects the line 7x − 9y − 19 = 0 at right angles. Find h.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope of 7x − 9y − 19 = 0: m1 = −(7)/(−9) = (7)/(9).
  • Step 2 — the required line is perpendicular, so m2 = −(9)/(7).
  • Step 3 — compute m2 from the two points and equate:
(1 − 3)/(4 − h) = −(9)/(7) → (−2)/(4 − h) = −(9)/(7) → (2)/(4 − h) = (9)/(7)
14 = 9(4 − h) = 36 − 9h → 9h = 22 → h = (22)/(9)

✅ Answer: h = (22)/(9).

Topic C — Angle between two lines

Q13. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the acute angle between the lines √(3)x + y = 1 and x + √(3)y = 1.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — write each line in slope form.
    • Line 1: y = −√(3)x + 1, so m1 = −√(3).
    • Line 2: √(3)y = −x + 1, so y = −(1)/(√(3))x + (1)/(√(3)), so m2 = −(1)/(√(3)).
  • Step 2 — apply the tangent formula:
tanθ = | (m2 − m1)/(1 + m1 m2) | = | (−(1)/(√(3)) + √(3))/(1 + (−√(3))(−(1)/(√(3)))) | = | ((2)/(√(3)))/(1 + 1) | = (1)/(√(3))
  • Step 3 — tanθ = (1)/(√(3)), so θ = 30°.

✅ Answer: the acute angle is 30°.

Q14. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the angle between the x-axis and the line joining the points (3, −1) and (4, −2).

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope of the line: m = (−2 − (−1))/(4 − 3) = (−1)/(1) = −1.
  • Step 2 — the angle with the x-axis is the inclination θ, with tanθ = −1.
  • Step 3 — tanθ is negative, so θ is obtuse. In (0°, 180°), tanθ = −1 gives θ = 180° − 45° = 135°.

✅ Answer: 135°.

Trap check. Do not answer −45°. An inclination always lies between 0° and 180°.
Q15. (3 marks · ★★☆) If the angle between two lines is (π)/(4) and the slope of one of the lines is (1)/(2), find the slope of the other line.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — let m1 = (1)/(2), let m2 = m, and let tanθ = tan(π)/(4) = 1.
  • Step 2 — apply the formula:
| (m − (1)/(2))/(1 + (1)/(2)m) | = 1
  • Step 3 — two cases.
    • Case +: m − (1)/(2) = 1 + (1)/(2)m → (1)/(2)m = (3)/(2) → m = 3.
    • Case −: m − (1)/(2) = −1 − (1)/(2)m → (3)/(2)m = −(1)/(2) → m = −(1)/(3).

✅ Answer: m = 3 or m = −(1)/(3).

Q16. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the equations of the lines through the point (3, 2) which make an angle of 45° with the line x − 2y = 3.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope of x − 2y = 3: m1 = (1)/(2).
  • Step 2 — let the required slope be m. Since tan 45° = 1:
| (m − (1)/(2))/(1 + (1)/(2)m) | = 1
  • Step 3 — two cases.
    • Case +: m − (1)/(2) = 1 + (1)/(2)m → m = 3.
    • Case −: m − (1)/(2) = −1 − (1)/(2)m → (3)/(2)m = −(1)/(2) → m = −(1)/(3).
  • Step 4 — write both lines through (3, 2).
    • With m = 3: y − 2 = 3(x − 3) → y − 2 = 3x − 9 → 3x − y − 7 = 0.
    • With m = −(1)/(3): y − 2 = −(1)/(3)(x − 3) → 3y − 6 = −x + 3 → x + 3y − 9 = 0.

✅ Answer: 3x − y − 7 = 0 and x + 3y − 9 = 0.

Q17. (3 marks · ★★★) If the lines y = 3x + 1 and 2y = x + 3 are equally inclined to the line y = mx + 4, find the value of m.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slopes: m1 = 3 (first line), m2 = (1)/(2) (second line), m3 = m (the line we want).
  • Step 2 — "equally inclined" means the angle between the first line and the third equals the angle between the second line and the third:
| (m − 3)/(1 + 3m) | = | (m − (1)/(2))/(1 + (1)/(2)m) |
  • Step 3 — Case + (same sign):
(m − 3)(1 + (1)/(2)m) = (m − (1)/(2))(1 + 3m)
m + (1)/(2)m2 − 3 − (3)/(2)m = m + 3m2 − (1)/(2) − (3)/(2)m
(1)/(2)m2 − 3 = 3m2 − (1)/(2) → −(5)/(2) = (5)/(2)m2 → m2 = −1

No real solution. Reject this case.

  • Step 4 — Case − (opposite signs):
(m − 3)(1 + (1)/(2)m) = −(m − (1)/(2))(1 + 3m)
m + (1)/(2)m2 − 3 − (3)/(2)m = −m − 3m2 + (1)/(2) + (3)/(2)m
−m + (7)/(2)m2 − (7)/(2) = 0 → 7m2 − 2m − 7 = 0
  • Step 5 — solve: m = (2 ± √(4 + 196))/(14) = (2 ± √(200))/(14) = (2 ± 10√(2))/(14) = (1 ± 5√(2))/(7).

✅ Answer: m = (1 + 5√(2))/(7) or m = (1 − 5√(2))/(7).

Topic D — Equation of a line (all forms)

Q18. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the equation of the line passing through (−4, 3) with slope (1)/(2).

Solution.

  • Step 1 — point-slope form with (x0, y0) = (−4, 3) and m = (1)/(2):
y − 3 = (1)/(2)(x + 4)
  • Step 2 — multiply by 2: 2y − 6 = x + 4, so 2y − x − 10 = 0.

✅ Answer: x − 2y + 10 = 0 (equivalently 2y − x − 10 = 0).

Q19. (2 marks · ★☆☆) Find the equation of the line passing through (0, 0) with slope m.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — point-slope form with (x0, y0) = (0, 0): y − 0 = m(x − 0).

✅ Answer: y = mx.

Q20. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the equation of the line passing through (2, 2√(3)) and inclined with the x-axis at an angle of 75°.

Solution.

So R((c2 − c1)/(m1 − m2), (m1 c2 − m2 c1)/(m1 − m2)).

  • Step 3 — take PQ as the base. It lies along the y-axis, so its length is |c1 − c2|.
  • Step 4 — the height is the horizontal distance from R to the y-axis:
h = | (c2 − c1)/(m1 − m2) | = (|c1 − c2|)/(|m1 − m2|)
  • Step 5 — area:
Area = (1)/(2) × base × height = (1)/(2) × |c1 − c2| × (|c1 − c2|)/(|m1 − m2|) = ((c1 − c2)2)/(2|m1 − m2|)

✅ Answer: Area = ((c1 − c2)2)/(2|m1 − m2|). ∎

Q57. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the distance of the line 4x − y = 0 from the point P(4, 1) measured along the line making an angle of 135° with the positive x-axis.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — the measuring line has slope tan 135° = −1. Its equation through P(4, 1):
y − 1 = −1(x − 4) → x + y − 5 = 0
  • Step 2 — find where this line meets 4x − y = 0. From y = 4x: x + 4x − 5 = 0, so x = 1 and y = 4. The meeting point is Q(1, 4).
  • Step 3 — the required distance is PQ:
PQ = √((1 − 4)2 + (4 − 1)2) = √(9 + 9) = √(18) = 3√(2)

✅ Answer: 3√(2) units.

Trap check. "Distance measured along another line" is not the perpendicular distance. Find the point of intersection, then use the distance formula.
Q58. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the distance of the line 4x + 7y + 5 = 0 from the point (1, 2) along the line 2x − y = 0.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — the measuring line is 2x − y = 0, that is y = 2x. A general point on it is (t, 2t).
  • Step 2 — this point lies on 4x + 7y + 5 = 0:
4t + 14t + 5 = 0 → 18t = −5 → t = −(5)/(18)

The meeting point is Q(−(5)/(18), −(10)/(18)) = (−(5)/(18), −(5)/(9)).

  • Step 3 — the required distance is from P(1, 2) to Q:
PQ = √((1 + (5)/(18))2 + (2 + (5)/(9))2) = √(((23)/(18))2 + ((23)/(9))2) = (23)/(18)√(1 + 4) = (23√(5))/(18)

✅ Answer: (23√(5))/(18) units.

Q59. (3 marks · ★★☆) Prove that the line through the point (x1, y1) and parallel to the line Ax + By + C = 0 is A(x − x1) + B(y − y1) = 0.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — slope of the given line: m = −(A)/(B).
  • Step 2 — a parallel line has the same slope. Point-slope form through (x1, y1):
y − y1 = −(A)/(B)(x − x1)
  • Step 3 — multiply by B: B(y − y1) = −A(x − x1), so A(x − x1) + B(y − y1) = 0.

✅ Answer: A(x − x1) + B(y − y1) = 0. ∎

Q60. (3 marks · ★★★) If p and q are the lengths of the perpendiculars from the origin to the lines xcosθ − ysinθ = kcos 2θ and xsecθ + y cosec θ = k, prove that p2 + 4q2 = k2.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — first line in general form: xcosθ − ysinθ − kcos 2θ = 0.
p = (|0 − 0 − kcos 2θ|)/(√(cos2θ + sin2θ)) = |kcos 2θ|
  • Step 2 — second line: multiply xsecθ + y cosec θ = k by sinθcosθ:
xsinθ + ycosθ = ksinθcosθ → xsinθ + ycosθ − ksinθcosθ = 0
q = (|0 + 0 − ksinθcosθ|)/(√(sin2θ + cos2θ)) = |ksinθcosθ| = (|ksin 2θ|)/(2)
  • Step 3 — square and add:
p2 + 4q2 = k2cos2 2θ + 4 · (k2 sin2 2θ)/(4) = k2(cos2 2θ + sin2 2θ) = k2

✅ Answer: p2 + 4q2 = k2. ∎

Q61. (3 marks · ★★☆) Show that the path of a moving point, whose distances from the lines 3x − 2y = 5 and 3x + 2y = 5 are equal, is a straight line.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — let the moving point be (h, k). Write both lines in general form: 3x − 2y − 5 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 5 = 0.
  • Step 2 — both have √(A2 + B2) = √(9 + 4) = √(13). Equal distances give
|3h − 2k − 5| = |3h + 2k − 5|
  • Step 3 — remove the absolute value. Two cases.
    • Case +: 3h − 2k − 5 = 3h + 2k − 5 → −4k = 0 → k = 0. This is the line y = 0.
    • Case −: 3h − 2k − 5 = −(3h + 2k − 5) → 6h − 10 = 0 → h = (5)/(3). This is the line x = (5)/(3).
  • Step 4 — both are straight lines.

✅ Answer: the path is y = 0 or x = (5)/(3) — two straight lines that are the angle bisectors. ∎

Q62. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the equation of the line parallel to the y-axis and drawn through the point of intersection of the lines x − 7y + 5 = 0 and 3x + y = 0.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — solve the two lines. From 3x + y = 0: y = −3x. Substitute in the first:
x − 7(−3x) + 5 = 0 → x + 21x + 5 = 0 → 22x = −5 → x = −(5)/(22)
  • Step 2 — a line parallel to the y-axis has the form x = constant.
  • Step 3 — through x = −(5)/(22):
x = −(5)/(22) → 22x + 5 = 0

✅ Answer: 22x + 5 = 0.

Q63. (3 marks · ★★☆) Find the equation of a line drawn perpendicular to the line (x)/(4) + (y)/(6) = 1 through the point where it meets the y-axis.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — put x = 0 to find the meeting point with the y-axis: (y)/(6) = 1, so y = 6. The point is (0, 6).
  • Step 2 — slope of the given line: (x)/(4) + (y)/(6) = 1 → 6x + 4y = 24 → y = −(3)/(2)x + 6. So m1 = −(3)/(2).
  • Step 3 — perpendicular slope: m2 = (2)/(3).
  • Step 4 — line through (0, 6): y − 6 = (2)/(3)x → 3y − 18 = 2x → 2x − 3y + 18 = 0.

✅ Answer: 2x − 3y + 18 = 0.

Q64. (3 marks · ★★★) A ray of light passing through the point (1, 2) reflects on the x-axis at point A, and the reflected ray passes through the point (5, 3). Find the coordinates of A.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — reflect the point (1, 2) in the x-axis to get (1, −2). The reflected ray, extended backwards, passes through this point.
  • Step 2 — so A lies on the line through (1, −2) and (5, 3):
m = (3 − (−2))/(5 − 1) = (5)/(4) → y + 2 = (5)/(4)(x − 1)
  • Step 3 — A is on the x-axis, so put y = 0:
2 = (5)/(4)(x − 1) → x − 1 = (8)/(5) → x = (13)/(5)

✅ Answer: A((13)/(5), 0).

Q65. (3 marks · ★★☆) The hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle has its ends at the points (1, 3) and (−4, 1). Find the equations of the legs (perpendicular sides) of the triangle, which are parallel to the axes.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — the legs are parallel to the axes, so the right angle is at a point with the x-coordinate of one end and the y-coordinate of the other.
  • Step 2 — two possibilities: (1, 1) or (−4, 3).
  • Step 3 — if the right angle is at (1, 1), the legs are the lines x = 1 and y = 1.
  • Step 4 — if the right angle is at (−4, 3), the legs are the lines x = −4 and y = 3.
  • Step 5 — check that both ends lie correctly: for the first case, (1, 3) has x = 1 ✓ and (−4, 1) has y = 1 ✓.

✅ Answer: x = 1 and y = 1; or x = −4 and y = 3.

Q66. (3 marks · ★★★) If the lines y = m1x + c1, y = m2x + c2 and y = m3x + c3 are concurrent, show that m1(c2 − c3) + m2(c3 − c1) + m3(c1 − c2) = 0.

Solution.

  • Step 1 — find the intersection of the first two lines by equating:
m1x + c1 = m2x + c2 → x = (c2 − c1)/(m1 − m2), y = (m1 c2 − m2 c1)/(m1 − m2)
  • Step 2 — the third line passes through this point:
(m1 c2 − m2 c1)/(m1 − m2) = m3 · (c2 − c1)/(m1 − m2) + c3
  • Step 3 — multiply by (m1 − m2):
m1 c2 − m2 c1 = m3(c2 − c1) + c3(m1 − m2)
  • Step 4 — move everything to the left and group by m:
m1 c2 − m2 c1 − m3 c2 + m3 c1 − m1 c3 + m2 c3 = 0
m1(c2 − c3) + m2(c3 − c1) + m3(c1 − c2) = 0

✅ Answer: proved. ∎

Q76. (4 marks · ★★☆ Case study) A city planner draws two roads on a map. Road 1 passes through the points (2, 1) and (6, 3). Road 2 is perpendicular to Road 1 and passes through the point (4, 5).

(i) Find the slope of Road 1. (ii) Find the slope of Road 2. (iii) Find the equation of Road 2. (iv) Find the point where Road 2 crosses the y-axis.

Solution.

  • Step 1 (i) — m1 = (3 − 1)/(6 − 2) = (2)/(4) = (1)/(2).
  • Step 2 (ii) — perpendicular slope: m2 = −(1)/(m1) = −2.
  • Step 3 (iii) — point-slope through (4, 5) with slope −2:
y − 5 = −2(x − 4) → y − 5 = −2x + 8 → 2x + y − 13 = 0
  • Step 4 (iv) — put x = 0: y = 13. The point is (0, 13).

Q77. (4 marks · ★★☆ Case study) A triangular park has corners at A(1, 1), B(5, 1) and C(1, 4) on a coordinate map. The units are in hundreds of metres.

(i) Find the equation of the side AB. (ii) Find the equation of the side AC. (iii) Find the equation of the side BC. (iv) Find the area of the park. (v) Find the length of the altitude from A to the side BC.

Solution.

  • Step 1 (i) — A(1, 1) and B(5, 1) have the same y-value. So AB is horizontal: y = 1.
  • Step 2 (ii) — A(1, 1) and C(1, 4) have the same x-value. So AC is vertical: x = 1.
  • Step 3 (iii) — slope of BC: m = (4 − 1)/(1 − 5) = (3)/(−4) = −(3)/(4).
y − 1 = −(3)/(4)(x − 5) → 4y − 4 = −3x + 15 → 3x + 4y − 19 = 0
  • Step 4 (iv) — AB and AC are perpendicular (one horizontal, one vertical), so the right angle is at A. Base AB = 4, height AC = 3.
Area = (1)/(2) × 4 × 3 = 6
  • Step 5 (v) — the altitude from A to BC is the perpendicular distance from (1, 1) to 3x + 4y − 19 = 0:
d = (|3(1) + 4(1) − 19|)/(√(9 + 16)) = (|−12|)/(5) = (12)/(5)

✅ Answers: (i) y = 1; (ii) x = 1; (iii) 3x + 4y − 19 = 0; (iv) 6 square units (that is 60{,}000 m2); (v) (12)/(5) units.

15. ✍️ Exam writing tips from a maths teacher

  1. Name the formula before you use it. Write "Slope m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1)" on its own line. Examiners give marks for visible method, not only for the final number.
  2. Check the slope sign first. Before you write an equation, decide whether the slope should be positive or negative from the picture in your head. A wrong sign early costs the whole question.
  3. State the condition explicitly. Write "Since the lines are perpendicular, m1 m2 = −1." This one line often carries a mark.
  4. Answer angle questions with both values. If the question gives an angle and asks for a slope, show both cases. Write "Case 1" and "Case 2" clearly. A single answer loses half the marks.
  5. In distance questions, rearrange to Ax + By + C = 0 first. Then write "Here A = …, B = …, C = …" before substituting. This prevents the most common arithmetic slip.
  6. For parallel lines, show the equalisation step. Write "Multiplying the second line by 3 gives …" so the examiner sees that both lines now have the same A and B.
  7. Keep the absolute value bars until the last line. Only remove them when you write the final positive number.
  8. Give the equation in general form. The NCERT answer style is Ax + By + C = 0 with integer A, B, C where possible. Clear the fractions before you stop.
  9. Check your answer by substituting. After finding an equation, put one of the given points into it. If it gives 0 = 0, the equation is correct. This takes ten seconds and catches most errors.
  10. Draw a rough sketch. For any question about two lines, angles, or a triangle, a quick sketch shows whether your slope signs and your final answer make sense.
  11. In image and foot-of-perpendicular questions, use the mid-point relation. Write "The line is the perpendicular bisector of PQ" — then use both conditions (perpendicular, and mid-point on the line). This shows the method clearly.
  12. End proof questions with a conclusion line. Write "∴ the three points are collinear" or "∴ the expression is divisible by …". The conclusion earns the last mark.

A.1 Normal form 📝 (formative only)

The syllabus keeps Normal form as a formative topic. Section 8.7 gives the formula: xcosω + ysinω = p, where p is the perpendicular distance from the origin and ω is the angle that normal makes with the positive x-axis.

How to convert a general equation to normal form. Given Ax + By + C = 0 with C < 0, divide throughout by √(A2 + B2):

(A)/(√(A2+B2))x + (B)/(√(A2+B2))y = (−C)/(√(A2+B2))

Then cosω = (A)/(√(A2+B2)), sinω = (B)/(√(A2+B2)), and p = (|C|)/(√(A2+B2)).

Worked example. Reduce x + y − 2 = 0 to normal form.

  • Here A = 1, B = 1, C = −2, and √(A2 + B2) = √(2).
  • Divide: (x)/(√(2)) + (y)/(√(2)) = (2)/(√(2)) = √(2).
  • So cosω = (1)/(√(2)) and sinω = (1)/(√(2)), giving ω = 45°. And p = √(2).

✅ Normal form: xcos 45° + ysin 45° = √(2).

A.2 General equation of a line 📝 (formative only)

The topic "General equation of a line" is formative only. But you must be able to read a line in the form Ax + By + C = 0, because the distance formula needs exactly that shape.

A.4 Family of lines and concurrency

"Family of lines" and "concurrency" are not syllabus topics. But NCERT's Miscellaneous Exercise uses them, and school tests often set them, so they are worth understanding.

  • Concurrent lines means three or more lines passing through one common point.
  • Method to test concurrency: solve any two lines, then substitute the point into the third. If it satisfies the third equation, the three lines are concurrent.
  • Family of lines through a fixed point (x1, y1): y − y1 = m(x − x1), where the parameter m lets you sweep through every line through that point.
  • Family of lines through the intersection of L1 = 0 and L2 = 0: L1 + λ L2 = 0, where λ is a parameter. This is the standard trick for Misc Q11 and Q5.

A.5 Foot of perpendicular and image of a point

Also not a syllabus topic, but a standard school-test question. Use this three-step method:

  1. Find the slope of the given line. The perpendicular slope is its negative reciprocal.
  2. Write the perpendicular line through the given point (point-slope form).
  3. Solve the two equations together. That gives the foot. For the image, use the mid-point relation: the foot is the mid-point of the point and its image.

A.6 Distance along a line, and angle bisectors

  • Distance along a line means you must find the actual point of intersection, then use the distance formula. It is not the perpendicular distance.
  • A point equidistant from two lines lies on an angle bisector. Equal distances give an absolute-value equation, which opens into two straight lines — just as in Q61.
