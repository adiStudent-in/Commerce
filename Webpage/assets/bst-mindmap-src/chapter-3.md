# Chapter 3 — Private, Public and Global Enterprises

## Mixed economy timeline
- After 1947, India committed to a Socialistic Pattern and adopted a mixed economy
- Industrial Policy Resolutions of 1948 and 1956 gave public sector dominance
- Private entry was restricted by licensing; some industries were reserved
- Public sector dominance for about 4 decades hampered growth
- 1991 policy: disinvestment (selling PSU holdings), enhanced private role, FDI encouraged
- After 1991, public, private and global enterprises coexist

## Two sectors and forms
- Mixed economy allots roles to both public and private sectors
- Public sector: owned and managed partly or wholly by Central or State Government
- Private sector: owned and managed by individuals or a group of individuals
- Private forms: sole proprietorship, joint Hindu family, partnership, cooperative society, joint stock company
- Public forms: departmental undertaking, statutory corporation, government company
- Private examples: Trump Towers, Reliance; public examples: Indian Railways, Defence, Post Office

## Departmental undertaking
- Oldest form; a government department with no separate legal existence
- Managed by officials under the Minister, answerable to Parliament
- Financed 100% from budget; revenue paid into Government Treasury
- Employees are government employees; pay, promotion and audit follow government rules
- Examples: Indian Railways, Defence, Post and Telegraph, broadcasting
- Merit: complete control, accountability, income, national security
- Limit: no flexibility, delays, bureaucracy, red tapism, conservative approach
- Limit: political interference such as extra trains for home state; consumer neglect
- Suitable for secrecy, absolute control, huge capital, revenue: defence, atomic power, iron and steel, Railways

## Statutory corporation
- Corporate body with separate legal existence, set up under a Special Act
- Special Act defines objects, powers and functions; wholly owned by government
- Board of Directors appointed by government; perpetual succession and common seal
- Financially independent; own audit and staffing rules
- Merit: operational flexibility, freedom from interference, autonomy, economic growth
- Limit: theoretical autonomy, interference, rigid structure, delay, misuse
- Examples: RBI, FCI, LIC, Air India, ICAI, ICSI
- Trap: LIC Act 1956 made LIC statutory; funds come from borrowings, not shares

## Government company 51%
- Section 2(45), Companies Act 2013 defines a government company
- Not less than 51% of paid-up capital held by Central or State Government
- Includes a subsidiary; shares purchased in the name of the President of India
- Board nominated by government; governed by Memorandum and Articles
- Free from budget controls, but Annual Report goes to Parliament or State Legislature
- Merit: easy formation, autonomy, independent status, quality at reasonable prices
- Demerit: freedom only in name, no accountability, purpose defeated
- Examples: SAIL, BHEL, BEML, BSNL, Indian Oil, GAIL

## Three forms compared
- Formation: government order, Special Act, Companies Act respectively
- Legal status: only departmental undertaking lacks a separate legal entity
- Staff: government employees versus contract of service in the other two
- Finance: 100% budget, borrowings, at least 51% share capital
- Management: ministry officials, appointed Board, nominated Board
- Autonomy: none, considerable, substantial in that order
- Accountability: concerned ministry, Parliament, concerned ministry respectively
- Memory hook: "Do See Great" for Departmental, Statutory, Government
- Statutory corporation suits special powers, huge capital, regular grants, accountability with autonomy
- Government company suits control without nationalisation, collaboration and big funded projects

## Private vs public: 7 bases
- Objective: private earns profit; public provides services
- Ownership: private individuals; public Central or State Government
- Capital: private investors; public at least 51% government
- Management: owners and professionals; public Board of Directors
- Freedom: private little interference; public political interference
- Area: private steady returns; public strategic industries and utilities
- Flexibility: private modifies easily; public rigid policies need approval

## Global enterprise basics
- Meaning: operations extend beyond the country of incorporation
- Home country holds the head office; host countries hold operations
- Also called Supernatural Companies or Transnational Companies
- Examples: Coca-Cola, McDonald's, Intel, Sony, Apple, Google
- McDonald's home is USA; India and Pakistan are host countries
- McDonald's India products: McAloo Tikki and Mc Maharaja Mac

## Global enterprise features
- 7 features: huge capital, centralised control, foreign collaboration, advanced technology
- Also product innovation, marketing strategies, expansion of market territory
- Huge capital: raises funds easily; host-country banks and investors invest
- Centralised control: Head Office controls all branches and subsidiaries
- Foreign collaboration: production, brand names, sale of technology with Indian firms
- Advanced technology and innovation: latest technology, continuous R&D
- Marketing and expansion: aggressive selling; branches and affiliates build international brands

## Global merits and demerits
- 7 merits: employment, advanced technology, foreign capital, living standard
- Also growth of domestic firms, healthy competition, world economy integration
- Demerit: disregard of national priorities, profit over host country needs
- Demerit: monopoly can dominate and wipe out local businesses
- Demerit: depletes natural resources and dumps obsolete technology
- Demerit: powerful MNCs can influence policy, threatening national sovereignty

## Joint venture
- Two or more firms join to establish a new enterprise
- Partners contribute capital and participate in management
- Example: Maruti of India and Suzuki of Japan formed Maruti Suzuki India Ltd.
- Benefit: larger financial and human resources, faster growth
- Benefit: access to markets, distribution networks and advanced technology
- Benefit: innovation, low production cost and established brand name
- Trap: joint venture is not a government-private contract like PPP

## Public private partnership
- PPP: legally binding government-private contract for public assets and services
- Public partner: ministries, departments, municipalities or state-owned enterprises
- Private partner brings expertise and bears financial, technical, operational risk
- Government ensures social obligations, sector reforms and public investment
- Cost borne by users or government; one-time capital subsidy may be given
- Revenue shared between government and private enterprise in an agreed ratio
- Example: DMRC provides metro service in Delhi
- Problem: private investors earn above the government bond rate

## Exam numbers and traps
- 51% minimum government holding; Section 2(45), Companies Act 2013
- ₹50 lakh of ₹1 crore equals 50%, so it fails the 51% test
- Trap: Board appointed in statutory corporation, nominated in government company
- Trap: Annual Report goes to Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, not one house
