# Chapter 5 — Emerging Modes of Business

## Emerging modes: meaning and need
- In the modern period, the manner of conducting business changed considerably
- Emerging modes of business means the recent trends taking place in the way business is done
- 5 needs: competition, quality standards, lower prices, faster processing, better customer care
- Strongest trend is e-business; the chapter studies e-business and outsourcing
- Memory hook: C-Q-P-F-C covers the five needs

## E-business: meaning and scope
- E-business means conducting business activities over internet or any other computer network
- Kotler: general term for buying and selling process that is supported by electronic means
- Two halves: Industry (production) and Commerce (trade and distribution)
- E-commerce covers distribution through B2B, B2C and C2C; Intra-B adds to e-business
- Wider than e-commerce, it adds production, accounting, finance, personnel and administration
- Trap: all e-commerce is e-business, but not all e-business is e-commerce

## B2B: business to business
- B2B means transactions taking place between business firms
- Maruti Udyog buying tyres, windscreen glass and door handles is B2B
- 5 B2B transactions: tenders, negotiations, distribution, utilities, collaborations
- Keyword is between firms; a firm and its customer is not B2B
- Trap: raw material quotations from one businessman to another are B2B

## B2C: business to customer
- B2C means transactions between a business and its customers
- It keeps the business in touch with customers round the clock
- ATM withdrawal is the classic B2C round-the-clock example
- 4 B2C transactions: selling and distribution, complaints, after-sale service, promotion
- Trap: ATM and 24x7 shopping belong to B2C, not C2C

## C2C: customer to customer
- C2C means transactions between two or more customers
- OLX, Quikr and CarDekho are the C2C platforms named in the notes
- 4 C2C uses: used goods, antiques, fraud warnings, quality information
- In C2C both buyer and seller are customers; platform is not a party
- Trap: Rajiv selling his sofa on OLX is C2C, not B2C

## Intra-B: within the firm
- Intra-B means dealings among departments and persons within the firm, over intranet
- Intra-B has facilitated flexible manufacturing to customer requirements
- 5 uses: inventory and cash, reporting, HRM, recruitment, training
- VPN lets employees access the network and work from anywhere
- Trap: a purchase-to-production mail is Intra-B, not B2B

## 11 bases: e-business vs traditional
- Formation: easy vs difficult; physical presence: not required vs required
- Location: none for e-business vs near raw material and market
- Set-up and operating costs: low for e-business vs high for traditional
- Contact: direct for e-business vs indirect through intermediaries
- Communication: any direction vs hierarchical; structure: flat vs tall
- Response: instant vs slow; process cycle: short vs long
- Interpersonal touch: less in e-business vs more in traditional

## 5 benefits of e-business
- 5 benefits: easy formation, convenience, speed, global reach, paperless
- Ease of formation comes with lower investment; network matters more than net worth
- Convenience means shopping anything, anywhere, anytime, 24x7
- Speed means the cycle finishes with the click of a mouse
- Global reach lets buyers and sellers from any part of the world interact
- Paperless society: income tax returns can be filed online

## 6 limitations of e-business
- 6 limitations: low touch, delay, technology, non-traceability, resistance, ethics
- Low personal touch means little face-to-face interaction; delivery is slow
- Technology capability is needed by both the firm and the customer
- Non-traceability of parties makes fraud and scams easier
- Ethical fallouts include spam, privacy breach, data theft and fake reviews

## Online transaction: 3 stages
- Online transaction has 3 stages: pre-purchase, purchase, post-purchase
- Pre-purchase stage: search, identify the product, select and negotiate
- Purchase stage: place the order and make payment
- Post-purchase stage: delivery, after-sales service, feedback and complaints
- Trap: payment belongs to the purchase stage, not post-purchase

## 7 online payment modes
- 7 modes: cash on delivery, cheque, net banking, credit card, debit card, wallet, UPI
- Cash on delivery: pay when goods arrive; seller bears non-payment risk
- Cheque: paper instrument cleared through banks, so payment is slow
- Net banking: transfer money through the bank's website
- Credit card pays later; debit card debits the customer's own account
- Digital wallet: preloaded money in an app for small payments
- UPI: instant bank-to-bank transfer using a virtual payment address

## Risks and security measures
- 3 risk families: transaction, data, IP and privacy
- Transaction risk: default on order, delivery or payment
- Data storage and transmission risk: loss, theft and virus attacks
- IP and privacy risk: illegal downloads, cookies and spyware
- 5 measures: encryption, digital signature, SSL, firewall, antivirus
- Encryption converts data into unreadable code using a key
- Digital signature verifies sender identity and integrity, unlike encryption
- SSL encrypts browser-server data; firewall filters traffic, antivirus removes malware

## Outsourcing: meaning and types
- Outsourcing means contracting out some activities to a third party, earlier done in-house
- 3 types: BPO, KPO and LPO
- BPO: routine processes like call centres, payroll and customer support
- KPO: knowledge-intensive work like research, analytics and legal advice
- LPO: legal process outsourcing, a specialised type of KPO

## 6 reasons for outsourcing
- 6 reasons: focus, excellence, cost, alliance, development, risk sharing
- Focusing attention: the firm concentrates on its core activities
- Quest for excellence: specialists deliver higher quality output
- Cost reduction: vendor economies of scale lower operational costs
- Growth through alliance: the firm builds strategic alliances with vendors
- Development and risk sharing: employment grows; part of the risk shifts

## Outsourcing: limits and concerns
- Confidentiality risk: sensitive data shared with the vendor may leak
- Loss of control over the outsourced activity and its quality
- Dependence on the vendor; vendor failure can disrupt operations
- Employee resistance and fear of job loss in the firm
- Ethical concern: cost cutting may exploit low-wage labour at vendor
