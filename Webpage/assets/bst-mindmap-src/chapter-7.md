# Chapter 7 — Sources of Business Finance

## Meaning of business finance
- Business finance is the money required to carry out business activities
- Definition: planning, raising, managing and controlling all capital funds used in business
- Four verbs: planning, raising, managing, controlling; initial capital depends on nature and size
- Finance includes both owners' funds and borrowed funds

## Nature and significance
- Nature has 7 points; significance has 7 benefits of adequate finance
- Chain: Necessary, Size, All Types, Continuous, Wider, Fluctuating, Determines size
- Need fluctuates with inflation, demand, supply, fashion and technology
- Benefits: meet liabilities, work smoothly, adopt technology, use opportunities
- Benefits also: face competition, replace assets, survive recession and depression

## Fixed vs working capital
- Fixed capital buys premises, furniture and fixtures, refrigeration equipment
- Working capital buys goods and meets wages, salaries and electricity expenses
- Fixed capital serves long-term assets; working capital serves day-to-day needs
- Capital need depends on nature, size, technology, demand and supply
- Anirudh's departmental store and Hina's export order illustrate this difference
- Trap: stock of goods is working capital, never fixed capital

## Owners' vs borrowed fund
- Two sources by ownership: owners' fund and borrowed fund
- Owners' fund is permanent, risk capital, with no security required
- Owners' return varies with profit; no return is compulsory in loss
- Borrowed fund has fixed time, security, regular interest and no control
- Owners control and bear risk; lenders get a legal regular return
- Trap: lenders can sue on default but cannot control the firm

## Equity shares
- Equity shares carry no special right to dividend or capital repayment
- 6 features: risk, residual income, permanent capital, dividend, limited liability, voting
- Equity holders are primary risk bearers and the real owners
- Dividend varies with earnings and is paid after creditors and preference holders
- Merit: permanent capital, no fixed dividend, no security, voting control
- Limitation: high risk, uncertain dividend, dilution; dividend is never mandatory

## Preference shares
- Two share types: equity and preference; preference gets dividend and capital priority
- 5 features: fixed dividend, no security, restricted voting, hybrid, no fixed liability
- Hybrid security combines profit-linked equity dividend with a fixed debenture rate
- Preference shareholders vote when dividend is unpaid for two years
- Types: cumulative, participating, convertible, redeemable, each with an opposite
- Merit: steady income and priority; limitation: no vote, dividend not compulsory

## Equity vs preference shares
- 6 bases: face value, dividend, voting, investor type, risk, refund
- Equity face value low, dividend last; preference face value high, dividend first
- Equity gets all votes and attracts bold investors; preference attracts cautious ones
- Equity bears primary risk and is refunded last; preference is safer and paid first
- Hook: equity is bold, risky and last; preference is safe, fixed and first

## Retained earnings
- Retained earnings are undistributed profits left after dividend and taxes
- Also called self-financing or ploughing back of profit
- 6 features: cushion, innovation funds, no dilution, medium- and long-term finance, dependable, stable dividend
- Cushion of security helps in adversity; no new shares means no dilution
- Limitation: only profitable firms can use it; trap: it is owners' fund

## Debentures and bonds
- Debenture or bond is a certificate under the company's seal acknowledging debt
- Creditorship securities: debenture holders are creditors with no voting rights
- 7 features: borrowed fund, fixed interest, compulsory interest, trustee, security, redeemable, no vote
- Interest is fixed at the time of issue and paid even in loss
- Types: secured or unsecured, convertible or non-convertible; always redeemed after a fixed period
- 8 bases vs shares: nature, status, vote, control, return, security, redemption, risk
- Merit: no dilution of control; limitation: fixed interest and mortgaged assets

## Trade credit
- Supplier grants credit for raw materials, inputs and spare parts
- Duration is three to six months; availability depends on nature, size, creditworthiness
- 6 features: ready, flexible, no floatation cost, no interest, promissory note, sales
- Buyer issues a promissory note; supplier can sue on default
- Seller's credit means the seller allows payment after delivery

## Public deposits
- Unsecured deposits invited from the public through a newspaper advertisement
- 6 features: unsecured, no control loss, simple, economical, limited, 6 months-3 years
- Period is six months to three years; renewal makes it medium-term
- Ceiling is 25 per cent of share capital and free reserves
- Cheaper than bank loans; pays the public more than bank deposits
- Mainly used to finance working capital needs
- Trap: depositors have no votes, so control is not diluted

## Bank and FI loans
- Commercial bank accepts deposits, grants loans and invests to earn profit
- 7 features: timely funds, secrecy, easy, flexible, short period, enquiry, restrictions
- Bank loans follow RBI rules; public deposits follow the Companies Act
- Bank loans are short and medium-term, costlier than deposits, suit all firms
- FI gives medium and long-term funds, advice, goodwill, depression cover
- FI also gives foreign currency loans and guarantees for importing machinery

## Inter-corporate deposits
- ICD is a deposit made by one company with another company
- Open to all public companies, with or without share capital
- Three types: three months 12%, six months 15%, call 10%
- Call deposit is withdrawable on one day's notice
- 6 features: secrecy, speed, no bureaucracy, short-term, high risk, costly

## Other sources of finance
- Commercial paper: unsecured promissory note, 15 days to one year, top-rated firms
- Factoring sells receivables to a factor who collects debts and may bear bad debt
- Lease financing gives the right to use an asset for periodic rent
- Customer advance is money paid by buyers before delivery of goods
- Trap: leasing gives use, not ownership, of the asset

## International sources
- GDR is issued abroad; ADR is issued in the USA, both against Indian shares
- IDR is an Indian depository receipt issued here against foreign shares
- FCCB is a foreign currency bond, borrowed fund until converted into equity
- Foreign currency loans finance imports of machinery and capital goods
- FDI buys ownership in a business; FII buys listed financial securities
- GDR, ADR and IDR are owners' fund sources; FCCB and foreign loans are borrowed

## Exam numbers and traps
- Trade credit 3 to 6 months; public deposits 6 months to 3 years
- Borrowed fund: short up to 1 year, medium 1 to 5, long 5 or more
- ICD: three months 12%, six months 15%, call 10% on one day notice
- Public deposits capped at 25 per cent of share capital and free reserves
- ₹50 crore capital plus reserves allows ₹12.5 crore of public deposits
- Trap: hybrid security is preference share, never equity share
- Trap: bank loans follow RBI; public deposits follow Companies Act
- Clues: 2 months supplier credit is trade credit; 3 years is public deposits
