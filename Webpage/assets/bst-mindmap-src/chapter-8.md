# Chapter 8 — Small Business and Enterprises

## Meaning of Entrepreneurship
- Entrepreneurship is systematic, purposeful, creative activity of identifying a need and mobilising resources
- It organises production and delivers value to customers, returns to investors and profit to self
- It accepts risks and uncertainties, so profit is never guaranteed
- Trap: entrepreneurship is planned work, not a lucky accident or guess

## Three National Needs
- Produce import substitute goods to stop the outflow of national wealth
- Produce more consumer goods and services locally to meet rising demand
- Produce goods for export to earn more and more foreign exchange
- All 3 national needs are met by more and more entrepreneurs

## Six Reasons Entrepreneurship Matters
- Life line of a nation: entrepreneurship is the yardstick of a country's development
- Provides innovation through new production methods, technology, marketing and business ideas
- Growth of the economy: entrepreneurs adapt to changes and adopt latest technology
- Increased profits by minimising cost and ensuring optimum utilisation of resources
- Employment opportunities through self-employment and jobs in successful enterprises
- Social benefits: higher standard of living, balanced regional growth, saved scarce resources

## Five Characteristics: SLI-OR
- Systematic activity: step-by-step, purposeful; skills can be learnt and developed
- Lawful and purposeful activity: runs a lawful business with the purpose of creating value
- Related to innovation: continuous search for new products, markets and supply sources
- Organisation of production: combines land, labour, capital and technology into a firm
- Risk-taking: bigger risk than a job because there is no assured payoff
- Memory hook SLI-OR: Systematic, Lawful, Innovation, Organisation, Risk

## Eight Steps of the Process
- Step 1 self-discovery: assess strengths, weaknesses, interests and work experience
- Step 2 identifying opportunities: find needs, wants and problems to solve
- Step 3 generating and evaluating ideas through discussion, brainstorming, creativity and experience
- Step 4 planning: conduct research and prepare the business plan and strategies
- Step 5 raising start-up capital using the business plan to attract venture capitalists and partners
- Step 6 start-up: launch the enterprise, add customers and adapt to change
- Step 7 growth: expand and grow the enterprise
- Step 8 harvest: sell the business, take the reward, often start anew

## Small Business: 1951 Definition
- Industries Development and Regulation Act, 1951 gave the historical definition
- Not more than 50 persons when using power and 100 when not
- Capital assets must not exceed ₹5 lakh under the 1951 definition
- Trap: 1951 is historical; today's working test is investment plus turnover

## MSME Classification
- 3 categories use investment and turnover; both conditions must be satisfied
- Micro: investment up to ₹1 crore, turnover up to ₹5 crore
- Small: investment above ₹1 crore up to ₹10 crore, turnover up to ₹50 crore
- Medium: investment above ₹10 crore up to ₹50 crore, turnover up to ₹250 crore
- Same limits apply to manufacturing and services; register on the Udyam portal
- Trap: 2025 revised the limits; write the NCERT 2020 table

## Six Roles of Small Business
- Small industries give almost 40% of gross industrial value added and 45% of exports
- Second largest employer after agriculture, using labour-intensive and less capital-intensive methods
- Balanced regional development: simple technology and local resources allow units anywhere
- Enormous variety: garments, hosiery, stationery, soaps, utensils, furniture and electronics
- Entrepreneurship platform: converts skill into business with little capital and government support
- Low cost production: local resources, low overheads and low running cost give competitive strength

## Startup India and Definition
- Startup India is a Government of India initiative announced by PM Modi on 15 August 2015
- Action plan to develop an ecosystem and nurture entrepreneurship across the country
- Startup: private limited, partnership, LLP or sole proprietor registered in India
- Must be less than 5 years old with annual turnover less than ₹25 crore
- Trap: DPIIT allows 10 years and ₹100 crore; write the NCERT figures

## Six Startup Benefits
- No inspection of startups for three years; only self-certification is needed
- April 2016 app and portal give one-day registration and single-window clearance
- Income tax exemption for first three years after inter-ministerial board certificate
- Capital gains tax exemption for first three years, conditional under Section 54GB
- 80% rebate in patent fees for a startup's patent application
- ₹10,000 crore startup fund plus ₹500 crore credit guarantee scheme

## Seven Funding Options
- Self funding: first option, where the entrepreneur uses his own money
- Crowdfunding: many people make small online pledges to a described business
- Angel investor: individual with surplus cash funding early startups; helped Google, Yahoo, Alibaba
- Venture capital: professionally managed fund investing in companies with huge potential
- Bank loans: SBI, HDFC, ICICI and Axis offer 7-8 collateral-free options
- NBFC loans: flexible terms, faster approval, less paperwork, but higher interest
- Government programmes include the ₹10,000 crore Startup Fund in the Union Budget

## Funding Differences and Traps
- Angel is a person; venture capital is a professionally managed fund
- Crowdfunding means contribution from more than one person, usually online
- NBFCs serve those without conventional banking access or favoured credit ratings
- Trap: more than one person points to crowdfunding, not venture capital

## Government Startup Schemes
- Bank of Ideas and Innovations programme boosts innovative product companies
- MUDRA extends benefits to 10 lakh small and medium enterprises
- SIDBI offers business loans to the MSME sector
- Kerala State Self Entrepreneur Development Mission and Rajasthan Startup Fest are state schemes
- Maharashtra Centre for Entrepreneurship Development is another state startup scheme

## IPR: Meaning and Categories
- IPR means legal property rights over creations of the mind
- IPR protects inventions, gives an edge over competitors and makes you a leader
- 2 categories: industrial property and copyright
- Industrial property covers patents, trademarks, industrial designs and geographical indications
- Copyright covers literary and artistic works like films, music, books and paintings

## Seven Types of IPR
- Copyright: exclusive right to copy, modify, distribute, perform and display a work
- Patent: novel, non-obvious, useful invention; monopoly on selling, using, making or importing
- Trademark: word or symbol that identifies and distinguishes a product, like Nike
- Geographical indication: product quality or reputation attributable to a definite territory
- Plant variety: grouping plants into categories based on botanical characteristics
- Design: shape, pattern, line arrangement or colour combination applied to an article
- Semiconductor layout design: layout of transistors, circuitry elements and connecting lead wires
- Memory hook C-P-T-G-P-D-S gives all seven IPR types in order

## Exam Numbers and Traps
- 40% is gross industrial value added; 45% is total exports; never swap
- 1951: 50 persons with power, 100 without power, capital assets ₹5 lakh
- MSME investment/turnover: micro 1/5, small 10/50, medium 50/250 crore
- Startup: less than 5 years old and turnover below ₹25 crore
- 80% patent rebate, ₹10,000 crore fund, ₹500 crore credit guarantee corpus
- Trap: startup forms include sole proprietor, not only companies
