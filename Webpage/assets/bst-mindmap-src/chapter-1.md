# Chapter 1 — Business, Trade and Commerce

## Business and Its Classification
- Business = Industry + Trade + Commerce, the widest term; production to consumption
- Business activities split into Industry and Commerce
- Industry converts resources into useful goods
- Trade buys and sells goods/services, linking producers to consumers
- Commerce = trade + auxiliaries; removes 6 hindrances in exchange

## Ancient Trade and Wealth
- Two main routes: Silk Route and maritime trade
- Largest economy in 1st–7th centuries CE; 1/3 to 1/4 of world wealth
- Travellers, six in number, called India Swaran Bhoomi and Swaran Deep
- Karkhanas were family-based manufacturing workshops
- Exports: spices, wheat, sugar, indigo, opium, cotton; imports: horses, silks, wine, gold
- 1 and 1000 AD: 32%; 1500, 1600, 1700 AD: 24.36%, 24.41%, 24.46%
- 1700–1750: 25% industrial output; 1850: 5–10%; 1870: 9.2%; 1900: 2% of global industrial output; 1952: 3.8%; 1973: 3.1%

## 14 Ancient Trade Centres
- Pataliputra (Patna): commercial town; exported stones
- Peshawar: exported wool, imported horses; trade with India, China and Rome
- Taxila: land route between India and Central Asia; also a university town
- Indraprastha (Delhi area): commercial junction on the royal road
- Mathura: emporium of trade
- Varanasi: gold silk cloth and sandalwood workmanship
- Mithila: sea trade with Java, Sumatra and Borneo; colonies in South China
- Ujjain: exported agate, carnelian and muslin
- Surat: emporium of western trade; its hundi was honoured in Egypt and Iran
- Kanchi (Kanchipuram): pearls, glass and rare stones; received gold and silk
- Madura (Madurai): pearl fisheries in the Gulf of Mannar; Roman traders
- Broach (Bharuch): greatest seat of commerce in western India, on the Narmada
- Kaveripatta (Kaveripatnam): trade with Malaysia, Indonesia and China; shipbuilding
- Tamralipti (Tamluk): greatest port; linked sea and land with the West and Far East

## Hundi: Indigenous Banking
- Hundi means 'to collect'; safe money transfer, written in vernacular
- 7 types: 4 Darshani and 3 Muddati
- Darshani: Dhani-jog, Sah-jog, Firman-jog, Dekhan-har
- Dhani-jog any person; Sah-jog a specific respectable person; Firman-jog payable to order; Dekhan-har bearer
- Muddati: Dhani-jog, Firman-jog, Jokhmi, all fixed-term; Jokhmi drawn against goods
- Trap: if Jokhmi goods are lost, drawer or holder bears cost

## British Era to 1991
- East India Company used Indian revenues to buy Indian raw materials
- India became raw-material exporter and manufactured-goods buyer
- After independence: centralised planning, public investment in basic industries
- Problems: lack of capital, rising population, weak finance, fiscal deficit
- 1991 reforms: stabilisation, restructuring, globalisation — S-R-G
- Today: preferred FDI destination; Make in India, Skill India, Digital India

## Meaning of Business
- Business: economic activity, production and sale of goods/services, profit motive, satisfying needs
- Word 'business' is derived from 'busy'
- Two essential parts: production or procurement, and sale or exchange
- Human activities: economic (livelihood) vs non-economic (love, sympathy, emotion)
- 3 economic activities: business, profession, employment

## 7 Characteristics of Business
- 7 characteristics: economic activity, production/procurement, sale/exchange, regular dealings, profit
- Plus uncertainty of return and element of risk complete the seven
- Regular dealings: a one-time sale is not business
- Uncertainty and risk: future profit uncertain; capital exposed to loss
- Trap: home cooking is non-economic; restaurant cooking is business

## Business vs Profession vs Employment
- Establishment: entrepreneur's decision vs professional body vs appointment letter
- Nature: goods/services vs personalised expert service vs service contract
- Qualification: none fixed vs prescribed vs employer-prescribed
- Reward: profit vs professional fee vs salary or wages
- Capital: required vs limited vs none
- Risk: high and irregular vs some vs none or little
- Transfer of interest: possible only in business, with formalities
- Code of conduct: none vs professional code vs employer's norms
- Examples: shop/factory vs doctor/lawyer/CA vs bank or government job

## Industry and Manufacturing
- Industry converts resources into useful goods; 3 types
- Primary: extractive (farming, mining) and genetic (seeds, poultry)
- Secondary: manufacturing plus construction of buildings, dams, bridges
- Four manufacturing types: analytical, synthetical, processing, assembling
- Analytical separates (oil); Synthetical combines (cement); Processing works in stages (sugar); Assembling joins (TV)
- Tertiary provides support services; overlaps with auxiliaries

## Six Hindrances of Exchange
- Commerce removes 6 hindrances: persons, place, time, risk, finance, information
- Persons removed by trade; place by transport
- Time removed by warehousing; risk by insurance
- Finance removed by banking; information by advertising
- Memory hook: Persons-Place-Time, then Risk-Finance-Information
- Trap: keep warehousing with time and insurance with risk

## Trade and Five Auxiliaries
- Internal trade is within a country: wholesale and retail
- External trade is between countries: import, export, entrepot
- Entrepot means importing goods for re-export
- Five auxiliaries: transport/communication, banking/finance, insurance, warehousing, advertising/PR
- Transport moves goods; banking funds via loans; insurance covers fire/theft loss; warehousing stores, stabilises prices
- Advertising is paid media space; PR is unpaid strategic communication

## Six Objectives of Business
- Six objectives, M-I-P-P-E-S: market standing, innovation, productivity, physical/financial resources, profit, social responsibility
- Market standing means goodwill, reputation and distinct identity
- Innovation: 2 types, products/services and skills/activities
- Productivity: output value divided by input value, a measure of efficiency
- Profit leads; five others alongside; funds expansion, shows efficiency, builds reputation
- Social responsibility: the social objective; uses resources to solve social problems

## Business Risk
- Business risk: possibility of inadequate profits or losses from uncertainties
- Speculative risk: gain or loss possible, e.g. price changes
- Pure risk: loss only, e.g. fire, theft, strike
- Nature: risk is essential, can be minimised, never removed
- Nature: risks arise from uncertainties; business nature/size decide degree; profit rewards risk
- Causes: natural flood, human strikes, economic demand/taxes, other politics/machines

## Starting a Business
- 8 factors: type, size, location, financing, physical facilities, workforce, tax planning, launching
- Entrepreneurship: setting up one's own business; entrepreneur: the job-provider; enterprise: the output unit
- Type and size depend on customers, knowledge, demand, capital
- Location affects cost; raw materials, labour, power, transport
- Financing covers fixed assets, current assets, daily expenses
- Physical facilities: machines, equipment, building; workforce: training, motivation
- Tax planning affects all functions; launching decides capital requirement, source, use
