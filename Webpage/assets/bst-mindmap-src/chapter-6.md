# Chapter 6 — Social Responsibilities of Business and Business Ethics

## Meaning of Social Responsibility
- Social responsibility: obligation of businessmen towards society
- Voluntary efforts of businessmen to contribute to social well-being
- Business cannot exist in isolation; it uses society's resources
- Business needs social objectives along with economic objectives to survive

## Kinds of Social Responsibility
- 4 kinds: economic, legal, ethical and discretionary responsibility
- Economic: produce wanted goods efficiently and earn profit for owners
- Legal: obey the laws and regulations laid down by government
- Ethical: conduct society expects but law does not compel
- Discretionary: purely voluntary acts like charity and disaster relief

## Case for Social Responsibility
- 6 reasons in the case; mnemonic S-B-P-A-S-C
- Self-interest: society can revoke resources if business ignores its needs
- Better environment: good society gives better labour and better customers
- Public image: satisfying consumers, workers, suppliers improves the business name
- Avoid government interference: costly, restricts freedom; Consumer Protection Act
- Social power: power must be balanced by responsibility or it is withdrawn
- Contribution: business created pollution, unsafe workplaces, discrimination and must solve them
- Trap: long-run benefit answer is self-interest, not social power

## Arguments against Social Responsibility
- Profit maximisation: social goals dilute the main purpose of business
- Burden on consumers: social costs are recovered through higher prices
- Lack of social skills: businessmen are not trained to solve social problems
- Lack of broad public support: society may not welcome business interference

## Duties towards Consumers
- Consumers: 7 duties, from safe goods to quick grievance handling
- Produce safe items with quality standards; ensure regular supply
- Educate customers about the use and contents of the product
- Be truthful in advertising and follow fair trade practices
- Handle complaints quickly; design products around customer needs

## Duties towards Employees
- Employees: 5 duties covering pay, safety, growth, belonging, benefits
- Provide fair compensation and benefits
- Provide good and safe working conditions
- Give opportunities for personal growth and development
- Develop belongingness; provide housing, medical, insurance, retirement benefits

## Duties towards Shareholders
- 5 duties towards owners, shareholders and investors
- Ensure safety of the investment
- Ensure fair and regular return on investment
- Give financial information; allow participation in decision-making
- Ensure appreciation of investment by proper utilisation of resources
- Trap: fair and regular return belongs to shareholders, not consumers

## Duties towards Government
- Government: 5 duties, from obeying laws to cooperating in planning
- Abide by rules, regulations and laws
- Pay taxes and duties on time
- Help solve social problems; fair dealing in external trade
- Cooperate in planning, investigation and administrative activities

## Duties towards Community
- Community: 7 duties covering environment, jobs, local area, culture
- Protect environment from all types of pollution
- Provide more employment opportunities; promote national integration
- Improve local area with drinking water, electricity and dispensaries
- Help weaker section; provide economic stability; preserve social and cultural values
- Trap: eco-friendly production is a duty to community, not consumers

## Duties towards Suppliers
- Suppliers: 4 duties built on regular payment and fair dealing
- Ensure regular payment to suppliers
- Adopt fair dealings with suppliers; do not exploit their position
- Protect and assist small-scale suppliers by placing orders with them
- Guide and assist suppliers to improve quality of their products

## Six Interest Groups
- 6 groups: consumers, employees, shareholders, government, community, suppliers
- Interests of the groups conflict; each presses its own demand
- Business reconciles conflicting interests and serves all satisfactorily
- Example: consumers want fair prices; shareholders want fair and regular returns

## Pollution and Its Types
- Pollution: injection of harmful substances into the environment
- Greatest problem from industry; emissions harm workers and local residents
- Air pollution: factory smoke and chemicals, vehicle smoke; ozone hole
- Water pollution: factories near rivers dump chemical waste into streams
- Land pollution: toxic waste kills fertility; 2 aspects: repair, stop spread
- Trap: factories and vehicles cause air pollution, not water pollution

## Need for Pollution Control
- 5 reasons for pollution control; mnemonic S-E-N-H-C
- Safety: poor visibility causes highway accidents and risky plane landings
- Economic losses: blackened buildings, dead sea life, barren land
- Natural beauty: polluted rivers smell foul; monuments get blackened
- Healthy life: air pollution hits heart and lungs; water hits liver, kidney
- Comfortable life: rivers unfit for swimming, fishing; eye irritation
- Trap: Taj Mahal example belongs only to economic losses

## Role in Environment Protection
- 7 actions by business to protect the environment
- Use eco-friendly techniques of production
- Recycle industrial waste and treat it before discharge into water or land
- Plant trees and clean rivers and ponds
- Use eco marks while producing eco-friendly products
- Abide by government bye-laws and spread public awareness
- Sustainable development: meet present needs without compromising future generations

## Business Ethics: Meaning and Importance
- Business ethics: expected conduct of businessman, what is right and wrong
- Trap: ethical behaviour is above the behaviour required by law
- Law reflects society's moral values; breach brings punishment or penalty
- Ethics breach loses society's trust; social duty is broader than legal duty
- Ethical: truthful ads, fair pricing; unethical: adulteration, black marketing
- Importance: earns society's trust, improves image and long-run goodwill
- Importance: reduces government interference and legal penalties

## Elements of Business Ethics
- 5 elements of business ethics; mnemonic T-C-C-I-M
- Top management commitment: CEO openly committed, guides all levels
- Publication of a code: written document of ethics for all employees
- Code covers health and safety at work, fair dealing in selling and marketing
- Compliance mechanism: checks decisions against ethical standards; free reporting
- Involving employees at all levels: small groups discuss ethical policies
- Measuring results: difficult; audit work against standards, review with staff
