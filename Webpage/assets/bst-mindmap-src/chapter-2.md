# Chapter 2 — Forms of Business Organisation

## Five forms and their laws
- Sole proprietorship: oldest and simplest; no separate Act governs it
- HUF business: only in India; governed by Hindu Succession Act, 1956
- Partnership: governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932
- Cooperative society: governed by the Cooperative Societies Act, 1912
- Joint stock company: governed by the Companies Act, 2013
- Memory hook: sole proprietorship → HUF → partnership → cooperative and company

## Master comparison
- Liability: Karta, sole trader and partners unlimited; co-parceners, cooperative and company limited
- Continuity: company most stable; sole proprietorship least stable
- Capital: company raises the most; sole proprietorship the least
- Control and decisions: owner alone in sole prop; Board in company
- Members: sole 1; HUF 2+; partnership 2–50; cooperative 10+ (minimum 10 adults); company private 2–200, public 7+

## Choosing the right form
- Best form = weighing merits and limitations against one's own requirements
- Capital and continuity favour the company; cost and ease favour sole prop
- Liability and risk: limited in company and cooperative; unlimited in sole prop and partnership
- Control, flexibility and legal formalities favour sole proprietorship
- Management and size: company for large scale; sole prop for small, personal contact
- Tax and support: cooperatives get low taxes, subsidies and cheap loans

## Sole proprietorship: core
- Owned, managed and controlled by one individual who takes all profits and risks
- Oldest and simplest form; no legal formalities and no separate law
- Unlimited liability: personal property may be sold to pay business debts
- One-man show; no separate entity between the firm and the owner
- Unstable continuity: death, illness or insolvency can close the business

## Sole proprietorship: merits and limits
- Merit: quick decisions, confidentiality, direct incentive and sense of accomplishment
- Merit: easy formation and closure with minimum legal formalities
- Limitation: limited resources from personal savings and borrowings
- Limitation: unlimited liability, limited life and limited managerial ability
- Best suited to retail shops, beauty parlours and medical shops

## HUF: Karta and co-parceners
- Unique form found only in India; owned by the Hindu Undivided Family
- Karta: eldest male member who manages; his decisions bind all members
- Co-parceners: members with equal ownership right over ancestral property
- Membership by birth; up to 3 successive generations can be members
- Dayabhaga: West Bengal and Assam, both male and female; Mitakshara: rest, males only

## HUF: features, merits and limits
- Formation: at least 2 members plus ancestral property; no agreement needed
- Karta unlimited liability; co-parceners limited to their share in property
- Minors can be members; business continues after the Karta's death
- Merit: effective control, continued existence, limited liability, family loyalty
- Limitation: limited resources, unlimited liability of the Karta, Karta's dominance, limited skills; declining today

## Partnership: meaning and features
- Indian Partnership Act, 1932: relation between persons agreeing to share profits
- Comes into existence through a legal agreement; registration is optional
- Minimum 2 and maximum 50 partners
- Unlimited liability, joint and several; one partner may pay the whole debt
- Mutual agency: every partner is agent and principal; decisions by mutual consent

## Partnership: six types of partners
- Active (working): contributes capital, manages actively and shares profits
- Sleeping (dormant): contributes capital, shares profits, no day-to-day role
- Secret: takes active part in management but identity unknown to the public
- Nominal: lends name and goodwill only; no capital, management or profit share
- Partner by estoppel: himself creates the impression of being a partner
- Partner by holding out: knowingly allows himself to be represented as a partner
- Trap: estoppel creates the impression; holding out does not deny; both are liable

## Partnership: types, minor, deed, registration
- Partnership at will: any partner can dissolve it by giving notice
- Particular partnership: formed for a project or period; dissolves on completion
- General: all partners unlimited; limited: at least one unlimited, registration compulsory
- Minor: admitted to benefits with consent, no management; liability limited to capital
- Minor must decide within 6 months of majority, else becomes full partner with unlimited liability
- Deed: written agreement covering capital, profit ratio, duties and dissolution
- Registration with Registrar of Firms optional; non-registration bars firm and partners from suing
- Trap: outsiders can still sue an unregistered partnership firm

## Partnership: merits and limits
- Merit: ease of formation, balanced decisions, more funds, shared risks, secrecy
- Limitation: unlimited liability; a wealthy partner may repay entire debts
- Limitation: limited resources due to the ceiling of 50 partners
- Limitation: conflicts, lack of continuity and lack of public confidence

## Cooperative society and its types
- Voluntary association of persons formed for the welfare of members
- Cooperative Societies Act, 1912; registration compulsory; minimum 10 adult members
- Separate legal entity; limited liability; one-man-one-vote; service motive
- Six types: consumer, producer, marketing, farmers, credit and housing
- Consumers' society buys in bulk; producers' society supplies inputs and sells members' goods
- Marketing pools output; farmers' jointly farm; credit lends cheaply; housing builds flats or plots
- Merit: equality, limited liability, stable existence, economy, ease of formation, government support
- Limitation: limited resources, inefficiency, no secrecy, government control, disputes

## Company: features, merits and limits
- Companies Act, 2013: association of persons with legal status independent of members
- Registration compulsory; formation complex, expensive and time-consuming
- Artificial person with separate legal entity; common seal is its signature
- Perpetual succession: members may come and go, company remains forever
- Limited liability to unpaid amount; 500 shares of ₹10 with ₹7 paid mean ₹1,500
- Merits: limited liability, transfer of shares, perpetual existence, expansion, professional management
- Limits: complex formation, no secrecy, impersonal, rules, delay, oligarchy, conflict

## Company types and privileges
- OPC: Sec 2(62), 1 member, 1–15 directors; Indian citizen resident 182 days
- OPC lock-in: no voluntary conversion for 2 years; ₹50 lakh or ₹2 crore exceptions
- Private: 2–200 members, 2 directors; restricts share transfer
- Public: minimum 7 members, no maximum, 3 directors; may invite public subscription
- Prospectus: document inviting public subscription; private needs none, public uses it
- Private privilege: starts right after incorporation; no index of members
- Trap: private needs 2 members and 2 directors; public needs 7 and 3

## Formation and company documents
- Stages: promotion → incorporation → capital subscription → commencement; stage 3 public only
- Promoter performs 6 functions: opportunity, feasibility, name approval, signatories, professionals, documents
- Incorporation documents: MOA signed by 7 public or 2 private, AOA, consent, declaration, fees
- Name approval from Registrar of Companies; 3 names in preference order
- MOA: charter with 6 clauses; defines objects; filing compulsory; hard to alter
- MOA clauses: name, registered office, objects, liability, capital, association
- AOA: internal management rules; subordinate to MOA; Table F may be adopted
- Capital clause: ₹2 crore divided into 20 lakh shares of ₹10
