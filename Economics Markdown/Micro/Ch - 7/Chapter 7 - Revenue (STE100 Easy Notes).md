## 1. What is Revenue

**Definition:** Revenue is amount received by a firm from the sale of a given quantity of a commodity in the market.

Formula:

```text
Revenue = Quantity of Product x Price
```

Point: Cost (Ch-6) is money that WENT OUT. Revenue (Ch-7) is money that CAME IN. Profit = Revenue - Cost.

Example: You sell 1000 toffees at Rs 5 each. Revenue = 1000 x 5 = Rs 5000.

CHECK: You sell 2000 teddy bears at Rs 100 each. What is Revenue? Answer: 200000.

---

## 2. TR

**Definition:** TR is total receipts from the sale of a given quantity of a commodity.

Formula:

```text
TR = Quantity x Price
```

Example: 2000 teddy bears x Rs 100 = Rs 200000. TR is 200000.

Points:

1. TR is total money from full sale.
2. TR is zero at zero Q. You sold nothing. You got nothing.
3. TR rises when you sell more at same price.

CHECK: Q is 2000. Price is 100. What is TR? Answer: 200000.

---

## 3. AR

**Definition:** AR is revenue per unit of output sold.

Formula:

```text
AR = TR / Quantity
AR = Price
```

Example: Kinder Surprise pack of 12 costs Rs 600 total. AR = 600 / 12 = Rs 50. Rs 50 is price of one pack.

Points:

1. Divide TR by Q. You get back the price.
2. So AR always equals Price.
3. AR tells you at what price each unit was sold.

Reason: TR = Q x Price. Divide both sides by Q. You get TR / Q = Price. So AR = Price.

CHECK: TR is 600. Q is 12. What is AR? Answer: 50.

---

## 4. AR Curve is Demand Curve

**Definition:** AR Curve is same as Demand Curve of the firm.

Points:

1. AR equals Price.
2. Demand curve shows relation between price and quantity demanded.
3. So AR curve shows the same relation.
4. AR curve slopes downward when price falls with more sales.
5. AR curve is flat when price stays constant.

Point: Demand curve and AR curve are two names for one curve.

CHECK: AR equals what? Answer: Price. So AR curve equals which curve? Answer: Demand curve.

---

## 5. MR

**Definition:** MR is additional revenue generated from the sale of one more unit of output.

Formulas:

```text
MR = TRn - TRn-1
MR = Change in TR / Change in Q
```

Where:

- MRn is revenue from nth unit
- TRn is total revenue from n units
- TRn-1 is total revenue from n-1 units
- n is number of units sold

Example: 4 iPhones give Rs 6 lakh. 5 iPhones give Rs 7.5 lakh. MR of 5th iPhone = 7.5 - 6 = Rs 1.5 lakh.

Points:

1. MR is extra money from one extra unit.
2. MR can be positive, zero, or negative.
3. MR is positive when TR rises.
4. MR is zero when TR is maximum.
5. MR is negative when TR falls.

CHECK: TR from 4 units is 6 lakh. TR from 5 units is 7.5 lakh. What is MR? Answer: 1.5 lakh.

## 7. Two Market Situations

### 7.1 Perfect Competition - Price Remains Constant

Points:

1. Industry fixes the price. Firm accepts it.
2. Firm is price taker.
3. Any quantity can be sold at that price.
4. Example: A wheat farmer sells at market price. He cannot charge more.

### 7.2 Imperfect Competition - Price Falls with Output

Points:

1. Firm follows its own pricing policy.
2. Firm is price maker.
3. To sell more, firm must lower the price.
4. Example: Airtel and Jio give cheaper rates on longer plans. 3-month plan costs more per month than 12-month plan.
5. Example: 1 pizza costs Rs 750. 2 pizzas cost Rs 500 each. 3 pizzas cost Rs 250 each. Buy more. Price falls.

| Basis | Perfect Competition | Imperfect Competition |
|-------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Price | Constant at all Q | Falls as Q rises |
| Firm role | Price taker | Price maker |
| To sell more | Sell any Q at same price | Lower the price |
| AR and MR | AR = MR = Price | MR is less than AR |

CHECK: Firm lowers price to sell more. Which market is this? Answer: Imperfect competition.

## 8. AR and MR When Price is Constant

This happens under Perfect Competition. Price = AR = MR.

Schedule with Price 5:

| Q | Price / AR | TR = Q x P | MR = TRn - TRn-1 |
|---|-----------|------------|------------------|
| 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 | 5 |
| 3 | 5 | 15 | 5 |
| 4 | 5 | 20 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 25 | 5 |

Read: Price stays 5. AR stays 5. MR stays 5.

Rules:

1. AR equals Price. MR equals Price. All three are one line.
2. Demand curve is horizontal. It is perfectly elastic. Ed = infinity.
3. AR and MR never change with Q. Values stay 5, 5, 5, 5, 5.
4. Each extra unit adds same money. So MR equals AR.
5. Firm earns maximum profit where MR = MC. Here MR equals Price.

CHECK: Price is 5. Q rises from 1 to 5. What are AR and MR? Answer: Both stay 5.

## 9. TR and MR When Price is Constant

Same schedule with Price 5:

| Q | Price / AR | TR = Q x P | MR = TRn - TRn-1 |
|---|-----------|------------|------------------|
| 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 | 5 |
| 3 | 5 | 15 | 5 |
| 4 | 5 | 20 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 25 | 5 |

Rules:

1. TR curve is a straight line with constant slope.
2. MR is constant. Each unit adds same Rs 5 to TR.
3. TR rises by same amount each time. Values go 5, 10, 15, 20, 25.
4. MR equals slope of TR curve. Slope is constant here.
5. TR is zero at zero Q. Nothing sold means nothing earned.
6. TR keeps rising positively. No diminishing here because MR never falls.

CHECK: Q is 4. Price is 5. What is TR? Answer: 20. What is MR? Answer: 5.

## 10. TR and Price Line

**Definition:** TR equals sum of all MR values. TR = Sum of MR.

Points:

1. When price is constant, Price = AR = MR.
2. So price line is the same as MR curve.
3. Area under MR curve (price line) equals TR.
4. TR at OQ output = OP x OQ = area of rectangle under price line.

```text
Price / AR
   |
 P +------------R     <- Price line = AR = MR
   |            |
   |            |
   |____________|____
   O     Q      Units Sold
```

Read: Height is OP (price). Width is OQ (quantity). Area OP x OQ is TR.

CHECK: Price is 5. Q is 4. Area under price line is what? Answer: 5 x 4 = 20 = TR.

## 11. AR and MR When Price Falls

This happens under Imperfect Competition. Firm lowers price to sell more.

Schedule with falling price:

| Q | Price / AR | TR = Q x P | MR = TRn - TRn-1 |
|---|-----------|------------|------------------|
| 1 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| 2 | 9 | 18 | 8 |
| 3 | 8 | 24 | 6 |
| 4 | 7 | 28 | 4 |
| 5 | 6 | 30 | 2 |
| 6 | 5 | 30 | 0 |
| 7 | 4 | 28 | -2 |

Read: Price falls from 10 to 4. MR falls from 10 to -2. At Q 7, MR is -2.

Rules:

1. AR curve slopes downward. Firm must lower price to sell more units.
2. MR is always less than AR. Lowering price cuts revenue from old units too.
3. MR falls faster than AR. Price cut hits all units sold.
4. MR can become negative. At Q 7, MR is -2.
5. AR shows price. MR shows change in TR from one more unit.
6. Firm earns maximum profit where MR = MC, even though MR is less than AR.

CHECK: At Q 7, AR is 4. What is MR? Answer: -2. Is MR less than AR? Answer: Yes.

## 12. General Relation Between AR and MR

Use these rules when nothing is said about price. They work in all markets.

Rules:

1. When MR is more than AR, AR rises.
2. When MR equals AR, AR is maximum and constant.
3. When MR is less than AR, AR falls.

| Condition | Effect on AR |
|-----------|--------------|
| MR is more than AR | AR rises |
| MR equals AR | AR is maximum and constant |
| MR is less than AR | AR falls |

Point: Same logic as AP and MP in Ch-5. Marginal pulls average toward itself.

CHECK: MR is 8. AR is 5. Does AR rise or fall? Answer: AR rises.

## 13. TR and MR When Price Falls

Use the falling-price schedule from Section 11.

Rules:

1. When MR is positive, TR rises. TR goes 10, 18, 24, 28, 30.
2. When MR is zero, TR is maximum. At Q 6, MR is 0. TR is 30.
3. When MR is negative, TR falls. At Q 7, MR is -2. TR falls to 28.

| MR Behaviour | TR Behaviour |
|--------------|--------------|
| MR is positive | TR rises |
| MR is zero | TR is maximum |
| MR is negative | TR falls |

Point: Watch MR sign. It tells you where TR is going.

CHECK: MR is zero. What is TR? Answer: TR is maximum.

## 14. Zero and Negative MR

Points:

1. MR can be zero. Then TR stops rising. TR is at maximum.
2. MR can be negative. Then TR starts falling.
3. TR = Sum of MR. Add all MR values to get TR.
4. But TC = Sum of MC is WRONG. It leaves TFC out. Correct is TC = Sum of MC + TFC.
5. TVC = Sum of MC. Only TVC comes from adding MC.
6. MR = AR = Price line holds only under perfect competition.

CHECK: Which formula is wrong: TR = Sum of MR or TC = Sum of MC? Answer: TC = Sum of MC is wrong. Correct is TC = Sum of MC + TFC.

## 18. Exam Questions With Answers

**Q1. Define Revenue. Give formula.**
A: Revenue is amount received from sale of a given quantity. Revenue = Quantity x Price.

**Q2. Define TR. Give formula.**
A: TR is total receipts from sale of a given quantity. TR = Quantity x Price.

**Q3. Define AR. Give formula.**
A: AR is revenue per unit of output sold. AR = TR / Q = Price.

**Q4. Define MR. Give formula.**
A: MR is additional revenue from sale of one more unit. MR = TRn - TRn-1.

**Q5. Why is AR equal to Price?**
A: TR = Q x Price. Divide both sides by Q. TR / Q = Price. So AR = Price.

**Q6. Why is AR curve called demand curve?**
A: AR equals price. AR curve shows relation between price and quantity. So it has all features of demand curve.

**Q7. "Average revenue curve represents law of demand." Comment.**
A: AR = TR / Q = Price. So AR curve shows relation between price and quantity demanded. It slopes downward because firm must lower price to sell more. So AR curve represents law of demand.

**Q8. Which condition is true under imperfect competition: AR = MR, AR < MR, or AR > MR?**
A: AR > MR. MR is less than AR under imperfect competition.

**Q9. What is the relation between AR and MR under perfect competition?**
A: AR = MR = Price. Both are equal to price. Curve is flat horizontal line.

**Q10. What happens to TR when MR falls?**
A: TR rises slowly at first. Then TR reaches maximum. Then TR falls.

**Q11. What happens to TR when MR rises?**
A: TR rises fast. TR rises at an increasing rate.

**Q12. State general relation between AR and MR.**
A: MR more than AR means AR rises. MR equals AR means AR is maximum. MR less than AR means AR falls.

**Q13. State relation between TR and MR when price falls.**
A: MR positive means TR rises. MR zero means TR is maximum. MR negative means TR falls.

**Q14. TR is 600. Q is 12. Find AR.**
A: AR = 600 / 12 = 50.

**Q15. TR from 4 units is 6 lakh. TR from 5 units is 7.5 lakh. Find MR.**
A: MR = 7.5 - 6 = 1.5 lakh.

**Q16. Price is 5. Q is 4. Find TR and MR under perfect competition.**
A: TR = 5 x 4 = 20. MR = 5.

**Q17. At Q 7, AR is 4 and MR is -2. What does this show?**
A: MR is less than AR. MR can be negative when price falls.

**Q18. What does area under price line show?**
A: It shows TR. TR = Price x Quantity = area of rectangle under price line.

**Q19. Why can MR become negative?**
A: To sell more, firm lowers price. Loss on old units is more than gain on new unit. So TR falls. MR becomes negative.

**Q20. Firm lowers price to sell more. Perfect or imperfect competition?**
A: Imperfect competition. Firm is price maker.

**Q21. TR = Sum of MR is correct. Then TC = Sum of MC is also correct. True or false?**
A: False. TC = Sum of MC leaves TFC out. Correct is TC = Sum of MC + TFC.

**Q22. What is Ed of demand curve under perfect competition?**
A: Ed = infinity. Demand curve is perfectly elastic horizontal line.

**Q23. Where does a firm earn maximum profit?**
A: Where MR = MC. Under perfect competition this equals Price.

**Q24. TR is 60 at 5 units. TR is 56 at 4 units. Find MR of 5th unit.**
A: MR = 60 - 56 = 4.

**Q25. AR values are 25, 23, 21 for Q 1, 2, 3. Find TR and MR at Q 3.**
A: TR = 21 x 3 = 63. MR = 63 - 46 = 17.
