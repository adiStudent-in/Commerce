# Class 11 Economics – Chapter 7: Revenue

## 1. Introduction — The Big Idea

### The Big Idea:

> Ab tak humne **producer** (banane wala) ke **cost** wale angle par padha — **Cost** (Ch-6) = production mein **kya kharcha** hua. Ab producer ka **doosra angle** — **REVENUE** (Aagman)!
>
> **Revenue** = production karke bechne se **jo paisa aata hai** (money received from sale).

### Simple Flow (From Lecture):

```
COST (Ch-6)          REVENUE (Ch-7)
paisa GAYA  ──────────────►  paisa AAYA
(input kharcha)            (sale se receipts)
                                    ↓
                      PROFIT = Revenue − Cost
```

> **Key Point:** Cost ne bataya — output banane mein **kitna paisa laga**. Revenue batata hai — woh output bechne se **kitna paisa mila**. Dono milke **profit** decide karte hain!

---

## 2. Meaning of Revenue

### Definition:

> **Revenue** refers to the **amount received by a firm from the sale of a given quantity of a commodity** in the market.

### Formula (From Lecture):

```
Revenue = Quantity of Product × Price
```

> **Lecture Line:** "Jitna maal becha × jitne ka becha = revenue! Jaise 1000 toffees × ₹5 = ₹5000 revenue."

---

## 3. Total Revenue (TR)

### Definition:

> **Total Revenue (TR)** refers to the **total receipts from the sale of a given quantity** of a commodity.

### Formula:

```
TR = Quantity × Price
```

### Example (From Lecture):

> 2,000 TEDDY BEARS ko ₹100 per bear pe becha:
>
> ```
> TR = 2,000 × ₹100 = ₹2,00,000
> ```

> **Lecture Line:** "2000 teddy × ₹100 = 2 lakh — yeh poori sale ka total paisa = Total Revenue."

---

## 4. Average Revenue (AR)

### Definition:

> **Average Revenue (AR)** refers to the **revenue per unit of output sold**.

### Formula:

```
AR = TR ÷ Quantity = Price
```

### Example (From Lecture):

> Kinder Surprise ka **pack of 12** = ₹600 total:
>
> ```
> AR = 600 ÷ 12 = ₹50  (= Price of one pack)
> ```

### Most Important Point:

> **AR = Price** — kyunki total revenue ko quantity se divide karte hi **per-unit price** milta hai. Isliye:
>
> **AR Curve aur Demand Curve ek hi hain!** (AR curve = demand curve of the firm)

> **Lecture Line:** "AR hi toh Price hai! Isliye jab hum demand curve kheenchte hain, wahi AR curve hota hai."

---

## 5. Marginal Revenue (MR)

### Definition:

> **Marginal Revenue (MR)** is the **additional revenue generated from the sale of an additional unit** of output.

### Formula:

```
MR = TRn − TRn−1

Where:
MRn   = Marginal Revenue of nth unit
TRn   = Total Revenue from n units
TRn−1 = Total Revenue from (n−1) units
n     = Number of units sold
```

### One More Way to Calculate MR (From Lecture):

```
          Change in Total Revenue     ΔTR
MR = ───────────────────────────  =  ────
        Change in number of units      ΔQ
```

### Example (From Lecture):

> 4 iPhones beche = ₹6 lakh; 5 iPhones beche = ₹7.5 lakh:
>
> ```
> MR = 7.5 − 6 = ₹1.5 lakh   (5th unit se extra paisa aaya)
> ```

---

## 6. TR, AR & MR — Quick Summary

| Concept | Kya hai? | Formula |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **Total Revenue (TR)** | Total sale se total receipts | TR = Quantity × Price |
| **Average Revenue (AR)** | Revenue per unit; **= Price** | AR = TR ÷ Quantity |
| **Marginal Revenue (MR)** | Additional unit se extra revenue | MR = TRn − TRn−1 = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ |

### Schedule (From Lecture — Price Falling Case):

| Units Sold (Q) | Price ₹ (P) | TR (₹) = Q × P | AR (₹) = TR ÷ Q = P | MR (₹) = TRn − TRn−1 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 (= 10 − 0) |
| 2 | 9 | 18 | 9 | 8 (= 18 − 10) |
| 3 | 8 | 24 | 8 | 6 (= 24 − 18) |
| 4 | 7 | 28 | 7 | 4 (= 28 − 24) |
| 5 | 6 | 30 | 6 | 2 (= 30 − 28) |
| 6 | 5 | 30 | 5 | 0 (= 30 − 30) |
| 7 | 4 | 28 | 4 | −2 (= 28 − 30) |

> **Observe:** Jab price girti hai (imperfect competition), **MR = Price se KAM** hota hai, aur kabhi **negative** bhi ho jaata hai! (7th unit pe MR = −2)

## 7. Two Market Situations — Price Constant vs Price Falling

### A) Perfect Competition → Price Remains Constant

> Jab market **Perfect Competition** mein ho, **price constant** rehti hai. Firm ko **industry ke fixed price** par hi bechna padta hai — firm **price taker** hoti hai. **Koi bhi quantity** us price par bechi ja sakti hai.

### B) Imperfect Competition → Price Falls with Output

> Jab market **Imperfect Competition** mein ho, **price falls with rise in output**. Firm **apni pricing policy** khud banati hai, par sales badhani ho toh **price kam karna padta hai**.

> **Lecture Examples:** Airtel/Jio recharge plans (3 month, 6 month, 12 month — long plan sasta), Pizza (1 pizza ₹750, 2 pizza ₹500 each, 3 pizza ₹250 each) — **zyada kharido, price giregi!**

### Quick Comparison:

| Basis | Perfect Competition | Imperfect Competition |
|-------|--------------------|----------------------|
| **Price** | **Constant** (fixed by industry) | **Falls** as output rises |
| **Firm ka role** | **Price taker** | **Price maker** (own pricing policy) |
| **Sales badhane ke liye** | Koi bhi quantity ussi price par | **Price kam karna** padta hai |
| **AR & MR** | **AR = MR = Price** | MR < AR (dono girti hain) |

---

## 8. Relationship Between AR & MR (When Price Remains Constant)

> Yeh situation **Perfect Competition** mein hoti hai — **Price = AR = MR**.

### Schedule (From Lecture):

| Units Sold (Q) | Price / AR (₹) | TR (₹) | MR (₹) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 | 5 |
| 3 | 5 | 15 | 5 |
| 4 | 5 | 20 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 25 | 5 |

### Properties (From Lecture):

> 1. **Equal to Price:** Dono AR aur MR price ke equal hain — kyunki perfect competition mein firm **price taker** hai.
> 2. **Horizontal Demand Curve:** Firm ki demand curve **perfectly elastic** hoti hai — **AR = MR = Price** aur **Ed = ∞**. (Firm koi bhi quantity usi price par bech sakti hai.)
> 3. **Constant AR & MR:** Price constant hai → AR aur MR output ke saath **change nahi hote** (5, 5, 5, 5...).
> 4. **AR = MR:** Har additional unit se **utna hi** extra revenue (MR) aata hai jitna per-unit revenue (AR) — kyunki price fixed hai.
> 5. **Profit Maximization:** Firm wahan produce karti hai jahan **MR = MC** — jo perfect competition mein **price ke equal** hota hai.

> **Lecture Line:** "Perfect competition mein price = AR = MR — teeno ek hi line pe. Demand curve bilkul **flat (horizontal)** — jitna chaaho bech lo, price wahi!"

---

## 9. Relationship Between TR & MR (When Price Remains Constant)

### Schedule (From Lecture):

| Units Sold (Q) | Price / AR (₹) | TR (₹) = Q × P | MR (₹) = TRn − TRn−1 |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 | 5 |
| 3 | 5 | 15 | 5 |
| 4 | 5 | 20 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 25 | 5 |

### Properties (From Lecture):

> 1. **Linear Relationship:** TR curve ek **straight line** hoti hai with **constant slope** — kyunki price fixed hai.
> 2. **MR is Constant:** MR constant = price; har additional unit TR mein **utna hi** jodta hai.
> 3. **TR Increases Proportionally:** Firm price taker hai → har unit ke saath TR **same amount** se badhti hai (5, 10, 15, 20, 25).
> 4. **MR = Slope of TR:** TR curve ki slope hi **MR** hoti hai — jo perfect competition mein **constant** hai.
> 5. **TR at Zero Output:** Output zero → TR bhi **zero** (kuch becha hi nahi).
> 6. **Positive TR Growth:** MR constant hai → TR bina diminishing ke **positively** badhti rehti hai.

## 10. Relationship Between TR & Price Line

### Key Idea (From Lecture):

> Jab price har level of output par **constant** ho:
>
> ```
> Price = AR = MR
> ```
>
> Isliye **price line hi MR curve hai**! Aur:
>
> ```
> TR = ΣMR
> ```
>
> Toh **MR curve (price line) ke neeche ka area = TR**.

### Diagram Explanation:

> TR at OQ level of output = **OP × OQ** = **Area under price line** (rectangle OP R Q).

```
Price/AR
   │
 P ───────────── R        ← Price line = AR = MR
   │             │
   │             │
   │_____________│______
   O      Q      Units Sold
```

> - Height = **OP** (price)
> - Width = **OQ** (quantity)
> - Area (OP × OQ) = **TR** = area under price line / MR curve

> **Lecture Line:** "TR = ΣMR — saari MR jod do, TR mil jayega. Isliye price line ke neeche ka poora area = TR!"

| Situation | AR & MR | TR |

| **AR = Price** | AR curve = Demand curve | — |
| **Area under MR curve / price line** | — | = **TR** |

## 12. Key Terms Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Revenue** | Amount received by a firm from sale of a given quantity of a commodity |
| **Total Revenue (TR)** | Total receipts from sale = Quantity × Price |
| **Average Revenue (AR)** | Revenue per unit = TR ÷ Quantity = **Price** |
| **Marginal Revenue (MR)** | Addition to revenue from one extra unit = TRn − TRn−1 = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ |
| **AR Curve = Demand Curve** | AR = Price, isliye demand curve hi AR curve hai |
| **Perfect Competition** | Price constant; firm = price taker; AR = MR = Price; demand perfectly elastic (Ed = ∞) |
| **Imperfect Competition** | Price falls with output; firm = price maker; MR < AR |
| **TR = ΣMR** | Total revenue = sum of all marginal revenues = area under MR curve (price line) |
| **Profit Maximization** | MR = MC (= Price in perfect competition) |

> - **TR = Q × P**
> - **AR = TR ÷ Q = Price**
> - **MR = TRn − TRn−1**
>
> **Memory Tricks:**
> - **AR = Price** → AR curve = Demand curve (same cheez!)
> - **Perfect competition:** Price = AR = MR → horizontal line, Ed = ∞
> - **Imperfect competition:** Price girti hai → MR < AR, MR negative bhi ho sakta hai
> - **TR = ΣMR** → area under MR curve (price line) = TR
> - **TR curve:** perfect competition mein straight line (constant slope = MR)
