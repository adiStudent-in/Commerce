## 1. Meaning of Supply

**Definition:** Supply refers to quantity of a commodity that a firm is willing and able to offer for sale at a given price during a given period of time.

Points:

1. Supply is a desired quantity.
2. Willingness must be backed by ability to supply.
3. Supply is always expressed with reference to price.
4. Supply is always with respect to a period of time.
5. Supply is a flow concept as it relates to a period of time.
6. Example: Seller has 50 tonnes sugar in godown. He is willing to sell 30 tonnes at Rs 37 per kg. Then supply is 30 tonnes. Stock is 50 tonnes.

CHECK: Supply is 100 mobiles or 100 mobiles at Diwali at Rs 20000? Answer: Second, price and time needed.

## 2. Demand Versus Supply

| Basis | Demand | Supply |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Meaning | Quantity consumer is willing and able to purchase | Quantity firm is willing and able to offer for sale |
| Person | Consumer | Producer or firm |
| Reference | At a given price during a given period | At a given price during a given period |
| Example | Wants to buy at given price | Wants to sell at given price |

CHECK: Willing and able to purchase is demand or supply? Answer: Demand.

## 3. Supply and Stock

**Definition:** Stock refers to total quantity of a particular commodity that is available with the firm at a particular point of time.

**Definition:** Stock is not a flow concept as it relates to a particular point of time.

| S.No. | Supply | Stock |
|-------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Supply refers to quantity a firm is willing and able to offer for sale at a given price during a given period of time | Stock refers to total quantity available with the firm at a particular point of time |
| 2 | Supply is a flow concept as it relates to a period of time | Stock relates to a particular point of time |
| 3 | Supply is always related to price as it changes with change in price | Stock indicates a fixed quantity, not related to price |
| 4 | Stock can never be less than the supply | Supply is 30 tonnes out of 50 tonnes stock |
| 5 | Example: 30 tonnes sugar offered at Rs 37 per kg | Example: 50 tonnes sugar in godown |

CHECK: Godown has 500 mobiles. Willing to sell 100 at Diwali. Supply and stock? Answer: Supply 100, stock 500.

## 4. Individual Supply and Market Supply

**Definition:** Individual supply is quantity one producer is willing to sell. Market supply is quantity all producers are willing to sell.

| Basis | Individual Supply | Market Supply |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| Meaning | Supply of one firm | Sum of supplies of all firms in market |
| Firms | One shop, for example one mobile shop | Many shops, for example chips, dal, rice, oil, sugar sellers |
| Symbol | SA, SB for firm A, B | SM = SA + SB + ... |

CHECK: Kirana shop supply versus all shops supply? Answer: First is individual, second is market.

## 5. Determinants of Individual Supply - 6 Factors

**Definition:** Determinants of supply are factors that influence quantity supplied of a commodity.

### 5.1 Price of the Given Commodity

1. Price and supply are directly related.
2. As price increases, quantity supplied rises.
3. As price falls, quantity supplied falls.
4. Reason: Higher price means greater chance of profit.

### 5.2 Prices of Other Goods

1. Rise in price of other goods makes them more profitable.
2. Firm shifts limited resources to other goods.
3. Supply of given commodity falls.
4. Example: Rise in price of wheat induces farmer to use land for wheat in place of rice. Supply of rice falls.

### 5.3 Prices of Factors of Production or Inputs

1. If input prices rise, cost of production rises.
2. Profit margin falls. Seller reduces supply.
3. If input prices fall, cost falls and profit rises.
4. Supply increases due to fall in cost.

### 5.4 State of Technology

1. Advanced technology reduces cost of production.
2. Profit margin rises. Seller increases supply.
3. Outdated technology raises cost.
4. Supply decreases with outdated technology.

### 5.5 Government Policy of Taxes and Subsidies

1. Increase in taxes raises cost of production.
2. Supply reduces due to lower profit.
3. Tax concessions and subsidies make supply more profitable.
4. Supply increases with subsidies.

### 5.6 Goals or Objectives of the Firm

1. Generally supply rises only at higher prices for profit maximisation.
2. Some firms supply more even without maximum profit.
3. Objective is to capture large market.
4. Objective is to enhance status and prestige.

CHECK: Wage Rs 21000, production cost Rs 1000. Effect? Answer: Input cost effect, supply falls.

---

## 6. Determinants of Market Supply - 3 Extra Factors

Market supply has all individual determinants plus three more.

### 6.1 Number of Firms in the Market

1. More firms means more market supply.
2. Firms leave industry due to losses.
3. Then market supply decreases.

### 6.2 Future Expectation Regarding Price

1. If sellers expect price rise in future, current supply decreases.
2. They want to sell later at higher price.
3. If sellers fear price fall, present supply increases.
4. They want to avoid future losses.

### 6.3 Means of Transportation and Communication

1. Proper roads and communication maintain supply.
2. Improvement helps move goods fast.
3. Poor means reduce supply.

CHECK: New firms enter market. Market supply? Answer: Increases.

## 7. Supply Function

**Definition:** Supply function shows functional relationship between quantity supplied and factors influencing it.

Formula:

```text
Individual Supply: Sx = f(Px, Po, Pf, St, T, G)
Market Supply: Sx = f(Px, Po, Pf, St, T, G, F, M, N)
```

Where:

1. Sx = Supply of given commodity x.
2. Px = Price of given commodity x.
3. Po = Price of other goods.
4. Pf = Prices of factors of production.
5. St = State of technology.
6. T = Taxation policy.
7. G = Goals of the firm.
8. F = Future expectation regarding Px.
9. M = Means of transportation and communication.
10. N = Number of firms.

CHECK: Which three are only in market function? Answer: F, M, N.

## 9. Market Supply Schedule

**Definition:** Market supply schedule is a tabular statement showing various quantities all producers are willing to sell at various prices during a given period of time.

**Definition:** Market supply is obtained by horizontal summation of individual supplies. SM = SA + SB + ...

| Price (Rs) | Firm A SA (units) | Firm B SB (units) | Market Supply SM = SA + SB (units) |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------|------------------------------------|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 | 15 |
| 3 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
| 4 | 15 | 25 | 40 |
| 5 | 20 | 35 | 55 |
| 6 | 25 | 40 | 65 |

CHECK: At Rs 5, market supply? Answer: 20 + 35 = 55 units.

## 10. Supply Curve

**Definition:** Supply curve is a graphical representation of supply schedule.

### 10.1 Individual Supply Curve

1. Plots price on Y-axis and quantity on X-axis.
2. Curve SS slopes upward from left to right.
3. Points: price 1 to 6, quantity 0 to 25.

### 10.2 Market Supply Curve

1. Market curve SM is obtained by horizontal summation of SA and SB.
2. SM is flatter than SA and SB.
3. Flatter means more quantity responds to price.
4. Points: price 1 to 6, quantity 0 to 65.

### 10.3 Slope of Supply Curve

Formula:

```text
Slope of Supply Curve = Change in Price (ΔP) / Change in Quantity (ΔQ)
```

CHECK: Market curve is flatter or steeper than individual? Answer: Flatter.

## 11. Law of Supply

**Definition:** Law of supply states direct relationship between price and quantity supplied, keeping other factors constant.

Points:

1. Price rises, supply rises.
2. Price falls, supply falls.
3. Only price changes. Other factors remain constant.
4. It is a qualitative statement. It tells direction, not magnitude.
5. It does not show proportional change.
6. It is one-sided. It tells effect of price on supply, not effect of supply on price.

### 11.1 Assumptions - Ceteris Paribus

1. Price of other goods is constant.
2. No change in state of technology.
3. Prices of factors of production remain same.
4. No change in taxation policy.
5. Goals of producer remain same.

CHECK: Law of supply states direct or inverse relation? Answer: Direct.

---

## 12. Reasons for Law of Supply

### 12.1 Profit Motive

1. Basic aim is maximum profit.
2. Price rises, cost same, profit rises.
3. Producers increase production.
4. Price falls, profit falls, supply falls.

### 12.2 Change in Number of Firms

1. Rise in price attracts new producers.
2. They enter to earn higher profit.
3. Market supply rises.
4. Price falls, some firms stop production. Supply falls.

### 12.3 Change in Stock

1. Price rises, sellers release more from stock.
2. Price falls, sellers hold stock.
3. They keep inventory for future price rise.
4. So supply follows price.

CHECK: Why does higher price raise supply? Answer: Higher profit motive.

---

## 13. Exceptions to Law of Supply

1. Agricultural goods: Production depends on climate. Low output due to weather cannot rise even at higher price.
2. Perishable goods: Vegetables and fruits cannot be held long. Sellers sell more even if price is falling.
3. Rare articles: Painting of Mona Lisa cannot be increased even if price rises.
4. Backward countries: Shortage of resources stops rise in supply even if price rises.

CHECK: Roses on Feb 14 or strawberries in winter? Answer: Supply limited, law may not apply.

## 14. Change in Quantity Supplied Versus Change in Supply

**Definition:** Change in quantity supplied happens due to change in own price. Change in supply happens due to factors other than price.

| Basis | Change in Quantity Supplied | Change in Supply |
|-------|-----------------------------|------------------|
| Meaning | Supply changes due to change in own price | Supply changes due to factors other than price |
| Curve | Movement along same curve | Shift to new curve |
| Reason | Increase or decrease in price of given commodity | Change in inputs, taxes, technology |
| Example | Supply of Close-Up changes due to price of Close-Up | Supply of Close-Up changes due to technology or tax |

CHECK: Close-Up supply changes due to tax. Which change? Answer: Change in supply.

## 15. Movement Along Supply Curve - Expansion and Contraction

**Definition:** When quantity supplied changes due to change in own price, keeping other factors constant, it is movement along same supply curve.

### 15.1 Expansion in Supply

**Definition:** Expansion refers to rise in quantity supplied due to increase in price, other factors constant. It leads to upward movement from A to B. It is also known as extension in supply.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 10 | 100 |
| 12 | 150 |

### 15.2 Contraction in Supply

**Definition:** Contraction refers to fall in quantity supplied due to decrease in price, other factors constant. It leads to downward movement from A to B.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 12 | 150 |
| 10 | 100 |

CHECK: Price 20 to 25, quantity 100 to 150. Which movement? Answer: Expansion, upward.

## 16. Shift in Supply Curve - Increase and Decrease

**Definition:** When supply changes due to any factor other than own price, it is shift in supply curve. Own price remains constant.

### 16.1 Increase in Supply

**Definition:** Increase refers to rise in supply due to favourable other factors at same price. Curve shifts right from SS to S1S1.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 10 | 100 |
| 10 | 150 |

Reason: Decrease in input price, decrease in tax, better technology.

### 16.2 Decrease in Supply

**Definition:** Decrease refers to fall in supply due to unfavourable other factors at same price. Curve shifts left from SS to S1S1.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 12 | 150 |
| 12 | 100 |

Reason: Increase in input price, increase in tax, outdated technology.

CHECK: Same price Rs 20, supply 100 to 200. Which shift? Answer: Increase, rightward.

## 17. Comparison Tables - Do Not Confuse

### 17.1 Movement Versus Shift

| Basis | Movement Along Supply Curve | Shift in Supply Curve |
|-------|-----------------------------|-----------------------|
| Meaning | Quantity changes due to price, other factors constant | Supply changes due to other factors, price constant |
| Curve | Same curve, upward expansion or downward contraction | Rightward increase or leftward decrease |
| Reason | Increase or decrease in price of given commodity | Change in inputs, taxes, technology and others |

### 17.2 Expansion Versus Increase

| Basis | Expansion in Supply | Increase in Supply |
|-------|---------------------|--------------------|
| Meaning | Quantity rises due to rise in price | Supply rises due to other factors at same price |
| Table | Price 10 to 12, supply 100 to 150 | Price 10 to 10, supply 100 to 150 |
| Curve | Upward movement along same curve | Rightward shift of curve |
| Reason | Rise in price of given commodity | Fall in input price, fall in tax, better technology |

### 17.3 Contraction Versus Decrease

| Basis | Contraction in Supply | Decrease in Supply |
|-------|-----------------------|--------------------|
| Meaning | Quantity falls due to fall in price | Supply falls due to other factors at same price |
| Table | Price 12 to 10, supply 150 to 100 | Price 12 to 12, supply 150 to 100 |
| Curve | Downward movement along same curve | Leftward shift of curve |
| Reason | Fall in price of given commodity | Rise in input price, rise in tax, outdated technology |

CHECK: Price same, supply falls. Contraction or decrease? Answer: Decrease.

## 18. Effect of Other Factors on Supply Curve

### 18.1 Rightward Shift - 8 Causes

Supply curve shifts right due to:

1. Decrease in price of other goods.
2. Decrease in price of factors of production.
3. Advanced and improved technology.
4. Favourable tax policy with decrease in taxes.
5. Goal of sales maximisation.
6. Increase in number of firms.
7. Expectation of fall in prices in future.
8. Improvement in transport and communication.

### 18.2 Leftward Shift - 8 Causes

Supply curve shifts left due to:

1. Increase in price of other goods.
2. Increase in price of factors of production.
3. Complex and outdated technology.
4. Unfavourable tax policy with increase in taxes.
5. Goal of profit maximisation.
6. Decrease in number of firms.
7. Expectation of rise in prices in future.
8. Poor transport and communication.

### 18.3 Four Key Effects

1. Other goods price rises: SS shifts left. Other goods price falls: SS shifts right.
2. Input price rises: SS shifts left. Input price falls: SS shifts right.
3. Technology upgrade: SS shifts right. Technology degradation: SS shifts left.
4. Tax increase: SS shifts left. Tax decrease: SS shifts right.

CHECK: Input cost Rs 500 to Rs 300. Shift? Answer: Rightward, supply rises.

## 19. Price Elasticity of Supply - Meaning and Formula

**Definition:** Price elasticity of supply refers to degree of responsiveness of supply with reference to change in price.

**Definition:** Elasticity is a unit free measure. It is a pure number. Price elasticity of supply is positive due to direct relation.

Formula - Percentage Method:

```text
Es = Percentage Change in Quantity Supplied / Percentage Change in Price
Es = (ΔQ / ΔP) x (P / Q)
```

Where:

1. Q = Initial quantity supplied.
2. ΔQ = Change in quantity, new minus old.
3. P = Initial price.
4. ΔP = Change in price, new minus old.

CHECK: Es positive or negative? Answer: Positive.

---

## 20. Numericals - Percentage Method

### 20.1 Q 400 to 300, P 60 to 58 - Es 7.5

Given Q = 400, Q1 = 300, P = 60, P1 = 58. ΔQ = 300 - 400 = -100. ΔP = 58 - 60 = -2.

```text
Es = (-100 / -2) x (60 / 400) = 50 x 0.15 = 7.5
```

### 20.2 Q 100 to 50, P 15 to 10 - Es 1.5

Given Q = 100, Q1 = 50, P = 15, P1 = 10. ΔQ = -50. ΔP = -5.

```text
Es = (-50 / -5) x (15 / 100) = 10 x 0.15 = 1.5
```

### 20.3 Q 200 to 275, P 8 to 10 - Es 1.5

Given Q = 200, P = 8, ΔP = 2, Es = 1.5. Find ΔQ: 1.5 = (ΔQ / 2) x (8 / 200). ΔQ = 75. New quantity = 200 + 75 = 275.

### 20.4 Price Falls 10%, Supply Falls 20% - Es 2

```text
Es = 20% / 10% = 2
```

### 20.5 TR Rs 400 at P Rs 2, P Rises to Rs 3, Q 300 - Es 1

Q = 400 / 2 = 200. Q1 = 300. ΔQ = 100. P = 2, P1 = 3. ΔP = 1.

```text
Es = (100 / 1) x (2 / 200) = 100 x 0.01 = 1
```

### 20.6 X and Y Equal Es - Y Falls 10%

Supply X rises 400 to 500. ΔQ = 100. % change Q = 100 / 400 x 100 = 25%. Price rises 20%.

```text
Es of X = 25% / 20% = 1.25
Es of Y = Es of X = 1.25
% fall in Y = 1.25 x 8% = 10%
```

### 20.7 Supply Function Sx = 6Px + 3, Supply 27 - Price Rs 4

27 = 6Px + 3. 6Px = 24. Px = 4.

CHECK: Sx = 6x2 + 3? Answer: 15, not 27. So price is 4.

## 21. Kinds of Price Elasticity of Supply

### 21.1 Perfectly Inelastic Supply - Es = 0

**Definition:** Supply does not change with change in price.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 20 | 20 |
| 30 | 20 |
| 40 | 20 |

Curve is vertical parallel to Y-axis.

### 21.2 Less Elastic Supply - Es < 1

**Definition:** Percentage change in quantity is less than percentage change in price.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 10 | 100 |
| 15 | 120 |

Price rises 50%. Supply rises 20%. 20% < 50%.

### 21.3 Unitary Elastic Supply - Es = 1

**Definition:** Percentage change in quantity equals percentage change in price.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 10 | 100 |
| 15 | 150 |

Price rises 50%. Supply rises 50%.

### 21.4 Highly Elastic Supply - Es > 1

**Definition:** Percentage change in quantity is more than percentage change in price.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 10 | 100 |
| 15 | 200 |

Price rises 50%. Supply rises 100%. 100% > 50%.

### 21.5 Perfectly Elastic Supply - Es = Infinity

**Definition:** Infinite supply at same price. Curve is horizontal parallel to X-axis.

| Price (Rs) | Supply (units) |
|------------|----------------|
| 30 | 100 |
| 30 | 200 |
| 30 | 300 |

CHECK: Price 10 to 15, supply 100 to 120. Which kind? Answer: Less elastic, Es < 1.

---

## 22. Important Observations on Elasticity Curves

1. All supply curves passing through origin are unitary elastic, whatever the angle.
2. Flatter curve is more elastic at point of intersection.
3. Steeper curve is less elastic.
4. Example: Flatter SS is more elastic than steeper S1S1.

| Type | Value | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Perfectly Inelastic | Es = 0 | Same supply at all prices |
| Less Elastic | Es < 1 | % rise in supply less than % rise in price |
| Unitary Elastic | Es = 1 | % rise in supply equal to % rise in price |
| Highly Elastic | Es > 1 | % rise in supply more than % rise in price |
| Perfectly Elastic | Es = Infinity | Infinite supply at same price |

CHECK: Curve through origin. Es? Answer: 1.

## 23. Time Period and Supply

### 23.1 Market Period - Very Short Period

1. Supply cannot be changed with demand.
2. Supply is perfectly inelastic.
3. Es = 0. Vertical curve.

### 23.2 Short Period

1. Output can be changed by changing only variable factors.
2. Supply is highly elastic in short period.

### 23.3 Long Period

1. Output can be changed by changing all factors.
2. Supply is highly elastic.
3. Longer the period, more elastic the supply.

CHECK: As price falls supply changes in same direction. Which relation? Answer: Law of supply.
