# Poem 1 - A Photograph - Practice Questions with Answers
**Poet:** Shirley Toulson · **Book:** Hornbill (Class 11) Poem · **Source:** Strictly from Rewritten Notes · Zero hallucination

> How schools ask poems (CBSE SQP 2026-27): Extract quote + (a) meaning of phrase, (b) poetic device, (c) contrast/idea. Devices here: alliteration (terribly transient), imagery (sea vs feet), contrast (permanence vs transience), repetition (silence silences). Exact phrases: cardboard, terribly transient feet, laboured ease, wry, silence silences, Betty/Dolly, before I was born.
> How to use: Step 1 MCQs cold → Step 2 Extract aloud → Step 3 SA without seeing → Step 4 LA 120-150 words → Step 5 VSA + hook.

## Step 1 — Confidence MCQs (1 mark each)
**1.** The cardboard shows —
(a) city crowd (b) two girl cousins paddling, each holding one of mother’s hands, she the big girl some twelve years or so (c) poet alone (d) wedding
**Answer:** (b) Betty and Dolly paddling with twelve-year-old mother as big girl.

**2.** “Cardboard” means —
(a) box (b) stiff backing of old photo frame, flimsy yet preserving moment before poet was born (c) ticket (d) book
**Answer:** (b) stiff backing of photo frame; poet knows mother’s childhood only through picture.

**3.** All three stood still to smile through hair at —
(a) mother (b) uncle with camera (c) poet (d) stranger
**Answer:** (b) uncle with camera; sweet face of mother, that was before I was born.

**4.** “Terribly transient feet” means —
(a) strong feet (b) mortal short-lived feet washed by sea, soon gone, unlike changeless sea (c) shoes (d) dancing feet
**Answer:** (b) human feet are short-lived and quickly erased; terribly adds dread-pity.

**5.** Device in “terribly transient” is —
(a) simile (b) alliteration of ‘t’ sound with imagery of permanence vs transience (c) pun (d) irony only
**Answer:** (b) alliteration — repetition of initial ‘t’; also contrast between sea and man.

**6.** The sea contrast is —
(a) sea dirty (b) sea appears to have changed less while human feet are transient; nature permanent, man momentary (c) sea dried (d) sea noisy
**Answer:** (b) changeless sea vs brief human life; nature outlives visitors.

**7.** Some twenty-thirty years later mother would —
(a) cry (b) laugh at snapshot saying See Betty and Dolly, look how they dressed us for beach (c) burn it (d) sell it
**Answer:** (b) laugh teasing dress; sea holiday was her past, laughter became poet’s past.

**8.** Both pasts were wry with laboured ease of loss means —
(a) easy joy (b) smile twisted by sorrow; grief accepted with effort wearing half-smile (c) anger (d) fear
**Answer:** (b) wry — twisted between smile and sorrow; laboured ease — accepted grief with effort.

**9.** “She’s been dead nearly as many years as that girl lived” means —
(a) girl twelve, mother dead almost twelve years (b) mother dead nearly as many years as twelve-year-old lived (c) mother alive (d) photo new
**Answer:** (b) death equals childhood span; loss total and measured against girlhood.

**10.** “Its silence silences” means —
(a) photo quiet (b) death’s silence leaves nothing to say, silencing poet completely; speech fails before total loss (c) sea calm (d) girls whisper
**Answer:** (b) about death nothing to say; silence of death silences poet.

## Step 2 — School Extracts (RTC)
### RTC 1
> “The cardboard shows me how it was / When the two girl cousins went paddling, / Each one holding one of my mother’s hands, / And she the big girl — some twelve years or so.”

**Q1. Who are the three girls and what are they doing? (1)**
**Answer:** Mother about twelve as big girl with cousins Betty and Dolly, paddling — standing in shallow beach water holding hands.

**Q2. What does “cardboard” denote? (1)**
**Answer:** Stiff backing of old photo frame — flimsy temporary picture preserving a moment before poet was born.

**Q3. Why is “before I was born” significant? (1)**
**Answer:** Poet can know mother’s childhood only through photo; sweet face loved at distance, introducing memory vs loss theme.

### RTC 2
> “A sweet face, My mother’s, that was before I was born. And the sea, which appears to have changed less, Washed their terribly transient feet.”

**Q1. What does “terribly transient feet” signify? (1)**
**Answer:** Feet in sea belong to mortal humans — short-lived, soon erased — unlike changeless sea; terribly adds dread-pity at mortality.

**Q2. Which poetic device in “terribly transient”? (1)**
**Answer:** Alliteration — repetition of initial ‘t’ sound; also imagery and contrast of permanence vs transience.

**Q3. How does sea contrast with human life? (1)**
**Answer:** Sea hardly changed over decades; girls who paddled grew, aged and died. Nature permanent, man momentary, preparing later loss where holiday fades and silence silences.

## Step 3 — Short Answers (40-50 words, 3 marks)
**Q1. Why is “cardboard” stressed?**
**Answer:** Cardboard is flimsy backing, yet holds irrecoverable time — twelve-year-old mother with Betty-Dolly paddling. Poet knows childhood only through picture before he was born. It fixes what life lets go: man transient, memory briefly preserved.

**Q2. Explain “sweet face before I was born” and uncle camera.**
**Answer:** Trio stood still smiling through wind-blown hair at uncle’s camera. Poet lovingly calls mother’s face sweet, from time before his existence. Loved at distance, it shows photography freezing youth against later ageing and death.

**Q3. Explain “terribly transient feet” with device.**
**Answer:** Sea appears changed less, but feet it washed are terribly transient — short-lived, quickly gone. Alliteration of ‘t’ stresses dread. Imagery-contrast: permanent nature vs momentary man, heart of poem, foreshadowing mother’s death and silence.

**Q4. What happened twenty-thirty years later?**
**Answer:** Grown mother laughed at snapshot — See Betty and Dolly, look how they dressed us for beach. Sea holiday became her past; her laughter became poet’s past. Same photo moved from record to play, memory layering before loss.

**Q5. What is “wry” and “laboured ease of loss”?**
**Answer:** Both holiday and laughter are wry — twisted between smile and sorrow. Laboured ease is grief accepted with effort wearing half-smile. Event becomes story, story becomes echo; joy thins as time passes, preparing final silence.

**Q6. Explain “silence silences” ending.**
**Answer:** Mother dead nearly as many years as girl lived. About death nothing to say; its silence silences poet completely. Language fails before total loss. Presence became recollection, recollection became absence — ultimate grief beyond words, only acceptance.

## Step 4 — Long Answers (120-150 words, 6 marks)
**Q1. How does the poem move from memory to speechlessness?**
**Answer:** It opens with cardboard record — Betty-Dolly paddling holding mother’s hands, still smiling through hair at uncle, sweet face before poet’s birth loved at distance. Sea unchanged washing terribly transient feet states philosophy: permanence vs brief life. Middle turns to mother’s laughter twenty-thirty years later — dress joke, holiday her past, laughter his past, both wry with laboured ease. Same photo moves from record to play to relic. End removes last layer: dead nearly as many years as girl lived, nothing to say, its silence silences. Language fails before total loss. Dress-laughter-silence trace thinning joy into quiet, proving objects change value with time and ultimate grief has no words, only acceptance.

**Q2. Contrast permanence and transience in the poem.**
**Answer:** Shirley Toulson sets changeless sea against terribly transient feet. Cardboard photo is flimsy, yet preserves twelve-year-old mother with Betty and Dolly before poet was born. Sea appears to have changed less over decades, while feet it washed belonged to mortals quickly gone. Alliteration in terribly transient and imagery stress dread-pity. Twenty-thirty years later, mother laughs at dress and holiday — her past becomes his past of laughter, both wry with laboured ease of loss. Then mother dies nearly as many years as girl lived. Holiday fades, laughter fades, only sea remains. Final repetition — silence silences — shows nature outlives man. Human joy is momentary; memory-art briefly holds it, but death leaves speechless acceptance.

**Q3. Trace the three phases in three stanzas.**
**Answer:** Stanza one captures moment — cardboard shows Betty-Dolly paddling, each holding mother’s hands, big girl twelve, smiling through hair at uncle’s camera, sweet face before poet was born. Stanza two shifts twenty-thirty years later — adult mother laughs See Betty and Dolly, how they dressed us for beach. Sea holiday is her past, her laughter is his past, both wry with laboured ease of loss — smile twisted by sorrow. Stanza three is now — mother dead nearly as many years as girl lived, nothing to say, its silence silences. Presence becomes recollection, recollection becomes absence. Photo moves from record to play to relic, proving time thins joy and ultimate loss ends language in quiet acceptance.
