## Step 1 — Confidence MCQs (1 mark each)
**1.** The author had known his grandmother as old for —
(a) ten years (b) twenty years (c) five years (d) twelve years
**Answer:** (b) twenty years — she stayed exactly the same age for two decades, short, fat and slightly bent.

**2.** Her face is described as —
(a) round and glowing (b) a criss-cross of wrinkles running from everywhere to everywhere (c) long and pale only (d) scarred
**Answer:** (b) a criss-cross of wrinkles running from everywhere to everywhere; with silver locks scattered untidily over her pale puckered face.

**3.** She was “always beautiful” because —
(a) she wore jewellery (b) she hobbled in spotless white, one hand on waist, other telling beads of rosary, lips in inaudible prayer, like winter landscape breathing peace and contentment (c) she sang film songs (d) she was tall
**Answer:** (b) spotless white, rosary in hand, lips in inaudible prayer — like the winter landscape in the mountains, an expanse of pure white serenity.

**4.** The grandfather’s portrait above the mantelpiece showed —
(a) young man in suit (b) big turban, loose clothes, long white beard, looking at least a hundred years old (c) soldier (d) child
**Answer:** (b) big turban, loose clothes, long white beard — a man who could only have lots of grandchildren.

**5.** In the village, the school was —
(a) English school by bus (b) attached to the temple, children in verandah singing alphabet and morning prayer with priest while grandmother read scriptures inside (c) convent (d) night school
**Answer:** (b) attached to the temple; on return they fed stale chapattis to village dogs at the temple door.

**6.** The turning-point came when —
(a) he failed (b) parents comfortably settled in city sent for them; though sharing same room, the bond started loosening (c) grandmother married (d) he left for England
**Answer:** (b) parents sent for them to the city; everything began quietly changing.

**7.** In the city she was distressed because —
(a) bus was late (b) he told English words and Western science — law of gravity, Archimedes’ Principle, world being round — which she could not help with, did not believe, and there was no teaching about God and scriptures (c) dogs barked (d) food was costly
**Answer:** (b) English/Western science she could not help with or believe, with no teaching about God and scriptures.

**8.** Music lessons shocked her because to her music —
(a) was too hard (b) had lewd associations; monopoly of harlots and beggars, not meant for gentlefolk (c) was illegal (d) needed fees
**Answer:** (b) monopoly of harlots and beggars, not for gentlefolk; she said nothing but rarely talked after.

**9.** At university, her routine was —
(a) travelling (b) accepted seclusion with resignation, spinning-wheel sunrise to sunset reciting prayers, only afternoon feeding sparrows — happiest half-hour with bedlam of chirrupings, birds on legs, shoulders, head (c) teaching (d) shopping
**Answer:** (b) spinning-wheel from sunrise to sunset with prayers; afternoon sparrows the happiest half-hour.

**10.** She died —
(a) in hospital crying (b) declaring end near despite mild fever, refusing to waste time talking, praying till lips stopped moving and rosary fell from lifeless fingers; pallor spread, body covered with red shroud; thousands of sparrows sat silent, ignored crumbs later swept to dustbin (c) abroad (d) singing
**Answer:** (b) peacefully praying, rosary falling from lifeless fingers; mourned by silent sparrows who ignored bread.

## Step 2 — School Extracts (RTC) — attempt like board

### RTC 1
> “My grandmother and I were good friends. My parents left me with her when they went to live in the city and we were constantly together. She used to wake me up in the morning and get me ready for school.”

**Q1. Where did the parents go, leaving him with grandmother? (1)**
**Answer:** They went to live in the city, leaving him in the village in her care, when they were constantly together.

**Q2. Describe the village morning routine that shows their bond. (1)**
**Answer:** She woke, bathed and dressed him singing morning prayer in monotonous sing-song, gave wooden slate washed and plastered with yellow chalk, tiny earthen ink-pot and red pen, breakfast of thick stale chapatti with butter and sugar, and walked him to the temple school.

**Q3. What happened at the temple school and on the way back? (1)**
**Answer:** Children sat in rows in the verandah singing alphabet and morning prayer under the priest while grandmother sat inside reading scriptures; on return village dogs met them growling for stale chapattis she threw.

### RTC 2
> “She was like the winter landscape in the mountains, an expanse of pure white serenity breathing peace and contentment.”

**Q1. Whose description is this and what is her appearance? (1)**
**Answer:** The grandmother of Khushwant Singh — short, fat, slightly bent, face criss-cross of wrinkles, in spotless white, one hand on waist, other telling beads, silver locks on puckered face, lips in inaudible prayer.

**Q2. Explain the winter landscape comparison. (1)**
**Answer:** Like snow-covered mountains, she was pure white, calm and serene, spreading peace and contentment around her through prayer and stillness.

**Q3. Why was it hard to believe she had been young and pretty? (1)**
**Answer:** For twenty years she looked unchanged and old; thought of youth and husband felt almost revolting — she could never have been pretty, but was always beautiful.

## Step 3 — Short Answers (40-50 words, 3 marks)
**Q1. Describe the grandmother’s portrait.**
**Answer:** For twenty years she looked old — short, fat, slightly bent, criss-cross wrinkles. In spotless white, one hand on waist, other telling rosary beads, silver locks on puckered face, lips in inaudible prayer, she was always beautiful like winter serenity breathing peace.

**Q2. How was village life the phase of togetherness?**
**Answer:** Parents left him with her in the village. She woke, bathed and dressed him with sing-song prayer, packed slate, ink-pot and red pen, fed chapatti-butter-sugar, walked to temple school, read scriptures inside, and fed village dogs together on return.

**Q3. Why did the city English school disturb her? Give three reasons.**
**Answer:** He went by motor bus, so she could not accompany him. She could not help with English words, gravity, Archimedes’ Principle and round world, nor believe them. She was distressed there was no teaching about God and scriptures, plus music lessons.

**Q4. How did she accept university seclusion?**
**Answer:** When he got his own room, the common link snapped. She accepted seclusion with resignation, spinning sunrise to sunset reciting prayers, rarely talking. Only in the afternoon she fed sparrows — happiest half-hour amid bedlam of chirrupings, never shooing them.

**Q5. Describe the goodbye abroad and homecoming.**
**Answer:** Going abroad for five years, she was not sentimental — silent prayer, beads, kiss on forehead cherished as last contact. After five years she clasped him reciting prayers, fed sparrows longer with frivolous rebukes, then sang warriors’ homecoming on dilapidated drum, not praying first time.

**Q6. How did she die and how did sparrows mourn?**
**Answer:** With mild fever she declared her end near for omitting prayer, refused to waste time talking, died praying till rosary fell, pallor spread, red shroud covered. Thousands of sparrows sat silent, ignored mother’s crumbs, flew quietly after corpse; crumbs were swept to dustbin.

## Step 4 — Long Answers (120-150 words, 6 marks)
**Q1. Describe the changing relationship. Did feelings change?**
**Answer:** In village childhood author and grandmother were best friends — morning sing-song prayer, slate plastered with yellow chalk, temple school with scriptures, and feeding village dogs. The turning-point came when parents called them to the city. He went by motor bus to English school; she could not help with gravity, Archimedes’ Principle or round world, was pained at no teaching of God and scriptures, and silently disapproved music as monopoly of harlots and beggars. At university with his own room, the link snapped; she resigned to spinning-wheel and sparrows. Yet feelings never changed. She kissed his forehead silently at his five-year abroad departure, welcomed him not a day older, and celebrated with warriors’ songs on a dilapidated drum. Next morning she died praying, mourned by silent sparrows. Distance changed routine, not love.

**Q2. Sketch the grandmother as a woman of faith and dignity.**
**Answer:** Khushwant Singh’s grandmother was old for twenty years — short, fat, bent, wrinkled, yet always beautiful in spotless white with rosary and inaudible prayer, like winter serenity. Faith ordered her day: village temple scriptures, city distress at godless English education, and university spinning-wheel from sunrise to sunset reciting prayers. She bore change with resignation, not complaint — silent disapproval of music, quiet acceptance of seclusion, joy only in feeding sparrows. Her end was chosen and calm: after first evening without prayer singing warriors’ homecoming, she declared with mild fever her end near, refused to waste time talking, and died praying till rosary fell from lifeless fingers. Covered in red shroud, she was mourned by thousands of silent sparrows. Prayer gave her life dignity and death peace.

**Q3. How does the sparrows’ mourning form the fitting end?**
**Answer:** The sparrows were her city family after village dogs disappeared. Every afternoon she broke bread in the verandah while hundreds collected in bedlam of chirrupings, perching on legs, shoulders and head while she smiled and never shooed them — her happiest half-hour. When she died peacefully and was laid in red shroud, that evening thousands sat scattered on floor and verandah in blaze of golden light with no chirruping. The mother broke bread as she did, but they took no notice. When the corpse was carried off, they flew quietly; next morning crumbs lay untouched in the dustbin. Their silence proves the bond was personal, not food-based. Nature itself observed mourning, honouring a prayerful life and closing the portrait with peace.

**Answer:** Accepted seclusion with resignation and prayer.
