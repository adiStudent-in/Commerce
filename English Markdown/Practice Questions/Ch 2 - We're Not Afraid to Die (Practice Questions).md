# Ch 2 - We're Not Afraid to Die - Practice Questions with Answers
**Authors:** Gordon Cook / Alan East · **Book:** Hornbill (Class 11) Prose · **Source:** Strictly from Rewritten Notes · Zero hallucination

> How schools ask this chapter: Prose Extract RTC 1x3, Short Answer 40-50 words (3 marks), Long Answer 120-150 words (6 marks), MCQs 1 mark.
> How to use: Step 1 MCQs cold → Step 2 Extract aloud → Step 3 SA without seeing → Step 4 LA 120-150 words → Step 5 VSA + hook.

## Step 1 — Confidence MCQs (1 mark each)
**1.** In July 1976 narrator set sail from Plymouth to —
(a) fish (b) duplicate Captain James Cook’s round-world voyage 200 years earlier (c) migrate (d) race
**Answer:** (b) duplicate Cook’s voyage; 37-year-old businessman with wife Mary dreamt longest time, 16 years honing seafaring skills.

**2.** The boat was —
(a) Titanic (b) Wavewalker, 23-metre 30-ton wooden-hulled beauty, professionally built, months fitted and tested in roughest weather (c) raft (d) steamer
**Answer:** (b) Wavewalker — wooden-hulled beauty for planned three-year 105,000-km journey down Africa to Cape Town.

**3.** At Cape Town they took —
(a) tourists (b) Larry Vigil American and Herb Seigler Swiss to tackle southern Indian Ocean, world’s roughest seas (c) navy (d) none
**Answer:** (b) Larry American and Herb Swiss; then gales for weeks, waves 15 metres high as main mast.

**4.** On Dec 25, 3500 km east of Cape Town they —
(a) sank (b) enjoyed wonderful Christmas with tree despite atrocious weather; New Year no improvement, then worse (c) returned (d) landed
**Answer:** (b) merry Christmas despite gales; weather had to change, did for worse.

**5.** To slow for gigantic waves captain —
(a) slept (b) dropped storm jib, lashed mooring rope loop stern, double-lashed, life-raft drill, lifelines, oilskins-jackets (c) jumped (d) shouted
**Answer:** (b) full storm precautions, then ominous silence, dark sky, roar, vertical wave twice others.

**6.** When giant wave struck, captain —
(a) unhurt (b) head smashed wheel, overboard, lifeline jerked taut, rag-doll round boom, left ribs cracked, blood-broken teeth, yet wheeled stern (c) slept (d) radioed only
**Answer:** (b) accepted death peaceful, popped out, Wavewalker near capsizing then upright, he hung on.

**7.** Below, flooding showed —
(a) dry (b) Mary screamed sinking, decks smashed full water, starboard bulged, timbers crazy angles, clothes-charts-toys sloshing; canvas-hatch covers, pumps blocked-shorted-overboard, spare under chartroom floor (c) fire (d) feast
**Answer:** (b) broken starboard, hand pumps blocked, electric shorted, spares overboard with jib-dinghies-anchor; third pump saved.

**8.** Sue hid injury because —
(a) feared scolding (b) “I didn’t want to worry you when you were trying to save us all” — bump, two black eyes, deep cut later six ops for clot (c) forgot (d) slept
**Answer:** (b) selfless maturity beyond seven years; Mayday calls unanswered in remote corner.

**9.** On Jan 4 hope was —
(a) Australia (b) Ile Amsterdam French base pinpricks few hundred km east; after 36 hours pumping, storm jib east, first meal corned beef-crackers in two days (c) Africa (d) England
**Answer:** (b) ribs smashed to keel held only by cupboards; never Australia, only pinpricks.

**10.** They sighted island Jan 6 by —
(a) luck only (b) sextant maths with lost main compass, spare uncorrected, 150,000 sq km for 65-km island, steered 185 degrees; Sue “as big as battleship”; 28 inhabitants cheered (c) helicopter (d) phone
**Answer:** (b) Jon’s hug best daddy-captain, Sue’s announcement, stark volcanic most-beautiful island; night anchored offshore.

## Step 2 — School Extracts (RTC)
### RTC 1
> “In July 1976, my wife Mary, son Jonathan, 6, daughter Suzanne, 7, and I set sail from Plymouth, England, to duplicate the round-the-world voyage made 200 years earlier by Captain James Cook.”

**Q1. What was purpose of voyage? (1)**
**Answer:** To duplicate Cook’s round-world voyage in Wavewalker on planned three-year 105,000-km journey down Africa to Cape Town and east.

**Q2. Name the children. (1)**
**Answer:** Jonathan six and Suzanne seven — calm beyond years, sustaining morale with courage.

**Q3. Name boat and its preparation. (1)**
**Answer:** Wavewalker — 23-metre 30-ton wooden-hulled beauty, professionally built, months fitted, tested in roughest weather after 16 years honing skills.

### RTC 2
> “But, Daddy, we aren’t afraid of dying if we can all be together — you and Mummy, Sue and I.”

**Q1. Who says this, when? (1)**
**Answer:** Six-year-old Jonathan on Jan 5 dawn when desperate, wind 40 knots, parents holding hands feeling end near.

**Q2. What did captain feel and resolve? (1)**
**Answer:** Speechless, he left determined to fight sea with everything he had; hove-to with port hull to waves using sea anchor.

**Q3. What is sea anchor here? (1)**
**Answer:** Improvised drag of heavy nylon rope and two 22-litre paraffin barrels to shield weakened starboard side.

## Step 3 — Short Answers (40-50 words, 3 marks)
**Q1. How did captain protect ship in rough weather?**
**Answer:** Dropped storm jib, lashed mooring rope loop stern, double-lashed everything, life-raft drill, lifelines, oilskins-jackets. Later hove-to with undamaged port hull to waves using sea anchor — nylon rope plus two 22-litre paraffin barrels.

**Q2. How did they fight flooding?**
**Answer:** Stretched canvas, fixed waterproof hatch covers over gaping holes. Hand pumps blocked, electric shorted, spares overboard with jib-dinghies-anchor. Rigged third electric pump under chartroom floor; pumped continuously 36 hours in cold with unanswered Maydays.

**Q3. Why had Sue not mentioned injuries?**
**Answer:** She had bump, swelling, two enormous black eyes and deep cut needing later six minor ops for clot. She said, “I didn’t want to worry you when trying to save us all” — selfless maturity beyond seven years.

**Q4. Describe mental state Jan 4 vs Jan 5.**
**Answer:** Jan 4 exhausted-relieved after 36 hours, first meal corned beef-crackers in two days, hope for Ile Amsterdam. Jan 5 respite gone, 40 knots desperate; Jon asked are we dying; parents holding hands feeling end, yet captain resolved to fight.

**Q5. How do adults vs children face danger?**
**Answer:** Adults feared through work — pumping, repairing, calculating, Mary screaming-steering, captain navigating without conviction. Children calm-innocent: Sue hid pain, Jon unafraid if together, hugged best daddy-captain. Work versus wisdom sustained all.

**Q6. How was Ile Amsterdam found?**
**Answer:** Jan 6 sextant maths with lost main compass, spare uncorrected, in 150,000 sq km for 65-km island. Steered 185 degrees, promised land by 5 p.m. without conviction. Sue announced battleship-big island; bleak volcanic yet most beautiful; 28 cheered ashore.

## Step 4 — Long Answers (120-150 words, 6 marks)
**Q1. Describe hazard and escape.**
**Answer:** After pleasant Africa-Cape Town leg and Christmas 3500 km east, Jan 2 gigantic waves hit. Ominous silence, then vertical wave twice others smashed decks, starboard bulged, ribs to keel, clothes-charts-toys sloshing. Captain ribs-teeth injured yet wheeled stern. Night-day battle followed: pumps blocked-shorted-overboard, canvas-hatch repair, third pump under chartroom floor, Mayday unanswered in remote corner. Sue bore bump-black eyes-cut silently; Jon’s “not afraid if together” hardened resolve. Inspection showed never Australia; charts gave Ile Amsterdam pinpricks few hundred km east. After 36 hours pumping, storm jib east, sea-anchor night Jan 5, sextant maths with imperfect compass Jan 6 steering 185, children’s announcement sighted stark volcanic most-beautiful island. Preparation, presence of mind, cheerful optimism under direst stress and family unity defeated certain death.

**Q2. How do courage and optimism endure direst stress?**
**Answer:** The Wavewalker crew met a vertical wave, cracked ribs, smashed hull and 36-hour pumping with unanswered Maydays, yet no panic. Captain lined stern, stretched canvas, calculated sextant courses without conviction, and hove-to with nylon-paraffin sea anchor. Mary screamed yet steered crucial hours; Larry Vigil and Herb Seigler stayed cheerful and optimistic under direst stress, pumping like madmen. Children gave moral fuel: Sue hid head injury needing six ops saying she did not want to worry, while six-year-old Jon declared they were not afraid if together and called father best daddy-captain. Hope of Ile Amsterdam pinpricks kept every stroke going. Optimism literally kept boat afloat, proving calm work, unity and never-say-die spirit turn nightmare into landfall cheered by 28 inhabitants.

**Q3. Why do people undertake risky voyages despite dangers?**
**Answer:** In July 1976 a 37-year-old businessman with Mary, Jonathan six and Suzanne seven left Plymouth to duplicate Captain Cook’s 200-year-old round-world voyage after 16 years honing seafaring skills. They chose specially built Wavewalker, tested in roughest weather, took Larry and Herb for southern Indian Ocean, drilled lifelines and rafts, and accepted 15-metre waves and atrocious Christmas weather. People risk for thrill, lifelong dream of sailing Cook’s wake, testing endurance, and satisfaction of conquering nature. Risks are reduced by preparation, skill and teamwork. Even when vertical wave smashed ribs to keel and Maydays failed, calculation, repair and Jon’s togetherness carried them to Ile Amsterdam. Adventure fulfils human urge to explore, proving careful daring plus family courage can face death without fear.
