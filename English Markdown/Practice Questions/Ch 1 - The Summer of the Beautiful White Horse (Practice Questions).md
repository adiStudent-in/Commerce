# Ch 1 - The Summer of the Beautiful White Horse - Practice Questions with Answers
**Author:** William Saroyan · **Book:** Snapshots (Class 11) · **Source:** Strictly from Rewritten Notes · Zero hallucination

> How schools ask: Snapshots Extract RTC 1x4, Short 40-50 words (3 marks), Long 120-150 words (6 marks), MCQ 1 mark. SQP pattern pages 13-15 + test_ch1-3.

## Step 1 — 10 Confidence MCQs

**1.** Narrator and time —
(a) Aram, nine, good old days full of magnificence, delightful mysterious dream, woken 4 am
(b) Adult Aram in city (c) Mourad aged 13 (d) John Byro aged 50
**Answer:** (a) — Aram stuck head out window, rubbed eyes; Mourad on beautiful white horse inviting ride.

**2.** Tribe fame —
(a) Wealth (b) Horsemanship (c) Honesty for eleven centuries, proud first, honest next, right-wrong, none steal
(d) Fighting
**Answer:** (c) — Garoghlanians in most amazing comical poverty, once wealthiest, none take advantage let alone steal.

**3.** Aram's justification for riding —
(a) Farmer gifted (b) Stealing horse for ride not like stealing money; becomes stealing only if offered to sell, which never
(c) Finders keepers (d) Paid $60
**Answer:** (b) — Child logic; knew they would never sell, so consoled himself it was borrowing.

**4.** Crazy streak before Mourad —
(a) Zorab (b) Uncle Khosrove — enormous, black hair, largest moustache in San Joaquin Valley, furious irritable impatient, roar It is no harm; pay no attention to it
(c) John Byro (d) Dikran Halabian
**Answer:** (b) — Even when son Arak ran 8 blocks to barber shouting house on fire, roared Enough, it is no harm.

**5.** Mourad's father —
(a) Khosrove (b) Zorab — practical and nothing else; flesh father but not spirit father
(c) Byro (d) Fetvajian
**Answer:** (b) — Explains Mourad natural descendant of Khosrove's crazy streak, not Zorab's practicality.

**6.** The ride cry —
(a) Vazire, run! Armenian for run; horse reared, snorted, fury of speed, crossed irrigation ditch, returned 5 mins dripping wet, loveliest thing
(b) Stop! (c) Slow! (d) Sell!
**Answer:** (a) — Mourad claimed I have a way with horse, simple honest understanding; Aram will know when thirteen.

**7.** Aram's fall + hiding —
(a) Fell in street (b) Rearing horse into Dikran Halabian's vineyard, leaped 7 vines, threw him; half hour to bring back; hid in Fetvajian's deserted vineyard barn with oats and dry alfalfa
(c) Sold oats (d) Returned same day
**Answer:** (b) — Mourad: I'm not worried about you, we've got to get horse; lied riding started only that morning.

**8.** John Byro visit —
(a) Armenian farmer (b) Assyrian farmer who learnt Armenian loneliness; horse stolen last month; walked 10 miles paining leg; surrey useless; cost $60
(c) Rich owner (d) Policeman
**Answer:** (b) — Khosrove over coffee-cigarettes roared What loss of horse? Haven't we all lost homeland? I spit on money, slammed screen door.

**9.** Robin + deadline —
(a) Shot robin (b) Mourad under peach tree repairing hurt wing of young robin; snapped Are you inviting Garoghlanian to steal? Horse must go back to true owner in six months; rode 2 weeks every morning
(c) Kept forever (d) Sold robin
**Answer:** (b) — Aram begged to keep till learned; every solo ride horse leaped vines-trees and threw him.

**10.** Meeting Byro on road —
(a) Ran away (b) Mourad: Let me do talking, way with farmers; named horse My Heart; Byro: Tooth for tooth, twin of my horse, fame honesty known, suspicious man believes eyes instead of heart, Good day young friends
(c) Paid fine (d) Fought
**Answer:** (b) — Next dawn returned to Byro's barn, dogs followed silently; afternoon: stronger ever, better-tempered, thank God; Khosrove: Quiet, returned, pay no attention.

## Step 2 — 2 School Extracts (RTC 1x4)

> Extract-1: "One day back there in the good old days when I was nine and the world was full of every imaginable kind of magnificence, and life was still a delightful and mysterious dream, my cousin Mourad came... at four in the morning..."

**Q1. Who is narrator and cousin? (1)**
**Answer:** Aram, nine-year-old narrator, and Mourad, deemed crazy by all except Aram.

**Q2. Which tribe and what fame? (1)**
**Answer:** Garoghlanian family — famous honesty eleven centuries, proud first, honest next, right-wrong; none steal.

**Q3. What unexpected sight? (1)**
**Answer:** Mourad sitting on beautiful white horse at window, inviting ride; fulfilling earliest longings to ride thought impossible in poverty.

**Q4. Find word meaning splendour? (1)**
**Answer:** Magnificence.

> Extract-2: "Tooth for tooth... I would swear it is my horse if I didn't know your parents. The fame of your family for honesty is well known to me. Yet the horse is the twin of my horse. A suspicious man would believe his eyes instead of his heart. Good day, my young friends."

**Q1. Who says to whom? (1)**
**Answer:** John Byro, Assyrian farmer ($60 horse, 10 miles walk), to Mourad and Aram on meeting road; Mourad called horse My Heart.

**Q2. What did he examine? (1)**
**Answer:** Studied horse eagerly, looked into mouth; tooth-for-tooth identical to stolen-last-month horse.

**Q3. Why refuse to suspect? (1)**
**Answer:** Knew parents, fame honesty well known; suspicion would insult honour; chose heart over eyes.

**Q4. Result next morning? (1)**
**Answer:** Boys put horse back in Byro's barn at dawn, dogs silent; Byro: stronger ever, better-tempered, thank God.

## Step 3 — 6 Short Answers (40-50 words)

**Q1. Why was Aram delighted yet frightened at first sight?**
**Answer:** Delight as earliest longings to ride seemed fulfilled — seeing, smelling, hearing breathing horse. Fear because comical poverty meant none could buy horse, and eleven-century honest tribe could never steal. He refused belief till asking where stolen.

**Q2. How did boys justify riding was not stealing?**
**Answer:** They argued crazy love for horses made borrowing for ride different from stealing money. It would become stealing only when offered to sell, which they never would. Intent to return kept tribal pride intact despite clear theft.

**Q3. Sketch Uncle Khosrove.**
**Answer:** Enormous man, powerful black hair, largest moustache in San Joaquin Valley; furious, irritable, impatient. Universal roar — It is no harm; pay no attention. Even to house on fire reported by Arak after 8 blocks run. Homesick, gentle heart, spits on money.

**Q4. Contrast Mourad and Zorab.**
**Answer:** Mourad is natural descendant of crazy streak — roaring song, Vazire run, way with horse-dogs-robin, enjoys alive most. Zorab, his father, is practical and nothing else. Tribe says man can father flesh yet not spirit, explaining difference.

**Q5. Why did Byro trust heart over eyes?**
**Answer:** Mouth examination proved tooth-for-tooth twin of his $60 horse stolen last month. Yet knowing parents and Garoghlanian fame for honesty, he refused suspicion. Generous faith — suspicious believes eyes not heart — shamed boys more than accusation.

**Q6. How was horse returned?**
**Answer:** Early next dawn boys left horse in Byro's vineyard barn; dogs followed silently for Mourad's way with dogs. That afternoon Byro drove surrey amazed — stronger ever, better-tempered, thank God. Khosrove roared quiet, returned, pay no attention.

## Step 4 — 3 Long Answers (120-150 words)

**Q1. Mourad natural descendant of crazy streak — justify. OR Conscience vs fear in return?**
**Answer:** Mourad inherits Khosrove's streak, not practical Zorab's flesh-without-spirit. He appears at four with stolen white horse, roaring while trotting down Olive Avenue. He shouts Vazire, run, making horse rear, snort, burst fury across ditch, returning dripping — loveliest sight. He claims simple honest understanding, promises Aram trick at thirteen, tames dogs silent, heals robin wing under peach tree, hides horse at Fetvajian's on oats-alfalfa, lies lightly Of course not about duration. Yet he draws moral line — Are you inviting Garoghlanian to steal, six months return limit. Return was conscience, not fear. Safe hiding two weeks showed no panic; Byro's tooth-for-tooth yet heart-over-eyes trust wounded honour, forcing dawn return proving eleven-century honesty triumphed.

**Q2. Role of John Byro in reforming boys. OR Sketch Aram.**
**Answer:** Byro, Assyrian who learnt Armenian from loneliness, lost $60 horse last month, walking ten miles paining leg with useless surrey. Khosrove mocks loss — lost homeland, spit on money — but Byro's gentleness reforms. On road he eagerly studies My Heart, finds tooth-for-tooth twin, yet citing parents and famed honesty refuses theft charge — suspicious believes eyes not heart. This trust shames riding-not-selling excuse. Boys quietly restore horse at dawn, dogs silent. Next day his honest report — stronger ever, better-tempered, thank God — confirms riding improved horse, but honour required return. OR Aram: nine-year-old in good old days of magnificence, torn between longing to ride and proud-honest tribe code, invents sell-test, falls over seven vines at Halabian's, yet learns from Byro to choose conscience.

**Q3. Honesty as identity in story. OR Humour + nostalgia charm without adventure?**
**Answer:** Garoghlanians live most amazing comical poverty yet famous honesty eleven centuries even when wealthiest — proud first, honest next, right-wrong, none take advantage let alone steal. Aram's riding-isn't-stealing-till-sell logic tests identity. Khosrove's It is no harm, house-on-fire absurdity and spit on money, Mourad's Vazire run and robin healing, and Byro's heart-over-eyes faith all push boys to live identity, not policy. Return at dawn with silent dogs and better-tempered horse proves reputation outweighs temptation. OR Charm lies in nostalgic good old days, delightful mysterious dream tone, Walnut-Olive avenues, roaring song, seven-vine fall, half-hour search, Fetvajian oats-alfalfa hideout, peach-tree robin, surrey thank God humour — irony of thieves from honest tribe and trust redeeming makes moral warm, never preachy.
