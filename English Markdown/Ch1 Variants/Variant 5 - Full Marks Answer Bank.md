## PART A: MODEL FULL-MARKS LONG ANSWERS

### Model Answer 1 — Prose: Portray the character of the Grandmother and her relationship with the author. [120-150 words]
Khushwant Singh's grandmother was **old, wrinkled, bent and short**, with **white hair and a stoop**, hobbled about with **one hand resting on her waist**. She was deeply **religious, kind and dignified**, telling the **beads of her rosary** constantly.

In the **village**, she was the author's **constant companion** — waking him, giving him **breakfast of stale chapatti with butter and sugar**, teaching **alphabet and morning prayer**, accompanying him to the **temple school**, and feeding the **village dogs** on return. In the **city**, she accepted **seclusion with dignity** when English school, **music, science and English words** shocked her, spending time in **spinning-wheel and prayer** and feeding **sparrows**.

She remained **calm at the railway station** during his **five-year stay abroad**, but celebrated his return by **singing with women of the neighbourhood beating the old drum**. Her **peaceful death while praying** and the **silent mourning of sparrows** prove her **spiritual purity and deep love**.

### Model Answer 2 — Poem: Explain the theme of loss and transience in A Photograph. [120-150 words]
Shirley Toulson's *A Photograph* contrasts **permanence of nature with mortality of humans** through a **cardboard photograph** of three girls — the poet's **mother aged about twelve**, with cousins **Betty and Dolly**, **paddling at the sea**, holding hands and **smiling at the uncle** who clicked it.

The mother would **laugh at the snapshot** recalling their **sea holiday**, showing **nostalgia and transient joy of childhood**. Now the mother has been **dead for nearly twelve years**, and the poet recalls her **laughter with a painful, wry feeling** — **both wry with the laboured ease of loss**.

While the **sea remains unchanged**, human lives change and end. The poem ends in **silence — Its silence silences** — expressing **emptiness after death** where **no words can console**. Thus the poem is a **tender elegy on loss, memory and the cruel passage of time**.

## PART B: SHORT-ANSWER Q&A (30-40 Words Each)

**Q1. Prose: Why did the grandmother dislike the city school?**
**Ans:** The grandmother disliked the city English school because **no teaching about God and scriptures** was given. She was shocked to learn about **English words, science and music lessons**, as she felt **music had lewd associations**.

**Q2. Prose: How did sparrows show their grief on the grandmother's death?**
**Ans:** Thousands of **sparrows sat scattered around the corpse** in silence. They **did not chirrup**, ignored the **breadcrumbs thrown** by the author's mother, and **flew away quietly** after the body was carried off, mourning her death.

**Q3. Prose: Describe the grandmother's routine in the village.**
**Ans:** She **woke the author, got him ready, gave breakfast**, taught the **alphabet and prayer**, took him to the **temple school**, helped with lessons, and on return **fed stale chapattis to village dogs** while walking back together.

**Q4. Poem: Describe the cardboard photograph.**
**Ans:** The photograph shows the poet's **mother at twelve with cousins Betty and Dolly**, **paddling in the sea holding hands**. They **smiled at the camera** while the poet's **uncle clicked** it during a **sea holiday**.

**Q5. Poem: Why did the mother laugh at the snapshot?**
**Ans:** The mother laughed recalling **her childhood sea holiday joy**. The **sight of their windblown hair and dresses**, and **happy memories of paddling** with her cousins, made her laugh with **nostalgia**.

**Q6. Poem: What does Its silence silences mean?**
**Ans:** It means the **death of the mother has left a deep void** that **cannot be expressed in words**. The **silence of death silences the poet**, showing **helpless grief and finality of loss**.

## PART C: LONG-ANSWER Q&A (100-120 Words Each)

**Q7. Prose: Trace the three phases of the author's relationship with his grandmother.**
**Ans:** The relationship passed through three stages. In the **village**, the grandmother was the author's **closest companion** — waking him, dressing him, giving breakfast, teaching prayers, taking him to **temple school** and feeding dogs.
In the **city**, a distance grew. She **could not accompany him to English school**, disapproved of **science, English and music**, and withdrew into **seclusion with spinning-wheel, rosary and sparrows**. They **saw less of each other** as he grew up and went to university.
When he left **abroad for five years**, she saw him off **calmly, praying and kissing his forehead**. On his return she **celebrated by singing**, then fell ill and **died peacefully praying**, showing **dignified acceptance of separation**.

**Q8. Prose: Why is the grandmother called a portrait of a lady? Justify her serenity and beauty.**
**Ans:** The title shows the author's effort to paint a **loving, respectful word-portrait** of his grandmother. Her physical beauty lay in **serenity, calmness and purity** — **white clothes, white hair, rosary in wrinkled hands**, like a **winter landscape in mountains**.
Inner beauty lay in her **kindness, religious devotion, dignity and selflessness**. She **never complained**, accepted **loneliness in the city**, found joy in **feeding birds**, and faced **parting and death bravely**. She **missed her prayers only on the last evening** to sing for her grandson, then **welcomed death in prayer**. Her life was a **picture of peace, contentment and spiritual strength**, hence a true portrait of a noble lady.

**Q9. Poem: Contrast childhood joy and adult loss in A Photograph.**
**Ans:** The poem contrasts **past joy with present grief**. Childhood is shown in the **three girls paddling, holding hands and smiling**, full of **innocence, fun and sea-holiday excitement**. The mother later **laughed remembering it**, showing life's continuity.
Adulthood brings **irreversible loss**. The mother is **dead for nearly twelve years**, and the poet is left with **only memory and a faded cardboard**. The **sea has not changed**, but humans have died. The poet feels **wry, laboured loss** with **no words to express grief**. This contrast highlights **transience of human life against permanence of nature**.

**Q10. Poem: How does Toulson use stillness and silence to convey grief?**
**Ans:** Toulson uses the **still photograph and final silence** as symbols of death. The **cardboard** freezes a **moment of laughter and movement** forever, but the persons in it are **gone or aged**. The **unchanged sea** emphasises that **nature continues while humans die**.
The mother's **laughter at the past** becomes the poet's **silence in the present**. After nearly twelve years of the mother's death, the poet has **nothing to say — Its silence silences**. This means **grief is beyond language**. The **absence of sound** mirrors **emptiness and finality**. Through this stillness, the poet shows that **memory preserves joy but cannot bring back the dead**.

## PART D: EXTRACT-BASED QUESTIONS WITH ANSWERS

### Extract 1 — Prose
> She hobbled about the house in spotless white with one hand resting on her waist to balance her stoop and the other telling the beads of her rosary. Her silver locks were scattered untidily over her pale, puckered face, and her lips constantly moved in inaudible prayer.

**a) Who is she? What image is created?**
**Ans:** She is the author's grandmother. The image is of a **bent, old, serene and deeply religious lady**, like a **winter landscape**.

**b) What does telling the beads show?**
**Ans:** It shows her **constant devotion to God, spirituality and detachment** from worldly life.

**c) Find a word meaning walked lamely.**
**Ans:** **Hobbled.**

### Extract 2 — Prose
> When we carried her corpse off, they flew away quietly. Next morning the sweeper swept the bread crumbs into the dustbin.

**a) Who are they? Where were they sitting?**
**Ans:** They are the **thousands of sparrows** fed daily by the grandmother. They sat **scattered on the verandah and in the room around her corpse**.

**b) Why did they come and why did they not eat?**
**Ans:** They came to **mourn her death / pay last tribute**. They did not eat the breadcrumbs or **chirrup**, showing **silent grief and attachment**.

### Extract 3 — Poem
> The cardboard shows me how it was / When the two girl cousins went paddling, / Each one holding one of my mother's hands, / And she the big girl — some twelve years or so.

**a) Who are the three girls? Who took the photo?**
**Ans:** The poet's **mother in the middle with cousins Betty and Dolly**. The poet's **uncle** took the photo.

**b) What does cardboard show?**
**Ans:** Cardboard means the **photograph** preserving the **sea-holiday childhood moment** — old, fragile memory.

### Extract 4 — Poem
> Both wry with the laboured ease of loss.

**a) Explain Both wry with the laboured ease of loss.**
**Ans:** Both mother remembering past and poet remembering mother feel a **painful, twisted smile after loss**. Remembering happy moments after death is **hard yet natural**, causing **wry grief**.

**b) What contrast does the poet draw?**
**Ans:** Mother's past was the **joy of sea holiday**; poet's present is **memory of mother's laughter**. Both joys are **lost to time and death**, while the **sea remains unchanged**.

## PART E: KEY QUOTES TO INSERT FOR EXTRA MARKS

2. The thought was almost revolting — **music had lewd associations**.
3. She accepted her **seclusion with resignation**.

## PART F: CBSE MARKING SCHEME TIPS — Keywords Examiners Look For

**Prose — must include:**
- Three stages: **village companion / city seclusion / abroad separation**
- Traits: **religious, dignified, selfless, serene, stoop, rosary, spinning-wheel**
- Events: **temple school, English school, music/science disapproval, five years abroad, railway station calm farewell, singing celebration, peaceful death praying, sparrows mourning**
- Values: **tradition vs modernity, generation gap, spiritual strength, dignified acceptance**

**Poem — must include:**
- **cardboard, Betty and Dolly, twelve years old, paddling, uncle, sea holiday**
- Themes: **transience, mortality, nostalgia, loss, permanence of nature vs impermanence of humans**
- Tone words: **wistful, elegiac, silent grief**
- Closing idea: **silence / void beyond words**

**Presentation Tips:**
1. Stick to **word limit**; underline key points.
2. For 4-5 marks, write **3-4 points with explanation**; for 6 marks, **intro + 4-5 points + 1 quote + conclusion**.
3. Quote accurately — **half-mark for correct quote**.
4. Do not add personal story; stay on **NCERT facts**.

## PART G: COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

1. **Do not swap dogs and sparrows:** Village = fed **dogs**; City = fed **sparrows**.
2. **Do not say she liked music:** She **disapproved of music** and withdrew silently.
3. **Do not call her sentimental at station:** She was **calm, kissed forehead**, praying, showing strength.
4. **Do not say she died of singing:** She had **mild fever next day, predicted her end, prayed and died peacefully**.
5. **Do not say photograph is recent or mother is alive:** It is an **old cardboard photo; mother dead nearly twelve years**.
6. **Do not confuse cousins:** **Betty and Dolly are mother's cousins**, mother was **twelve**, poet was **not yet born**.
7. **Do not write sea changed:** Sea is **unchanged** — humans changed. That contrast fetches marks.
8. Tone is **nostalgic, sad, elegiac**, not humorous.
