# PART A: THE PORTRAIT OF A LADY – Khushwant Singh

## 1. PERFECT EXAM-READY SUMMARY PARAGRAPH (Learn This = 80% Marks)

> **Khushwant Singh draws a tender pen-portrait of his grandmother – short, fat, slightly bent, with silver-white hair, wrinkled face criss-crossed with lines, always telling her rosary beads in prayer.** Left with her in the village when the author's parents went to the city, she woke him up, bathed and dressed him, gave him breakfast of stale chapattis with butter and sugar, and took him to the temple-school where the priest taught the alphabet and morning prayer while she read scriptures and fed stray village dogs. In the city she was unhappy with English school without teaching of God and scriptures, said nothing but disapproved of music lessons; later when the author went to university and then abroad for five years, she lived alone spinning the wheel, reciting prayers, and feeding sparrows – her happiest half-hour when birds sat on her head and shoulders. On his return she celebrated his homecoming by singing with neighbourhood women beating an old drum, fell ill the next morning with fever, knew her end was near, lay praying and telling her beads till her lips stopped moving and the rosary fell from her lifeless fingers, and was mourned even by thousands of sparrows who gathered silently, refused the crumbs offered by the mother, and flew away after the corpse was carried off.

- Who? Author + Grandmother
- Where? Village → City → Abroad (5 years)
- Tone? Love, respect, acceptance, serene spirituality
- Ending? Peaceful death + sparrows' mourning = nobility of soul

# PART B: POEM – A PHOTOGRAPH – Shirley Toulson

## 1. PERFECT EXAM-READY SUMMARY PARAGRAPH (Learn This = Full Marks)

> **In Shirley Toulson's elegy 'A Photograph', the poet looks at an old cardboard photograph of her mother at twelve with her two cousins Betty and Dolly paddling at the sea on a beach holiday, the mother as the big girl holding the younger one's hand while her uncle with a camera captured them smiling through their windswept hair.** The poet recalls how her mother later laughed at that snapshot remembering the sea-holiday, and contrasts that transient laughter of the past with the present silence – the mother has been dead for nearly twelve years and the poet is left with a deep, speechless woe, where the sea's ceaseless washing and the terrible transience of human life stand against the permanence of silence, captured in the famous line 'Its silence silences'.

- Form? Elegy / Remembrance, 3 stanzas
- Tone? Nostalgic, mournful, quiet
- Message? Life is transient, loss silences, memory preserves

## 8. 10 MCQ WITH ANSWERS (Self-Test in 3 mins)

**Q1.** Grandmother's appearance was:
a) tall, thin, erect b) short, fat, slightly bent c) tall, fat, stooping d) short, thin, bent
**Ans: (b)**

**Q2.** What did grandmother give author for breakfast in village?
a) bread-butter b) stale chapatti with butter and sugar c) paratha-curds d) milk-cornflakes
**Ans: (b)**

**Q3.** Village school was attached to:
a) mosque b) church c) temple d) gurudwara
**Ans: (c)**

**Q4.** In city grandmother disapproved of:
a) English b) science c) music d) sports
**Ans: (c) – music had lewd associations, not for gentlefolk**

**Q5.** Grandmother's happiest half-hour was:
a) spinning wheel b) feeding sparrows c) reading scriptures d) talking to author
**Ans: (b)**

**Q6.** Author was abroad for:
a) 3 years b) 4 years c) 5 years d) 6 years
**Ans: (c)**

**Q7.** In 'A Photograph', mother's age in photo was:
a) 10 b) 11 c) 12 d) 14
**Ans: (c)**

**Q8.** Cousins in photo were:
a) Betty and Dolly b) Kate and Dolly c) Betty and Mary d) Lucy and Dolly
**Ans: (a)**

**Q9.** Who took the photograph?
a) father b) uncle c) poet d) stranger
**Ans: (b) – mother's uncle**

**Q10.** 'Its silence silences' refers to:
a) sea's silence b) death's voiceless grief / photograph's silence c) mother's laughter d) camera click
**Ans: (b)**
