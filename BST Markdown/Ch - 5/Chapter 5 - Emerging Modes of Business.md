# Chapter 5 – Emerging Modes of Business

## 📌 Introduction

In the modern period, there has been a considerable change in the manner of conducting business. **'Emerging Modes of Business'** refers to the recent trends and technologies shaping the way business is done today.

> **Why the shift?** To meet ever-growing competition, maintain quality standards, offer lower prices, ensure faster order processing, and provide better customer care — businesses must take advantage of emerging technologies.

The **strongest trend** shaping modern business is:

> 🌐 **E-Business (Electronic Business)**

---

## 💻 E-BUSINESS

### What is E-Business?

**Electronic Business**, commonly known as **e-Business**, refers to **conducting business activities over the internet or any other computer network**.

> **In the words of Philip Kotler:**
> *"Electronic business is the general term for buying and selling processes that are supported by electronic means."*

```
        E-BUSINESS
             |
     ┌───────┴───────┐
     |               |
  Industry        Commerce
  (Production)    (Trade & Distribution)
```

---

### e-Business vs e-Commerce

| Basis | e-Commerce | e-Business |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **Scope** | Narrow — deals only with **distribution** of goods & services | **Wider** — includes e-commerce + all other business functions |
| **Functions Covered** | Buying & selling only | Production, accounting, finance, personnel, administration, etc. |

> **Think of it this way:** e-Business is like a full tree; e-Commerce is just one branch (the buying-selling branch). Every e-commerce is e-business, but NOT every e-business is e-commerce.

#### 1️⃣ B2B Commerce (Business to Business)

Transactions taking place **between business firms**.

**Example:** Maruti Suzuki buys tyres, glass for windscreens, door handles, etc. from different business suppliers — all are B2B transactions.

**Common B2B activities include:**
- Inviting tenders
- Commercial negotiations
- Distribution of goods
- Creation of utilities
- Collaborations

```
   [Supplier A] ──→ Tyres ──┐
   [Supplier B] ──→ Glass ──┤
   [Supplier C] ──→ Handles ──┼──→ [Maruti Suzuki]
   [Supplier D] ──→ Parts ──┘
```

---

#### 2️⃣ B2C Commerce (Business to Customer)

Transactions taking place **between business and customers**. It enables the business to remain in touch with its customers on a **round-the-clock basis**.

**Example:** Withdrawal of money from ATMs — previously tedious, now possible 24×7 in a convenient and fast manner.

**B2C activities include:**
- Selling and distribution of goods
- Lodging of complaints by customers
- After-sale service
- Promotional activities

> **Popular B2C platforms:** Amazon, eBay, Snapdeal, Flipkart

---

#### 3️⃣ C2C Commerce (Customer to Customer)

Transactions taking place **between two or more customers**.

**Example:** **OLX and Quikr** — consumers can sell their goods and services directly to other consumers.

**C2C activities include:**
- Selling used books, clothes, etc.
- Selling antique items
- Warning customers about fraudulent suppliers
- Sharing information about product quality & durability

> **Popular C2C platforms:** OLX, CarDekho

---

#### 4️⃣ Intra-B Commerce (Within the Business)

e-Business is **wider than e-commerce**. It includes not only B2B, B2C, and C2C but also **Intra-B Commerce** — interaction and dealings among various **departments and persons within the same firm**.

It involves the use of **Intranet** to interact and coordinate between departments and persons within a firm. The use of Intra-B Commerce has facilitated **flexible manufacturing**.

**Example:** The marketing department interacts regularly with the production department to get products made as per customer requirements.

```
     ┌───────────┐
     │ Marketing │──┐
     └───────────┘  │  (Intranet)
                    ├─────→ ┌────────────┐
     ┌───────────┐  │       │ Production │
     │ Purchase  │──┘       └────────────┘
     └───────────┘
```

**Intra-B Commerce transactions may involve:**
- Inventory & cash management
- Reporting by employees to their seniors
- Human resource management
- Recruitment & selection
- Training, development & education
- **VPN Technology:** Companies use Virtual Private Networks so employees can access the organisation's network and work from anywhere.

---

### ⚡ e-Business vs Traditional Business

| Basis | e-Business | Traditional Business |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Ease of Formation** | ✅ Easy to form — no physical presence required | ❌ Difficult to form — requires many formalities |
| **Physical Presence** | Not required | Required |
| **Locational Requirements** | No need for a physical location — operate from anywhere | Location preferred near raw materials and markets |
| **Cost of Setting Up** | ✅ **Low** — no need for physical facilities | ❌ **High** — requires investment in infrastructure |
| **Operating Cost** | ✅ Low operating cost | ❌ High — due to investment in production, marketing, etc. |
| **Contact with Suppliers & Customers** | ✅ **Direct contact** through digital channels | ❌ **Indirect contact** through intermediaries |
| **Nature of Internal Communication** | Communication can flow in **any direction** (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) | **Hierarchical** flow of communication (top-down) |
| **Response Time** | ✅ **Instant response** to customer needs | ❌ Response takes a lot of time |
| **Organisational Structure** | **Flat structure** — direct command & communication | **Tall/Vertical structure** — chain of command |
| **Business Process Cycle** | ✅ **Short cycle** — done with a click | ❌ **Long cycle** — many sequential processes |
| **Interpersonal Touch** | ❌ Less opportunity for personal interaction | ✅ More opportunity for face-to-face interaction |

---

### ✅ Benefits of e-Business

#### 1️⃣ Ease of Formation & Lower Investment
- Very easy to start — no procedural requirements as needed for traditional industry
- Success depends on **network (contacts)**, not on **investment (net worth)**
- Benefits of internet technology accrue to **big or small businesses alike**

#### 2️⃣ Convenience
- Access anything, **anywhere, anytime** (24×7)
- Customers can shop at their own convenience

#### 3️⃣ Speed
- Very less time to complete the business cycle — entire process with a **click of a mouse**
- Digital products (music, movies, e-books) delivered instantly online
- Through **electronic funds transfer**, money can be transferred instantly

#### 4️⃣ Global Reach / Global Access
- With well-developed computerised networking, sellers can operate at **national and global levels**
- Buyers and sellers from **any part of the world** can interact

#### 5️⃣ Movement Towards a Paperless Society
- Internet has considerably reduced dependence on paperwork
- Most work done electronically through computers
- Even government departments use computers — e.g., filing income tax returns online

---

### ❌ Limitations of e-Business

| Limitation | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Low Personal Touch** | No face-to-face interaction — lack of human element in transactions |
| **Delay in Delivery** | Physical products still need shipping — no instant gratification |
| **Need for Technology Capability** | Both buyer and seller need internet access & technical skills |
| **Non-Traceability of Parties** | Parties may be fraudulent or untraceable — risk of scams |
| **People Resistance** | Many people still prefer traditional shopping due to habit or trust issues |
| **Ethical Fallouts** | Issues like spam emails, privacy breaches, data theft, fake reviews |

### 🏢 OUTSOURCING

### What is Outsourcing?

**Outsourcing** refers to **contracting out some of its activities to a third party** which were earlier performed by the organisation itself.

> **In simple words:** Instead of doing everything yourself, hire another company to do certain tasks for you.

---

### 📌 Need for Outsourcing

```
       NEED FOR OUTSOURCING
              │
      ┌───────┼───────┐
      |       |       |
  Focusing   Cost   Quest for
  Attention Reduction Excellence
      |               |
   ┌──┴──┐     ┌──────┴──────┐
   |     |     |             |
Growth  Boost to       Risk
through  Economic     Sharing
Alliance Development
```

#### 1️⃣ Focusing of Attention
Outsourcing allows the organisation to **focus on its core business activities** while non-core tasks are handled by specialists.

#### 2️⃣ Quest for Excellence
Third-party vendors are often **specialists** who deliver high-quality output — the organisation benefits from their expertise.

#### 3️⃣ Cost Reduction
Outsourcing helps **reduce operational costs** — the third party may have economies of scale or lower labour costs.

#### 4️⃣ Growth Through Alliance
Outsourcing creates a **partnership relationship** — the organisation builds strategic alliances with vendors.

#### 5️⃣ Boost to Economic Development
Outsourcing creates employment and economic activity in the vendor's location (e.g., BPO industry in India).

#### 6️⃣ Risk Sharing
By outsourcing, some of the **business risk is transferred** to the third-party vendor.

### Case-Based Questions

#### Case 1: OLX Sofa Sale

**Scenario:** Mr. Rajiv wants to buy a new sofa set but doesn't have space. He plans to sell his old sofa through OLX to get some money and space. He gets a great response and chooses the buyer who gives him the best price.

**Questions:**
(a) Which are the two parties involved in the transaction mentioned above?
(b) What branch of e-business is referred to in the above case?

**Answers:**
(a) **Customer and Customer** (Consumer-to-Consumer)
(b) **C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer)** Commerce

---

#### Case 2: Work from Home (Intra-B/VPN)

**Scenario:** Software engineers at 'General Technologies' get freedom to work from home. They take daily assigned tasks from the work manager, save them on their laptops, and complete them at home. The General Manager took this initiative to allow employees to spend more time with their families, which in turn increases their potential, creativity, and efficiency.

**Questions:**
(a) Identify the dimension of e-business talked about in the above situation.
(b) State any two other dimensions of e-business.

**Answers:**
(a) **Intra-B Commerce** (employees accessing company network remotely)
(b) Any two of: **B2B, B2C, C2C**

---

#### Case 3: Ritika's Online Store

**Scenario:** Ritika starts an online store named **TrendZilla** selling handmade jewellery and accessories. She uses Instagram and a website to showcase products. Customers place orders and pay online. Ritika ties up with a courier partner for home delivery.

**Questions:**
(a) Which type of e-business model is Ritika using?
(b) State any two benefits of running a business online.

**Answers:**
(a) **B2C (Business to Consumer)** — she is selling directly to customers
(b) Benefits: **Convenience** (24×7 shopping), **Speed** (quick ordering), **Ease of Formation** (low setup cost)

---

#### Case 4: B2B vs B2C Identification

**Scenario:** Two companies use online platforms. **Company A** supplies raw materials like steel and plastic to manufacturers. **Company B** sells electronics like mobiles and headphones directly to customers through its website.

**Questions:**
(a) Identify which company is operating under B2B and which under B2C.
(b) Give one example of each type of business (B2B and B2C) other than the ones given.

**Answers:**
(a) **Company A = B2B** (supplies raw materials to other businesses)
    **Company B = B2C** (sells directly to customers)
(b) **B2B example:** A tyre manufacturer supplying to Maruti Suzuki
    **B2C example:** Amazon selling products to individual customers

### ✅ Quick Comparison Trick:

> **e-Commerce** = Buying & selling only (tip of iceberg)
> **e-Business** = Everything including production, finance, HR (full iceberg)

> **B2B** = Firm sells to another firm
> **B2C** = Firm sells to individual customer
> **C2C** = Customer sells to another customer
> **Intra-B** = Department talks to another department
