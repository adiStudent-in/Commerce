## 📌 Big Picture

> Business activities cannot be performed in isolation. Choosing the **right form of organisation** is one of the most important decisions while starting or expanding a business.
>
> The best form is chosen by **weighing the merits and limitations of each type against one's own requirements** (capital, risk, control, continuity, secrecy).

### The 5 Forms of Business Organisations

```
FORMS OF BUSINESS ORGANISATION
├── 1. Sole Proprietorship
├── 2. Joint Hindu Family (HUF) Business
├── 3. Partnership
├── 4. Cooperative Society
└── 5. Joint Stock Company
```

> 💡 **Why each new form emerged:** Sole proprietorship → too little capital & skills → HUF → limited resources & Karta's dominance → Partnership → limited resources & unlimited liability → Cooperative (service motive) & Company (large-scale needs).

## 1. 🧍 Sole Proprietorship

> **Definition:** A form of business organisation which is **owned, managed and controlled by an individual** who is the recipient of all profits and bearer of all risks.
>
> It is the **oldest and simplest** form of business organisation.

### Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Formation & Closure** | Very easy to start; **no legal formalities**, no separate law governs it; can be dissolved at the will of the owner |
| **Liability** | **Unlimited** – personal property may be sold to pay business debts if business assets are insufficient |
| **Sole Risk Bearer & Profit Recipient** | Owns all business affairs; bears all risk alone; receives all profits as reward |
| **Control** | One-man show; all decisions lie with the proprietor; no need to consult anyone |
| **No Separate Entity** | No legal distinction between firm and owner; owner is responsible for all business activities |
| **Lack of Business Continuity** | Death, physical ailment or insolvency of owner directly affects business – may even close it |

### ✅ Merits
- **Quick decision making** – complete freedom, no consultation needed
- **Confidentiality of information** – sole authority keeps business info secret
- **Direct incentive** – keeps all profits; no sharing
- **Sense of accomplishment** – personal satisfaction & confidence for self-employed
- **Ease of formation and closure** – minimum legal formalities

### ❌ Limitations
- **Limited resources** – only personal savings + borrowings; small size, low growth rate
- **Unlimited liability** – creditors can recover dues from personal assets too
- **Limited life of business** – illness, death, insolvency of owner affects the business
- **Limited managerial ability** – one person cannot be expert in all areas

> 💡 Suited for: **small-scale businesses, personalised services** (retail shops, beauty parlours, medical shops) where limited capital is needed and personal contact with customers matters.

## 2. 🏠 Joint Hindu Family (HUF) Business

> **Definition:** A unique form of business organisation **found only in India**, owned and carried on by members of the **Hindu Undivided Family**.
>
> Governed by the **Hindu Law (Hindu Succession Act, 1956)**; one of the oldest forms in the country.

### Key Members
- **Karta (Manager)** – the eldest male member; manages & controls the business; his decisions are **binding on all members**
- **Co-parceners** – all members having equal ownership right over ancestral property
- Membership basis = **birth** in the family; up to **3 successive generations** can be members

### Two Systems of Inheritance (Hindu Law)

| System | Where Prevails | Who Can Be Co-parcener |
|--------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Dayabhaga** | West Bengal & Assam only | Both **male and female** members |
| **Mitakshara** | All over India **except** West Bengal & Assam | **Only male** members; right acquired by **birth** |

### Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Formation** | At least **2 members** + **ancestral property** inherited; **no agreement** needed |
| **Liability** | Karta = **unlimited** (self-acquired property can be attached) | Co-parceners = **limited** (to their share in coparcenary property) |
| **Control** | Lies with Karta; decisions binding on all members |
| **Continuity** | Not affected by death of members; next eldest male becomes Karta |
| **Minor Members** | Minors **can** be members (inclusion by birth) |

### ✅ Merits
- **Effective control** – Karta's absolute decision-making avoids conflicts
- **Continued business existence** – death of Karta doesn't stop business
- **Limited liability of members** – risk of co-parceners is well-defined and precise
- **Increased loyalty & cooperation** – family members work together

### ❌ Limitations
- **Limited resources** – depends mainly on ancestral property; limited scope for expansion
- **Unlimited liability of Karta** – burdened with decisions + full liability
- **Dominance of Karta** – may cause conflict, even breakdown of the family unit
- **Limited managerial skills** – Karta can't be expert in all areas; unwise decisions → losses

> 📉 On the decline in modern India due to shrinking joint-family system.

## 3. 🤝 Partnership

> **Definition (Indian Partnership Act, 1932):**
> *"Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profit of the business carried on by all or any one of them acting for all."*
>
> Partnership emerged from the limitations of Sole Proprietorship and HUF – it answers the needs of **greater capital, varied skills and sharing of risks**.

### Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Formation** | Governed by **Indian Partnership Act, 1932**; comes into existence through a **legal agreement** (terms, profit-sharing, manner of business) |
| **Liability** | **Unlimited** – personal assets usable for debts; partners are liable **jointly AND individually** (a paying partner can recover proportionate share from others) |
| **Risk Bearing** | Risks borne as a team; profits & losses shared in **agreed ratio** |
| **Decision-Making & Control** | Shared among partners; decisions by **mutual consent** |
| **Membership** | **Min: 2** (Sec 464, Companies Act 2013) | **Max: 50** (Rule 10, Companies (Miscellaneous) Rules, 2014) |
| **Mutual Agency** | Every partner acts as **agent** (binds others) **and principal** (bound by others' acts) |

### ✅ Merits
- **Ease of formation & closure** – agreement-based; can start even without registration
- **Balanced decision-making** – varied knowledge, skills & experience; division of work
- **More funds** – capital from many partners; larger resources than sole proprietorship
- **Sharing of risks** – reduces anxiety and burden on individual partners
- **Secrecy** – not required to publish accounts

### ❌ Limitations
- **Unlimited liability** – joint & individual; a wealthy partner may have to repay entire debts if others fail
- **Limited resources** – legal ceiling on partners restricts large-scale growth
- **Possibility of conflicts** – differences of opinion; one wrong decision can ruin all
- **Lack of continuity** – ends on death/insolvency/insanity of a partner (fresh agreement needed to continue)
- **Lack of public confidence** – accounts not published; true financial status unknown

---

### 3.1 Types of Partners

| Type | Capital | Management | Shares P/L | Liability |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Active (Working)** | ✅ | ✅ takes active part | ✅ | Unlimited |
| **Sleeping (Dormant)** | ✅ | ❌ no day-to-day role | ✅ | Unlimited (jointly & individually) |
| **Secret** | ✅ | ✅ (takes part, identity unknown to public) | ✅ | Unlimited |

> 💡 **Types of partners MCQs:** lends name & goodwill → Nominal | contributes capital, no day-to-day role → Sleeping | capital + management but identity hidden → Secret
| **Nominal** | ❌ (lends name & goodwill only) | ❌ | ❌ (does not share profits) | Unlimited |
| **Partner by Estoppel** | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Unlimited (liable as partner in eyes of third party) |
| **Partner by Holding Out** | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Unlimited (liable to outsiders who lent money/sold goods on credit based on that impression) |

> 🔑 **Estoppel vs Holding Out:** Estoppel = person **himself creates** the impression of being a partner. Holding Out = person **knowingly does not deny** being represented as a partner.

### Minor as Partner
- A minor **cannot be a full partner** (incompetent to contract), but **can be admitted to benefits** with consent of all partners
- Liability limited to his capital contribution; cannot take part in management
- On attaining majority → must decide within **6 months** whether to remain a partner; if no notice given → becomes a **full partner with unlimited liability**

---

### 3.2 Types of Partnerships

**On basis of Duration:**
| Type | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Partnership at Will** | Life depends on will of partners; can be dissolved by any partner on giving **notice**; not for a fixed period or specific venture |
| **Particular Partnership** | Formed for a **particular project / specified period**; dissolves automatically on completion/expiry |

**On basis of Liability:**
| Type | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **General Partnership** | Liability of **every partner unlimited**; all may take active part in management; acts of each bind the firm |
| **Limited Partnership** | **At least one partner unlimited**; rest may be limited; limited partners **cannot manage** (their acts don't bind the firm); registration **compulsory** |

---

### 3.3 Partnership Deed
Written agreement specifying terms & conditions of partnership, including:
- Name of the firm
- Nature & location of business
- Duration of business
- Investment by each partner
- Distribution of profits & losses
- Duties & obligations of partners
- Salaries & withdrawals; interest on capital & drawings
- Procedure for dissolution
- Preparation & auditing of accounts
- Method of solving disputes
- Terms of admission, retirement & expulsion

> The agreement may be **verbal or written**; written is advisable as it is evidence of agreed conditions.

> ⚠️ **Registration – consequences of non-registration:** partner of unregistered firm **cannot file suit** against the firm/other partners; firm **cannot file suit** against third parties; firm **cannot file case** against partners. (Outsiders *can* sue the unregistered firm.)

### 3.4 Registration of Partnership Firm

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Nature | **Optional** but highly advisable; registration gives conclusive proof of existence |
| Procedure | ① Submit application to **Registrar of Firms** (name, location, other business places, date of joining, names & addresses of partners, duration) → ② deposit fees → ③ entry in register → ④ certificate of registration |
| Consequences of non-registration | Partner **cannot file suit** against firm/other partners; firm **cannot file suit** against third parties; firm **cannot file case** against partners |

## 4. 🤲 Cooperative Society

> **Definition:** A **voluntary association of persons** who join together with the motive of **welfare of the members** – an association of **persons, not of capital**.
>
> Registration under **Cooperative Societies Act, 1912** is **compulsory**. Minimum **10 adult persons** required.

### Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Voluntary Membership** | Open to anyone 18+ with common interest; no force, no bar on religion/caste/gender |
| **Legal Status** | Separate legal entity after registration; can own property, make contracts, sue & be sued in own name; unaffected by entry/exit of members |
| **Limited Liability** | Limited to capital contributed; max risk = subscribed share capital |
| **Control** | Democratic pattern; members elect a **managing committee**; **one-man-one-vote** |
| **Service Motive** | Formed for service, not profit maximisation; surplus distributed as **dividend** |

### ✅ Merits
- **Equality in voting status** – one vote per member irrespective of capital
- **Limited liability** – risk limited and known
- **Stable existence** – corporate body; not affected by death/insolvency/insanity of members
- **Economy in operations** – managed on honorary basis; eliminates middlemen
- **Support from government** – low taxes, subsidies, low interest loans
- **Ease of formation** – just 10 adult members; governed by Cooperative Societies Act, 1912

### ❌ Limitations
- **Limited resources** – members have limited means; low dividend deters capital
- **Inefficiency in management** – can't pay experts; committee not professionally equipped
- **Lack of secrecy** – affairs discussed openly in meetings
- **Government control** – bound by rules on auditing, submission of accounts, etc.
- **Differences of opinion** – internal quarrels; difficulty in decision-making

---

### 4.1 Types of Cooperative Societies

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Consumers'** | Formed by consumers for good quality products at reasonable prices; buys in bulk from manufacturers/wholesalers to eliminate middlemen; surplus shared by capital contribution or purchases |
| **Producers' (Industrial)** | Formed by small producers to procure inputs for production; fights big capitalists, enhances bargaining power; 2 types – (i) supplies raw materials/equipment, (ii) sells members' produced goods; profits by contribution to total pool |
| **Marketing** | Voluntary association of small producers to sell output through one centralised agency; pools production, eliminates middlemen; performs transport, warehousing, packaging; profits by contribution to common pool |
| **Farmers' (Agricultural)** | Farmers jointly take up farming for large-scale benefits; provides quality seeds, fertilisers, machinery; consolidates fragmented holdings into viable economic holdings |
| **Credit** | Short-term loans to members at low interest; protects from exploitative lenders charging high rates; lends out of capital & deposits collected |
| **Housing** | Helps people with limited income construct houses at reasonable cost; constructs flats or provides plots; payment in instalments |

## 5. 🏢 Joint Stock Company

> **Definition:** An association of persons formed for carrying out business activities, having a **legal status independent of its members**.
>
> The most suitable form for **large-scale business**. Governed by **Companies Act, 2013**.

### Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Artificial Person** | Created by law – no body, soul, conscience; can't breathe/eat/run/talk, BUT can own property, borrow, contract, sue & be sued |
| **Separate Legal Entity** | On incorporation, assets & liabilities are separate from owners |
| **Formation** | Time-consuming, expensive, complicated; several documents & legal requirements; registration **compulsory** |
| **Perpetual Succession** | Not affected by death/insolvency/entry/exit of members; *"Members may come, members may go, but the company remains forever"*; ends only through legal **winding up** |
| **Control** | Ownership (shareholders) & management (Board of Directors) in different hands; directors are legal representatives accountable to shareholders; shareholders can't manage day-to-day |
| **Liability** | **Limited** to amount unpaid on shares held. *e.g. 500 shares of ₹10, ₹7 paid → liability only ₹3 × 500 = ₹1,500* |
| **Common Seal** | Company acts through directors using the **common seal** as its signature; if no seal, signing person must be authorised by board resolution |
| **Risk Bearing** | Risk spread over a large number of shareholders |
| **Transfer of Shares** | Shareholders can transfer shares in open market/stock exchange at prevailing price |

### ✅ Merits
- **Limited liability** – personal assets safe; debts settled only from company assets
- **Transfer of interest** – free transfer of shares avoids blockage of investment
- **Perpetual existence** – not affected by death/retirement/insolvency/insanity of members; liquidated only per Companies Act
- **Scope for expansion** – large financial resources; capital from public + banks
- **Professional management** – specialists head each department; balanced, rational decisions

### ❌ Limitations
- **Complexity in formation** – time, effort, legal knowledge required
- **Lack of secrecy** – information filed with Registrar available to public
- **Impersonal work environment** – no direct link between effort and reward; no personal contact
- **Numerous regulations** – audit, voting, filing of reports, certificates; time-consuming & expensive
- **Delay in decision-making** – important decisions by Board/general meeting; opportunities lost
- **Oligarchic management** – democracy in theory, rule by a few in practice; scattered shareholders take little interest; directors may act against their interests
- **Conflict of interests** – employees want higher salaries, consumers want low prices, shareholders want high dividends

---

### 5.1 Types of Companies (on basis of ownership)

#### A. One Person Company (OPC) – Sec 2(62)
- Only **one person as member**; incorporated as a private company
- Avails benefits of private company: separate legal entity, protection of personal assets, perpetual succession
- Only a **natural person, Indian citizen & resident** (stay ≥ 182 days in preceding calendar year) can incorporate/be nominee
- One person cannot have more than one OPC or be nominee in more than one
- **No minor** can be member/nominee
- Cannot be incorporated under **Section 8**; cannot do financial investment in other corporations
- Cannot convert voluntarily into any other kind of company until **2 years** from incorporation, unless paid-up capital crosses ₹50 lakh or average annual turnover exceeds ₹2 crore
- Words **"One Person Company"** must appear in brackets below its name

#### B. Private Company – Sec 2(68)
By its Articles of Association:
- **Restricts transfer of shares**
- **Min 2, Max 200 members** (excluding past/present employees who are members)
- **Prohibits invitation** to public to subscribe for shares
- Must use **"Private Limited"** in name
- Contravening any provision → ceases to be private company, loses all privileges

#### C. Public Company – Sec 2(71)
- Any company which is **not a private company**
- **Min 7 members, no maximum limit**
- **No restriction** on transfer of shares
- **Can invite** subscription from general public
- A private company which is a **subsidiary of a public company** is also treated as a public company

### 5.2 Privileges of Private Company over Public Company
- Can be started with just **2 members** (public needs 7)
- **No prospectus** needed
- Can allot shares **without minimum subscription**
- Can start work **right after certificate of incorporation**
- Only **2 directors** needed (public needs 3)
- No need to keep **index of members**
- Can lend money/give guarantee to directors **without government approval**

### 5.3 Comparison: OPC vs Private vs Public

| Basis | OPC | Private Company | Public Company |
|-------|-----|----------------|----------------|
| No. of Members | 1 | Min 2, Max 200 | Min 7, no max |
| No. of Directors | Min 1, Max 15 | Min 2 | Min 3 |
| Public Deposits | Cannot invite | Cannot invite | Can invite |
| Transfer of Shares | Not applicable | Restricted | No restriction |
| Invitation to Public | No | No | Yes (shares & debentures) |
| Name | "One Person Company" in brackets | "Private Limited" | "Limited" |

## 6. 📄 Formation of a Company (4 Stages)

> ⚠️ **Stages (in order):** ① Promotion → ② Incorporation → ③ Capital Subscription (public company only) → ④ Commencement of Business. A private company can start work right after incorporation.

> Formation of a company is **complex & lengthy** – unlike sole proprietorship or partnership. The 4 stages:

```
1. PROMOTION  →  2. INCORPORATION  →  3. CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION  →  4. COMMENCEMENT OF BUSINESS
```

### Stage 1: Promotion
Identification of business opportunity, analysis of its prospects, steps to implement it.
- The person performing these tasks = **Promoter** (individual, group or institution); conceives the idea, takes all initiatives

**Functions of a Promoter (6 Steps):**
1. **Identification of Business Opportunity** – new business / expansion / merger; preliminary analysis of profitability, risk, resources
2. **Feasibility Studies** (before investing money):
   - *Technical* – can it be implemented? (technology, raw materials availability)
   - *Financial* – can funds be arranged within available means?
   - *Economic* – will it be profitable?
3. **Name Approval** – select name, get it approved from **Registrar of Companies**; must not be identical/too close to an existing name (3 names given in order of preference)
4. **Fixing up Signatories to Memorandum of Association** – signatories usually become first directors; written consent to act as director & buy qualification shares; MOA signed by **min 7 persons (public) / 2 persons (private)**
5. **Appointment of Professionals** – merchant bankers, auditors, etc. to prepare & submit documents
6. **Preparation of Necessary Documents** – MOA, AOA, consent of directors, etc.

### Stage 2: Incorporation (Important Documents)

**Documents submitted to Registrar:**

| Document | What It Is |
|----------|-----------|
| **Memorandum of Association (MOA)** | Principal document = **"Charter of the Company"**; contains powers & objectives; defines scope of operations; company works within limits laid down in it |
| **Articles of Association (AOA)** | Rules & regulations for **internal management**; subsidiary to MOA; cannot include powers prohibited by MOA |
| **Consent of Proposed Directors** | Written consent to act as directors & buy qualification shares |
| **Agreement** | If company appoints Managing Director/Whole-time Director/Manager |
| **Statutory Declaration** | That all legal requirements of incorporation are complied with; signed by Advocate (HC/SC), practising CA/CS, director named in articles, or manager/secretary |
| **Fees** | Filing + registration fees (based on authorised share capital) |

**Forms of MOA (Schedule 1, Companies Act):** Table A – company limited by shares | Table B – limited by guarantee & not having share capital | Table C – limited by guarantee & having share capital | Table D – unlimited & not having share capital | Table E – unlimited & having share capital

**Forms of AOA:** Table F – company limited by shares | Table G – limited by guarantee & having share capital | Table H – limited by guarantee & NOT having share capital | Table I – unlimited & having share capital | Table J – unlimited & NOT having share capital

**Contents of MOA (6 Clauses):**
1. **Name Clause** – name of company
2. **Registered Office Clause** – state where registered office is situated
3. **Objects Clause** – purpose; company can't do business not authorised by it (divided into *Main Objects* and *Other Objects*)
4. **Liability Clause** – liability limited to unpaid amount on shares
5. **Capital Clause** – maximum (**authorised**) capital; e.g. ₹2 crore divided into 20 lakh shares of ₹10
6. **Association Clause** – signatories state intention to be associated + consent to buy qualification shares; signed by min 7 (public) / 2 (private)

**MOA vs AOA:**

| Basis | MOA | AOA |
|-------|-----|-----|
| Objectives | Defines objects for which company is formed | Rules of internal management; how objectives are achieved |
| Position | Main document; subordinate to Companies Act | Subsidiary; subordinate to both MOA & Companies Act |
| Necessity | Filing **compulsory** | Not compulsory for public company (may adopt Table F) |
| Alteration | Difficult, lengthy; government approval needed | Easy – by special resolution |
| Validity | Acts beyond MOA invalid – can't be ratified even unanimously | Acts beyond AOA can be ratified by special resolution (if not violating MOA) |
| Relationship | Company ↔ outsiders | Members ↔ management |

### Stage 3: Capital Subscription (only for public companies)
- Invitation to public through **Prospectus** (document inviting general public to subscribe to shares)
- Private companies & OPCs **cannot** issue prospectus

### Stage 4: Commencement of Business
- Company obtains **Certificate of Commencement of Business** and starts operations

### 7.1 Partnership vs Sole Proprietorship

| Basis | Partnership | Sole Proprietorship |
|-------|-------------|--------------------|
| Governing body | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 | No separate act |

| Basis | Partnership | HUF Business |
|-------|-------------|--------------|

| Liability | Unlimited (all partners) | Except Karta, all limited |

| Minor | Can't become partner (may share benefits) | Becomes coparcener by birth |
| Legal existence | Dissolved on death/insolvency of any partner | Continues even after death of Karta |

### 7.3 Company vs Partnership

| Basis | Company | Partnership |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| Formation | Registration under Companies Act, 2013 | Agreement by partners; registration not compulsory |
| No. of members | Public: min 7, no max; Private: min 2, max 200 | Min 2, Max 50 |
| Liability | Limited to unpaid capital | Unlimited |
| Management | Board of Directors | All partners |
| Transfer of interest | Possible (public company) | Not possible |
| Legal status | Separate legal entity | No separate legal entity |
| Stability | Not affected by death/insolvency | Dissolved with death/insolvency of a partner |

### 7.4 Company vs Cooperative Society

| Basis | Company | Cooperative Society |
|-------|---------|--------------------|
| Formation | Companies Act, 2013 | Cooperative Societies Act, 1912 |
| No. of members | Public: min 7; Private: 2–200 | Min 10, no max |
| Management | Board of Directors | Elected managing committee |
| Motive | Profit | Service to members |
| Government support | No special support | Low taxes, subsidies, etc. |
| Transfer of shares | Free (public company) | Not permitted |

|-------|-----------|-----|-------------|-------------|---------|

| Registration | Not needed | Not needed | Optional | Compulsory | Compulsory |

## 9. 🎯 Factors Affecting the Choice of Form of Organisation

> The best form is chosen by **weighing the merits and limitations of each type against one's own requirements**. Key factors:

| Factor | Most Advantageous | Least Advantageous |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Availability of Capital | Company | Sole Proprietorship |
| Cost of Formation | Sole Proprietorship | Company |
| Ease of Formation | Sole Proprietorship | Company |
| Transfer of Ownership | Company (except private) | Partnership |
| Managerial Skills | Company | Sole Proprietorship |
| Regulations | Sole Proprietorship | Company |
| Flexibility | Sole Proprietorship | Company |
| Continuity | Company | Sole Proprietorship |
| Liability | Company | Sole Proprietorship |

**Summary of the factors:**
1. **Cost & ease of setup** – sole proprietorship cheapest; company most expensive
2. **Liability** – company & cooperative = limited; sole proprietorship & partnership = unlimited
3. **Continuity** – company & cooperative most stable; sole proprietorship most unstable
4. **Management ability** – company (professionals) vs sole proprietorship (one person does all)
5. **Capital** – company can raise the most; sole proprietorship the least
6. **Degree of control** – want full control → sole proprietorship; okay sharing → partnership/company
7. **Nature of business** – personal contact needed → sole proprietorship; large-scale manufacturing → company; professional services → partnership

## 📝 Key Terms to Remember

- **Karta** – eldest male member managing the HUF business; unlimited liability
- **Co-parceners** – members of HUF with equal ownership right over ancestral property
- **Mutual Agency** – every partner is both agent and principal
- **Partnership Deed** – written agreement specifying terms of partnership
- **Partnership at Will** – dissolves on notice by any partner
- **Particular Partnership** – for a specific project/period
- **Prospectus** – invitation to general public to subscribe to shares (public company)
- **Memorandum of Association** – "Charter of the Company"; defines objectives & powers
- **Articles of Association** – rules for internal management
- **Artificial Person** – company exists in law; has rights but no physical existence
- **Perpetual Succession** – company continues despite changes in membership
- **Winding Up** – legal procedure to close a company
- **Oligarchy** – rule by a few (directors in practice, in a company)
- **OPC** – One Person Company (Sec 2(62)); nominee must be Indian citizen & resident

## 10. 🎯 One-Liners for Quick Revision

- Oldest & simplest form → **Sole Proprietorship**
- Unique to India; governed by Hindu Law → **HUF**
- "Carried on by all or any one of them acting for all" → **Partnership (mutual agency)**
- Association of persons, not of capital → **Cooperative Society**
- "Members may come, members may go, but company remains forever" → **Perpetual succession**
- Most suitable for large-scale business → **Joint Stock Company**
- Chosen form depends on → weighing **merits & limitations vs one's own requirements**
- Charter of the company → **Memorandum of Association**
- Invitation to general public to subscribe to shares → **Prospectus** (issued only by a public company)
- Table F (Companies Act) → **AOA of a company limited by shares**; public company need not file AOA, may adopt Table F
- Preliminary contracts are signed → **before incorporation**
- Documents submitted to Registrar → **MOA, AOA, Consent of Proposed Directors, Agreement, Statutory Declaration + fees**
- Statutory Declaration signed by → **Advocate (HC/SC) or practising CA/CS or director named in articles or manager/secretary**
