# Chapter 7 – Sources of Business Finance

---

## 📌 Concept of Business Finance

**Business Finance** = money required for carrying out business activities.

> **Definition:** Business finance means **planning, raising, managing and controlling** all the money/capital funds used in connection with business.
>
> Money needed for → raw materials, machines, salaries, rent, electricity bill, etc.

---

## 🧭 Nature of Business Finance

| Point | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Necessary for all businesses** | Needed in every business — manufacturing or trading, large or small |
| **Depends on nature & size of business** | Small firm → less funds; large firm → more funds |
| **Includes all types of funds** | Both **Owners' Funds** and **Borrowed Funds** |
| **Required on continuous basis** | Needed throughout the life of the business |
| **Wider term** | Involves estimation, procurement, utilisation and investment of funds |
| **Fluctuating nature** | Need changes with inflation, demand, supply, fashion, technology, etc. |
| **Determines size of business** | More availability of funds → larger scale of operations |

---

## ⭐ Significance of Business Finance

Every business needs money to start — called **initial capital** (depends on nature & size).

Adequate finance lets the firm:
- Meet its **liabilities on time**
- Carry on business **smoothly**
- Adopt **latest technology** & innovative production methods
- Use its **business opportunities**
- Face **competition** strongly
- **Replace assets/machinery** whenever necessary
- Face **recession & depression** phases of trade cycle

## 👑 Owners' Fund

Funds provided by the **owners** of the enterprise. Remains with the company — **no liability to return it**. E.g., capital of sole trader or shareholders.

### Features
1. **Source of Permanent Capital** — stays invested permanently, not refundable like a loan; available for all purposes throughout the life of the business.
2. **Risk Capital** — return depends on company's earnings. Loss → no compulsion to pay any return; continued loss → owners may not even recover original investment. Prosperity → high return. Hence called *risky capital*.
3. **No Security Required** — no asset pledged against ownership capital.

### Sources of Owners' Fund
- **Share Capital** (Equity + Preference)
- **Retained Earnings** (accumulated profit)

---

## 💳 Borrowed Fund

All funds available by way of **loans or credit**.

### Features
1. **Fixed Time** — raised for specified periods:

   | Type | Period |
   |---|---|
   | Short-term | Upto 1 year |
   | Medium-term | 1 – 5 years |
   | Long-term | 5 years or more |

2. **Security** — usually obtained against the security of assets.
3. **Regular Payment of Interest** — legal compulsion; interest + principal payable irrespective of earnings.
4. **Control** — lenders get **no right to control/manage**; they can only sue the firm on default of payment.
5. **Sources** — debentures, loans from commercial banks, loans from financial institutions, public deposits, inter-corporate deposits, trade credit, etc.

---

## ⚖️ Owners' Fund vs Borrowed Fund

| Basis | Owners' Fund | Borrowed Fund |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | Funds contributed by owners + retained earnings | Borrowings — loans or credit |
| **Time Period** | Permanent capital | Fixed period of time |
| **Security** | Not required | Generally secured by assets |
| **Control** | Contributors control the company | Lenders get no control |
| **Sources** | Share capital, retained earnings, GDR, ADR, IDR | Debentures, loans, trade credit, public deposits, etc. |
| **Right to Return** | No right to regular return | Legal right to regular return |
| **Risk Bearer** | Holders are **primary risk bearers** | Bear no risk |

# 📈 ISSUE OF SHARES
*(Owners' Fund — Long-Term Source)*

**Share** = smallest unit into which owner's capital of a company is divided; a measure of a shareholder's interest in the company.

Per **Companies Act**, a public company can issue two types of shares:
1. Equity Shares
2. Preference Shares

---

## 🅰️ Equity Shares

Most important source of raising **long-term capital**. These shares carry **no special/preferential rights** in payment of annual dividend or repayment of capital. On winding up, equity capital is returned only **after every claim is settled**.

### Features
1. **Primary Risk Bearers** — enjoy rewards as well as bear the risk of ownership.
2. **Claim over Residual Income** — dividend paid only after claims of all creditors, outsiders and preference shareholders are satisfied.
3. **Maturity (Permanent Capital)** — cannot be redeemed during the lifetime of the company.
4. **Return** — called **dividend**; varies with the earnings of the company.
5. **Limited Liability** — limited to the capital contributed by them.
6. **Voting Rights** — vote in proportion to shares held (equity shareholders = real owners).

---

## 🅱️ Preference Shares

Get preference over equity shares in respect of:
1. Right to receive a **fixed rate of dividend**
2. Right to receive **repayment of capital** before equity shareholders

### Features
1. **Fixed Rate of Dividend** — paid before any dividend to equity shareholders.
2. **No Security** — no asset offered against preference shares; part of owner's fund capital.
3. **No Voting Rights** under general conditions.
4. **Hybrid Security** — combines features of both:
   - Like **equity**: dividend paid only when company earns profit
   - Like **debentures**: fixed rate of return (dividend)
5. **No Fixed Liability** — during loss there is no fixed liability to pay dividend (unlike loans).

---

## ⚖️ Equity Shares vs Preference Shares

| Basis | Equity Shares | Preference Shares |
|---|---|---|
| **Face Value** | Generally low | Generally high |
| **Dividend** | Paid after paying dividend to all other shares | Fixed rate, paid **before** equity shareholders |
| **Voting Rights** | Get all voting rights | None normally; get voting right when dividend unpaid for **two years** |
| **Attraction** | Bold & adventurous investors | Cautious & conservative investors |
| **Risk** | Primary risk bearers | Relatively less risk |
| **Refund of Capital** | At winding up, refunded only after preference shares are paid | Priority over equity at winding up |

---

## 💰 Retained Earnings
*(Owners' Fund — Long-Term Finance)*

Undistributed profits left **after payment of dividend and taxes**, kept as reserve for future use.

- Provides basis of **expansion and growth** of companies
- Internally generated → known as **self-financing** (ploughing back of profits)

### Features
1. **Cushion of Security** — supports the company in adversity when outside finance is hard to arrange.
2. **Funds for New & Innovative Projects** — used for research work, expansion projects, risky ventures.
3. **No Dilution of Control** — number of shareholders does not increase.
4. **Medium & Long-term Finance** — an ownership fund.
5. **Most Dependable Source** — internal source; external sources depend on investors' preference and market conditions.
6. **Stability of Dividend** — adequate surplus allows stable dividend even in years of insufficient profit.

# 🏦 BORROWED FUNDS — SOURCES

## 1️⃣ Trade Credit *(Short-Term Finance)*

Arrangement whereby a manufacturer is granted credit by the **supplier** of raw materials, inputs, spare parts, etc.

- General duration: **3 to 6 months**
- Availability depends on: **nature, size, status/creditworthiness of the firm**

### Features
1. **Readily Available** — without special efforts.
2. **Flexible** — no rigid rules/regulations.
3. **No Floatation Cost**.
4. **No Interest Commitment** — no interest payable.
5. **Legal Action** — buyer issues a **promissory note**; supplier can sue if commitment fails.
6. **Promotes Sales** of the organisation.

---

## 2️⃣ Public Deposits *(Medium-Term Finance)*

**Unsecured deposits invited from the public** — company places an advertisement in newspapers; anyone can deposit money with it.

### Features
1. **Unsecured** — no assets mortgaged.
2. **No Loss of Control** — depositors have no voting rights.
3. **Time Period** — **six months to 3 years** (renewable → works as medium-term finance).
4. **Simple Procedure to Raise** — just a newspaper ad; no restrictive conditions/legal formalities of loan agreements.
5. **Economical** — cost lower than borrowings from banks & financial institutions (interest paid > bank deposit rates → attracts public).
6. **Limited Amount** — cannot exceed **25% of share capital and free reserves**.

---

## 3️⃣ Debentures / Bonds *(Long-Term Finance)*

A **document/certificate issued by a company under its seal** as an acknowledgement of its debt. Instrument for raising **long-term debt capital**. Called **creditorship securities** — debenture holders are creditors of the company.

### Features
1. **Borrowed Fund** — holders are creditors.
2. **Fixed Rate of Interest** — rate decided in the **Annual General Meeting**.
3. **Compulsory Payment of Interest** — legal obligation, paid irrespective of profits.
4. **Appointment of Trustee** — when debentures sold to general public, a trustee ensures the borrowing firm fulfils contractual obligations.
5. **Security** — mostly issued against charge on some **fixed assets** of the company.
6. **Redeemable** — always repaid on expiry of fixed period.
7. **No Voting Rights** — no say in management.

---

## ⚖️ Shares vs Debentures

| Basis | Shares | Debentures |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Part of owner's fund | Part of borrowed fund |
| **Status** | Shareholders = **owners** | Debenture holders = **creditors** |
| **Voting Rights** | Yes | Never granted |
| **Control** | Lies with equity shareholders | No control over company affairs |
| **Right to Return** | No right to regular return in case of loss | Legal right to regular interest even in loss |
| **Security** | No assets mortgaged | Assets mortgaged as security |
| **Redemption** | Only redeemable preference shares are redeemable; others paid back at winding up after settling all claims | Generally redeemed on expiry of fixed period |
| **Risk** | Primary risk bearers | Minimum risk |

---

## 4️⃣ Loan from Commercial Banks *(Short & Medium-Term Finance)*

Institution that performs functions of **accepting deposits, granting loans and making investments** with the aim of earning profits. (E.g., SBI, PNB, Canara Bank.)

### Features
- Provide funds **as and when required**
- Keep borrower information **confidential** → business secrecy maintained
- **Easy & economical** — no prospectus/formalities like share issue
- **Flexible** — loan amount can be increased or decreased
- Funds generally available for a **short period**
- Make **detailed investigation** of company's affairs & financial structure before lending
- May put **restrictions and difficult terms & conditions**

---

## ⚖️ Public Deposits vs Loan from Commercial Banks

| Basis | Public Deposits | Loan from Commercial Banks |
|---|---|---|
| **Security** | Not required | Generally required |
| **Governed by** | Companies Act provisions | Rules & regulations of **RBI** |
| **Rate of Interest** | Comparatively low | Comparatively higher |
| **Period** | Up to 3 years | Short and medium term |
| **Suitability** | Companies with good reputation & financial standing | Suitable for all enterprises |

---

## 5️⃣ Loan from Financial Institutions *(Long-Term Finance)*

Public financial institutions = **lending institutions / development banks**. After Independence, Government of India realised commercial banks alone are **not sufficient** for economic development of the country.

### Features
1. **Medium & Long-term Finance**.
2. **Managerial Advice** — provide managerial + financial advice on various matters.
3. **Source of Goodwill** — borrowing from them raises the company's goodwill → easier to raise funds from other sources too.
4. **Finance even during Depression** — funds available even in recession/depression.
5. **Loan in Foreign Currency** — for importing machinery; also give guarantee for purchase of capital goods from foreign countries.

---

## 6️⃣ Inter-Corporate Deposits (ICD) *(Short-Term Finance)*

**Deposits made by one company with another company.** Available to all public companies (with or without share capital). Includes:
- When a company acquires securities of another company
- When a company gives a loan to another company
- When a company gives guarantee to a person/institution providing loan to another company

### Types of ICD

| Type | Detail | Interest Rate (p.a.) |
|---|---|---|
| **Three Months' Deposits** | Most common — overcome short-term finance problem | 12% |
| **Six Months' Deposits** | Made with first-class borrowers | 15% |
| **Call Deposits** | Withdrawable by lender with **one day notice** | 10% |

### Features
1. **Secrecy** — brokers never reveal their list of lenders/borrowers.
2. Solve **short-term fund problems immediately**.
3. **Free from bureaucratic and legal problems**.
4. **Not suitable for long-term finance**.
5. **High Risk** — unsecured deposits.
6. **Higher rate of interest than banks**.

---

## 🎯 Quick Exam Pointers

- **Hybrid security** = Preference shares (features of equity + debentures).
- Company **not bound** to pay dividend/interest → **Equity shares**.
- Dividend to equity shareholders is **NOT mandatory** (payment is discretionary).
- **Debentures represent loan capital** of the company.
- Fixed capital needs → buy premises, furniture, equipment; Working capital needs → day-to-day expenses (wages, salaries, electricity).
- Real owners of a company = **Equity shareholders**.
