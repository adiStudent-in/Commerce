# Retail Unit 3 — Mechanism for Customers to Choose Right Products (Notes)

> **Why this unit?** Products are displayed only after analysing **customer demand**. The more customers **know about a product**, the better they can make a purchase decision. A smart sales associate adopts logical steps to deliver the best output and get **maximum sales turnover**.
> The product with **maximum demand** must be made available **instantly** to deal flexibly with customer choices.

**Unit structure — 4 Sessions:**
> 1. **Session 1** — Methods of Selling
> 2. **Session 2** — Sales Promotional Activities
> 3. **Session 3** — Responding to Questions and Comments
> 4. **Session 4** — Techniques of Closing a Sale

## Session 1 : Methods of Selling

### 1. Meaning of Sale
- **Sale** = the **act of selling a product in return for money**.
- It is the **beginning of a relation** between customer and vendor (or an **extension** of that relationship).

### 2. Retail Selling Methods
| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **(a) Direct sales** | Direct **personal demonstration and sale** of products/services to the consumer. Two business models: **single-level marketing** and **multilevel marketing**. Makes money by selling products directly to consumers. |
| **(b) Proforma sales** | "Proforma" = a document that is a **courtesy, satisfies a minimum requirement, conforms to a norm, or is done as a formality**. The proforma sale is a **sales quote prepared in the form of a proforma invoice**. |
| **(c) Agency-based** | A **contractual arrangement** in which the **agent** has the right to **negotiate** on the sale of the **principal's** goods/services, and gets a **commission or fee** in exchange. |
| **(d) Travelling salesman** | A **representative of a firm** who visits shops and businesses to **show samples and gain orders**. Also called a **commercial traveller, door-to-door salesman or hawker**. |
| **(e) Auction sale** | A **public sale** — goods are sold to the **highest bidder** (one who quoted the highest price). |
| **(f) B2B sale** | **Business-to-business** — sales made to another **businessman** rather than an individual customer (industrial/professional sale). |
| **(g) Electronic-based sales** | A **web-based sale** — includes both B2B and B2C sales. Uses **EDI (Electronic Data Interchange)**. |
| **(h) Indirect sales** | Sale of a good/service by a **third party** (partner or affiliate) rather than the company's own personnel. |

**Types of agents in agency-based sales:** Sales agents · Sales outsourcing through direct branded representation · Transaction sales · Consultative sales · Consignment sales · Telemarketing/telesales · Retail sales

### 3. Arrangement of Products for Sale in Store
- Products from different manufacturers are displayed and arranged **systematically in shelves** based on the **space** provided in the store.
- Display of goods is **prioritised on the basis of the manufacturer's competitiveness** — some manufacturers **buy the display window section and floor space** to promote their product.

**Need for arranging products:**
- Products are divided **section-wise** → gives a clear view of all products, makes it easy for the customer to **pick up products** while moving in the store, and **improves the profit** of the retail store.

**Why rearrangement is done:**
| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Repeat customers** | Gives the store a **fresh look** → current customers come back; more products displayed → they visit again and **sales volume increases**. |
| **Sales promotions** | Rearranging stock for sales promotion **increases customers**. |
| **Brand image** | Some retailers rearrange **routinely** to maintain brand image; **monthly rearrangement** and changing colour schemes build a new, fresh style. |
| **Seasonal** | Products are rearranged to **display seasonal products**. |

### 4. Procedure for Arranging Products — Floor Plans
| Floor Plan | Features |
|---|---|
| **Straight floor plan** | Products arranged in a **straight line**; **most commonly used** in Indian retail industry; **economical**. |
| **Diagonal floor plan** | **More visibility** for store staff and customers; requires a **small space**. |
| **Angular floor plan** | Gives a **spacious look**; used by **high-value/high-end retailers**; reduces display area and focuses only on a **few popular products**. |
| **Geometric floor plan** | Uses **racks and other fixtures** to create a different style; used for **trendy products** like cosmetics, watches. |

### 5. Identifying the Products Responsible for Sale (CSA Steps)
1. The CSA must have **full information** about the retail store and the products being sold.
2. Must **follow the instructions** of the store manager/supervisor regarding stock received from different manufacturers.
3. The manager **allocates product sections** to the CSA (vegetable section, housekeeping material, stationery, clothes, etc.).
4. The CSA is **responsible for the allotted section** and must hold **full record of stock** from receipt till the product is sold.
5. The CSA must have **complete knowledge** about the product and the **skills to sell it**.

## Session 2 : Sales Promotional Activities

### 1. Meaning of Sales Promotion
- Sales promotion is the **fastest growing area** of marketing activities and a **major component of promotion** — it improves sales and generates higher profits.
- **Promotion** = communicating with an audience through a **variety of non-personal and non-media channels**.
- In sales promotion, the firm gives **special offers** to customers (discounts, etc.); offers are a **direct proposition** that forms part of the deal.
- Sales promotion = **all activities which supplement, coordinate and make an effective effort of personal selling and advertising**.

**4 types of sales promotion activities:**
| Type | Target |
|---|---|
| **Consumer promotion** | Final buyers |
| **Business promotion** | Business customers |
| **Trade promotion** | Wholesalers and retailers |
| **Sales force promotion** | Sales force members |

### 2. Objectives of Sales Promotion
(Sales promotion is a **short-term tactic** to boost sales.)
1. **Stimulate inquiries** from consumers asking for detailed information about a product.
2. **Increase product trials** — expecting the trial leads to customer satisfaction.
3. **Encourage repurchase** of goods and **develop loyalty** in the customer's mind.
4. **Initiate inventory building** by retailers to give consumers a ready supply.
5. **Get dealers' promotional assistance** by coordinating with personal selling.

### 3. Sales Promotion Techniques
Techniques are originated under **two heads**:

**(a) Vendor or producer originated:**
| Technique | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **(i) In-store activities** | Targeted to give **cash or kind advantage** to customers: price-off pack, premiums, small gifts, cooperative promotion, sampling, coupons, **buy-one-get-one-free**, multipacks, etc. |
| **(ii) Point-of-sale display material** | Materials provided by vendors for placing **near products customers often buy**: special fixtures (paperback publishers' racks, dry battery stand, cold drink racks), plus display signs, leaflets, demonstration, etc. |
| **(iii) Trade incentives** | **Tradeoffs or cash incentives directly provided by vendors to retailers** (benefit may not reach customers): cash discounts, special credit terms, **goods in lieu of cash**, staff incentives, etc. |
| **(iv) Direct promotion to customers** | Vendor promotes **at the consumer's doorsteps**: distribution of **coupons and free samples**. |
| **(v) Pack design** | One of the most important techniques; a retailer may accept a package design **over its contents**; retailers support packs with a **better package design**. |

**(b) Retailer originated:**
| Technique | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **(i) Window display** | Displaying items for sale **in the shop window** to attract customers to the store. |
| **(ii) In-store display** | Includes: **mass display, multiple product promotion, demonstrations, store loyalty cards, free gifts, point-of-sale display, celebrity promotion, joint promotion with other retailers, incentives to staff**, etc. |

### 4. Ways to Influence Customer Buying (10 ways)
1. **Identify the prospective customer** and start a dialogue in a **friendly and humorous** manner.
2. **Collect as much relevant information** as possible about the product's sale presentation.
3. **Enlighten the product's features and advantages** in comparison with the competitor's product.
4. **Present or demonstrate the product** with patience, in an interesting manner.
5. **Attend to objections immediately** and clarify the issues.
6. **Enlighten on the product's warranty period** and assure other sales services if needed.
7. **Maintain continuous relations** with customers to get feedback on product satisfaction.
8. **Improve selling skills** — patience, good communication, reliability, politeness, caring for customers.
9. **Do not lose temper** when dealing with aggressive behaviour of a potential consumer.
10. **Use sales promotion techniques** if required, to convince customers to buy.

### 5. Knowledge of the Nature of Product
The sales associate needs **detailed knowledge of the product** to attend to grievances, complaints, or clarifications.

**Explicit characteristics** — characteristics over which there is **common agreement** (existence and attributes). Five attributes:
| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **(a) Physical configuration** | Every product has **shape, size, density, odour, taste, texture, colour, weight** and other physical attributes. |
| **(b) Associated service** | **Before-sales** service (product demonstration, informing about credit facilities) and **after-sales** service (delivery, installation, spare parts, repair service, warranties). |
| **(c) Package and brand name** | Helps **differentiate** the product from competitors' brands and highlight product features. |
| **(d) Product size** | **Width** = product lines; **depth** = number of items in every product line; **consistency** = similarity among product lines. |
| **(e) Product life cycle** | Stages: **introduction → growth → maturity → decline**. The SA must know which stage the product is in to formulate selling strategies. |

**Implicit characteristics** — **points of disagreement**; every person sees a product differently (perception varies).
| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **(a) Product symbolism** | A product is a **symbol** by virtue of form, size, colour and function; significance varies with needs and social interaction — study product as **status symbol, economy, performance**, etc. |
| **(b) Communication media** | The consumer's **personal interpretation** of symbols, mediated by **culture groups, group influence and personality**; SA must know whether product info is **given or hidden** — what the product is used for, at what rate, made by whom, where available. |
| **(c) Product perception** | Perception is a **physio-psychological process**; how consumers **perceive** products influences their **marketability** → helps reformulate product policies. |
| **(d) Product evaluation** | Tells the retailer about **consumer satisfaction**; evaluation = **comparing the efforts involved and rewards received** by the consumer. |

## Session 3 : Responding to Questions and Comments

> The sales associate is the **only person directly approached by the customer** — training is needed to handle **tough questions and situations**, and gives the flexibility to adapt and add to their own personality.

### 1. When the Sales Associate is Unable to Answer
- **Do not panic** — the responsibility is to **show firmness to make things right**.
- **Never say "I don't know"** — instead say: **"It is a valid question, let me find out the right thing for you."**
- Place the **customer's need as important** over the situation of not knowing the answer.
- Take the question to the **concerned staff or superior**, find the answer, and respond.

### 2. When an Item is Not Available
- **Use positive language** — it avoids nasty or rash reactions.
- ✅ Positive: *"The product will be available next month and I will place an order for you, if needed."*
- ❌ Without positive language: *"The product required is unavailable."*
- Positive language **replaces negative phrases** ("I can't…").

### 3. When Transferring a Call
- Transfer the customer's call to the **concerned department** to help them.
- Customers feel **happy if their concerns are properly addressed**.

### 4. Comments Made on the Product
- Customers may have **valuable comments and suggestions** on usage and improvement.
- **Holding the product's vision is the responsibility of the sales associate**.
- Do **NOT** respond *"We will take a look"* — this gives **false hope** and may disappoint customers later.

### 5. Favours That Cannot Be Granted
- Most requests are reasonable — make every effort to make the customer happy.
- For requests that are difficult to say 'Yes' to, respond with **positive language and very politely**.
- **Showing possible alternatives is one of the best ways to say 'No'.**

### 6. When a Product is Defective
- Receiving a defective product is very discouraging to the customer → **showing empathy is very important**.
- Example response: *"I am so sorry to disappoint you. There was a mistake in the manufacturing process or shipping. Can I send a new one to you?"*
- This response:
  - (a) **Understands the customer's frustrating experience** — explains what the problem is and how an immediate solution can be given.
  - (b) Depending on product and business, the SA can offer: *"Should I send you a full refund?"*

### 7. Closing with a Customer
- A sales associate should always aim to **'close' a conversation** — this does **NOT** mean closing the sale; it means the **customer is happy about the product**.
- Ensure the customer leaves **perfectly happy** — three important things:
  1. **Care about getting what the customer requires.**
  2. Provide the **right information** if they need any.
  3. The **customer determines what is needed** and that the need is **met in the right way**.
- End conversations with phrases like **"I am happy to help you, Sir or Ma'am"**, **"We are happy to serve you"** — they make a big impact.

### 8. Dealing with Angry Customers
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| **(i) Apologise sincerely** | "I am sorry" is a **mandatory** response; make a **personal apology** for the ugly experience. |
| **(ii) Sympathise** | Angry customers need to be **empathised with** — *"I understand how upsetting that must have been."* |
| **(iii) Accept responsibility** | Accept responsibility for the customer's **unhappiness** — it does **not** make the SA 'at fault'. |
| **(iv) Prepare to help** | **Fix the problem** — place a replacement order; even if handled perfectly, if a customer cannot calm down, still try to make the **best effort**. |

## Session 4 : Techniques of Closing a Sale

### 1. Meaning of Closing a Sale
- **Closing a sale** = an important part of the **personal selling process** — **completing a sales transaction** and the **final step while making a sales call**.
- Effectively, it is the **customer signing on the dotted line** of the agreement, completing the sale.
- The sales process is **adaptive** — each situation is different; the SA must understand what is important to each customer and where each is in the buying process.

### 2. Factors Influencing Successful Closing of Sales
| Factor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Salesman's personality** | The **sum total of everything about a sales person** that influences prospects favourably — divided into **physical** (sound health, neat appearance, voice), **mental** (alertness, confidence, enthusiasm, observation), **social** (friendly appearance, courtesy, cooperation, well-mannered) and **character**. |
| **Awareness of company policies** | Basic knowledge of the company — **history, origin and later changes, current affairs, reputation, philosophy and policies** — helps clarify all queries and induce purchases. |
| **Product information** | Every possible information about the product — **models, size, weight, ingredients, packaging, quality, price, discount offered, benefits, replacement, after-sales service**, etc. |
| **Need of customer** | Study the prospective customer's **buying behaviour, likes, dislikes, nature and type** to adopt suitable strategies. |
| **Information on competing products** | Knowledge of product availability and competitors helps **highlight relative merits** — closing becomes easy. |

### 3. Techniques of Closing a Sale
| Technique | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Natural close** ('ask for it' close) | Customer should **not sense a special formula**; closing appears as a **natural development** from the sales conversation — a natural resolution of the sale interview. |
| **The order form close** | Questions asked are **not in direct reference to the purchase**; sound natural and avoid rejection — e.g., *"Sir, shall I make the invoice or bill?"* |
| **The alternative close** | Assumes the customer **will definitely buy** — only design, colour, style, shape are undecided; product presentation is important; present **alternative options** to provoke a **final choice**. |
| **The summary close** | SA **summarises the list of benefits** the customer receives; e.g., *"We have reached the end of sale, so may I highlight the main points so that we could close a deal."* One of the most **natural** methods — used for **unpredictable customers**. |
| **The cautionary tale close** | **Motivates undecided customers** to take a purchase decision by showing the **implication of failing to decide at that very moment**; the SA tries **not to make up stories**. |
| **The final objection close** | Customer may have an **objection right at the end** of the sale process and feel **hesitant to mention it**; the SA must persuade the customer to **communicate the objection**, as it may affect the buying decision. |
| **The conditional close** | For customers **very slow in making decisions** who keep finding excuses to delay; the SA **assures and develops confidence** (help will be at their service) — offered in a **subtle way**. |
| **The lost sales approach** | Don't feel the sale is lost with hesitant/objecting customers — lost sales can be **converted into actual sales with further conversation** and **additional selling efforts**; maintain a sense of balance. |
| **The assumption close** | The customer's **manners, body language or comments** may indicate a decision to buy; gives the customer a chance to make a **painless decision**. |
| **The balance sheet close** | The SA assists an **indecisive prospect** to list on paper the **'arguments for' and 'arguments against'** a particular product choice. |

## 📋 Key Terms (Quick Recap)
- **Sale** — act of selling a product in return for money.
- **Direct sales** — personal demonstration and sale directly to the consumer (single-level / multilevel marketing).
- **Proforma sales** — sales quote prepared in the form of a proforma invoice.
- **Auction sale** — public sale to the highest bidder.
- **B2B sale** — sale from one business to another.
- **EDI** — Electronic Data Interchange, used in electronic-based sales.
- **Sales promotion** — activities supplementing, coordinating, and making effective the efforts of personal selling and advertising.
- **Point-of-sale display** — vendor materials placed near products customers often buy.
- **Trade incentives** — cash incentives given by vendors to retailers.
- **Product life cycle** — introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
- **Explicit characteristics** — product attributes with common agreement (physical configuration, associated service, package/brand, product size, life cycle).
- **Implicit characteristics** — product aspects where perceptions differ (symbolism, communication media, perception, evaluation).
- **Closing a sale** — final step of the sales process — completing the sales transaction.
- **Five factors of closing** — salesman's personality, company policy awareness, product information, customer need, competing product info.
