# Retail Unit 2 — Process of Credit Application (Notes)

> **Credit sale** = goods supplied now, payment **deferred** for a period after supply.
> Offering credit **increases the risk** of being paid late (or not at all) → for **new customers**, always consider **upfront or on-delivery** payment, especially when large amounts have been outlaid to supply the goods.

**Steps the sales associate follows while granting customer credit:**
1. Create credit policy
2. Obtain credit application
3. Check customer references
4. Get a personal guarantee
5. Run a credit check
6. Set limits of credit and payment terms

**Credit requisition contains:** desired products · possible vendors · delivery instructions · accounting details · contact information.

> 1. **Session 1** — Features and Conditions for Credit Sales

## Session 1 : Features and Conditions for Credit Sales

### 1. Meaning of Credit Sales
- **Credit sales** = sales that involve **extending credit** to the customer — the customer **takes the product now** and **agrees to pay later**.
- Credit sales are a type of **trade credit**.
- They create **receivables** (money owed to the company from customers).
- Terms often require payment **within one month of the invoice date**; longer periods are also possible.
- The due amount may be collected in different forms:
  - **Lump-sum payment**
  - **Hire Purchase system**
  - **Installment Purchase System**

### 2. Benefits of Credit Sales (why retail firms sell on credit)
1. **Meet the competition** — when competitors sell on credit, a business must do the same to stay competitive.
2. **Increase in sales** — if competitors do NOT offer credit terms, you gain sales by offering them (customers buy from you instead of paying cash to competitors).
3. **Better customer loyalty** — offering credit shows you **respect and trust** customers to pay before due dates; customers reward this confidence by continuing to buy.

### 3. Characteristics of Credit Sales
- The **transferor** normally deals in **goods and services**.
- The **title of the goods lies with the seller** before it is sold on credit.
- There are **fewer formalities**, especially in case of **open account**.
- It is usually extended for **three months**.
- It depends on **terms imposed by the seller**.
- **No security** is required.
- It can be facilitated with different **financial institutions** with easy terms and at a continuous rate.
- Almost **half of the short financial requirement** of retail is met by this type of mutual trust and good relation.

### 4. Credit Sale Agreement
- A **credit sale agreement** is an agreement for the **sale of goods** under which the **purchase price (or part of it) is payable by installments**.

### 5. Retail Credit Facility
- **Retail credit facility** = a **financing method** that provides a **loan facility** to retail consumers for **purchasing goods and services**.
- It lends funds to customers who want high-valued items but are **short on capital** → enables more consumers to access a retailer's goods.
- ⚠️ **Risk of default** is the main factor behind the **high rate of interest** charged by retail credit facilities.

### 6. Structure of a Credit Agreement
The credit agreement details the **borrower's responsibilities**, and includes:
- Loan warranties
- Lending amounts
- Interest rates
- Loan duration
- Default penalties
- Repayment terms and conditions
- Basic information of the customer
- Purpose of the loan

**Repayment terms of credit facility:**
- **Term loan** → interest rate and **date for repayment**.
- **Revolving loan** → **minimum payment amount** and **recurring payment dates**.
- The agreement details whether interest rates **may change**, and specifies the date on which the loan **matures** (if applicable).

### 7. Conditions Used for Sale of Goods on Credit — Contract of Sale
- A **contract of sale** = a **legal contract** for the exchange of goods, services or property from seller to buyer for an agreed value in **money paid** or the **promise to pay** the same.
- It is made when there is **both an offer as well as agreement** to buy or sell goods for a price.
- It can be made **in writing or by word of mouth**.
- It is a **generic term** which includes:
  - (a) **Sale**, and
  - (b) **Agreement to sell**

**Agreement to sell** = constitutes the **terms and conditions of sale** by the seller to the buyer, including:
- The **amount** at which it is to be sold, and
- The **future date of full payment**.

### 8. Essential Elements of a Contract of Sale
| Element | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **(a) Essential elements of a contract** | All essentials of a valid contract under the **Indian Contract Act, 1872** must be present — parties competent, consent free, object lawful, etc. |
| **(b) Bilateral contract** | At least **two distinct parties** — a seller and a buyer. |
| **(c) Transfer of property** | Objective is to transfer the **general property** (ownership) of the goods from seller to buyer. |
| **(d) Goods** | The **subject-matter** must be some goods; purpose is to transfer property in these goods. |
| **(e) Price — the consideration** | The consideration is **price**, which may be **partly in money and partly in goods**. |

### 9. Difference Between Condition and Warranty
- **Condition** = the **fundamental stipulation** of the contract of sale.
- **Warranty** = an **additional stipulation** — a written guarantee issued to the buyer by the manufacturer/seller to repair or replace the product within a specified time if required.

| Basis for Comparison | Condition | Warranty |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | A requirement or event that should be **performed before the completion of another action**. | An **assurance** given by the seller to the buyer about the **state of the product** — that the prescribed facts are genuine. |
| **Defined in** | **Section 12 (2)** of Indian Sale of Goods Act, 1930 | **Section 12 (3)** of Indian Sale of Goods Act, 1930 |
| **What is it?** | Directly associated with the **objective of the contract**. | A **subsidiary provision** related to the object of the contract. |
| **Result of breach** | **Termination of contract** | **Claim damages** for the breach |
| **Violation** | Violation of a condition can be regarded as a **violation of the warranty**. | Violation of a warranty **does not affect** the condition. |

## Session 2 : Credit Checks and Getting Authorisation

> **Why?** A retail store's primary objective is to **enhance profits**. Credit checks identify potential customers with **repaying capacity** → reduces the **risk of bad debts**.

### 1. Meaning of Credit Check
- A **credit check** = a sort of **search** performed by the retailer to **evaluate a customer's creditworthiness**.
- After the check, the retailer can assess whether the customer can **handle money matters** and **fulfill the requirements for credit**.

### 2. Need for Credit Checking
- **Protects the interests of parties** — ensures each party has the **capacity to enter into a transaction**.
- Retail firms should run a credit check **whenever customers apply for** a loan, hire purchase, credit card, store card or **line of credit**.
- A credit check provides information about the customer's:
  - Mortgage, credit cards, **arranged overdrafts**
  - Personal loans, car finance, hire purchases
  - **Repayment history** of phone accounts, etc.
- **Credit record** = an account of **any type of credit** given for the **last six years** — reveals how much money the customer accessed and whether the customer failed to make any obligations.
- When applying for credit, the customer **gives consent** to check his/her credit file → shows the customer's **address, current commitments and reliability**.
- ✅ Criteria **vary from customer to customer** — based on **financial profile and credit history**.

### 3. Positive Credit Reporting
- In the past, credit checks included only **'negative' behaviour** (when customers failed to meet obligations in financial dealings).
- Now the **credit score** is calculated on this information **together with other credit activity** in the file — e.g., **previous enquiries from credit providers**.
- This gives a clear picture of the customer's finances and shows if the customer has **recovered from any credit difficulties** in the past.

### 4. Procedure for Credit Check on a Prospective Customer
Before extending credit, retailers get a **credit application form filled and signed** by the customer.

Information collected from the customer for the credit check:
| Info | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Release of information** | Retailer needs the customer's **permission** to do a complete credit check. |
| **Signature** | Means the customer has **read, understood and agrees** with all terms and conditions listed. |
| **Address** | A correct address ensures the credit check **pulls up the right information**. |
| **Employment** | Accurate employment info lets the retailer look into the customer's **employment history** (how long at each job). |

### 5. Legal & Company Procedures for Getting Authorisation
- A person's **credit history is private** → a retail firm must seek **permission from the customer before accessing any information legally**.

**How to get a credit report:**
- Customers provide the necessary information for the credit purchase.
- The retailer accesses the information and makes a report.
- The retailer can **directly ask customers** for their creditworthiness and write it in their credit report.

### 6. Steps to Follow Before Granting a Credit
1. **Create credit policy** — every retail store must create its credit policy; includes **payment policies and expectations**.
2. **Customers must complete the credit application** — the application should provide **key information** about the customers.
3. **Check the customer's references** — asking customers to list references helps.
4. **Run credit check** — reveals any **outstanding payments** against the customer.
5. **Request personal guarantee from customer** — not necessary in the case of a retail store, but it is a personal guarantee from the customer.
6. **Take security interest in products** — since customers can refuse to pay as agreed, a retailer should ideally charge security interests.
7. **Set credit limits and payment terms** — set limits for customers who seem creditworthy; decide **how many days after delivery** of products the full payment will be made.

## Session 3 : Processing Credit Requisitions

### 1. Requisition vs Credit Requisition
- **Requisition** = a **formal request** by a **buyer** to the **seller** to sell the desired goods on **conditions agreed upon**. It generally includes the **brand and model name, quantity and the required delivery date**, etc.
- **Credit requisition** = a **request for credit** — when a requisition is made by a buyer to the seller to provide **credit facility** for the purchase of goods.
- A valid credit requisition includes:
  - The **amount and type of credit** requested
  - The applicant's **credit score** and **report**
  - **Means of security** for the loan
- The rules for availing credit facilities are normally provided by the **organisation**; normally, **credit limits are prescribed by the vendor**.

### 2. Credit Limit
- **Credit limit** = the **maximum amount of money** extended through a **line of credit**, and the maximum amount of credit allowed to a customer to purchase in a retail store.

### 3. Information Required in a Credit Requisition Document
| Required info | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Desired items or services** | Customers seeking credit must mention details of the items/services to be purchased on credit. |
| **Possible vendors to fulfill order** | Details of the possible vendors who can supply the required goods. |
| **Budget quotations or proposals received** | Vendor's name and other details of quotations/proposals received. |
| **Delivery instructions** | Information about delivery instructions of the goods. |
| **Capture initial capital details** | Detailed information about initial capital. |
| **Contact information** | Buyers must mention their contact information. |
| **Related accounting detail** | Information about related accounting details. |

### 4. Process of Applications (Performance Criteria)
1. **Identify the customer's needs** and provide credit facilities.
2. **Clearly explain** the features and conditions of credit facilities to the customer.
3. The customer should **submit the application**, and the requisition must be **approved by the retailer**.
4. **Promptly refer to difficulties** in processing applications.
5. Once approved, the requisitions go to the **Sales Manager for authorisation procedures**.

## Session 4 : Techniques for Determining Credit Worthiness

### 1. Meaning of Creditworthiness
- **Creditworthiness** = a **valuation performed by the retailer** that determines the **possibility of a customer to default** based on his/her **earlier debt obligations**.
- It considers factors like **repayment history and credit score**.
- **Credit reporting agencies** measure the creditworthiness of customers.
- Creditworthiness can also be determined by studying and analysing the **income statement and balance sheet** of the business.

### 2. How Customers Can Improve Their Credit Score
- **Pay on time**
- **Pay more than the minimum monthly payment**
- **Pay their debt faster**
- **Reduce the assessment of late fees**

### 3. How to Check a Customer's Creditworthiness
1. **(a) Require a credit application** — every customer should fill the credit application.
2. **(b) Check publicly available information** — every retailer must check the customer's information before issuing credit.
3. **(c) Use credit evaluation tools** — calculates the customer's creditworthiness.

### 4. Techniques — The Five Cs of Credit
When retailers expand credit, they are essentially providing customers **credit equal to the amount of their purchases**. The guidelines include **'The Five Cs of Credit'**:

| C | What it refers to |
|---|---|
| **Character** | The customer's **integrity and willingness to repay** the financial obligation. |
| **Capacity** | The customer's **cash inflow and ability to repay** the debt. |
| **Capital** | The customer's **financial net worth**. |
| **Collateral** | The **security against credit**. |
| **Conditions** | The **economic, family and personal conditions** of customers. |

## 📋 Key Terms (Quick Recap)
- **Receivables** — money owed to the company from customers.
- **Credit sale agreement** — sale of goods where price (or part) is payable by installments.
- **Retail credit facility** — financing method giving loans to retail consumers for buying goods/services.
- **Contract of sale** — legal contract to transfer goods/services/property for money or promise to pay.
- **Condition** — fundamental stipulation (Section 12(2), Sale of Goods Act 1930); breach → termination.
- **Warranty** — additional stipulation/assurance (Section 12(3)); breach → claim damages.
- **Credit check** — search by retailer to evaluate a customer's creditworthiness.
- **Credit record** — account of any credit given for the last six years.
- **Requisition** — formal request by buyer to seller for goods on agreed conditions.
- **Credit requisition** — a request for credit.
- **Credit limit** — maximum amount of credit extended to a customer.
- **Creditworthiness** — valuation of the possibility of a customer defaulting.
- **Five Cs of Credit** — Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions.
