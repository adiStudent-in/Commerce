## UNIT 1: Fundamentals of Retailing

### Q1. Who is a retailer? Why is retailer called an intermediary?

**Ans:** Retailer is a merchant who sells goods directly to ultimate consumers (end-users) in small quantities.
Retailer is an intermediary because he operates between wholesaler and consumer. He buys from wholesaler, maintains direct contact with both producer and consumer, performs buying, selling, grading and collects information about customer wants.

### Q2. Explain the distribution channel with example.

**Ans:** Manufacturer -&gt; Wholesaler -&gt; Retailer -&gt; Consumer.
Wholesaler buys in large quantity from manufacturer and sells in small quantity to retailer. Retailer sells in small quantity to final consumer. Example: Parle factory -&gt; wholesale dealer -&gt; kirana shop -&gt; you.

### Q3. Differentiate between organised and unorganised retailing (6 points).

**Ans:**

1. Concept: Organised is modern systematic sale under one roof. Unorganised is traditional family-run sale.
2. Registration: Organised is compulsory registered for tax. Unorganised has no registration.
3. Items: Organised keeps large variety. Unorganised keeps few items.
4. Capital and Size: Organised needs very high capital and large store. Unorganised needs less capital and small shop.
5. Staff: Organised has large staff with specialisation and job security. Unorganised has few persons doing all work with no security.
6. Example: Organised - supermarket, mall, hypermarket. Unorganised - kirana, paan shop, haat vendor.

### Q4. What is a hypermarket? How is it different from supermarket and departmental store?

**Ans:** Hypermarket combines departmental store + grocery supermarket in one very large store. It sells appliances, clothing and groceries together.
Supermarket is self-service store for food + household products in aisles. Departmental store sells wide range in separate departments with decentralised buying and centralised selling, huge capital, home delivery and restaurant inside.

### Q5. Explain retail stores based on ownership with examples.

**Ans:**

1. Independent retailer: Owned by one individual, small shop, direct contact. Ex: Baniya, Kirana, Paanwala.
2. Corporate chain: Same owner, many stores at different places. Ex: Reliance, Bata, Food World.
3. Franchising: Owner gives trademark and system to franchisee for fee. Ex: McDonald's, Pizza Hut.
4. Consumer cooperative: Owned and managed by consumers themselves. Ex: Apna Bazaar, Mumbai.

### Q6. Explain any five functions of a retailer.

**Ans:**

1. Breaking bulk: Opens large packs and sells as per customer need.
2. Product information: Explains how product meets need, increases sales.
3. Customer services: Home delivery, gift wrap, credit, after-sales help.
4. Pleasant experience: Right place, right time, welcome, lighting, music, courteous staff.
5. Feedback to producer: Shares customer needs first-hand so producer improves product.

### Q7. Who is a customer? How is customer different from consumer?

**Ans:** Customer is the person who purchases goods or services. Two types: entrepreneur who buys for resale, end-user who buys for consumption.
Customer buys, consumer uses. Example: Mother buys chocolate (customer), child eats it (consumer). You can be both when you buy and use yourself.

### Q8. Explain five types of customers.

**Ans:**

1. Loyal: Less in number, give more sales, visit regularly.
2. Discount: Buy only when discount is offered or only cheap products.
3. Impulsive: No fixed list, buy what looks good at that moment.
4. Need-based: Buy only specific habitual need product.
5. Wandering: New, only enquire and compare, least interest in buying.

### Q9. What are 4Ps of customer service? Explain with example.

**Ans:**

1. Promptness: Deliver on committed time without delay.
2. Politeness: Welcome customer, use name, listen respectfully, keep customer need first.
3. Professionalism: Be knowledgeable, competent, handle without fear or frustration.
4. Personalisation: Remember name and preference, give unique service. Example: greeting regular customer by name and showing his favourite brand increases loyalty.

### Q10. What is customer satisfaction? Why is it important?

**Ans:** Customer satisfaction means value and service meet or exceed customer expectations.
Importance: shows repurchase and loyalty, separates from competitors, reduces customer loss, increases lifetime value, reduces negative word of mouth, helps retain customers.

### Q11. Define skill. Explain essentials of skill development.

**Ans:** Skill is the ability to do a task with pre-determined result within given time and energy with expertise.
Essentials: 1. Taking stock - check where you stand, list personal assets. 2. Creating a plan - make development plan to reach goal. 3. Creating environment - daily self-learning, note mistakes, interact with peers, take care of health.

### Q12. Explain any six skills of a sales associate.

**Ans:**

1. Patience: Stay calm with difficult customers.
2. Attentiveness: Be accurate in billing and display.
3. Communication: Listen well, explain benefits clearly.
4. Product knowledge: Know product fully to answer correctly.
5. Time management: Solve problem in limited time.
6. Goal orientation: Focus on task and end result and use strategy. Others: Positive language, Acting skills, Resilience.

### Q13. Explain duties and responsibilities of a sales associate.

**Ans:** Sales associate is also called customer service associate.
Duties (4): handling complaints within authority, assisting sales and restocking, clerical tasks like billing and payments, job-specific work like greeting and operating cash register.
Responsibilities (5): interact face-to-face/phone/email, work directly in store, resolve issues like wrong price and returns, listen effectively to solve fast, remain friendly and polite.

## UNIT 2: Process of Credit Application

### Q14. What is credit sale? What are its three benefits?

**Ans:** Credit sale means customer takes product now and agrees to pay later. It is trade credit and creates receivables (money owed by customers).
Benefits: 1. Meets competition when competitors give credit. 2. Increases sales when competitors do not give credit. 3. Builds loyalty as it shows trust and respect.

### Q15. Explain characteristics of credit sales.

**Ans:** Seller deals in goods, title stays with seller before sale, fewer formalities in open account, usually for three months, terms fixed by seller, no security required, help from financial institutions on easy terms, almost half of short retail finance met through trust.

### Q16. What is credit sale agreement and retail credit facility?

**Ans:** Credit sale agreement is agreement where price is payable by installments.
Retail credit facility is financing method giving loan to retail consumers to buy high-value goods when short on capital. It helps more consumers access goods. High interest is charged because risk of default is high.

### Q17. What are five essentials of a contract of sale?

**Ans:**

1. Valid contract as per Indian Contract Act 1872 - competent parties, free consent, lawful object.
2. Bilateral - at least two distinct parties, seller and buyer.
3. Transfer of property from seller to buyer.
4. Subject matter must be goods.
5. Consideration must be price, partly money and partly goods allowed. Contract can be written or oral.

### Q18. Differentiate between condition and warranty.

**Ans:** Condition is fundamental stipulation before completion of action. Warranty is additional assurance about product quality.
Condition defined in Sec 12(2), Warranty in Sec 12(3) of Sale of Goods Act 1930. Condition linked directly to objective, warranty is subsidiary. Breach of condition ends contract, breach of warranty gives only damages. Violation of condition can become violation of warranty, but violation of warranty does not affect condition.

### Q19. What is credit check? Why is it needed?

**Ans:** Credit check is search by retailer to evaluate creditworthiness and repaying capacity of customer. It reduces risk of bad debts.
Needed to protect both parties, required for loan, hire purchase, credit card applications, gives history of mortgage, loans, overdrafts, phone payments. Credit record shows last six years credit. Customer consent is compulsory before checking.

### Q20. Explain steps to be followed before granting credit.

**Ans:**

1. Create credit policy with payment expectations.
2. Get credit application filled by customer.
3. Check customer references.
4. Run credit check for outstanding payments.
5. Take personal guarantee if needed.
6. Take security interest in products.
7. Set credit limit and payment terms - limit for creditworthy customers and days for full payment after delivery.

### Q21. What is requisition, credit requisition and credit limit?

**Ans:** Requisition is formal request by buyer to seller to sell desired goods on agreed conditions with brand, quantity, delivery date.
Credit requisition is request for credit facility with amount, type, credit score, report and security.
Credit limit is maximum amount allowed through line of credit, normally prescribed by vendor.

### Q22. What information is required in credit requisition document?

**Ans:** Desired items or services, possible vendors, budget quotations with vendor name, delivery instructions, initial capital details, contact information, related accounting details.

### Q23. Explain process of credit application (5 steps).

**Ans:** 1. Identify customer need and provide facility. 2. Explain features and conditions clearly. 3. Get application submitted and approved by retailer. 4. Refer difficulties promptly. 5. After approval send to Sales Manager for authorisation.

### Q24. What is creditworthiness? Explain Five Cs of credit.

**Ans:** Creditworthiness is valuation by retailer of possibility of customer default based on repayment history and credit score. Measured by Credit Reporting Agencies.
Five Cs: 1. Character - integrity and willingness to repay. 2. Capacity - cash inflow and ability to repay. 3. Capital - financial net worth. 4. Collateral - security against credit. 5. Conditions - economic, family and personal conditions.
Score improves by paying on time, paying more than minimum, paying debt faster, reducing late fees.

## UNIT 3: Mechanism for Customers to Choose Right Products

### Q25. Explain various methods of selling (any six).

**Ans:**

1. Direct sales: Direct personal demonstration to consumer, single-level and multilevel.
2. Proforma sales: Sales quote as proforma invoice.
3. Agency-based: Agent negotiates for principal for commission. Ex: telemarketing, consignment.
4. Travelling salesman: Visits shops with samples, also called hawker.
5. Auction sale: Public sale to highest bidder.
6. B2B sale: Sale from one business to another.
7. Electronic sale: Web-based B2B and B2C using EDI.
8. Indirect sales: Sale by third-party partner, not own staff.

### Q26. Why do retailers arrange and rearrange products? Explain floor plans.

**Ans:** Systematic section-wise arrangement gives clear view, easy picking and improves profit.
Rearranged for: repeat customers get fresh look and visit again, sales promotion increases customers, brand image through monthly change, seasonal display in one area.
Floor plans: Straight - straight line, most common in India, economical. Diagonal - more visibility, needs small space. Angular - spacious, high-end retailers, few products. Geometric - racks for trendy products like cosmetics, watches.

### Q27. What is sales promotion? What are its objectives?

**Ans:** Sales promotion is fastest growing marketing area giving special offers like discounts to improve sales and profit. It supports personal selling and advertising and stimulates buying.
Objectives: stimulate inquiries, increase trials for satisfaction, encourage repurchase and loyalty, build retailer inventory for ready supply, get dealer help with personal selling.

### Q28. Explain vendor-originated and retailer-originated promotion techniques.

**Ans:** Vendor-originated (5): in-store activities like coupons, buy one get one, sampling; point-of-sale display like racks and signs near products; trade incentives like cash discount to retailers; direct promotion like doorstep coupons and samples; pack design with better packaging.
Retailer-originated (2): window display to attract inside store, in-store display like mass display, loyalty cards, free gifts, celebrity and joint promotion.

### Q29. Differentiate between explicit and implicit characteristics of product.

**Ans:** Explicit means common agreement on existence and attributes.
Five: physical configuration (shape, size, weight, colour), associated service (before-demo and credit, after-delivery and repair), pack and brand name, product size (Width = product lines, Depth = items per line, Consistency = similarity), life cycle (introduction, growth, maturity, decline).
Implicit means perception differs person to person.
Four: symbolism (status, economy by form and colour), communication media (culture and group influence), perception (physio-psychological process affecting marketability), evaluation (compare efforts vs rewards to know satisfaction).

### Q30. How should a sales associate respond when he has no answer, item unavailable, or product defective?

**Ans:** No answer: Do not panic, never say I don't know. Say it is a valid question, let me find out for you. Take to superior and reply.
Unavailable: Use positive language. Say product will be available next month, I will order for you. Avoid saying unavailable. Positive replaces I can't.
Defective: Show empathy. Say sorry for disappointment, mistake in manufacturing or shipping, can I send new one or full refund. Understand frustration and give immediate solution.

### Q31. How should a sales associate handle angry customers?

**Ans:** 1. Apologise sincerely with personal I am sorry. 2. Sympathise - I understand how upsetting it was. 3. Accept responsibility for unhappiness without feeling at fault. 4. Prepare to help - place replacement order and make best effort even if customer stays upset. Always remain polite, close conversation with happy to help you.

### Q32. What is closing a sale? Explain factors for successful closing.

**Ans:** Closing means completing sales transaction, final step of personal selling when customer signs agreement and gives order. Process is adaptive to each customer situation.
Factors (5): salesman personality - physical health and appearance, mental alertness and confidence, social friendly and courteous nature; awareness of company history and policies; full product information on model, price, discount, service; knowledge of customer likes and buying behaviour; information on competing products to highlight merits.

### Q33. Explain any six techniques of closing a sale.

**Ans:**

1. Natural close (ask for it): Close as natural end of conversation without formula feel.
2. Order form close: Indirect question like shall I make bill.
3. Alternative close: Assume will buy, offer choice of design or colour.
4. Summary close: Summarise benefits to close, best for unpredictable customers.
5. Conditional close: For slow decision makers, assure best service subtly.
6. Balance sheet close: List for vs against on paper for indecisive customer. Others: cautionary tale to decide now, final objection to bring out last doubt, lost sales to convert with talk, assumption from body language.
