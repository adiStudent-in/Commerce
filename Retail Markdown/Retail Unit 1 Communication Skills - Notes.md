# Unit 1 — Communication Skills (Employability Skills · Notes)

> **Communication** = the *sharing* of information between two or more people to reach a **common understanding**.
> Communication skills = the ability to **speak and write properly** and **listen and read well** in any situation.

---

## Session 1 : Introduction to Communication

### 1. Why Communication Matters
Communication skills are needed to:
- **Inform** – give facts or information (e.g., telling a friend the exam timetable).
- **Influence** – change or convince someone (e.g., negotiating a lower price with a shopkeeper).
- **Express feelings** – share emotions (e.g., your happiness about scoring well).

### 2. Elements of Communication
Communication is a **two-way exchange** — **giving** and **receiving** information:
- **Giving:** Speaking, Writing
- **Receiving:** Listening, Reading

### 3. Perspectives in Communication
**Perspectives** = our ideas, views, or fixed ways of thinking that affect communication (e.g., thinking your father is strict even when he is polite).

**8 factors that act as barriers:**

| Factor | How it blocks communication |
|--------|------------------------------|
| **Language** | Wrong words, unfamiliar language, lack of detail (e.g., a Hindi speaker vs a Mandarin speaker) |
| **Visual Perception** | Brain making sense of what we see (e.g., reading a half-drawn picture wrongly) |
| **Past Experience** | Past hurt stops clarity ("This shopkeeper cheated me before") |
| **Prejudice** | Fixed ideas ("No one in my class likes me") |
| **Feelings** | Emotions like distrust or lack of interest ("I'm not feeling well, I don't want to talk") |
| **Environment** | Noise or disturbance (loud music at a function) |
| **Personal factors** | Fear, low confidence, habits |
| **Culture** | Same sign may mean different things (a thumbs-up = 'good job' for some, insult for others) |

### 4. The 7 Cs of Effective Communication
**Clear · Concise · Concrete · Correct · Coherent · Complete · Courteous**

## Session 2 : Verbal Communication

**Verbal communication** = sharing information **using words**. Two forms:

| Type | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral / Spoken** | Talking, where you can see/listen | Face-to-face talk, phone calls, classroom teaching, public speeches |
| **Written** | Written or typed words | Letters, notes, emails, SMS, books, newspapers |

**Advantages:** Easy and quick; get a quick response; easier to exchange ideas.
**Disadvantages:** Cultural differences (different languages, accents, colloquial phrases); meaning can become confusing if the right words are not used.

### Public Speaking — The 3 Ps
1. **Prepare** – Think about your topic, your listeners' needs, and the best way to explain; write what you plan to say.
2. **Practice** – Practice alone, in front of a mirror, then with family/friends; speak clearly, loudly, at the right speed.
3. **Perform** – Take deep breaths if nervous; think of your prep and speak confidently.

## 📌 Session 3 : Non-Verbal Communication

**Non-verbal communication** = the message we send **without using words** — through expressions, gestures, and body posture.

**Why it's important:** Most of our day-to-day communication is non-verbal (body movements and voice control). The right gestures make your message stronger and help you understand your audience's reaction.

### Types of Non-Verbal Communication

| Type | What it means | How to use it well |
|---|---|---|
| **Facial Expression** | Shows feelings (smile = happy, gloomy face = sad) | Keep face relaxed; match your expression to your words; nod when agreeing |
| **Posture** | Position of the body; shows confidence (& feelings) | Sit/stand straight, shoulders relaxed, hands by your sides |
| **Gestures / Body Language** | Movements of hands/head (waving, pointing) | Avoid pointing fingers; keep hands out of pockets; bend head slightly to show attention (biting nails = nervousness) |
| **Touch** | Shake hands, pat on the back | Firm handshake shows confidence; avoid stroking hair, scratching nose during formal talk |
| **Space** | Distance between two people | Maintain the right space based on how close the relationship is (formal vs informal) |
| **Eye Contact** | Looking at the speaker shows interest | Look at the speaker, keep a pleasant face, break the look every few seconds |
| **Paralanguage** | Tone, speed, volume of voice | Use proper tone & volume; moderate speed (too fast = excited/nervous, too slow = serious/sad) |

### Visual Communication
Sending/understanding messages **only through images or pictures**. Its advantage: **no language needed** — simple, easy, same everywhere.

**Examples:** Traffic lights (red = stop, yellow = wait, green = go), no-horn sign, ladies/gents toilet signs, railway-crossing sign, flammable/substance signs, slippery-surface sign, pause icon, no-smoking sign.

## 📌 Session 4 : Writing Skills — Parts of Speech

- **Sentence** = a group of words that communicates a **complete thought** ("Raju goes to school"). Starts with a capital letter; ends with `?`, `.`, or `!`.
- **Phrase** = a group of words that does NOT make complete sense ("Raju goes").

### Capitalisation Rule: **M·I·N·T·S**

| Letter | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Months | "I go to college in **J**une." |
| **I** | The letter "I" | "**I** play tennis." |
| **N** | Names of people, places, days | "**T**his **T**uesday, **V**idya will be in **R**ajasthan." |
| **T** | Titles before names | "**D**r Shah and **M**r Patel work together." |
| **S** | Starting letter of every sentence | "**T**he cat ran out of the house." |

### Punctuation Marks

| Mark | Sign | Use | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full stop** | `.` | End of a sentence; short form of long words | "Dr." for doctor |
| **Comma** | `,` | Pause in a sentence; separate listing items | "After the menu, I ordered food." / "apples, oranges and grapes" |
| **Question mark** | `?` | End of an answer/question/question | "What is your name?" |
| **Exclamation mark** | `!` | Strong feeling — surprise, shock, anger | "You are late!" |
| **Apostrophe** | `'` | Shows possession; shortened form | "That is Divya's pen." / "Let's go" = Let us go |

### The 8 Parts of Speech
- **Noun** – naming word for a person/place/thing/idea (dog, table, India, Sanjay)
- **Pronoun** – in place of a noun (I, She, He, They, You)
- **Adjective** – describes a noun (small, blue, sharp, loud)
- **Verb** – shows action (run, eat, think, sit)
- **Adverb** – adds meaning to a verb/adjective; answers *How? How often? When? Where?* (easily, always, inside)
- **Preposition** – before a noun to show relation (on, at, under, in) — a *supporting* part of speech
- **Conjunction** – joins words/phrases/sentences (and, or, but)
- **Interjection** – expresses strong emotion (Wow! Oh! Oh no! Help!)

### Supporting Parts of Speech
| Type | Use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Articles** | `a, an, the` — placed before nouns (`an` before vowel sound, `a` before consonant sound, `the` for specific) | *A* book, *An* apple, *the* sun |
| **Conjunctions** | Join two nouns/phrases/sentences | and, or, but |
| **Prepositions** | Connect one word with another; answer *where, when, how* (on, at, under, in) | "The cat is **on** the roof." |
| **Interjections** | Express strong emotions, often with `!` | Wow! Oh! Thanks! Help! |

## 📌 Session 5 : Writing Skills – Sentences

### Parts of a Sentence
- **Subject** – the person/thing that *does* the action
- **Verb** – the action
- **Object** – the person/thing that *receives* the action (`"Divya reads a book."` → Subject: Divya, Verb: reads, Object: a book)

**Types of objects:**
- **Direct object** – directly acted on by the verb (answers "what?"). *"Abdul gave a **gift** to his mother."* → gift = direct
- **Indirect object** – receives the direct object (answers "to whom?"). *...to his **mother**"* → mother = indirect

### Active vs Passive Voice
- **Active voice:** subject does the action → *"Sanjay broke the glass."*
- **Passive voice:** subject receives the action → *"The glass was broken by Sanjay."*

### 4 Types of Sentences (by purpose)
| Type | Purpose | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| **Statement / Declarative** | States a fact / gives information | `.` full stop |
| **Question / Interrogative** | Asks a question | `?` |
| **Exclamation / Exclamatory** | Strong emotion (joy, sadness, fear) | `!` |
| **Order / Imperative** | Gives an order, command, request, advice | `.` or `!` |

### Paragraphs
A group of sentences sharing a common idea = a **paragraph**. Change paragraph when the idea changes.

## 📌 Session 6 : Pronunciation Basics

**Pronunciation** = the way you say a word (*spelling* is how you write it). Knowing it helps you speak clearly and confidently.

**Phonetics** = the study of the sounds we make when we speak.

- English has **26 letters** (A–Z) but **more than 26 sounds** — so spelling doesn't always match pronunciation (e.g., the letter "a" differs in *cat, car, call*).

**Three types of sounds:**
1. **Vowels** – 5 letters (a, e, i, o, u) but **12 vowel sounds**. Long vs short (sleep vs slip).
2. **Diphthongs** – a combination of two vowel sounds (house, boat, cow, loud).
3. **Consonants** – any sound that is neither a vowel nor a diphthong (p, t, k, l, etc.). *(pen, book)*

| Sound Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Vowels** | 5 letters (a,e,i,o,u) → 12 vowel sounds; made with an open mouth (bit, sleep, book) |
| **Diphthongs** | Combination of two vowels made from one to another (house 'ou', boat 'oa', cow) |
| **Consonants** | Any sound that is not a vowel/diphthong (p, t, k, l sounds) |

## 📌 Session 7 : Greetings and Introductions

**Greetings** = words we use before we start talking to someone. **Introduction** = telling others (or ourselves) about ourselves to people who don't know us.

### Greetings
- **Formal greetings:** For people we don't know, seniors, elders, teachers, customers — "Good morning Sir/Ma'am!", "Hello Sir. How are you?" (used in schools, colleges, offices).
- **Informal greetings:** For friends, family, known people — "Hey!", "Hi!", "Hey, how are you doing?"

### Greetings by time of day
| Time | Greeting |
|---|---|
| Early morning – 11.59 am | Good **morning** |
| 12 pm – 5 pm | Good **afternoon** |
| 5 pm – midnight | Good **evening** (we say "Good night" at the *end* of a conversation) |

### Introducing Yourself & Others
- **Introducing yourself:** tell your name + general info (hometown, job/subjects, interests). Look at the person and smile.
- **Introducing others:** tell their name, what they do, how you know them, or something interesting about them.

## Session 8 : Talking About Self

You may need to talk/write about yourself. Use **nouns** (naming words — Ali, Delhi, football) and **verbs** (doing words — play, like, eat). Verbs ending in `-ing` can act as nouns (swimming, cycling).

**A self-introduction covers:** Name → What you do → Age → Hometown → Likes → Dislikes → Strengths → Weaknesses.

> Example: *"I am Harish Sethi. I am 15 years old and I live in Agra. I like playing chess and I dislike summertime. I can write stories very well."*

### Filling a Form
A **form** = a printed/typed page with blank spaces for information. A form usually has: **Name, Date of Birth, Address, PIN code, Signature**.

Example: *Date of Birth: 05-12-1999 · Address: Sukh Nivas, No. 13, Raja Nagar, Agra · Pin: 282001*

## Session 9 : Asking Questions I (5W + 1H)

A **question** = a request for information. Asking questions helps us **gain knowledge, check correctness, avoid confusion, and start conversations**.

To get **complete information**, use the **5W + 1H** method:

| Question word | Used to ask about | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | People | Who is the new student? |
| **Where** | A place | Where does she live? |
| **When** | Time | When did she join school? |
| **What** | A thing, idea, action | What is her favourite subject? |
| **Why** | Reason | Why is she not talking to anyone? |
| **How** | Method / way | How can I help her? |

---

## Session 10 : Asking Questions II — Open & Closed Questions

**There are two types of questions:**

1. **Close-ended** – answered with a *yes* or *no* ("Do you have a TV at home?")
   - Form by adding a **helping/auxiliary verb** (Be, Do, Have) before the subject, or by **swapping subject & verb.**
     - "I cook dinner" → "Do I cook dinner?" (auxiliary)
     - "He is there" → "Is he there?" (subject-verb swap)
   - Note: the helping verb changes with the subject, but the **main verb stays unchanged**.

2. **Open-ended** – answers have more detail, not limited to yes/no. Formed with question words (What, Why, Who, How, When, Where).
   - Example: "What did you do?" → "I went to the park."
