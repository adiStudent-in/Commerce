# Class 11 Accountancy – Chapter 1: Introduction to Accounting

> **In one line:** Accounting means keeping a proper record of money matters of a business, so the owner can know profit, loss, and money position clearly.

---

## 1. What is Accounting?

Imagine you run a small shop.

Daily many things happen:
- You buy goods for ₹5,000
- You sell goods for ₹7,000
- You pay shop rent ₹2,000
- A customer takes goods on credit (will pay later)

After 1 month, you will forget everything. So you need a notebook to write it properly.

**Accounting is that proper notebook system.**

### Simple Definition:

> Accounting is the art of recording, classifying, summarising, analysing and communicating the money transactions of a business.

Break it into simple words:

| Word | Simple Meaning |
|------|----------------|
| **Recording** | Writing daily money dealings in a book (Journal) |
| **Classifying** | Putting same type of items together (Ledger) |
| **Summarising** | Making final summary – Profit & Loss Account and Balance Sheet |
| **Analysing** | Checking – is business earning well or not? |
| **Communicating** | Telling results to owner, bank, government |

**Only money dealings are recorded.** If a worker is hardworking but it is not in money terms, accounting will not record it.

---

## 2. Book-Keeping vs Accounting

Students get confused here. Very simple:

| Basis | Book-Keeping | Accounting |
|-------|--------------|------------|
| **Meaning** | Just writing daily transactions | Full work – record + classify + summary + report |
| **Work** | First step, routine work | Wider work, includes book-keeping |
| **Skill needed** | Basic skill is enough | Special knowledge and analysis skill |
| **Done by** | Junior staff | Senior accountant |
| **Example** | Writing in notebook – sold pen for ₹10 | Finding monthly profit from that notebook |

**Easy trick to remember:**
> Book-keeping = writing. Accounting = writing + understanding + reporting.

---

## 3. Accounting Process – Step by Step

Think of it like making food:

```
1. Identify → 2. Record → 3. Classify → 4. Summarise → 5. Analyse → 6. Communicate
```

1. **Identify** – Find which dealings are money dealings. Example: buying stock for ₹10,000 is recorded. Quarrel with worker is not recorded.
2. **Record** – Write them date-wise in Journal.
3. **Classify** – Open separate accounts for sales, purchases, rent, salary in Ledger.
4. **Summarise** – Make Trial Balance, Trading and Profit & Loss Account, Balance Sheet.
5. **Analyse** – Compare this year profit with last year. Find strong and weak points.
6. **Communicate** – Give reports to owner, bank, tax office.

---

## 4. Objectives of Accounting

Why do we do accounting? 4 main goals:

### 1. Keep proper record
To keep full and correct record of all money dealings. Human memory is weak, records are proof.

### 2. Find profit or loss
At year end, owner wants to know: Did I earn or lose? Trading and Profit & Loss Account tells this.

### 3. Show financial position
Owner wants to know: What I own and what I owe? Balance Sheet shows assets and liabilities.

### 4. Give information to users
To give useful information to owner, bank, investors, government for taking decisions.

**One-line memory trick:** Record – Profit – Position – Information.

---

## 5. Accounting as a Source of Information

Accounting works like an information machine.

Business gives raw data (bills, vouchers) → Accounting cleans it → Gives useful reports.

Example:
- Raw data: 100 bills of sales
- Useful information: Total sales this month = ₹2,00,000, profit = ₹30,000

This information helps in:
- Taking decisions (should I open a new shop?)
- Planning future (how much stock to buy?)
- Controlling costs (salary cost is too high, reduce it)

So accounting is called an **information system**.

---

## 6. Types of Accounting Information

Mainly 3 types:

| Type | What it tells | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **1. Information about Profit** | Is business earning? | Net profit ₹50,000 this year |
| **2. Information about Financial Position** | What business owns and owes? | Shop owns stock ₹1 lakh, owes loan ₹40,000 |
| **3. Information about Cash Flow** | Where cash came from and where it went? | ₹20,000 came from sales, ₹8,000 spent on rent |

Branches linked to this:
- **Financial Accounting** – records profit and position
- **Cost Accounting** – finds cost of one product
- **Management Accounting** – helps managers take decisions

---

## 7. Users of Accounting Information and Their Needs

Two groups: Inside people and Outside people.

### A. Internal Users (inside the business)

**1. Owners**
Need: Is my money safe? Am I getting profit? Should I put more money?
Example: Shop owner checks yearly profit.

**2. Management (managers)**
Need: Which product sells more? Where to cut cost? What price to fix?
Example: Manager sees Maggi sells fast, so orders more Maggi.

**3. Employees**
Need: Is company stable? Will I get salary hike and bonus?
Example: Workers ask for bonus when company profit is high.

### B. External Users (outside the business)

**4. Investors**
Need: Should I invest money here? Will I get good return?
Example: A person wants to buy shares, checks profit reports first.

**5. Lenders and Banks**
Need: Will this business return my loan with interest?
Example: Bank checks Balance Sheet before giving loan.

**6. Creditors (suppliers)**
Need: Will this shop pay me on time if I give goods on credit?
Example: Wholesaler checks payment history before giving goods.

**7. Government**
Need: Is tax paid correctly? Is business following law?
Example: Income Tax office checks profit to fix tax.

**8. Customers**
Need: Is company stable? Will I get regular supply and good service?
Example: Big customer gives long order only to stable company.

**9. Researchers and Public**
Need: For study, comparison, and social awareness.
Example: Student studies profit of 5 companies for a project.

**Memory table:**

| User | Wants to know |
|------|---------------|
| Owner | Profit? |
| Management | Decision? |
| Employees | Salary safe? |
| Investors | Return? |
| Bank | Loan safe? |
| Supplier | Payment on time? |
| Government | Tax correct? |
| Customer | Supply regular? |

## 8. Qualitative Characteristics of Accounting Information

Good accounting information must have 4 qualities:

### 1. Reliability
Information should be true and honest. Based on bills and proof.
Example: Sales shown as ₹1,00,000 must match actual bills.

### 2. Relevance
Information should be useful for taking decisions. Old useless data has no value.
Example: Current year profit is relevant for decisions, 20-year-old data is less useful.

### 3. Understandability
Reports should be simple so users can understand easily.
Example: Profit and Loss Account should be clear, not confusing.

### 4. Comparability
Reports should allow comparison – this year vs last year, one company vs another.
Example: Profit rose from ₹40,000 to ₹60,000 – we can compare growth.

**Trick to remember 4 qualities:** R-R-U-C = Reliable, Relevant, Understandable, Comparable.

---

## 9. Advantages of Accounting

1. **Complete record:** All dealings are safely recorded, no need to remember.
2. **Profit known:** Owner knows exact profit or loss.
3. **Position known:** Owner knows assets, liabilities, and capital.
4. **Helps in decisions:** Reports guide future plans.
5. **Proof in court:** Proper books work as proof in disputes.
6. **Helps in loan:** Bank gives loan easily if books are clean.
7. **Helps in tax:** Correct tax can be calculated.
8. **Helps in comparison:** Compare performance year by year.

---

## 10. Limitations of Accounting

Accounting is useful but not perfect. 5 limits to remember:

1. **Only money matters recorded:** Good workers, happy customers, shop location value are not recorded because they are not in money terms.
2. **Based on estimates:** Some items like depreciation are guesses, not exact.
3. **Shows past, not future:** Books tell what happened last year, not what will happen next year.
4. **Price change ignored:** Old building bought for ₹1 lakh still shown at ₹1 lakh, even if market value is ₹10 lakh now.
5. **Can be manipulated:** A dishonest accountant can hide true profit (window dressing).

**Simple example:** A shop has very honest and hardworking staff. This is a big strength, but accounting will not show it because it cannot be measured in rupees.

---

## 11. Role of Accounting in Business

Why is accounting called the language of business?

1. **Helper in planning:** Tells how much stock, cash, and staff are needed.
2. **Helper in control:** Shows wasteful expenses. Owner can control them.
3. **Helper in decisions:** Should price increase? Should new branch open? Reports guide.
4. **Helper in trust:** Clean books build trust with banks, suppliers, and investors.
5. **Helper in legal work:** Needed for tax filing, court cases, and company law.

> In short: No accounting = business runs blind. With accounting = business runs with clear eyes.

## 📝 Key Terms to Remember

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|------|----------------|
| **Transaction** | Any money dealing of business (sale, purchase, rent) |
| **Capital** | Money owner puts into business |
| **Assets** | What business owns (cash, stock, machine) |
| **Liabilities** | What business owes (loan, unpaid bills) |
| **Revenue** | Money earned from main work (sales) |
| **Expense** | Money spent to run business (rent, salary) |
| **Profit** | Revenue minus expense, when earning is more |
| **Voucher** | Written proof or bill for a transaction |

## 🔄 Quick Recap

```
Accounting = Record + Classify + Summarise + Analyse + Communicate

Objectives = Record keeping + Profit finding + Position showing + Information giving

Users:
Inside = Owner, Management, Employees
Outside = Investors, Bank, Suppliers, Government, Customers, Researchers

Good information = Reliable + Relevant + Understandable + Comparable

Advantage = Full record, profit known, helps in loan, tax, decisions
Limitation = Only money items, based on estimates, past data, ignores price change
```
