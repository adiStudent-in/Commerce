# Class 11 Accountancy – Chapter 3: Theory Base of Accounting

> **In one line:** Accounting is not done randomly — it follows fixed rules called GAAP, assumptions and concepts, a base (cash or accrual), standards (AS/IndAS) and GST rules, so every business shows true and comparable profit.

---

## 1. GAAP – Rules of Accounting

Imagine every shopkeeper kept books in his own style.

One shows profit ₹50,000, another shows ₹80,000 for the same work — only because methods are different. Bank and tax office will be confused.

So accounting needs common rules. These rules are called **GAAP**.

### Meaning

> GAAP = Generally Accepted Accounting Principles. Common rules, assumptions, concepts and procedures used for recording business transactions.

- Developed from long experience, law, ICAI and practice.
- Followed in Journal, Ledger, Trial Balance and Final Accounts.
- Makes accounts **uniform, comparable and reliable**.

**Example:** All shops charge depreciation on machine, value stock at cost or market price whichever is less, and record credit sale even if cash is not received — because GAAP says so.

### Why GAAP is needed

1. Same type of business shows results on same base — comparison possible.
2. No personal bias or fraud — accountant cannot change method every year.
3. Bank, investors, government can trust the books.
4. True profit and true position can be known.

**Easy trick:**
> GAAP = traffic rules of accounting. No rules = accident. Same rules = safe comparison.

## 2. Fundamental Accounting Assumptions

As per Accounting Standard-1 (AS-1: Disclosure of Accounting Policies), three assumptions are called **fundamental**. If a business does NOT follow them, it must clearly disclose it.

### 1. Going Concern

> Business will continue for a long time and will not close in near future.

- Machine bought for ₹1,00,000 is shown as fixed asset, not as stock for quick sale — because we assume shop will use it for years.
- Prepaid rent, deferred expenses are carried forward only on this assumption.
- If business is about to close, everything is shown at sale value, not cost.

**Example:** Ramesh buys furniture for ₹25,000 for 10 years. He charges only ₹2,500 depreciation this year. Balance ₹22,500 is carried forward. This is possible only because shop will continue.

### 2. Consistency

> Same accounting method will be followed every year.

- If stock is valued at FIFO this year, same FIFO next year.
- If SLM depreciation this year, same SLM next year.
- Method can change only if law requires it, or new method shows better true picture — but change and its effect must be disclosed.

**Example:** Shop uses Straight Line depreciation for 3 years. In 4th year it suddenly uses Written Down Value only to show less profit and pay less tax — not allowed without disclosure.

### 3. Accrual

> Income and expenses are recorded when they are earned or incurred, not when cash is received or paid.

- Credit sale of ₹10,000 in March is March sale, even if cash comes in May.
- March salary unpaid ₹8,000 is March expense, even if paid in April.
- Follows Matching Concept and Revenue Recognition.

**Example:** Sold goods ₹20,000 on credit on 31st March + salary for March ₹5,000 still unpaid. Profit of March = 20,000 – 5,000 = ₹15,000. Cash received or paid does not matter.

**Memory table:**

| Assumption | One line | If not followed? |
|------------|----------|------------------|
| **Going Concern** | Business will continue | Must disclose, show assets at sale value |
| **Consistency** | Same method every year | Must disclose change + effect |
| **Accrual** | Record when earned/incurred | Must disclose if cash basis used |

**Exam trap:** Only these 3 are fundamental assumptions. Business Entity, Money Measurement, etc. are concepts, not fundamental assumptions.

## 3. Business Entity, Money Measurement, Accounting Period

### Business Entity Concept

> Business and owner are separate persons.

Already read in Chapter 2, but here it is a base rule:

- Capital owner puts in = liability of business.
- Drawings owner takes = reduction in capital.
- Personal car, house, personal loan are never recorded in shop books.

**Example:** Owner starts shop with ₹1,00,000. Shop shows Cash ₹1,00,000 and Capital ₹1,00,000. Shop owes this to owner.

### Money Measurement Concept

> Only those dealings are recorded which can be measured in money.

- Sale ₹10,000, rent ₹5,000, salary ₹8,000 = recorded.
- Honest workers, good location, quarrel with worker, skill of manager = not recorded, because no rupee value.
- Money is taken as stable — price change / inflation is ignored (limitation).

**Simple example:** Shop has very loyal customers and hardworking staff. Big strength, but Balance Sheet will not show it.

### Accounting Period Concept

> Life of business is divided into equal time gaps (usually 1 year) to find profit regularly.

- Year is normally 1st April to 31st March in India (financial year).
- At every year end: Trading and P&L Account + Balance Sheet are made.
- Without this, owner would have to wait till shop closes to know profit.

**Example:** Shop earns ₹3,00,000 sales from April to March, expenses ₹2,40,000. Profit for this accounting year = ₹60,000. Next year fresh counting starts.

**One-line trick:** Entity = shop separate. Money = only rupees. Period = profit every year.

## 4. Historical Cost, Dual Aspect, Revenue Recognition, Matching

### Historical (Cost) Concept

> Asset is recorded at its original purchase cost, not at present market price.

- Building bought for ₹1,00,000 ten years ago is still shown at ₹1,00,000 (minus depreciation), even if market value is ₹10,00,000 now.
- Cost = purchase price + all expenses to bring it to use (freight, installation).

**Example:** Machine price ₹1,00,000 + installation ₹10,000 = recorded at ₹1,10,000. Next year market price ₹1,50,000 — still shown at cost minus depreciation.

**Exam trap:** Rise in market price is not recorded. Fall may be considered only due to conservatism (stock at cost or NRV whichever less).

### Dual Aspect Concept

> Every transaction has two sides — giving benefit and receiving benefit. Every debit has equal credit.

This gives the Accounting Equation:

```
Assets = Capital + Liabilities
Capital = Assets – Liabilities
```

**Example:**
1. Started business with cash ₹50,000: Cash (asset +) = Capital (owner claim +).
2. Bought goods ₹10,000 on credit from Sohan: Stock (asset +) = Creditor Sohan (liability +).
3. Paid salary ₹5,000 cash: Cash (–) and Capital reduces via expense.

Balance always holds. This is the base of Double Entry System.

### Revenue Recognition (Realisation) Concept

> Revenue is recorded only when it is earned or realised, not when order is received or advance is received.

- Goods sold on credit = sale today, even if cash later.
- Order received for ₹50,000 = no sale yet.
- Advance ₹10,000 received for future supply = not sale, it is liability (unearned income).

**Example:** Cloth shop gets order for ₹30,000 on 10th March, delivers goods on 25th March, receives cash on 5th April. Sale belongs to March (when goods delivered), not April.

### Matching Concept

> Expenses of a period are matched with revenues of that same period to find correct profit.

```
Profit = Revenue of the year – Expenses of the year
```

Adjustments come from this concept:

| Adjustment | Treatment |
|------------|-----------|
| **Outstanding salary** | Add to salary, show as liability |
| **Prepaid rent** | Deduct from rent, show as asset |
| **Accrued income** | Add to income, show as asset |
| **Income received in advance** | Deduct from income, show as liability |
| **Depreciation** | Charge as expense of the year |
| **Closing stock** | Deduct from purchases, shown as asset |

**Example:** Paid rent ₹60,000 for 12 months, but year ends after 10 months. Rent for this year = ₹50,000. Prepaid ₹10,000 is asset for next year. Profit must bear only ₹50,000.

**Easy trick:**
> Cost = old price. Dual = two sides. Revenue = only when earned. Matching = same year expense with same year income.

## 5. Full Disclosure, Objectivity, Conservatism, Materiality

### Full Disclosure Concept

> All important facts that affect decisions must be shown in books.

- Notes to Accounts show contingent liabilities, market value of investments, change in method.
- Window dressing (hiding losses, showing extra profit) is against this.
- Companies Act requires prescribed format of Balance Sheet for this reason.

**Example:** Shop has a court case of ₹50,000 pending. Even if not yet paid, it is shown as footnote — bank must know the risk.

### Verifiability and Objectivity Concept

> Every entry must have proof and must be free from personal bias.

- Voucher / bill / cash memo is needed for every entry.
- Two different accountants should reach same result from same vouchers.
- Depreciation method, stock valuation once chosen should be applied objectively.

**Example:** Purchase ₹5,000 is recorded only after Bill No. 45 is seen. Auditor first checks vouchers.

### Consistency Concept

Already a fundamental assumption (Section 2). As a concept it means:

- Same method for stock, depreciation, provisions every year.
- Comparison of this year vs last year is possible only if method is same.
- Change allowed only for better presentation or legal need, with full disclosure.

### Conservatism (Prudence) Concept

> Anticipate no profit, provide for all possible losses.

- Policy of playing safe.
- Stock valued at cost or net realisable value, whichever is less.
- Provision for doubtful debts, provision for discount on debtors, provision for repairs.
- Joint venture or fictitious profits are never recorded early.

**Example:** Stock cost ₹40,000, market value ₹35,000 = shown at ₹35,000. If market value ₹45,000, still shown at ₹40,000. Loss provided, profit ignored until actually earned.

**Exam trap:** Conservatism does not mean creating secret reserves or showing extra-low profit deliberately. Excess conservatism is also wrong.

### Materiality Concept

> Ignore trivial items, record only items that affect decisions.

- Material = if knowledge changes user's decision.
- Waste-paper basket ₹50 may be shown as expense, not as asset, though it lasts 2 years — too small to bother.
- A ₹5,00,000 machine error is material, a ₹50 rounding error is not.

**Example:** Pencils worth ₹200 used in office are charged as stationery expense at once, not spread over life, because amount is immaterial.

**One-line trick:** Disclosure = tell all. Objectivity = with proof. Conservatism = no advance profit, all losses provided. Materiality = small items ignore. Consistency = same method.

| Basis | Conservatism | Consistency |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| **Meaning** | Provide for losses, ignore expected profits | Same method every year |
| **Purpose** | Safety | Comparability |
| **Example** | Stock at cost or NRV whichever less | FIFO every year |

## 6. Basis of Accounting – Cash vs Accrual

Basis means: on what point of time will we record income and expenses?

### 1. Cash Basis

> Record only when cash is received or paid. Credit and outstanding items ignored.

- Sale on credit = no entry till cash comes.
- Outstanding salary, prepaid rent = no adjustment.
- Simple, but true profit not known. No matching.
- Used by professionals (doctor, lawyer), small shops, charitable trusts for some records. Companies cannot use it.

**Example:** March credit sale ₹20,000 + March salary unpaid ₹5,000.
Cash basis profit in March = 0 (no cash yet). Wrong picture.

### 2. Accrual (Mercantile) Basis

> Record when earned or incurred, whether cash comes or not.

- Credit sale = sale today. Outstanding salary = expense today.
- Follows Matching + Revenue Recognition + Accrual assumption.
- Shows true profit and true position. Required by Companies Act.

**Example:** Same data: Accrual profit = 20,000 – 5,000 = ₹15,000. Correct.

### 3. Hybrid / Mixed Basis

> Mix of both — incomes on cash basis, expenses on accrual basis. Used for tax purposes in some cases.

CBSE / NCERT focuses on first two. Hybrid is only for understanding.

| Basis | Cash Basis | Accrual Basis |
|-------|------------|---------------|
| **Record when?** | Cash received / paid | Earned / incurred |
| **Credit sale?** | No entry till cash | Entry on sale date |
| **Outstanding / Prepaid?** | Ignored | Adjusted |
| **Matching followed?** | No | Yes |
| **True profit?** | No | Yes |
| **Used by?** | Professionals, small units | All companies, GAAP |
| **Example** | Doctor records fees only when patient pays | Shop records credit sale at once |

**Exam trap:** Distinguish "Accrual assumption" vs "Accrual basis" — assumption is the rule, basis is the system that follows that rule.

## 7. System of Accounting – Single vs Double Entry

### Single Entry System

> Incomplete record. Only cash and personal accounts maintained. No full debit-credit for every deal.

- Small sole trader maintains only diary of cash and debtors/creditors.
- Profit is guessed by Statement of Affairs, not exact.
- No Trial Balance possible. Frauds hidden easily.

### Double Entry System

> Complete record. Every transaction has two aspects — debit and credit of equal amount.

- Based on Dual Aspect Concept.
- Journal → Ledger → Trial Balance → Final Accounts possible.
- Errors can be traced. True profit known. Followed by all big businesses and companies.

| Basis | Single Entry | Double Entry |
|-------|--------------|--------------|
| **Record** | Incomplete, only some accounts | Complete, every debit = credit |
| **Trial Balance** | Cannot be prepared | Can be prepared |
| **Profit** | Approximate | Exact |
| **Suitable for** | Very small shop | All firms and companies |
| **Example** | Only cash book + customer diary | Full Journal + Ledger |

**Easy trick:**
> Single = single side, half truth. Double = double side, full truth.

Note: Indian Mahajani Bahi Khata system is an old form of double entry in local language — same logic, different books.

## 8. Accounting Standards and IndAS

### Meaning

> Written rules issued for uniform accounting treatment and disclosure.

- In India issued by **Accounting Standards Board (ASB) of ICAI**, notified by MCA / NACAS.
- Internationally harmonised with **IFRS** (International Financial Reporting Standards).
- **IndAS** = Indian Accounting Standards converged with IFRS.

### Why needed

1. Same transaction shown same way by all companies — comparability.
2. True and fair profit and position.
3. No manipulation by changing methods.
4. Helps auditors, banks, investors to trust books.

**Example:** Without standard, one company charges depreciation 10%, another 20% on same machine — profit cannot be compared. Standard fixes method and disclosure.

### Nature

- Standards are like law for accounts — flexible only within allowed options.
- If fundamental assumption not followed, fact must be disclosed (AS-1).
- Accounting policies, change in policies, and effects must be disclosed.

### Applicability – AS vs IndAS (simple view for Class 11)

- **AS (Accounting Standards):** apply to sole traders, partnership firms, small unlisted companies, non-corporate entities.
- **IndAS:** mandatory in phases from 2016-17 for listed companies, large unlisted companies (net worth ₹250 crore or more), banks, NBFCs, insurance companies.
- Small entities get relaxations, but basic concepts remain same.

**Easy trick:**
> AS = Indian rules. IndAS = Indian rules matched with world rules (IFRS). Big listed companies = IndAS. Small shop = AS logic.

**Exam trap:** Accounting Standards are not rigid like physics laws — they allow choice (e.g. FIFO or Weighted Average for stock) but require consistent use + disclosure.

## 9. Goods and Services Tax (GST) – Basic Idea

GST is part of Theory Base now because tax affects vouchers, sales, purchases and input credit entries.

### What is GST

> Indirect tax levied on supply of goods and services. One nation, one tax from 1st July 2017. Destination-based tax — tax goes to state where goods are consumed.

- Old taxes (excise, VAT, service tax, octroi) merged into GST.
- Two parts in intra-state sale: **CGST + SGST**. Inter-state sale: **IGST**.
- Seller collects GST from buyer and deposits with government, but gets **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** for GST already paid on purchases.

**Example:** Wholesaler buys goods for ₹10,000 + 18% GST ₹1,800 = ₹11,800. Sells for ₹15,000 + 18% GST ₹2,700. He pays government only ₹2,700 – ₹1,800 = ₹900. Tax only on value added (₹5,000).

### Characteristics of GST

1. **Supply-based:** charged when goods/services are supplied, not on manufacture or sale alone.
2. **Destination-based:** tax belongs to consuming state.
3. **Dual model:** CGST (Centre) + SGST (State) on local sale; IGST on inter-state sale.
4. **Input Tax Credit chain:** tax paid on inputs adjusted against tax on output — no tax on tax.
5. **Threshold and composition:** small dealers below limit exempt or pay flat low rate with simple return.
6. **Digital record:** invoice, e-way bill, returns filed online — voucher becomes very important.

### Advantages of GST

1. **Removes cascading (tax on tax):** only value addition taxed due to ITC.
2. **One uniform tax:** same rate across India, easy comparison.
3. **Less evasion:** buyer asks for bill to claim ITC, so seller must show sale.
4. **Lower cost to consumer:** no hidden double tax.
5. **Simple for business:** one return system instead of many taxes.
6. **Helps accounting:** clean purchase-sale-invoice chain improves vouchers and audit trail.

| Before GST | After GST |
|------------|-----------|
| Many taxes: excise + VAT + service tax + octroi | Single GST (CGST + SGST / IGST) |
| Tax on tax (cascading) | ITC removes cascading |
| Different rate in each state | Same rate across India |
| Sale without bill common | Bill needed for ITC, evasion reduced |

**Exam trap:** GST is indirect tax (burden on final consumer, collected by seller). Income tax is direct tax. Discount treatment: trade discount reduces taxable value, cash discount does not.
