# Class 11 Accountancy – Chapter 2: Basic Accounting Terms

> **In one line:** Before passing any journal entry, you must know the language of accounting — what is capital, asset, liability, expense, revenue, stock, debtor, creditor, voucher and discount.

---

## 1. Business Entity and Business Transaction

Imagine you and your shop are two different people.

You put ₹1,00,000 into your shop. In your mind it is still your money. But for accounting, the **shop is separate from you**.

### Entity (Business Entity)

> Business and owner are treated as two separate persons for accounting.

- Money owner puts in = **Capital** (shop owes it to owner).
- Shop's profit = shop's profit, not owner's personal income until withdrawn.
- Owner takes goods for home = **Drawings** (must be recorded).

**Example:** Ramesh starts a shop with ₹50,000 cash. Shop will show: Cash ₹50,000 + Capital ₹50,000. Ramesh and shop are separate.

### Business Transaction

> Any money dealing of business that can be measured in rupees and supported by a bill.

Conditions for a transaction:
1. Must be in **money terms**.
2. Must affect business.
3. Must have proof (bill / voucher).

| Is it a transaction? | Yes / No | Why |
|----------------------|----------|-----|
| Bought stock for ₹10,000 | Yes | Money + bill |
| Paid salary ₹8,000 | Yes | Money + receipt |
| Quarrel with worker | No | No money value |
| Honest workers, good location | No | Cannot measure in ₹ |
| Owner promises to bring more money next month | No | Not happened yet |

## 2. Capital and Drawings

### Capital

> Money, goods or assets owner puts into business to start or run it.

- Shown on **liabilities side** of Balance Sheet, because business owes it to owner.
- Increases with fresh capital + profit, decreases with drawings + loss.

```
Capital = Assets – Liabilities
```

**Example:**
- Started business with cash ₹1,00,000 + stock ₹20,000 = Capital ₹1,20,000.
- Earned profit ₹30,000 = Capital becomes ₹1,50,000.

### Drawings

> Cash, goods or assets owner takes out from business for personal use.

- Opposite of capital. Reduces capital.
- Goods taken for home, school fees of son paid from shop cash, personal car petrol paid from shop = all drawings.

**Example:** Owner takes ₹5,000 cash + goods worth ₹2,000 for home. Total drawings = ₹7,000. Capital will reduce by ₹7,000.

**Easy trick:**
> Capital = owner gives to business. Drawings = owner takes from business.

| Basis | Capital | Drawings |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Meaning** | Owner puts in | Owner takes out |
| **Effect** | Capital increases | Capital decreases |
| **Example** | ₹1,00,000 to start shop | ₹5,000 for home use |

## 3. Liabilities – What Business Owes

> Amount business owes to outsiders and to owner.

Two main types as per NCERT / CBSE:

### 1. Non-Current (Long-term) Liabilities
Payable after more than 1 year.

- Long-term loan from bank, debentures, loan from financial institution.
- Example: 5-year loan of ₹2,00,000 from SBI.

### 2. Current (Short-term) Liabilities
Payable within 1 year.

- Short-term loan, bank overdraft, creditors (suppliers to be paid), outstanding salary, unpaid bills.
- Example: Goods bought on credit ₹15,000 to be paid next month = creditor = current liability.

**Memory table:**

| Liability | Pay when? | Example |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Non-Current** | After 1 year | 5-year bank loan, debentures |
| **Current** | Within 1 year | Creditors, outstanding rent, bank overdraft |

Capital is also called **internal liability** because business owes it to owner.

## 4. Assets – What Business Owns

> Anything owned by business which gives future benefit and has money value.

### 1. Non-Current (Fixed) Assets
Used for long time, not for sale, kept for running business.

- Land, building, machine, furniture, computers, vehicles.
- Example: Shop bought a counter for ₹25,000 to use for 10 years = fixed asset.

### 2. Current Assets
Cash or items that will convert into cash within 1 year.

- Cash in hand, bank balance, stock, debtors (customers who will pay later), prepaid rent, short-term investments.
- Example: Stock ₹40,000, debtors ₹10,000, cash ₹5,000 = all current assets.

Simple test used in exams:
- **For use** = fixed asset. **For sale / to become cash soon** = current asset.

| Basis | Non-Current Asset | Current Asset |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Use** | Used to run business | Held to sell or convert to cash |
| **Time** | Stays more than 1 year | Converts within 1 year |
| **Example** | Machine, furniture, building | Cash, stock, debtors |

**One more useful word:**
- **Fictitious assets** = expenses not yet written off, like heavy advertisement cost spread over years. No real value, but shown as asset for some time. NCERT asks this as trap.

**Formula to remember:**
```
Assets = Capital + Liabilities
```

## 5. Expenditure, Expense – Most Confusing Pair

Students mix these two every year. Learn with shop example.

### Expenditure
> Amount spent to buy an asset or to get a benefit for long time or short time.

Two types:

**1. Capital Expenditure**
- Spent to buy fixed asset or to increase its power.
- Benefit for many years.
- Shown in Balance Sheet as asset.
- Example: Bought machine ₹1,00,000, spent ₹10,000 on its installation, built a shed ₹50,000.

**2. Revenue Expenditure**
- Spent to run business day-to-day. Benefit only for this year.
- Shown in Trading and Profit & Loss Account.
- Example: Rent ₹5,000, salary ₹8,000, repair of machine ₹500, purchase of goods ₹20,000.

### Expense
> That part of expenditure whose benefit is used up in this year.

Simple way:
> All expenses are expenditures, but all expenditures are not expenses.

**Example:** Bought goods for ₹20,000 (expenditure). Sold goods costing ₹12,000. Then expense = ₹12,000. Balance stock ₹8,000 is still asset, not expense yet.

| Basis | Capital Expenditure | Revenue Expenditure |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Purpose** | Buy fixed asset | Run daily business |
| **Benefit** | Many years | Current year only |
| **Shown in** | Balance Sheet | Profit & Loss Account |
| **Example** | Machine, building, furniture | Rent, salary, repairs, goods |

**Exam trap:** Repair that increases life of machine (e.g. new engine) = capital expenditure. Normal repair = revenue expenditure.

## 6. Revenue, Income, Profit, Gain, Loss

### Revenue
> Money earned from main work of business.

- For a shop = sale of goods.
- For a tailor = stitching charges.
- For a transport company = freight received.

Example: Cloth shop sells cloth for ₹80,000 = revenue ₹80,000.

### Income
> Revenue minus expense of earning that revenue. Wider term than revenue.

```
Income = Revenue – Expense
```

In normal language we say profit, but in accounts income = profit from operations + other income.

### Profit
> When earning is more than cost.

```
Profit = Revenue – Expense (when positive)
Gross Profit = Sales – Cost of goods sold
Net Profit = Gross Profit – All other expenses
```

Example: Sold goods for ₹50,000, cost of those goods ₹30,000, shop rent + salary ₹10,000.
- Gross Profit = 50,000 – 30,000 = ₹20,000
- Net Profit = 20,000 – 10,000 = ₹10,000

### Gain
> Profit from side work, not main work. Unexpected.

- Sold old machine (book value ₹20,000) for ₹25,000 = gain ₹5,000.
- Won a court case compensation = gain.

Profit = from main business. Gain = from other activities.

### Loss
> When cost is more than earning, or asset value reduces.

- Sold goods costing ₹30,000 for ₹25,000 = loss ₹5,000.
- Machine destroyed by fire = loss.
- Goods stolen = loss.

**One-line trick:** Revenue – Expense = Profit (if +), Loss (if –). Side profit = Gain.

## 7. Purchase, Sales, Goods, Stock

### Purchase
> Goods bought for resale or for making finished products.

- Cash purchase = paid at once.
- Credit purchase = will pay later.
- Only goods purchase is called purchase. Machine purchase is not purchase, it is asset bought.

Example: Cloth shop buys cloth for ₹30,000 to sell = purchase. Same shop buys counter for ₹10,000 = asset, not purchase.

### Sales
> Goods sold that were bought for resale.

- Cash sales + credit sales.
- Old machine sold is not sales, it is asset sold.

### Goods
> Articles bought to resell at profit. What a business deals in.

- For cloth shop, cloth = goods. Furniture in shop = asset, not goods.
- For furniture shop, chairs = goods.

### Stock (Inventory)
> Unsold goods lying in shop at a point of time.

- **Opening stock** = stock at start of year.
- **Closing stock** = stock at end of year.
- Closing stock of this year = opening stock of next year.

Example: Started year with stock ₹15,000 (opening). Bought ₹40,000. Sold goods costing ₹35,000. Closing stock = 15,000 + 40,000 – 35,000 = ₹20,000.

**Formula used in Trading Account:**
```
Cost of Goods Sold = Opening Stock + Purchases – Closing Stock
```

## 8. Debtor, Creditor, Voucher

### Debtor
> Person who owes money TO business. Customer who bought on credit.

- Business will receive money. Debtor is an asset.
- Example: Sold goods ₹10,000 on credit to Mohan. Mohan = debtor.

### Creditor
> Person to whom business owes money. Supplier who gave goods on credit.

- Business will pay money. Creditor is a liability.
- Example: Bought goods ₹12,000 on credit from wholesaler Sohan. Sohan = creditor.

**Trick:**
> Debtor = will GIVE money. Creditor = will TAKE money.

| Basis | Debtor | Creditor |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Who?** | Customer | Supplier |
| **Why?** | Bought on credit from us | Sold on credit to us |
| **Business will?** | Receive money | Pay money |
| **Shown as?** | Current asset | Current liability |

### Voucher
> Written proof of a transaction. Base of accounting.

- Cash memo, bill, receipt, salary slip, bank statement.
- No voucher = no entry.
- Auditor first checks vouchers.

Example: Bill No. 45 for ₹5,000 purchase = voucher for purchase entry.

## 9. Discount – Trade Discount vs Cash Discount

Shopkeepers give two types of discount. Exams ask difference every year.

### Trade Discount
> Discount given on printed price to increase sales or to resellers. Given at time of sale.

- Given on list price, e.g. 10% off on ₹1,000 = sale at ₹900.
- **Not recorded separately** in books. Entry is passed with net amount (₹900).
- Example: Wholesaler gives retailer 10% trade discount.

### Cash Discount
> Discount given for quick / early payment. To get cash fast.

- E.g. Bill ₹10,000, pay within 7 days get 2% off = pay ₹9,800.
- **Recorded separately** in books as expense (discount allowed) or income (discount received).
- Example: Debtor pays early, we allow ₹200 discount.

| Basis | Trade Discount | Cash Discount |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| **Purpose** | Increase sales | Get early payment |
| **When?** | At time of sale | At time of payment |
| **On what?** | List price | Amount due |
| **Recorded?** | No separate entry, net sale recorded | Separate entry in books |
| **Example** | 10% off on price tag | 2% off if paid in 7 days |

**Exam trap:** Both discounts can come together. First cut trade discount to find sale value, then if early payment, give cash discount. Trade discount is never shown, cash discount is always shown.

Example: List price ₹10,000, 10% trade discount, then 2% cash discount on early payment.
- Sale = 10,000 – 10% = ₹9,000 (recorded).
- If paid early: cash discount = 2% of 9,000 = ₹180.

## 📝 Key Terms to Remember

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|------|----------------|
| **Entity** | Business separate from owner |
| **Transaction** | Money dealing with proof |
| **Capital** | Owner's money in business |
| **Drawings** | Owner's taking from business |
| **Liability** | What business owes (current / non-current) |
| **Asset** | What business owns (current / non-current) |
| **Capital Expenditure** | Spent on fixed asset, long benefit |
| **Revenue Expenditure** | Spent to run business, this year benefit |
| **Expense** | Used-up cost of this year |
| **Revenue** | Earning from main work |
| **Income / Profit** | Revenue minus expense |
| **Gain** | Side profit, not main work |
| **Loss** | Cost more than earning |
| **Purchase / Sales** | Goods bought / sold for resale |
| **Goods** | Items business deals in |
| **Stock** | Unsold goods (opening / closing) |
| **Debtor** | Will give money (customer) |
| **Creditor** | Will take money (supplier) |
| **Voucher** | Written proof / bill |
| **Trade Discount** | Off on price, not recorded separately |
| **Cash Discount** | Off for early payment, recorded separately |

## 🔄 Quick Recap

```
Entity = Business separate from owner
Transaction = Money dealing + proof

Capital = Owner gives. Drawings = Owner takes.
Assets = Capital + Liabilities

Liabilities: Current (within 1 yr) / Non-Current (after 1 yr)
Assets: Current (cash/stock/debtors) / Non-Current (machine/building)

Capital Exp. = Asset, Balance Sheet. Revenue Exp. = Running cost, P&L.
Revenue – Expense = Profit / Loss. Side profit = Gain.

Purchase/Sales = only goods. Stock = unsold goods.
Debtor = to receive. Creditor = to pay. Voucher = proof.
Trade Discount = on price, no entry. Cash Discount = on payment, entry.
```

## ❓ Important Exam Questions

**Short Questions (2-3 marks):**
1. Define business entity with one example.
2. What is a business transaction? Give two examples and two non-examples.
3. Distinguish between capital and drawings.
4. What are current and non-current liabilities? Give two examples each.
5. Distinguish between fixed and current assets.
6. Distinguish between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure.
7. Distinguish between expense and expenditure with example.
8. Distinguish between profit and gain with example.
9. Who is a debtor and creditor? Give one example each.
10. Distinguish between trade discount and cash discount.

**Long Questions (4-6 marks):**
1. Explain any six basic accounting terms with examples.
2. Explain capital, revenue, income, profit, gain and loss with one example each.
3. Explain purchase, sales, goods and stock. How is closing stock calculated?
4. Explain assets and liabilities with full classification and examples.
5. Explain trade discount and cash discount. How are they treated in books?
